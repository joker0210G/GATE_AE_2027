### Question 01 `[MCQ — 1 Mark]`
For an $n \times n$ matrix $A$, a scalar $\lambda$ is an **Eigenvalue** if and only if there exists a vector $\vec{v}$ such that $A\vec{v} = \lambda\vec{v}$, with the mandatory mathematical condition that:
- (A) $\vec{v}$ must be the zero vector $\vec{0}$
- (B) $\vec{v}$ must be a non-zero vector ($\vec{v} \neq \vec{0}$)
- (C) $\lambda$ must be strictly positive
- (D) $\det(A)$ must equal zero

> [!faq]- 🔍 View Solution & Detailed Explanation
> **Correct Answer:** **(B) $\vec{v}$ must be a non-zero vector ($\vec{v} \neq \vec{0}$)**
> 
> **Fundamental Definition:**
> $A\vec{v} = \lambda\vec{v}$ always holds trivially for $\vec{v} = \vec{0}$ for ANY scalar $\lambda$. Therefore, an eigenvector is strictly defined as a **NON-ZERO vector** ($\vec{v} \neq \vec{0}$) that satisfies the transformation.

### Question 02 `[NAT — 1 Mark]`
The eigenvalues of the $3 \times 3$ upper triangular matrix:
$$A = \begin{pmatrix} 4 & 7 & 9 \\ 0 & -2 & 5 \\ 0 & 0 & 8 \end{pmatrix}$$

> [!faq]- 🔍 View Solution & Detailed Explanation

> 
> **Key Formula / Concept (Triangular Matrix Spectra):**
> For any Upper Triangular Matrix (UTM), Lower Triangular Matrix (LTM), or Diagonal Matrix, the eigenvalues are PRECISELY its principal diagonal entries:
> $$\lambda_1 = 4, \quad \lambda_2 = -2, \quad \lambda_3 = 8$$
> 
> **Step-by-Step Solution:**
> $$\sum \lambda_i = \text{tr}(A) = 4 + (-2) + 8 = 10$$

### Question 08 `[MCQ — 1 Mark]`
If $\lambda$ is an eigenvalue of matrix $A$ with corresponding eigenvector $\vec{v}$, then for any non-zero scalar $c$, the vector $c\vec{v}$ is:
- (A) An eigenvector corresponding to eigenvalue $c\lambda$
- (B) An eigenvector corresponding to the same eigenvalue $\lambda$
- (C) Not an eigenvector of $A$
- (D) An eigenvector corresponding to eigenvalue $\lambda/c$

> [!faq]- 🔍 View Solution & Detailed Explanation
> **Correct Answer:** **(B) An eigenvector corresponding to the same eigenvalue $\lambda$**
> 
> **Eigenspace Linearity:**
> $$A(c\vec{v}) = c(A\vec{v}) = c(\lambda\vec{v}) = \lambda(c\vec{v})$$
> Any non-zero scalar multiple of an eigenvector lies in the same 1-dimensional eigenspace and corresponds to the identical eigenvalue $\lambda$.

### Question 09 `[NAT — 1 Mark]`
The eigenvalues of the $2 \times 2$ matrix $A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 4 \\ 2 & 3 \end{pmatrix}$ are $\lambda_1$ and $\lambda_2$ with $\lambda_1 > \lambda_2$. The value of $\lambda_1 - \lambda_2$ is ________ (Answer in integer).

> [!faq]- 🔍 View Solution & Detailed Explanation
> **Correct Answer:** **$6$** (Acceptable range: `6` to `6`)
> 
> **Step-by-Step Solution:**
> 1. $\text{tr}(A) = 1 + 3 = 4 \implies \lambda_1 + \lambda_2 = 4$.
> 2. $\det(A) = (1)(3) - (4)(2) = 3 - 8 = -5 \implies \lambda_1 \lambda_2 = -5$.
> 3. Characteristic equation: $\lambda^2 - 4\lambda - 5 = 0 \implies (\lambda - 5)(\lambda + 1) = 0$.
> 4. Eigenvalues: $\lambda_1 = 5, \lambda_2 = -1$.
> 5. Difference: $\lambda_1 - \lambda_2 = 5 - (-1) = 6$.

### Question 10 `[MCQ — 1 Mark]`
The eigenvalues of a $2 \times 2$ matrix $A$ are $3$ and $-2$. The determinant of the matrix $(A - I)$ is:
- (A) $-6$
- (B) $-4$
- (C) $6$
- (D) $0$

> [!faq]- 🔍 View Solution & Detailed Explanation
> **Correct Answer:** **(A) $-6$**
> 
> **Key Formula / Concept (Eigenvalue Shift Property):**
> If $A$ has eigenvalues $\lambda_i$, then $(A - k I)$ has eigenvalues $(\lambda_i - k)$.
> 
> **Step-by-Step Solution:**
> 1. Eigenvalues of $A - I$ are:
>    - $\mu_1 = 3 - 1 = 2$
>    - $\mu_2 = -2 - 1 = -3$
> 2. Determinant is the product of eigenvalues:
>    $$\det(A - I) = \mu_1 \mu_2 = (2)(-3) = -6$$

### Question 15 `[MCQ — 1 Mark]`
For an eigenvalue $\lambda$ of a matrix $A$, let $\text{AM}(\lambda)$ be its Algebraic Multiplicity (multiplicity as a root of the characteristic polynomial) and $\text{GM}(\lambda) = \dim N(A - \lambda I)$ be its Geometric Multiplicity (number of linearly independent eigenvectors). ### Question 16 `[NAT — 1 Mark]`
Let $A$ be a $3 \times 3$ matrix with $\det(A) = 12$ and eigenvalues $2, 3, \lambda_3$. The largest eigenvalue of the **Adjoint Matrix** $\text{adj}(A)$ is ________ (Answer in integer).

> [!faq]- 🔍 View Solution & Detailed Explanation
> **Correct Answer:** **$6$** (Acceptable range: `6` to `6`)
> 
> **Key Formula / Concept (Eigenvalues of Adjoint Matrix):**
> For an invertible matrix $A$:
> $$\lambda_i(\text{adj}(A)) = \frac{\det(A)}{\lambda_i(A)}$$
> 
> **Step-by-Step Solution:**
> 1. $\det(A) = 12 = 2 \times 3 \times \lambda_3 \implies 6 \lambda_3 = 12 \implies \lambda_3 = 2$.
> 2. Eigenvalues of $\text{adj}(A)$ are:
>    - $\mu_1 = \frac{12}{2} = 6$
>    - $\mu_2 = \frac{12}{3} = 4$
>    - $\mu_3 = \frac{12}{2} = 6$
> 3. The largest eigenvalue is $6$.

### Question 23 `[MCQ — 2 Marks]`
A $3 \times 3$ matrix $A$ satisfies the polynomial equation:

According to the **Cayley-Hamilton Theorem**, the inverse matrix $A^{-1}$ can be computed as:

- (B) $6(A^2 - 6A + 11I)$

> 3. Isolate $A^{-1}$:
>    $$A^{-1} = \frac{1}{6}(A^2 - 6A + 11I)$$

### Question 31 `[MCQ — 2 Marks]`
According to the **Rayleigh-Ritz Theorem**, for any real symmetric $n \times n$ matrix $A$ with minimum eigenvalue $\lambda_{\min}$ and maximum eigenvalue $\lambda_{\max}$, the Rayleigh quotient $R(\vec{x}) = \frac{\vec{x}^T A \vec{x}}{\vec{x}^T \vec{x}}$ satisfies:
- (A) $\lambda_{\min} \le \frac{\vec{x}^T A \vec{x}}{\vec{x}^T \vec{x}} \le \lambda_{\max}$ for all $\vec{x} \neq \vec{0}$
- (B) $\frac{\vec{x}^T A \vec{x}}{\vec{x}^T \vec{x}} = \text{tr}(A)$
- (C) $\frac{\vec{x}^T A \vec{x}}{\vec{x}^T \vec{x}} \ge \det(A)$
- (D) $\frac{\vec{x}^T A \vec{x}}{\vec{x}^T \vec{x}} = 1$

> [!faq]- 🔍 View Solution & Detailed Explanation

> 
> **Rayleigh-Ritz Variational Theorem:**
> The Rayleigh quotient is strictly bounded between the smallest and largest eigenvalues of a real symmetric matrix, achieving the minimum at the eigenvector of $\lambda_{\min}$ and the maximum at the eigenvector of $\lambda_{\max}$.

### Question 33 `[MCQ — 2 Marks]`
According to the **Gershgorin Circle Theorem**, every eigenvalue of the matrix:
$$A = \begin{pmatrix} 10 & 1 & 2 \\ 1 & 5 & 1 \\ 2 & 2 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$$
must lie within the union of three closed discs in the complex plane. The maximum possible real value that ANY eigenvalue of $A$ can attain is bounded above by:
- (A) $10$
- (B) $13$
- (C) $7$
- (D) $5$

> [!faq]- 🔍 View Solution & Detailed Explanation
> **Correct Answer:** **(B) $13$**
> 
> **Gershgorin Disc Radii Calculation:**
> 1. Row 1 Disc: Center $c_1 = 10$, Radius $R_1 = |1| + |2| = 3 \implies [10 - 3, 10 + 3] = [7, 13]$.
> 2. Row 2 Disc: Center $c_2 = 5$, Radius $R_2 = |1| + |1| = 2 \implies [5 - 2, 5 + 2] = [3, 7]$.
> 3. Row 3 Disc: Center $c_3 = 1$, Radius $R_3 = |2| + |2| = 4 \implies [1 - 4, 1 + 4] = [-3, 5]$.
> 4. Maximum upper bound $= 10 + 3 = 13$.

### Question 40 `[NAT — 2 Marks]`
Let $A$ be a $3 \times 3$ matrix with $\text{tr}(A) = 6, \text{tr}(A^2) = 14, \text{tr}(A^3) = 36$. The determinant $\det(A)$ is ________ (Answer in integer).

> [!faq]- 🔍 View Solution & Detailed Explanation
> **Correct Answer:** **$6$** (Acceptable range: `6` to `6`)
>

> Let $p_k = \sum \lambda_i^k = \text{tr}(A^k)$:
> 1. $e_1 = p_1 = 6$.
> 2. $e_2 = \frac{1}{2}(e_1 p_1 - p_2) = \frac{1}{2}(6 \times 6 - 14) = \frac{22}{2} = 11$.

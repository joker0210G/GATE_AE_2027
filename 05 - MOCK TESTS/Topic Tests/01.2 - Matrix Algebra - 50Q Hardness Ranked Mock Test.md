### Question 01 `[MCQ — 1 Mark]`
Let $A$ and $B$ be two $3 \times 3$ matrices with real entries. Which of the following statements is **ALWAYS TRUE**?
- (A) $(A + B)^T = A^T + B^T$
- (B) $(A B)^T = A^T B^T$
- (C) $A B = B A$
- (D) $\text{tr}(A B) = \text{tr}(A) \cdot \text{tr}(B)$

> [!faq]- 🔍 View Solution & Detailed Explanation
> **Correct Answer:** **(A) $(A + B)^T = A^T + B^T$**
> 
> **Key Formula / Concept:**
> Matrix transpose is a linear operation: $(A + B)^T = A^T + B^T$ and $(k A)^T = k A^T$.
> 
> **Step-by-Step Analysis of Options:**
> - (A) **TRUE:** Transpose distributes directly across addition. ✅
> - (B) **FALSE:** Matrix transpose follows the **Reversal Law**: $(A B)^T = B^T A^T$. ❌
> - (C) **FALSE:** Matrix multiplication is non-commutative in general ($A B \neq B A$). ❌
> - (D) **FALSE:** Trace is linear over addition ($\text{tr}(A+B) = \text{tr}(A)+\text{tr}(B)$), but NOT multiplicative ($\text{tr}(AB) \neq \text{tr}(A)\text{tr}(B)$). ❌

### Question 02 `[NAT — 1 Mark]`
The determinant of the $3 \times 3$ matrix:
$$A = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 0 & 0 \\ 4 & 5 & 0 \\ 7 & 8 & 3 \end{pmatrix}$$
is ________ (Answer in integer).

> [!faq]- 🔍 View Solution & Detailed Explanation
> **Correct Answer:** **$30$** (Acceptable range: `30` to `30`)
> 
> **Key Formula / Concept (Triangular Matrix Determinant):**
> For any Lower Triangular Matrix (LTM), Upper Triangular Matrix (UTM), or Diagonal Matrix, the determinant is the product of its principal diagonal elements:
> $$\det(A) = \prod_{i=1}^n a_{ii} = a_{11} \cdot a_{22} \cdots a_{nn}$$
> 
> **Step-by-Step Solution:**
> 1. Identify diagonal entries: $a_{11} = 2$, $a_{22} = 5$, $a_{33} = 3$.
> 2. Multiply diagonal elements:
>    $$\det(A) = 2 \times 5 \times 3 = 30$$

### Question 03 `[MCQ — 1 Mark]`
If $A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 3 & 4 \end{pmatrix}$ and $B = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 0 \\ 1 & 3 \end{pmatrix}$, the trace of the matrix $(2A - 3B)$ is:
- (A) $-5$
- (B) $-10$
- (C) $5$
- (D) $10$

> [!faq]- 🔍 View Solution & Detailed Explanation
> **Correct Answer:** **(A) $-5$**
> 
> **Key Formula / Concept:**
> Trace is a linear functional:
> $$\text{tr}(c_1 A + c_2 B) = c_1 \text{tr}(A) + c_2 \text{tr}(B)$$
> 
> **Step-by-Step Solution:**
> 1. $\text{tr}(A) = 1 + 4 = 5$
> 2. $\text{tr}(B) = 2 + 3 = 5$
> 3. Compute $\text{tr}(2A - 3B)$:
>    $$\text{tr}(2A - 3B) = 2\text{tr}(A) - 3\text{tr}(B) = 2(5) - 3(5) = 10 - 15 = -5$$

### Question 04 `[NAT — 1 Mark]`
If $A = \begin{pmatrix} 3 & 1 \\ 2 & 4 \end{pmatrix}$, the determinant of the matrix $A^2$ is ________ (Answer in integer).

> [!faq]- 🔍 View Solution & Detailed Explanation
> **Correct Answer:** **$100$** (Acceptable range: `100` to `100`)
> 
> **Key Formula / Concept (Determinant Power Rule):**
> $$\det(A^k) = (\det A)^k$$
> 
> **Step-by-Step Solution:**
> 1. Compute $\det(A)$:
>    $$\det(A) = (3)(4) - (1)(2) = 12 - 2 = 10$$
> 2. Apply the power rule for $k = 2$:
>    $$\det(A^2) = (\det A)^2 = 10^2 = 100$$
> 
> **⚠️ Common Trap & Exam Tip:**
> Never multiply $A \times A$ to get a new $2 \times 2$ matrix before calculating its determinant. Compute $\det(A)$ first and square it in 3 seconds!

### Question 05 `[MCQ — 1 Mark]`
The number of independent elements in a real $n \times n$ **symmetric** matrix is:
- (A) $n^2$
- (B) $\frac{n(n-1)}{2}$
- (C) $\frac{n(n+1)}{2}$
- (D) $n(n-1)$

> [!faq]- 🔍 View Solution & Detailed Explanation
> **Correct Answer:** **(C) $\frac{n(n+1)}{2}$**
> 
> **Key Formula / Concept:**
> In an $n \times n$ symmetric matrix ($A^T = A$), elements below the main diagonal are fixed by the elements above the main diagonal ($a_{ji} = a_{ij}$).
> 
> **Step-by-Step Count:**
> 1. Diagonal elements: $n$ independent choices.
> 2. Strictly upper triangular elements: $\frac{n^2 - n}{2} = \frac{n(n-1)}{2}$ choices.
> 3. Total independent elements:
>    $$n + \frac{n(n-1)}{2} = \frac{2n + n^2 - n}{2} = \frac{n(n+1)}{2}$$

### Question 06 `[NAT — 1 Mark]`
Let $A$ be a $3 \times 3$ skew-symmetric matrix. The value of $\det(A)$ is ________ (Answer in integer).

> [!faq]- 🔍 View Solution & Detailed Explanation
> **Correct Answer:** **$0$** (Acceptable range: `0` to `0`)
> 
> **Key Formula / Concept (Odd-Order Skew-Symmetric Determinant):**
> For any skew-symmetric matrix $A$ of **odd order** ($n = 1, 3, 5, \dots$):
> $$\det(A) = 0$$
> 
> **Proof:**
> 1. By definition, $A^T = -A$.
> 2. Taking determinant on both sides:
>    $$\det(A^T) = \det(-A) = (-1)^n \det(A)$$
> 3. Since $\det(A^T) = \det(A)$ and $n = 3$ is odd ($(-1)^3 = -1$):
>    $$\det(A) = -\det(A) \implies 2\det(A) = 0 \implies \det(A) = 0$$

### Question 08 `[NAT — 1 Mark]`
The inverse of the $2 \times 2$ matrix $A = \begin{pmatrix} 5 & 2 \\ 2 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$ is $A^{-1} = \begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{pmatrix}$. The value of $a + d$ is ________ (Answer in integer).

>    $$A^{-1} = \frac{1}{1} \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -2 \\ -2 & 5 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -2 \\ -2 & 5 \end{pmatrix}$$
> 3. Values: $a = 1, d = 5 \implies a + d = 1 + 5 = 6$.

### Question 10 `[NAT — 1 Mark]`
Let $A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 & 3 \\ 0 & 4 & 5 \\ 0 & 0 & 6 \end{pmatrix}$. The trace of the inverse matrix $A^{-1}$ is ________ (Round off to 2 decimal places).

>    $$\text{tr}(A^{-1}) = 1 + \frac{1}{4} + \frac{1}{6} = 1 + 0.25 + 0.1667 = 1.4167 \approx 1.42$$

### Question 11 `[MCQ — 1 Mark]`
Let $A$ be a $4 \times 4$ real matrix with $\det(A) = 3$. The determinant of the matrix $2 A$ is:
- (A) $6$
- (B) $24$
- (C) $48$
- (D) $81$

> [!faq]- 🔍 View Solution & Detailed Explanation
> **Correct Answer:** **(C) $48$**
> 
> **Key Formula / Concept (The Golden GATE Determinant Scaling Law):**
> For an $n \times n$ matrix $A$ and any scalar $k$:
> $$\det(k A) = k^n \det(A)$$
> 
> **Step-by-Step Solution:**
> 1. Given $n = 4$, $k = 2$, and $\det(A) = 3$.
> 2. Apply formula:
>    $$\det(2 A) = 2^4 \cdot \det(A) = 16 \times 3 = 48$$
> 
> **⚖️ Matrix vs. Determinant Scalar Multiplication Comparison:**
> - **Matrix Scalar Multiplication ($K [A]$):** Multiplies **EVERY element** in the matrix.
>   $$K \begin{bmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} Ka & Kb \\ Kc & Kd \end{bmatrix}$$
> - **Determinant Scalar Multiplication ($K |A|$):** Multiplies **ONLY ONE row or column**.
>   $$K \begin{vmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{vmatrix} = \begin{vmatrix} Ka & Kb \\ c & d \end{vmatrix}$$
> 
> **⚠️ Related GATE Variant ($6 \times 6$ Matrix Trap):**
> If $A$ and $B$ are $6 \times 6$ matrices with $A = 2 B$ and $\det(B) = \Delta$, then:
> $$\det(A) = \det(2 B) = 2^6 \det(B) = \mathbf{64 \Delta} \quad (\text{Trap: } 2\Delta \text{ is WRONG!})$$

### Question 13 `[MSQ — 1 Mark]`
Let $A$ and $B$ be two $n \times n$ non-singular matrices. Which of the following statements are **ALWAYS TRUE**? *(Select all that apply)*
- [ ] (A) $\det(A B) = \det(B A)$
- [ ] (B) $\det(A + B) = \det(A) + \det(B)$
- [ ] (C) $\text{tr}(A B) = \text{tr}(B A)$
- [ ] (D) $(A B)^{-1} = A^{-1} B^{-1}$

> [!faq]- 🔍 View Solution & Detailed Explanation
> **Correct Answer:** **(A) and (C)**
> 
> **Concept Breakdown:**
> - **(A) TRUE:** $\det(AB) = \det(A)\det(B) = \det(B)\det(A) = \det(BA)$ (scalar multiplication is commutative). ✅
> - **(B) FALSE:** Determinant is NOT linear over full matrix addition: $\det(A + B) \neq \det(A) + \det(B)$ in general (e.g., $\det(I + I) = \det(2I) = 2^n \neq 1 + 1 = 2$). ❌
> - **(C) TRUE:** Cyclic permutation property of trace: $\text{tr}(AB) = \text{tr}(BA)$. ✅
> - **(D) FALSE:** Matrix inverse follows the **Reversal Law**: $(AB)^{-1} = B^{-1} A^{-1}$. ❌
> 
> **💡 Sum Split Property of Determinants:**
> While $\det(A + B) \neq \det(A) + \det(B)$ for whole matrices, if elements of **only one row (or column)** are expressed as sums, the determinant splits into two determinants across that single row:
> $$\begin{vmatrix} a_1 + \lambda_1 & a_2 + \lambda_2 \\ b_1 & b_2 \end{vmatrix} = \begin{vmatrix} a_1 & a_2 \\ b_1 & b_2 \end{vmatrix} + \begin{vmatrix} \lambda_1 & \lambda_2 \\ b_1 & b_2 \end{vmatrix}$$
> 
> ⚠️ **Simultaneous & Target Row Operation Rules:**
> 1. **Target Row Scaling Trap:** $R_i \to c R_i + K R_j$ with $c \neq 1$ multiplies the determinant by $c$ ($\Delta_{\text{new}} = c \Delta$). E.g., $R_3 \to 3 R_3 - 4 R_1 \implies \Delta_{\text{new}} = 3\Delta \neq \Delta$!
> 2. **Mixed Row & Column Operations:** Performing row and column operations in the same step (e.g. $R_2 \to R_2 + 3R_1$ AND $C_3 \to C_3 + 2C_2$) is STRICTLY INVALID.
> 3. **Dependent Chain Operations:** Modifying a row while simultaneously using it to modify another row (e.g. $R_2 \to R_2 + 3R_1$ AND $R_3 \to R_3 + 2R_2$) is INVALID.
> 4. **$4 \times 4$ Reduction Shortcut:** Create 3 zeros in a column via valid row operations to reduce a $4 \times 4$ determinant directly to a single $3 \times 3$ cofactor evaluation!

### Question 14 `[NAT — 1 Mark]`
Let $A = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 4 \\ 1 & 6 \end{pmatrix}$. Matrix $A$ is uniquely decomposed as $A = P + Q$, where $P$ is a symmetric matrix and $Q$ is a skew-symmetric matrix. The entry $Q_{12}$ of matrix $Q$ is ________ (Round off to 1 decimal place).

If $A$ is an $n \times n$ matrix such that $A^2 = A$ (Idempotent Matrix), then the determinant of $A$ can only be:

### Question 16 `[NAT — 1 Mark]`

### Question 19 `[MCQ — 1 Mark]`
If $A$ and $B$ are symmetric matrices of the same order, then the matrix $(A B - B A)$ is ALWAYS:
- (A) Symmetric
- (B) Skew-Symmetric
- (C) Orthogonal
- (D) Diagonal

> [!faq]- 🔍 View Solution & Detailed Explanation
> **Correct Answer:** **(B) Skew-Symmetric**
> 
> **Step-by-Step Proof:**
> 1. Given $A^T = A$ and $B^T = B$.
> 2. Let $C = A B - B A$. Take the transpose of $C$:
>    $$C^T = (A B - B A)^T = (A B)^T - (B A)^T$$

> 4. Since $C^T = -C$, the commutator $A B - B A$ is strictly **Skew-Symmetric**!

### Question 21 `[MCQ — 2 Marks]`
Let $A$ be a $3 \times 3$ matrix such that $\det(A) = 2$. The value of $\det(\text{adj}(\text{adj}(A)))$ is:
- (A) $4$
- (B) $8$
- (C) $16$
- (D) $64$

> [!faq]- 🔍 View Solution & Detailed Explanation
> **Correct Answer:** **(C) $16$**
> 
> **Key Formula / Concept (Double Adjoint Determinant Formula):**
> For an $n \times n$ matrix $A$:
> $$\det(\text{adj}(\text{adj}(A))) = (\det A)^{(n-1)^2}$$
> 
> **Step-by-Step Solution:**
> 1. Here $n = 3$ and $\det(A) = 2$.
> 2. The exponent is $(n - 1)^2 = (3 - 1)^2 = 2^2 = 4$.
> 3. Calculate:
>    $$\det(\text{adj}(\text{adj}(A))) = 2^4 = 16$$
> 
> **Double Adjoint Matrix Identity:**

The value of $D_5$ is ________ (Answer in integer).

> [!faq]- 🔍 View Solution & Detailed Explanation
> **Correct Answer:** **$6$** (Acceptable range: `6` to `6`)
> 
> **Key Formula / Concept (Tridiagonal Recurrence Relation):**
> Expanding along the first row yields the recurrence:
> $$D_n = 2 D_{n-1} - 1^2 D_{n-2} = 2 D_{n-1} - D_{n-2}$$
> 
> **Step-by-Step Solution:**
> 1. Base cases:
>    - $D_1 = |2| = 2$
>    - $D_2 = \begin{vmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 \end{vmatrix} = 4 - 1 = 3$
> 2. Compute higher orders:
>    - $D_3 = 2(3) - 2 = 6 - 2 = 4$
>    - $D_4 = 2(4) - 3 = 8 - 3 = 5$
>    - $D_5 = 2(5) - 4 = 10 - 4 = 6$
> 
> *(General Formula: $D_n = n + 1$).*

### Question 42 `[MCQ — 2 Marks]`
The determinant of the $3 \times 3$ **Vandermonde Matrix**:
$$V = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 & 4 \\ 1 & 3 & 9 \\ 1 & 5 & 25 \end{pmatrix}$$
is:
- (A) $6$
- (B) $12$
- (C) $24$
- (D) $30$

> [!faq]- 🔍 View Solution & Detailed Explanation
> **Correct Answer:** **(A) $6$**
> 
> **Key Formula / Concept (Classical Vandermonde Determinant Formula):**
> For $x_1 = 2, x_2 = 3, x_3 = 5$:
> $$\begin{vmatrix} 1 & 1 & 1 \\ x_1 & x_2 & x_3 \\ x_1^2 & x_2^2 & x_3^2 \end{vmatrix} = (x_1 - x_2)(x_2 - x_3)(x_3 - x_1) = (x_2 - x_1)(x_3 - x_1)(x_3 - x_2)$$
> 
> **Step-by-Step Solution:**
> 1. $(x_2 - x_1) = 3 - 2 = 1$
> 2. $(x_3 - x_1) = 5 - 2 = 3$
> 3. $(x_3 - x_2) = 5 - 3 = 2$
> 4. Multiply differences:
>    $$\det(V) = (1)(3)(2) = \mathbf{6}$$

### Question 43 `[NAT — 2 Marks]`
Let $A = I_3$ (the $3 \times 3$ identity matrix), and let $\vec{u} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}$ and $\vec{v} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 2 \\ 3 \end{pmatrix}$. By the **Sherman-Morrison Formula**, the determinant $\det(I_3 + \vec{u} \vec{v}^T)$ is ________ (Answer in integer).

> [!faq]- 🔍 View Solution & Detailed Explanation
> **Correct Answer:** **$7$** (Acceptable range: `7` to `7`)
> 
> **Key Formula / Concept (Matrix Determinant Lemma):**
> For any invertible matrix $A$ and column vectors $\vec{u}, \vec{v}$:
> $$\det(A + \vec{u} \vec{v}^T) = \det(A) (1 + \vec{v}^T A^{-1} \vec{u})$$
> 
> **Step-by-Step Solution:**
> 1. For $A = I_3$, $\det(I_3) = 1$ and $A^{-1} = I_3$.
> 2. Compute scalar dot product $\vec{v}^T \vec{u}$:
>    $$\vec{v}^T \vec{u} = (1)(1) + (2)(1) + (3)(1) = 1 + 2 + 3 = 6$$
> 3. Substitute into formula:
>    $$\det(I_3 + \vec{u} \vec{v}^T) = 1 \cdot (1 + 6) = 7$$

### Question 44 `[MCQ — 2 Marks]`
The determinant of the $3 \times 3$ **Circulant Matrix**:
$$C = \begin{pmatrix} a & b & c \\ c & a & b \\ b & c & a \end{pmatrix}$$
is algebraically identical to:
- (A) $a^3 + b^3 + c^3 - 3abc$
- (B) $a^3 + b^3 + c^3 + 3abc$
- (C) $(a + b + c)(a^2 + b^2 + c^2)$
- (D) $(a - b)(b - c)(c - a)$

> [!faq]- 🔍 View Solution & Detailed Explanation
> **Correct Answer:** **(A) $a^3 + b^3 + c^3 - 3abc$**
> 
> **Key Formula / Concept:**
> Direct expansion along Row 1:
> $$\det(C) = a(a^2 - bc) - b(ac - b^2) + c(c^2 - ab) = a^3 - abc - abc + b^3 + c^3 - abc = a^3 + b^3 + c^3 - 3abc$$
> 
> **Eigenvalue Factorization Form:**
> The eigenvalues of a $3 \times 3$ circulant matrix are $\lambda_k = a + b\omega^k + c\omega^{2k}$ ($k=0,1,2$), whose product gives $(a+b+c)(a+b\omega+c\omega^2)(a+b\omega^2+c\omega) = a^3+b^3+c^3-3abc$.

### Question 47 `[NAT — 2 Marks]`
Let $A$ and $B$ be $2 \times 2$ matrices with $\det(A) = 3$ and $\det(B) = 4$. The determinant of the $4 \times 4$ **Kronecker Product Matrix** $\det(A \otimes B)$ is ________ (Answer in integer).

> [!faq]- 🔍 View Solution & Detailed Explanation

> 
> **Key Formula / Concept (Kronecker Product Determinant Property):**
> If $A$ is $m \times m$ and $B$ is $n \times n$:
> $$\det(A \otimes B) = (\det A)^n \cdot (\det B)^m$$
> 
> **Step-by-Step Solution:**

> 2. Apply formula:
>    $$\det(A \otimes B) = (\det A)^2 \cdot (\det B)^2 = 3^2 \times 4^2 = 9 \times 16 = 144$$

### Question 48 `[MSQ — 2 Marks]`
Let $P$ be a real $n \times n$ matrix representing an **orthogonal projection** onto a $k$-dimensional subspace $W \subset \mathbb{R}^n$ ($k < n$). Which of the following statements are **ALWAYS TRUE**? *(Select all that apply)*
- [ ] (A) $P^2 = P$ (Idempotent)
- [ ] (B) $P^T = P$ (Symmetric)
- [ ] (C) $\text{tr}(P) = k$
- [ ] (D) $\det(P) = 1$

> [!faq]- 🔍 View Solution & Detailed Explanation
> **Correct Answer:** **(A), (B), and (C)**
> 
> **Concept Breakdown:**
> - **(A) TRUE:** Projecting twice is the same as projecting once: $P^2 = P$. ✅
> - **(B) TRUE:** Orthogonal projections are strictly self-adjoint (symmetric): $P^T = P$. ✅
> - **(C) TRUE:** The eigenvalues of $P$ are $1$ (with multiplicity $k$) and $0$ (with multiplicity $n-k$). Thus $\text{tr}(P) = \sum \lambda_i = k = \dim(W)$. ✅
> - **(D) FALSE:** Since $k < n$, at least one eigenvalue is $0$, so $\det(P) = 0$. ❌

### Question 50 `[MCQ — 2 Marks]`
Let $A$ be an $n \times n$ matrix with real entries. The matrix $A$ is **Orthogonal** if and only if $A$ preserves the standard Euclidean inner product, meaning:
- (A) $\langle A\vec{x}, A\vec{y} \rangle = \langle \vec{x}, \vec{y} \rangle$ for all $\vec{x}, \vec{y} \in \mathbb{R}^n$
- (B) $\langle A\vec{x}, \vec{y} \rangle = \langle \vec{x}, A\vec{y} \rangle$ for all $\vec{x}, \vec{y} \in \mathbb{R}^n$
- (C) $\det(A) = 1$
- (D) $\text{tr}(A) = n$

> [!faq]- 🔍 View Solution & Detailed Explanation
> **Correct Answer:** **(A) $\langle A\vec{x}, A\vec{y} \rangle = \langle \vec{x}, \vec{y} \rangle$ for all $\vec{x}, \vec{y} \in \mathbb{R}^n$**
> 
> **Step-by-Step Proof:**
> 1. $\langle A\vec{x}, A\vec{y} \rangle = (A\vec{x})^T (A\vec{y}) = \vec{x}^T (A^T A) \vec{y}$.
> 2. For this to equal $\langle \vec{x}, \vec{y} \rangle = \vec{x}^T \vec{y}$ for ALL $\vec{x}, \vec{y}$, we must have:
>    $$A^T A = I_n$$
> 3. This is the exact definition of an **Isometry / Orthogonal Matrix** (preserves lengths and angles).

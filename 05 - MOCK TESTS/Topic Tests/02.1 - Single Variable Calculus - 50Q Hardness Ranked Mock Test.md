### Question 01 `[MCQ — 1 Mark]`
The value of the limit $\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin(5x)}{x}$ is:
- (A) $0$
- (B) $1$
- (C) $5$
- (D) $\infty$

> [!faq]- 🔍 View Solution & Detailed Explanation
> **Correct Answer:** **(C) $5$**
> 
> **Standard Trigonometric Limit:**
> $$\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin(kx)}{x} = k \cdot \lim_{kx \to 0} \frac{\sin(kx)}{kx} = k(1) = 5$$

### Question 03 `[MCQ — 1 Mark]`
A function $f(x)$ is defined on $\mathbb{R}$ as $f(x) = |x|$. At $x = 0$, the function $f(x)$ is:
- (A) Continuous and Differentiable
- (B) Continuous but NOT Differentiable
- (C) Differentiable but NOT Continuous
- (D) Neither Continuous nor Differentiable

> [!faq]- 🔍 View Solution & Detailed Explanation
> **Correct Answer:** **(B) Continuous but NOT Differentiable**
> 
> **Fundamental Calculus Hierarchy:**
> 1. Continuity: $\lim_{x \to 0^-} |x| = \lim_{x \to 0^+} |x| = f(0) = 0 \implies$ Continuous at $x = 0$.
> 2. Differentiability: Left derivative $\text{LHD} = -1$, Right derivative $\text{RHD} = +1$. Since $\text{LHD} \neq \text{RHD}$, the derivative does not exist (sharp corner/cusp).

Find the value of $k$ such that the function:

### Question 05 `[MCQ — 1 Mark]`
The stationary (critical) point of the function $f(x) = 2x^3 - 9x^2 + 12x + 5$ in the open interval $(0, 3)$ where a local maximum occurs is:
- (A) $x = 1$
- (B) $x = 2$
- (C) $x = 0$
- (D) $x = 3$

> [!faq]- 🔍 View Solution & Detailed Explanation
> **Correct Answer:** **(A) $x = 1$**
> 
> **First & Second Derivative Tests:**
> 1. $f'(x) = 6x^2 - 18x + 12 = 6(x^2 - 3x + 2) = 6(x-1)(x-2) = 0 \implies x = 1, 2$.
> 2. $f''(x) = 12x - 18$.
> 3. At $x = 1$: $f''(1) = 12(1) - 18 = -6 < 0 \implies \mathbf{Local\ Maximum}$.
> 4. At $x = 2$: $f''(2) = 12(2) - 18 = +6 > 0 \implies \mathbf{Local\ Minimum}$.

The coefficient of $x^2$ in the **Maclaurin Series** expansion of $f(x) = \cos x$ is:

### Question 10 `[NAT — 1 Mark]`
If $y = x^x$ for $x > 0$, the value of the derivative $\frac{dy}{dx}$ at $x = 1$ is ________ (Answer in integer).

> [!faq]- 🔍 View Solution & Detailed Explanation
> **Correct Answer:** **$1$** (Acceptable range: `1` to `1`)
> 
> **Logarithmic Differentiation:**
> 1. $\ln y = x \ln x$.
> 2. Differentiate: $\frac{1}{y}\frac{dy}{dx} = \ln x + x(1/x) = 1 + \ln x \implies \frac{dy}{dx} = x^x (1 + \ln x)$.

The function $f(x) = x^3 - 3x$ satisfies the hypotheses of **Rolle's Theorem** on the closed interval $[-\sqrt{3}, \sqrt{3}]$. The positive value of $c \in (-\sqrt{3}, \sqrt{3})$ such that $f'(c) = 0$ is:

### Question 14 `[NAT — 1 Mark]`
According to **Lagrange's Mean Value Theorem (LMVT)** for $f(x) = x^2$ on the interval $[1, 5]$, there exists a point $c \in (1, 5)$ such that $f'(c) = \frac{f(5) - f(1)}{5 - 1}$. The value of $c$ is ________ (Answer in integer).

### Question 21 `[MCQ — 2 Marks]`
Using the **Leibniz Rule**, find the derivative $\frac{dF}{dx}$ at $x = 2$ for the integral function:
$$F(x) = \int_{1}^{x^2} \sqrt{1 + t^3} \, dt$$
- (A) $4 \times \sqrt{65}$
- (B) $2 \times \sqrt{65}$
- (C) $\sqrt{65}$
- (D) $4 \times \sqrt{9}$

> [!faq]- 🔍 View Solution & Detailed Explanation
> **Correct Answer:** **(A) $4 \times \sqrt{65}$**
> 
> **Leibniz Rule Formula:**
> $$\frac{d}{dx} \left[ \int_{u(x)}^{v(x)} f(t) \, dt \right] = f(v(x)) v'(x) - f(u(x)) u'(x)$$
> 
> **Step-by-Step Solution:**
> 1. Upper limit $v(x) = x^2 \implies v'(x) = 2x$.
> 2. Lower limit $u(x) = 1 \implies u'(x) = 0$.
> 3. $\frac{dF}{dx} = \sqrt{1 + (x^2)^3} \cdot (2x) = 2x \sqrt{1 + x^6}$.
> 4. At $x = 2$: $\left.\frac{dF}{dx}\right|_{x=2} = 2(2) \sqrt{1 + 2^6} = 4 \sqrt{1 + 64} = 4 \sqrt{65}$.

### Question 22 `[NAT — 2 Marks]`
Using **King's Property** $\int_{a}^{b} f(x) \, dx = \int_{a}^{b} f(a+b-x) \, dx$, evaluate:
$$I = \int_{0}^{\pi/2} \frac{\sin^{2027}(x)}{\sin^{2027}(x) + \cos^{2027}(x)} \, dx$$
The value of $I$ is $\frac{\pi}{k}$. The value of $k$ is ________ (Answer in integer).

> [!faq]- 🔍 View Solution & Detailed Explanation
> **Correct Answer:** **$4$** (Acceptable range: `4` to `4`)
> 
> **King's Property Symmetric Cancellation:**
> 1. Apply $x \to \frac{\pi}{2} - x$:
>    $$I = \int_{0}^{\pi/2} \frac{\cos^{2027}(x)}{\cos^{2027}(x) + \sin^{2027}(x)} \, dx$$
> 2. Add both equations:
>    $$2I = \int_{0}^{\pi/2} \frac{\sin^{2027}(x) + \cos^{2027}(x)}{\sin^{2027}(x) + \cos^{2027}(x)} \, dx = \int_{0}^{\pi/2} 1 \, dx = \frac{\pi}{2}$$
> 3. $I = \frac{\pi}{4} \implies k = 4$.

### Question 23 `[MCQ — 2 Marks]`
Find the **Global (Absolute) Maximum Value** of $f(x) = 2x^3 - 3x^2 - 12x + 5$ on the closed interval $[0, 3]$:
- (A) $5$
- (B) $-4$
- (C) $-15$
- (D) $0$

> [!faq]- 🔍 View Solution & Detailed Explanation
> **Correct Answer:** **(A) $5$**
> 
> **Extreme Value Theorem (EVT) Algorithm:**
> 1. Find critical points: $f'(x) = 6x^2 - 6x - 12 = 6(x^2 - x - 2) = 6(x-2)(x+1) = 0 \implies x = 2$ (since $-1 \notin [0, 3]$).
> 2. Evaluate at critical points & endpoints:
>    - $f(0) = 5$
>    - $f(2) = 2(8) - 3(4) - 12(2) + 5 = 16 - 12 - 24 + 5 = -15$
>    - $f(3) = 2(27) - 3(9) - 12(3) + 5 = 54 - 27 - 36 + 5 = -4$
> 3. $\text{Global Maximum} = \max\{5, -15, -4\} = 5$ (occurring at endpoint $x = 0$).

### Question 24 `[NAT — 2 Marks]`
Using **Wallis' Formula**, evaluate:
$$I = \int_{0}^{\pi/2} \sin^6(x) \, dx$$
The value of $I$ is $\frac{k \pi}{32}$. The value of $k$ is ________ (Answer in integer).

> [!faq]- 🔍 View Solution & Detailed Explanation
> **Correct Answer:** **$5$** (Acceptable range: `5` to `5`)
> 
> **Wallis Reduction Formula:**
> For even power $n = 6$:
> $$I = \frac{n-1}{n} \cdot \frac{n-3}{n-2} \cdot \frac{n-5}{n-4} \cdot \frac{\pi}{2} = \frac{5}{6} \cdot \frac{3}{4} \cdot \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{\pi}{2} = \frac{15}{96} \pi = \frac{5}{32} \pi$$
> Thus, $k = 5$.

Evaluate the limit using **L'Hôpital's Rule**:

### Question 01 `[MCQ — 1 Mark]`
The **Rank** of an $m \times n$ matrix $A$ is defined as $r$. Which of the following statements is **ALWAYS TRUE**?
- (A) $r$ is the maximum number of linearly independent row vectors in $A$.
- (B) $r$ is the maximum number of linearly independent column vectors in $A$.
- (C) $r$ is the order of the largest non-zero minor (submatrix determinant) of $A$.
- (D) All of the above (A, B, and C) are mathematically equivalent definitions.

> [!faq]- 🔍 View Solution & Detailed Explanation
> **Correct Answer:** **(D) All of the above (A, B, and C) are mathematically equivalent definitions.**
> 
> **Fundamental Theorem of Matrix Rank:**
> The row rank, column rank, and determinantal minor rank of any matrix are mathematically identical:
> $$\text{Row Rank}(A) = \text{Column Rank}(A) = \text{Minor Rank}(A) = \text{Rank}(A)$$

Which of the following operations on a matrix $A$ will **CHANGE** its rank?

### Question 13 `[MCQ — 1 Mark]`
Let $A$ be an $m \times n$ real matrix. The rank of the **Gram Matrix** $A^T A$ is ALWAYS equal to:
- (A) $\text{Rank}(A)^2$
- (B) $\text{Rank}(A)$
- (C) $\min(m, n)$
- (D) $\text{Rank}(A) + \text{Rank}(A^T)$

> [!faq]- 🔍 View Solution & Detailed Explanation
> **Correct Answer:** **(B) $\text{Rank}(A)$**
> 
> **Master Gram Matrix Rank Identity:**
> For any real matrix $A$:
> $$\text{Rank}(A^T A) = \text{Rank}(A A^T) = \text{Rank}(A) = \text{Rank}(A^T)$$
> *(Proof: The nullspace $N(A^T A) = N(A)$ because $A^T A \vec{x} = \vec{0} \iff \|A\vec{x}\|^2 = 0 \iff A\vec{x} = \vec{0}$).*

Let $A$ be an $m \times n$ matrix with $\text{Rank}(A) = r$. If $P$ is an $m \times m$ invertible matrix and $Q$ is an $n \times n$ invertible matrix, then $\text{Rank}(P A Q)$ is:

### Question 17 `[MCQ — 1 Mark]`
If $A$ is an $n \times n$ matrix of rank 1, then $A^2$ is ALWAYS:
- (A) The zero matrix $O$
- (B) An identity matrix $I_n$
- (C) A scalar multiple of $A$ ($\text{tr}(A) \cdot A$)
- (D) A full-rank matrix

> [!faq]- 🔍 View Solution & Detailed Explanation
> **Correct Answer:** **(C) A scalar multiple of $A$ ($\text{tr}(A) \cdot A$)**
> 
> **Rank-1 Algebraic Property:**
> Any rank-1 matrix can be factored as $A = \vec{u} \vec{v}^T$.
> $$A^2 = (\vec{u} \vec{v}^T)(\vec{u} \vec{v}^T) = \vec{u} (\vec{v}^T \vec{u}) \vec{v}^T = (\vec{v}^T \vec{u}) (\vec{u} \vec{v}^T) = \text{tr}(A) \cdot A$$

For what value of $k$ will the matrix:

### Question 19 `[MSQ — 1 Mark]`
Let $A$ and $B$ be two $n \times n$ matrices of rank $n$. Which of the following matrices are **GUARANTEED to have full rank $n$**? *(Select all that apply)*

### Question 21 `[MCQ — 2 Marks]`
Let $A$ be a $4 \times 6$ matrix with real entries. If the nullspace of $A$ has dimension $\dim N(A) = 4$, the rank of $A$ is:
- (A) $0$
- (B) $2$
- (C) $4$
- (D) $6$

> [!faq]- 🔍 View Solution & Detailed Explanation
> **Correct Answer:** **(B) $2$**
> 
> **Key Formula / Concept (Domain Rank-Nullity Theorem):**
> For any $m \times n$ matrix:
> $$\text{Rank}(A) + \text{Nullity}(A) = n$$
> *(where $n$ is the number of **COLUMNS**, not rows!).*
> 
> **Step-by-Step Solution:**
> 1. Total columns $n = 6$.
> 2. $\text{Nullity}(A) = 4$.
> 3. $\text{Rank}(A) = 6 - 4 = 2$.

### Question 22 `[NAT — 2 Marks]`
Let $A$ be a $5 \times 5$ matrix with $\text{Rank}(A) = 3$, and let $B$ be a $5 \times 5$ matrix with $\text{Rank}(B) = 4$. According to **Sylvester's Law of Nullity**, the minimum possible rank of the product matrix $A B$ is ________ (Answer in integer).

> [!faq]- 🔍 View Solution & Detailed Explanation
> **Correct Answer:** **$2$** (Acceptable range: `2` to `2`)
> 
> **Key Formula / Concept (Sylvester's Rank Inequality):**
> For two $n \times n$ matrices $A$ and $B$:
> $$\text{Rank}(A) + \text{Rank}(B) - n \le \text{Rank}(A B) \le \min(\text{Rank}(A), \text{Rank}(B))$$
> 
> **Step-by-Step Solution:**
> 1. $n = 5$, $\text{Rank}(A) = 3$, $\text{Rank}(B) = 4$.
> 2. Lower bound:
>    $$\text{Rank}(AB) \ge 3 + 4 - 5 = 2$$
> 3. Minimum possible rank is $2$.

### Question 23 `[MCQ — 2 Marks]`
Let $A$ be an $n \times n$ matrix with $\text{Rank}(A) = n - 1$. The rank of the **Adjoint Matrix** $\text{adj}(A)$ is:
- (A) $n$
- (B) $n - 1$
- (C) $1$
- (D) $0$

> [!faq]- 🔍 View Solution & Detailed Explanation
> **Correct Answer:** **(C) $1$**
> 
> **Master Rank of Adjoint Theorem:**
> For any $n \times n$ matrix $A$:
> $$\text{Rank}(\text{adj}(A)) = \begin{cases} n & \text{if } \text{Rank}(A) = n \\ 1 & \text{if } \text{Rank}(A) = n - 1 \\ 0 & \text{if } \text{Rank}(A) \le n - 2 \end{cases}$$
> 
> **Explanation:**
> When $\text{Rank}(A) = n - 1$, at least one $(n-1) \times (n-1)$ minor is non-zero, so $\text{adj}(A) \neq O$. Since $A \cdot \text{adj}(A) = \det(A) I = O$, by Sylvester's inequality $\text{Rank}(\text{adj}(A)) \le n - (n-1) = 1$. Thus, $\text{Rank}(\text{adj}(A)) = 1$.

### Question 24 `[NAT — 2 Marks]`
Let $A$ be a $4 \times 4$ real matrix with $\text{Rank}(A) = 2$. The rank of the double adjoint matrix $\text{adj}(\text{adj}(A))$ is ________ (Answer in integer).

> 
> **Step-by-Step Solution:**

> 3. The adjoint of the null matrix is also the null matrix: $\text{adj}(O) = O$.
> 4. Therefore, $\text{Rank}(\text{adj}(\text{adj}(A))) = 0$.

### Question 25 `[MCQ — 2 Marks]`
Let $A$ be a $3 \times 5$ matrix of rank 2. The dimension of the **Left Nullspace** $N(A^T) = \{\vec{y} \in \mathbb{R}^3 \mid A^T \vec{y} = \vec{0}\}$ is:

>

> 
> **Step-by-Step Solution:**
> 1. Number of rows $m = 3$.
> 2. $\text{Rank}(A) = 2$.
> 3. $\dim N(A^T) = m - \text{Rank}(A) = 3 - 2 = 1$.

### Question 27 `[MCQ — 2 Marks]`
Every $m \times n$ matrix $A$ of rank $r$ can be reduced by a sequence of EROs and ECOs to the **Normal (Canonical) Form**:
$$N = \begin{pmatrix} I_r & O \\ O & O \end{pmatrix}$$
This guarantees the existence of two non-singular matrices $P_{m \times m}$ and $Q_{n \times n}$ such that:
- (A) $P A Q = \begin{pmatrix} I_r & O \\ O & O \end{pmatrix}$
- (B) $A = P Q$
- (C) $P A P^{-1} = I_r$

> **Correct Answer:** **(A) $P A Q = \begin{pmatrix} I_r & O \\ O & O \end{pmatrix}$**

> **Non-Singular Equivalence Theorem:**
> Every matrix of rank $r$ is equivalent to the canonical projection block matrix $\begin{pmatrix} I_r & O \\ O & O \end{pmatrix}$ via pre-multiplication by row-operation matrix $P$ and post-multiplication by column-operation matrix $Q$.

### Question 31 `[MCQ — 2 Marks]`
According to the **Frobenius Rank Inequality**, for any three conformable matrices $A, B, C$:
- (A) $\text{Rank}(A B C) \ge \text{Rank}(A B) + \text{Rank}(B C) - \text{Rank}(B)$
- (B) $\text{Rank}(A B C) \le \text{Rank}(A B) + \text{Rank}(B C) - \text{Rank}(B)$

> 
> **The Frobenius Rank Theorem:**
> This is the fundamental generalization of Sylvester's inequality:
> $$\text{Rank}(A B C) + \text{Rank}(B) \ge \text{Rank}(A B) + \text{Rank}(B C)$$

Let $A$ be an $n \times n$ **Idempotent Matrix** ($A^2 = A$). Which of the following relationships between its rank and trace is **ALWAYS TRUE**?

- (B) $\text{Rank}(A) = \text{tr}(A)^2$

For any upper block triangular matrix $M = \begin{pmatrix} A & B \\ O & D \end{pmatrix}$, which of the following rank inequalities is **ALWAYS SATISFIED**?

### Question 41 `[MCQ — 2 Marks]`
According to the **Eckart-Young-Mirsky Theorem**, the optimal rank-$k$ matrix approximation $A_k$ to a matrix $A$ with Singular Value Decomposition $A = \sum_{i=1}^r \sigma_i \vec{u}_i \vec{v}_i^T$ (minimizing the Frobenius norm error $\|A - A_k\|_F$) is given by:
- (A) $A_k = \sum_{i=1}^k \sigma_i \vec{u}_i \vec{v}_i^T$ (Truncated SVD)
- (B) $A_k = \sum_{i=k+1}^r \sigma_i \vec{u}_i \vec{v}_i^T$
- (C) $A_k = \frac{k}{r} A$
- (D) $A_k = (A^T A)^k A$

> [!faq]- 🔍 View Solution & Detailed Explanation
> **Correct Answer:** **(A) $A_k = \sum_{i=1}^k \sigma_i \vec{u}_i \vec{v}_i^T$ (Truncated SVD)**
> 
> **The Eckart-Young-Mirsky Theorem (Core of PCA & Data Compression):**
> The best rank-$k$ approximation to any matrix in both Frobenius and spectral norms is obtained by keeping the first $k$ dominant singular values and setting the remaining $r - k$ singular values to zero:
> $$\min_{\text{Rank}(B) \le k} \|A - B\|_F = \|A - A_k\|_F = \sqrt{\sum_{i=k+1}^r \sigma_i^2}$$

Let $A$ be an $n \times n$ matrix with $\text{Rank}(A) = r$. If a rank-1 matrix $\vec{u} \vec{v}^T$ is added to $A$, the rank of the perturbed matrix $M = A + \vec{u} \vec{v}^T$ can ONLY take values in:

- (B) $\{0, r\}$
- (C) $\{r, 2r\}$

> 2. By lower triangle inequality: $\text{Rank}(A + \vec{u}\vec{v}^T) \ge \text{Rank}(A) - \text{Rank}(\vec{u}\vec{v}^T) = r - 1$.
> 3. Therefore, adding a rank-1 outer product can change the rank by at most $\pm 1$.

### Question 45 `[MCQ — 2 Marks]`
Let $A^+$ denote the **Moore-Penrose Pseudoinverse** of an $m \times n$ real matrix $A$. Which of the following rank statements is **ALWAYS TRUE**?
- (A) $\text{Rank}(A^+) = \text{Rank}(A)$
- (B) $\text{Rank}(A^+) = \min(m, n) - \text{Rank}(A)$
- (C) $\text{Rank}(A^+) = \text{Rank}(A)^2$
- (D) $\text{Rank}(A^+) = \text{Nullity}(A)$

> [!faq]- 🔍 View Solution & Detailed Explanation
> **Correct Answer:** **(A) $\text{Rank}(A^+) = \text{Rank}(A)$**
> 
> **Moore-Penrose Rank Identity:**
> By the four Moore-Penrose conditions ($A A^+ A = A$, $A^+ A A^+ = A^+$, $(A A^+)^T = A A^+$, $(A^+ A)^T = A^+ A$):
> $$\text{Rank}(A^+) = \text{Rank}(A^+ A A^+) \le \text{Rank}(A^+ A) \le \text{Rank}(A)$$
> and $\text{Rank}(A) \le \text{Rank}(A^+)$.
> Thus, $\text{Rank}(A^+) = \text{Rank}(A)$ identically.

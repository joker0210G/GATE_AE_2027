### Question 01 `[MCQ — 1 Mark]`
For the matrix $A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 3 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$, which of the following vectors is an **Eigenvector**?
- (A) $\begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}$
- (B) $\begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ -1 \end{pmatrix}$
- (C) $\begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}$
- (D) $\begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix}$

> [!faq]- 🔍 View Solution & Detailed Explanation
> **Correct Answer:** **(B) $\begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ -1 \end{pmatrix}$**
> 
> **2-Second Inspection Filter:**
> Multiply $A$ by each option vector and check if $A\vec{v} = k\vec{v}$:
> 1. Testing Option (B):
>    $$A \vec{v} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 3 & 2 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ -1 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 - 2 \\ 3 - 2 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} -1 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix} = -1 \cdot \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ -1 \end{pmatrix}$$
> 2. Since $A\vec{v} = -1\vec{v}$, $\vec{v} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ -1 \end{pmatrix}$ is strictly an eigenvector for eigenvalue $\lambda = -1$.

### Question 02 `[MCQ — 1 Mark]`
If $\vec{v}$ is an eigenvector of matrix $A$ corresponding to eigenvalue $\lambda$, which of the following is **NEVER** an eigenvector of $A$?
- (A) $2\vec{v}$
- (B) $-5\vec{v}$
- (C) $\frac{1}{3}\vec{v}$
- (D) The zero vector $\vec{0} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}$

> [!faq]- 🔍 View Solution & Detailed Explanation
> **Correct Answer:** **(D) The zero vector $\vec{0} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}$**
> 
> **Fundamental Definition:**
> By definition, an eigenvector must be a **NON-ZERO vector** ($\vec{v} \neq \vec{0}$). Any non-zero scalar multiple $c\vec{v}$ ($c \neq 0$) is a valid eigenvector, but the zero vector is strictly excluded.

### Question 03 `[NAT — 1 Mark]`
An eigenvector of $A = \begin{pmatrix} 4 & 1 \\ 2 & 3 \end{pmatrix}$ for eigenvalue $\lambda = 5$ is given by $\vec{v} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ k \end{pmatrix}$. The value of $k$ is ________ (Answer in integer).

> [!faq]- 🔍 View Solution & Detailed Explanation
> **Correct Answer:** **$1$** (Acceptable range: `1` to `1`)
> 
> **2x2 Fast Shortcut Method:**
> For $A = \begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{pmatrix}$ with eigenvalue $\lambda$:
> $$\vec{v} = \begin{pmatrix} -b \\ a - \lambda \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} -1 \\ 4 - 5 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} -1 \\ -1 \end{pmatrix} \equiv \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}$$
> Comparing $\begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}$ with $\begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ k \end{pmatrix}$, we get $k = 1$.

The set of ALL eigenvectors corresponding to a specific eigenvalue $\lambda$, combined with the zero vector $\vec{0}$, forms:

### Question 10 `[NAT — 1 Mark]`
If $A \vec{v} = 4 \vec{v}$ for a non-zero vector $\vec{v}$, then $(A^2 + 3A) \vec{v} = k \vec{v}$. The value of scalar $k$ is ________ (Answer in integer).

> [!faq]- 🔍 View Solution & Detailed Explanation
> **Correct Answer:** **$28$** (Acceptable range: `28` to `28`)
> 
> **Matrix Polynomial Action:**
> $$(A^2 + 3A)\vec{v} = A(A\vec{v}) + 3(A\vec{v}) = A(4\vec{v}) + 3(4\vec{v}) = 4(4\vec{v}) + 12\vec{v} = 16\vec{v} + 12\vec{v} = 28\vec{v}$$
> Thus, $k = 28$.

### Question 11 `[MCQ — 1 Mark]`
The **Geometric Multiplicity (GM)** of an eigenvalue $\lambda$ for an $n \times n$ matrix $A$ is computed as:
- (A) $n - \text{Rank}(A)$
- (B) $n - \text{Rank}(A - \lambda I)$
- (C) $\det(A - \lambda I)$
- (D) $\text{tr}(A) - \lambda$

> [!faq]- 🔍 View Solution & Detailed Explanation
> **Correct Answer:** **(B) $n - \text{Rank}(A - \lambda I)$**
> 
> **Geometric Multiplicity Formula:**
> $\text{GM}(\lambda)$ is the dimension of the nullspace of $(A - \lambda I)$:
> $$\text{GM}(\lambda) = \dim N(A - \lambda I) = n - \text{Rank}(A - \lambda I)$$

### Question 13 `[MCQ — 1 Mark]`
Let $\vec{v}$ be an eigenvector of an invertible matrix $A$ corresponding to eigenvalue $\lambda \neq 0$. Which of the following statements is **TRUE**?
- (A) $\vec{v}$ is an eigenvector of $A^{-1}$ corresponding to eigenvalue $1/\lambda$.
- (B) $\vec{v}$ is an eigenvector of $A^{-1}$ corresponding to eigenvalue $-\lambda$.
- (C) $A^{-1}$ has no eigenvectors in common with $A$.
- (D) $\vec{v}^T$ is an eigenvector of $A^{-1}$ with eigenvalue $\lambda$.

> [!faq]- 🔍 View Solution & Detailed Explanation
> **Correct Answer:** **(A) $\vec{v}$ is an eigenvector of $A^{-1}$ corresponding to eigenvalue $1/\lambda$.**
> 
> **Inverse Eigenvector Invariance:**
> Pre-multiply $A\vec{v} = \lambda\vec{v}$ by $A^{-1}$:
> $$\vec{v} = \lambda A^{-1}\vec{v} \implies A^{-1}\vec{v} = \frac{1}{\lambda}\vec{v}$$
> The eigenvector $\vec{v}$ remains completely unchanged!

### Question 16 `[NAT — 1 Mark]`
Let $\vec{v}_1 = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix}$ be a right eigenvector of $A$ for $\lambda_1 = 3$, and $\vec{w}_2^T = \begin{pmatrix} k & 1 \end{pmatrix}$ be a left eigenvector of $A$ for $\lambda_2 = 5$. By the **Biorthogonality Property** ($\vec{w}_2^T \vec{v}_1 = 0$), the value of $k$ is ________ (Answer in integer).

> [!faq]- 🔍 View Solution & Detailed Explanation
> **Correct Answer:** **$-2$** (Acceptable range: `-2` to `-2`)
> 
> **Biorthogonality Principle:**
> Left and right eigenvectors corresponding to distinct eigenvalues ($\lambda_1 \neq \lambda_2$) are strictly orthogonal:
> $$\vec{w}_2^T \vec{v}_1 = 0 \implies \begin{pmatrix} k & 1 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix} = k(1) + 1(2) = k + 2 = 0 \implies k = -2$$

### Question 21 `[MCQ — 2 Marks]`
A $3 \times 3$ real matrix $A$ has eigenvalues $\lambda_1 = 1, \lambda_2 = 2, \lambda_3 = 3$ with corresponding eigenvectors $\vec{v}_1, \vec{v}_2, \vec{v}_3$. The modal matrix is formed as $P = \begin{pmatrix} \vec{v}_1 & \vec{v}_2 & \vec{v}_3 \end{pmatrix}$. The product $P^{-1} A P$ is:
- (A) $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 2 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 3 \end{pmatrix}$
- (B) $\begin{pmatrix} 3 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 2 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$
- (C) $I_3$
- (D) $\det(A) I_3$

> [!faq]- 🔍 View Solution & Detailed Explanation
> **Correct Answer:** **(A) $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 2 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 3 \end{pmatrix}$**
> 
> **Matrix Diagonalization Theorem:**
> Placing eigenvectors as columns of $P$ in order $(\vec{v}_1, \vec{v}_2, \vec{v}_3)$ diagonalizes $A$ into a spectral matrix $D$ whose diagonal entries are strictly the corresponding eigenvalues in the exact same order $(\lambda_1, \lambda_2, \lambda_3)$.

### Question 22 `[NAT — 2 Marks]`
Let $A = P D P^{-1}$ where $P = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & -1 \end{pmatrix}$ and $D = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$. The $(1, 1)$ entry of the matrix $A^3$ is ________ (Round off to 1 decimal place).

> [!faq]- 🔍 View Solution & Detailed Explanation
> **Correct Answer:** **$4.5$** (Acceptable range: `4.4` to `4.6`, exact: $4.5$)
> 
> **Step-by-Step Solution:**
> 1. Compute $D^3 = \begin{pmatrix} 2^3 & 0 \\ 0 & 1^3 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 8 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$.
> 2. $P = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & -1 \end{pmatrix} \implies P^{-1} = \frac{1}{-2} \begin{pmatrix} -1 & -1 \\ -1 & 1 \end{pmatrix} = \frac{1}{2} \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & -1 \end{pmatrix}$.
> 3. Multiply $P D^3 P^{-1}$:
>    $$P D^3 = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & -1 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} 8 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 8 & 1 \\ 8 & -1 \end{pmatrix}$$
> 4. $(A^3)_{11} = \frac{1}{2} [ (8)(1) + (1)(1) ] = \frac{9}{2} = 4.5$.

Which of the following matrices is **NOT DIAGONALIZABLE** (i.e., defective)?

### Question 24 `[NAT — 2 Marks]`
For a $2 \times 2$ symmetric matrix $A$, the normalized eigenvectors are $\vec{q}_1 = \begin{pmatrix} 3/5 \\ 4/5 \end{pmatrix}$ for $\lambda_1 = 10$, and $\vec{q}_2 = \begin{pmatrix} -4/5 \\ 3/5 \end{pmatrix}$ for $\lambda_2 = 5$. Using the **Spectral Decomposition** $A = 10 \vec{q}_1 \vec{q}_1^T + 5 \vec{q}_2 \vec{q}_2^T$, the entry $a_{11}$ of matrix $A$ is ________ (Round off to 1 decimal place).

> [!faq]- 🔍 View Solution & Detailed Explanation
> **Correct Answer:** **$6.8$** (Acceptable range: `6.7` to `6.9`, exact: $6.8$)
> 
> **Spectral Decomposition Computation:**

> 🎯 **GATE Weightage:** 1–2 marks per paper. The **Poisson Distribution** models the occurrence of **rare events** over a specified interval of time, area, volume, or distance. In GATE Aerospace and Engineering Mathematics, questions frequently test the unique property $\text{Mean} = \text{Variance} = \lambda$, rate scaling across time intervals, additivity of independent Poisson sources, and Poisson approximation to Binomial.

---

## 📌 Part I: Foundation & Physical Context

The Poisson distribution is a discrete probability distribution used when:
1. Events occur **randomly and independently** in a continuous medium (time, length, area, volume).
2. The **average rate** of occurrence ($\alpha$) is constant.
3. The probability of more than one event occurring in an infinitely small sub-interval is negligible.

### Common Engineering & Aerospace Examples
- Number of meteoroid impacts on a satellite per month
- Number of defective welds per 100 meters of aircraft fuselage
- Number of air traffic calls arriving at a control tower per minute
- Number of particles emitted by a radioactive source per second

---

## 🧠 Part II: Probability Mass Function (PMF)

Let $X$ be the random variable representing the number of event occurrences in a given interval ($X \in \{0, 1, 2, 3, \ldots\}$).

$$\boxed{P(X = k) = \frac{e^{-\lambda} \lambda^k}{k!}, \quad k = 0, 1, 2, 3, \ldots}$$

where:
- $\lambda > 0$: **Mean rate parameter** (average number of events in the given interval)
- $e \approx 2.71828$: Euler's constant
- $k! = k \times (k-1) \times \cdots \times 1$ (with $0! = 1$)

### Sum of Probabilities Check
$$\sum_{k=0}^{\infty} P(X = k) = \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \frac{e^{-\lambda} \lambda^k}{k!} = e^{-\lambda} \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \frac{\lambda^k}{k!} = e^{-\lambda} \cdot e^\lambda = 1$$

---

## 🧠 Part III: Key Statistics & Properties (HIGH YIELD!)

### 1. Mean (Expected Value)
$$\boxed{\mu = E[X] = \lambda}$$

### 2. Variance
$$\boxed{\sigma^2 = Var(X) = \lambda}$$

### 3. Standard Deviation
$$\boxed{\sigma = \sqrt{\lambda}}$$

---

### 4. THE UNIQUE IDENTIFYING RULE: Mean = Variance!

$$\boxed{\text{Mean} = \text{Variance} = \lambda}$$

> 🔴 **GATE FAST-CHECK RULE:**
> - If a problem states *"For a distribution, Mean = 4 and Variance = 4"* $\implies$ **Poisson Distribution**!
> - Binomial: Mean > Variance ($np > npq$)
> - Poisson: **Mean = Variance** ($\lambda = \lambda$)
> - Negative Binomial: Mean < Variance

---

### 5. Mode of Poisson Distribution (Most Probable Value)

1. **If $\lambda$ is NOT an integer:** Unique mode at $k = \lfloor \lambda \rfloor$ (integer part of $\lambda$).
2. **If $\lambda$ IS an integer $m$:** Bimodal with two equal maximum probabilities at $k = m$ and $k = m - 1$.

---

### 6. Shape & Skewness
- **Always Positively Skewed** (Right-skewed) for any $\lambda > 0$.
- Skewness $\gamma_1 = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda}}$.
- As $\lambda \to \infty$, skewness $\to 0$, and the Poisson distribution approaches a **Normal Distribution** $N(\lambda, \lambda)$.

---

## 🧠 Part IV: Essential Operations & Properties for GATE

### 1. Rate Scaling across Time/Space Intervals (CRITICAL!)

If events occur at an average rate of $\alpha$ per unit time, then in a time interval of length $t$:

$$\boxed{\lambda = \alpha \cdot t}$$

$$P(X = k \text{ in time } t) = \frac{e^{-\alpha t} (\alpha t)^k}{k!}$$

> ⚡ **Example:** If calls arrive at rate $\alpha = 4\text{ per hour}$, then:
> - In $1\text{ hour}$ ($t=1$): $\lambda = 4(1) = 4$
> - In $30\text{ mins}$ ($t=0.5$): $\lambda = 4(0.5) = 2$
> - In $2\text{ hours}$ ($t=2$): $\lambda = 4(2) = 8$

---

### 2. Additive Property of Independent Poisson Variables

If $X_1 \sim \text{Poisson}(\lambda_1)$ and $X_2 \sim \text{Poisson}(\lambda_2)$ are **independent**:

$$\boxed{X_1 + X_2 \sim \text{Poisson}(\lambda_1 + \lambda_2)}$$

> ⚡ **Application:** If Machine A produces flaws at rate $\lambda_1 = 2/\text{day}$ and Machine B produces flaws at rate $\lambda_2 = 3/\text{day}$, total flaws $W = X_1 + X_2 \sim \text{Poisson}(2 + 3 = 5)$.

---

### 3. Connection to Exponential Distribution (Inter-Arrival Time)

In a Poisson process with rate $\alpha$:
- $X$ = Number of events in time $t \implies \text{Poisson}(\lambda = \alpha t)$
- $T$ = Time between two consecutive events $\implies$ **Exponential Distribution** with PDF:

$$\boxed{f(t) = \alpha e^{-\alpha t}, \quad t \ge 0}$$

$$E[T] = \frac{1}{\alpha}, \quad Var(T) = \frac{1}{\alpha^2}$$

---

### 4. Poisson as Limiting Form of Binomial Distribution

When $n$ is very large ($n \to \infty$) and $p$ is very small ($p \to 0$) such that $\lambda = np$ remains constant:

$$\boxed{\lim_{\substack{n\to\infty \\ p\to 0}} \binom{n}{k} p^k (1-p)^{n-k} = \frac{e^{-\lambda} \lambda^k}{k!} \quad \text{where } \lambda = np}$$

**Rule of Thumb for GATE:** Use Poisson approximation to Binomial when $n \ge 20$ and $p \le 0.05$.

## ⚡ AIR-1 Speed Tricks & Mnemonics

### 🚀 Trick 1: Recurrence Relation for Fast PMF Computation
Avoid factorials! Calculate sequential probabilities using:

$$\boxed{P(X = k+1) = \frac{\lambda}{k+1} \cdot P(X = k)}$$

- $P(X=0) = e^{-\lambda}$
- $P(X=1) = \lambda e^{-\lambda}$
- $P(X=2) = \frac{\lambda}{2} P(X=1) = \frac{\lambda^2}{2} e^{-\lambda}$
- $P(X=3) = \frac{\lambda}{3} P(X=2) = \frac{\lambda^3}{6} e^{-\lambda}$

---

### 🚀 Trick 2: "At Least 1" Complement Formula
$$\boxed{P(X \ge 1) = 1 - P(X = 0) = 1 - e^{-\lambda}}$$

$$\boxed{P(X \ge 2) = 1 - [P(X=0) + P(X=1)] = 1 - e^{-\lambda}(1 + \lambda)}$$

---

### 🚀 Trick 3: Finding $\lambda$ from Probability Ratios
If $P(X = k_1) = P(X = k_2)$, set up the ratio:
$$\frac{e^{-\lambda} \lambda^{k_1}}{k_1!} = \frac{e^{-\lambda} \lambda^{k_2}}{k_2!} \implies \lambda^{k_2 - k_1} = \frac{k_2!}{k_1!}$$

*Example:* If $P(X=1) = P(X=2) \implies \frac{\lambda^1}{1!} = \frac{\lambda^2}{2!} \implies \mathbf{\lambda = 2}$.

---

### 🧠 Mnemonic: "**RIPE**" for Poisson Model Conditions
- **R**are events in a continuous domain
- **I**ndependent occurrences
- **P**roportional rate: $\lambda = \alpha t$
- **E**qual Mean and Variance ($\mu = \sigma^2 = \lambda$)

---

### 🧠 Mnemonic: "**MVL-P**"
- **M**ean $= \lambda$
- **V**ariance $= \lambda$
- **L**imiting form of Binomial ($n \to \infty, p \to 0, \lambda = np$)
- **P**eak mode $= \lfloor \lambda \rfloor$

## 📝 Step-by-Step Solved GATE Examples

### Example 1: Basic Poisson Probability (GATE 1-Marker)
**Problem:** The number of accidents on a highway follows a Poisson distribution with mean $\lambda = 3$ per week. Find the probability of exactly 2 accidents in a given week.

**Solution:**
Using $P(X = k) = \frac{e^{-\lambda} \lambda^k}{k!}$ with $\lambda = 3$ and $k = 2$:

$$P(X = 2) = \frac{e^{-3} (3)^2}{2!} = \frac{e^{-3} \times 9}{2} = 4.5 e^{-3}$$

Since $e^{-3} \approx 0.049787$:
$$P(X = 2) = 4.5 \times 0.049787 \approx \mathbf{0.2240}$$

$$\boxed{P(X = 2) \approx 0.2240}$$

---

### Example 2: Time Interval Rate Scaling (GATE 2-Marker)
**Problem:** Aircraft arrive at an airport at an average rate of 6 per hour according to a Poisson process. What is the probability that no aircraft arrives in a 10-minute period?

**Solution:**
Rate $\alpha = 6\text{ per hour} = 6\text{ per } 60\text{ minutes}$.
Interval $t = 10\text{ minutes} = \frac{10}{60} = \frac{1}{6}\text{ hour}$.

Scale $\lambda$:
$$\lambda = \alpha \cdot t = 6 \times \frac{1}{6} = \mathbf{1}$$

Now find $P(X = 0)$ for $\lambda = 1$:
$$P(X = 0) = \frac{e^{-1} (1)^0}{0!} = e^{-1} \approx \mathbf{0.3679}$$

$$\boxed{P(X = 0) \approx 0.3679}$$

---

### Example 3: Finding $\lambda$ from Given Condition (GATE 2-Marker)
**Problem:** If $X$ is a Poisson random variable such that $P(X = 1) = P(X = 2)$, find $P(X = 0)$ and $P(X \ge 1)$.

**Solution:**
Set $P(X = 1) = P(X = 2)$:
$$\frac{e^{-\lambda} \lambda^1}{1!} = \frac{e^{-\lambda} \lambda^2}{2!}$$

Cancel $e^{-\lambda}$ (since $e^{-\lambda} \ne 0$) and divide by $\lambda$ (since $\lambda > 0$):
$$1 = \frac{\lambda}{2} \implies \mathbf{\lambda = 2}$$

Now compute:
$$P(X = 0) = e^{-2} \approx \mathbf{0.1353}$$
$$P(X \ge 1) = 1 - P(X = 0) = 1 - e^{-2} \approx 1 - 0.1353 = \mathbf{0.8647}$$

$$\boxed{P(X = 0) = e^{-2} \approx 0.1353, \quad P(X \ge 1) \approx 0.8647}$$

---

### Example 4: Additive Property of Independent Sources (GATE 2-Marker)
**Problem:** A satellite receives noise signals from two independent sources. Source 1 emits noise pulses following Poisson($\lambda_1 = 1.5$ per sec) and Source 2 emits noise pulses following Poisson($\lambda_2 = 2.5$ per sec). Find the probability that the satellite receives at most 1 total noise pulse in a 1-second interval.

**Solution:**
Total pulses $W = X_1 + X_2 \sim \text{Poisson}(\lambda_{total} = \lambda_1 + \lambda_2) = \text{Poisson}(1.5 + 2.5) = \text{Poisson}(4)$.

We want $P(W \le 1) = P(W = 0) + P(W = 1)$:
$$P(W = 0) = e^{-4}$$
$$P(W = 1) = \frac{e^{-4} (4)^1}{1!} = 4 e^{-4}$$

$$P(W \le 1) = e^{-4} + 4e^{-4} = 5 e^{-4}$$

Since $e^{-4} \approx 0.0183156$:
$$P(W \le 1) = 5 \times 0.0183156 \approx \mathbf{0.0916}$$

$$\boxed{P(W \le 1) = 5e^{-4} \approx 0.0916}$$

---

### Example 5: Poisson Approximation to Binomial (GATE 2-Marker)
**Problem:** In a manufacturing process of aircraft rivets, $1\%$ of rivets are defective. A batch of $200$ rivets is randomly selected. Using Poisson approximation, find the probability that the batch contains exactly 2 defective rivets.

**Solution:**
Binomial parameters: $n = 200$, $p = 0.01$.
Since $n \ge 20$ and $p \le 0.05$, use Poisson approximation with:
$$\lambda = np = 200 \times 0.01 = \mathbf{2}$$

Probability of $k = 2$ defectives:
$$P(X = 2) = \frac{e^{-2} (2)^2}{2!} = \frac{4 e^{-2}}{2} = 2 e^{-2}$$

Since $e^{-2} \approx 0.135335$:
$$P(X = 2) = 2 \times 0.135335 \approx \mathbf{0.2707}$$

$$\boxed{P(X = 2) = 2e^{-2} \approx 0.2707}$$

## ⚠️ Common Exam Traps & Pitfalls

> 🔴 **Trap 1: Forgetting to Scale $\lambda$ for the Time Interval**  
> If rate is 6 per hour, and the question asks for a 20-minute interval, you MUST adjust $\lambda = 6 \times \frac{20}{60} = 2$. Using $\lambda = 6$ will lead to an incorrect answer!

> 🔴 **Trap 2: Assuming $X_1 - X_2$ is Poisson**  
> While $X_1 + X_2 \sim \text{Poisson}(\lambda_1 + \lambda_2)$ for independent variables, the difference $X_1 - X_2$ is **NOT** Poisson (it can take negative values!). However, $Var(X_1 - X_2) = \lambda_1 + \lambda_2$.

> 🔴 **Trap 3: Misinterpreting $P(X \ge 1)$ as $1 - P(X=1)$**  
> $P(X \ge 1) = 1 - P(X = 0) = 1 - e^{-\lambda}$. Subtracting $P(X=1)$ instead of $P(X=0)$ is a common careless mistake.

> 🔴 **Trap 4: Confusing Poisson Rate $\alpha$ with Exponential Mean $1/\alpha$**  
> If events arrive at Poisson rate $\alpha = 4/\text{hour}$, the mean time between events is $\frac{1}{\alpha} = \frac{1}{4}\text{ hour} = 15\text{ minutes}$. Don't confuse the event count mean ($\lambda = \alpha t$) with inter-arrival time mean ($1/\alpha$).

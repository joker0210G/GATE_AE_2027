> 🎯 **GATE Weightage:** 2–4 marks per year. The Method of Least Squares is the **universal curve-fitting engine**. It applies to straight lines, polynomials, exponentials, power laws — any model that is **linear in its coefficients**. Mastering this gives you the tools for data analysis in experimental aerodynamics, engine performance characterization, and flight test data reduction.

---

## 📌 Part I: The Universal Least Squares Principle

### Core Idea

Given $n$ data pairs $(x_i, y_i)$ and a model function $\hat{y}(x; \mathbf{a})$ with parameters $\mathbf{a} = [a_0, a_1, \ldots, a_m]^T$:

Find parameters $\mathbf{a}$ that minimize the **Sum of Squared Residuals (SSR)**:

$$\boxed{S(\mathbf{a}) = \sum_{i=1}^n e_i^2 = \sum_{i=1}^n \left[y_i - \hat{y}(x_i; \mathbf{a})\right]^2}$$

This is called the **Least Squares Criterion**.

---

### Why Squared Residuals? (Not Absolute Values)
1. Squaring makes positive and negative errors comparable
2. Squaring penalizes large errors more heavily (outlier-sensitive)
3. Leads to a **smooth, differentiable** objective function → closed-form normal equations
4. Maximizes statistical efficiency for Gaussian noise (Gauss-Markov theorem)

## 🧠 Part II: Linear Regression — Quick Reference

*(See full derivation in [[02 - SUBJECTS/Engineering Mathematics/04.6 - Linear Regression]])*

Model: $\hat{y} = a_0 + a_1 x$

$$\boxed{a_1 = \frac{n\sum x_i y_i - \sum x_i \sum y_i}{n\sum x_i^2 - (\sum x_i)^2}, \qquad a_0 = \bar{y} - a_1 \bar{x}}$$

**Key Identity:** The best-fit line ALWAYS passes through $(\bar{x}, \bar{y})$.

---

## 🧠 Part III: Polynomial Regression — Quadratic & Higher (HIGH YIELD!)

### 1. Quadratic (Parabola) Fitting

Model: $\hat{y} = a_0 + a_1 x + a_2 x^2$

Minimize $S = \sum_{i=1}^n (y_i - a_0 - a_1 x_i - a_2 x_i^2)^2$.

Setting $\partial S / \partial a_0 = 0$, $\partial S / \partial a_1 = 0$, $\partial S / \partial a_2 = 0$ gives **3 Normal Equations** (3×3 system):

$$\boxed{\begin{bmatrix}
n & \sum x_i & \sum x_i^2 \\
\sum x_i & \sum x_i^2 & \sum x_i^3 \\
\sum x_i^2 & \sum x_i^3 & \sum x_i^4
\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix} a_0 \\ a_1 \\ a_2 \end{bmatrix}
=
\begin{bmatrix} \sum y_i \\ \sum x_i y_i \\ \sum x_i^2 y_i \end{bmatrix}}$$

---

### 2. Summary Table for Quadratic Fitting

Build a **7-column table** ($n = $ data points):

| $i$ | $x_i$ | $y_i$ | $x_i^2$ | $x_i^3$ | $x_i^4$ | $x_i y_i$ | $x_i^2 y_i$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $\vdots$ | | | | | | | |
| **Σ** | $\sum x$ | $\sum y$ | $\sum x^2$ | $\sum x^3$ | $\sum x^4$ | $\sum xy$ | $\sum x^2y$ |

---

## 🧠 Part IV: Matrix Formulation — The General Normal Equations (CORE CONCEPT)

For a general linear model $\hat{y} = a_0 \phi_0(x) + a_1 \phi_1(x) + \cdots + a_m \phi_m(x)$, where the **basis functions** $\phi_j(x)$ can be anything ($1, x, x^2, \sin x, e^x$, etc.):

### Design Matrix $\mathbf{A}$

$$\mathbf{A} = \begin{bmatrix}
\phi_0(x_1) & \phi_1(x_1) & \cdots & \phi_m(x_1) \\
\phi_0(x_2) & \phi_1(x_2) & \cdots & \phi_m(x_2) \\
\vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \\
\phi_0(x_n) & \phi_1(x_n) & \cdots & \phi_m(x_n)
\end{bmatrix}_{n \times (m+1)}$$

For linear regression ($\phi_0 = 1, \phi_1 = x$):
$$\mathbf{A} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & x_1 \\ 1 & x_2 \\ \vdots & \vdots \\ 1 & x_n \end{bmatrix}$$

### General Normal Equations (Master Matrix Formula)

$$\boxed{(\mathbf{A}^T \mathbf{A})\, \mathbf{a} = \mathbf{A}^T \mathbf{y}}$$

### Solution (when $\mathbf{A}^T \mathbf{A}$ is invertible):

$$\boxed{\mathbf{a} = (\mathbf{A}^T \mathbf{A})^{-1} \mathbf{A}^T \mathbf{y}}$$

> ⚡ **The matrix $(\mathbf{A}^T \mathbf{A})$ is called the Gram Matrix (or Information Matrix). It is always symmetric and positive semi-definite.**

---

## 🧠 Part V: Error & Quality Metrics

### 1. Residuals
$$e_i = y_i - \hat{y}(x_i) \quad \text{(Observed minus Fitted)}$$

### 2. Sum of Squared Residuals (SSR) / Residual Sum of Squares (RSS)
$$\text{SSR} = \sum_{i=1}^n e_i^2 = \mathbf{y}^T \mathbf{y} - \mathbf{a}^T \mathbf{A}^T \mathbf{y}$$

### 3. Standard Error of Estimate $S_E$
$$\boxed{S_E = \sqrt{\frac{\sum_{i=1}^n e_i^2}{n - (m+1)}} = \sqrt{\frac{\text{SSR}}{n - (m+1)}}}$$

where $m+1$ = number of parameters. A smaller $S_E$ means a better fit.

### 4. Coefficient of Determination $R^2$
$$\boxed{R^2 = 1 - \frac{\text{SSR}}{\text{SST}} = 1 - \frac{\sum (y_i - \hat{y}_i)^2}{\sum (y_i - \bar{y})^2}}$$

- $R^2 = 1$: Perfect fit
- $R^2 = 0$: Model explains nothing (same as using $\bar{y}$ as predictor)
- For linear regression: $R^2 = r^2$ (square of Pearson correlation coefficient)

---

## ⚡ AIR-1 Speed Tricks & Mnemonics

### 🚀 Trick 1: Identify Model Linearity BEFORE Applying Least Squares
Least squares normal equations work for models **linear in their coefficients** $a_0, a_1, \ldots$

| Model | Linear in Coefficients? | Approach |
|---|---|---|
| $a_0 + a_1 x + a_2 x^2$ | ✅ YES | Direct polynomial normal equations |
| $a_0 e^{a_1 x}$ | ❌ NO ($a_1$ is in exponent) | **Linearize first**: $\ln y = \ln a_0 + a_1 x$ |
| $a_0 x^{a_1}$ | ❌ NO ($a_1$ is in power) | **Linearize first**: $\ln y = \ln a_0 + a_1 \ln x$ |
| $a_0 + a_1 x + a_2 \sin x$ | ✅ YES | Treat $\phi_0=1, \phi_1=x, \phi_2=\sin x$ |

---

### 🚀 Trick 2: Quadratic Model Normal Equations from Memory

$$\begin{cases}
\sum y = na_0 + a_1\sum x + a_2\sum x^2 \\
\sum xy = a_0\sum x + a_1\sum x^2 + a_2\sum x^3 \\
\sum x^2y = a_0\sum x^2 + a_1\sum x^3 + a_2\sum x^4
\end{cases}$$

**Memory Pattern:** Each equation starts with a higher power of $x$ on the left side RHS, and the coefficient matrix is symmetric (Gram matrix property)!

---

### 🚀 Trick 3: $R^2$ Calculation Shortcut
Instead of computing SSR and SST separately, use:
$$R^2 = 1 - \frac{S_E^2 \cdot (n - m - 1)}{\frac{1}{n-1}\sum(y_i - \bar{y})^2 \cdot (n-1)}$$

Or for linear regression: just compute $r$ and square it → $R^2 = r^2$.

---

### 🚀 Trick 4: GATE Polynomial Fitting — Always Check Degrees of Freedom
You need at least $m+2$ data points to fit a degree-$m$ polynomial:
- Linear ($m=1$): need $\ge 3$ points minimum (otherwise perfect fit, SSR = 0)
- Quadratic ($m=2$): need $\ge 4$ points
- If $n = m+1$: polynomial passes exactly through ALL points (interpolation, not regression!)

---

### 🧠 Mnemonic: "**PANAM**" for Polynomial Normal Equations
- **P**ower the RHS vector: $[\sum y, \sum xy, \sum x^2y, \ldots]$
- **A**ugment the columns of the Gram matrix: $[1, \sum x, \sum x^2, \ldots]$
- **N**ote symmetry: Gram matrix $\mathbf{A}^T\mathbf{A}$ is always symmetric
- **A**pply normal equations: $\mathbf{A}^T\mathbf{A} \, \mathbf{a} = \mathbf{A}^T \mathbf{y}$
- **M**inimize SSR: Solve the linear system by Gaussian elimination / Cramer's rule

## 📝 Step-by-Step Solved GATE Examples

### Example 1: Quadratic Least Squares Fit (GATE 4-Marker)
**Problem:** Fit a parabola $y = a_0 + a_1 x + a_2 x^2$ to the data:

| $x$ | $0$ | $1$ | $2$ | $3$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $y$ | $1$ | $4$ | $7$ | $8$ |

**Solution:**
**Step 1:** Build 7-column table ($n = 4$):

| $x$ | $y$ | $x^2$ | $x^3$ | $x^4$ | $xy$ | $x^2y$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 1 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| 1 | 4 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 4 | 4 |
| 2 | 7 | 4 | 8 | 16 | 14 | 28 |
| 3 | 8 | 9 | 27 | 81 | 24 | 72 |
| **Σ** | **20** | **14** | **36** | **98** | **42** | **104** |

**Step 2:** Write 3 Normal Equations:
$$4a_0 + 6a_1 + 14a_2 = 20 \quad \cdots (1)$$
$$6a_0 + 14a_1 + 36a_2 = 42 \quad \cdots (2)$$
$$14a_0 + 36a_1 + 98a_2 = 104 \quad \cdots (3)$$

**Step 3:** Solve the 3×3 system (by elimination):
- From (1): $2a_0 + 3a_1 + 7a_2 = 10$ ... (1')
- $(2) - 3 \times (1')$: $(6-6)a_0 + (14-9)a_1 + (36-21)a_2 = 42 - 30 \implies 5a_1 + 15a_2 = 12$ ... (4)
- $(3) - 7 \times (1')$: $(14-14)a_0 + (36-21)a_1 + (98-49)a_2 = 104-70 \implies 15a_1 + 49a_2 = 34$ ... (5)
- $3 \times (4) - (5)$: $(15-15)a_1 + (45-49)a_2 = 36-34 \implies -4a_2 = 2 \implies a_2 = -0.5$
- From (4): $5a_1 + 15(-0.5) = 12 \implies 5a_1 = 19.5 \implies a_1 = 3.9$
- From (1'): $2a_0 + 3(3.9) + 7(-0.5) = 10 \implies 2a_0 = 10 - 11.7 + 3.5 = 1.8 \implies a_0 = 0.9$

$$\boxed{\hat{y} = 0.9 + 3.9x - 0.5x^2}$$

---

### Example 2: Matrix Normal Equation Identification (GATE MCQ)
**Problem:** For a least squares fit of the model $y = a_0 + a_1 x$ to 5 data points, the normal equations in matrix form are $\mathbf{A}^T \mathbf{A} \mathbf{a} = \mathbf{A}^T \mathbf{y}$. Which of the following is the correct form of the Gram matrix $\mathbf{A}^T\mathbf{A}$?

$$\text{(a) } \begin{bmatrix} n & \sum x \\ \sum x & \sum x^2 \end{bmatrix} \qquad \text{(b) } \begin{bmatrix} \sum x & \sum x^2 \\ n & \sum x \end{bmatrix} \qquad \text{(c) } \begin{bmatrix} n & 0 \\ 0 & \sum x^2 \end{bmatrix}$$

**Solution:**
Design matrix $\mathbf{A} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & x_1 \\ 1 & x_2 \\ \vdots \\ 1 & x_n \end{bmatrix}$, so $\mathbf{A}^T\mathbf{A} = \begin{bmatrix} n & \sum x_i \\ \sum x_i & \sum x_i^2 \end{bmatrix}$.

$$\boxed{\text{Correct Answer: (a)}}$$

---

### Example 3: Fitting Engine Performance Data (GATE Aerospace Type)
**Problem:** Specific fuel consumption data for a jet engine is modeled as $SFC = a_0 + a_1 T$ (linear in thrust $T$). Given data:

| $T$ (kN) | $10$ | $20$ | $30$ | $40$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $SFC$ (g/kNs) | $22$ | $19$ | $17$ | $16$ |

Find the best-fit coefficients.

**Solution:**
$n=4$, $\sum T = 100$, $\sum SFC = 74$, $\sum T^2 = 3000$, $\sum T \cdot SFC = 1720$.

$$a_1 = \frac{4(1720) - 100(74)}{4(3000) - 100^2} = \frac{6880 - 7400}{12000 - 10000} = \frac{-520}{2000} = \mathbf{-0.26}$$

$$a_0 = \bar{SFC} - a_1 \bar{T} = 18.5 - (-0.26)(25) = 18.5 + 6.5 = \mathbf{25}$$

$$\boxed{SFC = 25 - 0.26T}$$

> **Interpretation:** As thrust increases by 1 kN, SFC decreases by 0.26 g/kNs (engine becomes more efficient at higher thrust — physically expected!).

## 📐 Master Summary: Method of Least Squares

| Scenario | Model | Normal Equations | Key Formulas |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Linear Fit** | $a_0 + a_1 x$ | 2×2 system | $a_1 = \frac{n\sum xy - \sum x\sum y}{n\sum x^2 - (\sum x)^2}$, $a_0 = \bar{y} - a_1\bar{x}$ |
| **Quadratic Fit** | $a_0 + a_1 x + a_2 x^2$ | 3×3 system | Build 7-col table; solve 3 equations |
| **General** | $\sum_j a_j \phi_j(x)$ | $\mathbf{A}^T\mathbf{A}\mathbf{a} = \mathbf{A}^T\mathbf{y}$ | $\mathbf{a} = (\mathbf{A}^T\mathbf{A})^{-1}\mathbf{A}^T\mathbf{y}$ |
| **Power Law** | $ax^b$ | Linearize: $\ln y = \ln a + b\ln x$ | Linear fit in $(\ln x, \ln y)$ space |
| **Exponential** | $ae^{bx}$ | Linearize: $\ln y = \ln a + bx$ | Linear fit in $(x, \ln y)$ space |

---

## ⚠️ Common Exam Traps & Pitfalls

> 🔴 **Trap 1: Applying Least Squares to Model Non-Linear in Coefficients**  
> $y = a_0 e^{a_1 x}$ — here $a_1$ is in the **exponent**, making it non-linear. You MUST take $\ln$ first: $\ln y = \ln a_0 + a_1 x$. Only then apply least squares directly.

> 🔴 **Trap 2: Confusing $R^2$ and $r^2$ for Non-Linear Models**  
> For linear regression: $R^2 = r^2$ exactly.  
> For polynomial/non-linear regression: $R^2 \ne r^2$ (they are different measures).

> 🔴 **Trap 3: Underdetermined System (Too Few Data Points)**  
> For a degree-$m$ polynomial, you need $n > m+1$ data points to get a proper least squares (regression) solution. If $n = m+1$, you get exact interpolation (every point lies on the curve), and SSR = 0.

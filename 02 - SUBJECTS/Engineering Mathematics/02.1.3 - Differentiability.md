## 📌 Part I: The Concept of Differentiability (Zero to Clear)

### 1. Intuitive Idea

A function $f(x)$ is **differentiable at $x = a$** if its graph has a **unique, well-defined, non-vertical tangent line** at the point $(a, f(a))$.

- **What differentiability looks like:** Smooth, rounded curve with no sudden kinks or jumps.
- **What kills differentiability:** Sharp corners, vertical tangents, or any break in the graph.

---

### 2. Formal Definition — First Principles

$$f'(a) = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{f(a+h) - f(a)}{h}$$

This limit must **exist**, be **finite**, and be the **same** whether $h \to 0^+$ or $h \to 0^-$.

#### Equivalent "Symmetric" Form:
$$f'(a) = \lim_{x \to a} \frac{f(x) - f(a)}{x - a}$$

---

### 3. One-Sided Derivatives

| | Symbol | Definition |
|---|---|---|
| **Right-Hand Derivative (RHD)** | $f'(a^+)$ | $\lim_{h \to 0^+} \dfrac{f(a+h) - f(a)}{h}$ |
| **Left-Hand Derivative (LHD)** | $f'(a^-)$ | $\lim_{h \to 0^+} \dfrac{f(a-h) - f(a)}{-h} = \lim_{h \to 0^+} \dfrac{f(a) - f(a-h)}{h}$ |

$$\boxed{f \text{ differentiable at } a \iff \text{LHD} = \text{RHD} \text{ (both finite)}}$$

## 🧠 Part II: The Critical Hierarchy & Counter-Examples

### The Golden Rule (Most Important for GATE!):

$$\text{Differentiable at } a \implies \text{Continuous at } a$$
$$\text{Continuous at } a \;\not\!\!\!\implies \text{Differentiable at } a$$
$$\text{Not Continuous at } a \implies \text{Not Differentiable at } a$$

### Master Counter-Example Table:

| Function | Continuous at $x=0$? | Differentiable at $x=0$? | Why? |
|---|---|---|---|
| $f(x) = x^2$ | ✅ Yes | ✅ Yes | Smooth parabola |
| $f(x) = \|x\|$ | ✅ Yes | ❌ No | LHD $= -1$, RHD $= +1$ |
| $f(x) = x^{1/3}$ | ✅ Yes | ❌ No | Vertical tangent at $0$ ($f' \to \infty$) |
| $f(x) = x \sin(1/x)$, $f(0)=0$ | ✅ Yes | ❌ No | Derivative oscillates, no limit |
| $f(x) = x^2 \sin(1/x)$, $f(0)=0$ | ✅ Yes | ✅ Yes | $f'(0) = 0$ by squeeze |
| $f(x) = \lfloor x \rfloor$ | ❌ No (at integers) | ❌ No | Not even continuous |

> 💡 **Mnemonic:** **"D-C-L" Hierarchy:** Differentiable → Continuous → Limit exists  
> Going up requires each step; going down skips are impossible.

## 🧠 Part III: Standard Differentiation Rules (All You Need)

### Foundational Rules:

| Rule | Formula | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Constant | $\frac{d}{dx}[c] = 0$ | $\frac{d}{dx}[7] = 0$ |
| Power | $\frac{d}{dx}[x^n] = nx^{n-1}$ | $\frac{d}{dx}[x^5] = 5x^4$ |
| Sum/Difference | $(f \pm g)' = f' \pm g'$ | Linear in differentiation |
| Product Rule | $(fg)' = f'g + fg'$ | *(See mnemonic below)* |
| Quotient Rule | $\left(\frac{f}{g}\right)' = \frac{f'g - fg'}{g^2}$ | *(See mnemonic below)* |
| Chain Rule | $\frac{d}{dx}[f(g(x))] = f'(g(x)) \cdot g'(x)$ | $\frac{d}{dx}[\sin(x^2)] = \cos(x^2) \cdot 2x$ |
| Constant Multiple | $(cf)' = cf'$ | $\frac{d}{dx}[5\sin x] = 5\cos x$ |

> 🎯 **Product Rule Mnemonic:** "**d-UV = V dU + U dV**"  
> (Differentiate first, keep second) + (Keep first, differentiate second)

> 🎯 **Quotient Rule Mnemonic:** **"Low d-High minus High d-Low, over Low squared"**  
> $\left(\frac{H}{L}\right)' = \frac{L \cdot H' - H \cdot L'}{L^2}$

---

### Derivatives of Standard Functions (Must Memorize):

#### Algebraic & Exponential:
| $f(x)$ | $f'(x)$ |
|---|---|
| $x^n$ | $nx^{n-1}$ |
| $e^x$ | $e^x$ |
| $e^{ax}$ | $ae^{ax}$ |
| $a^x$ | $a^x \ln a$ |
| $\ln x$ | $\dfrac{1}{x}$ ($x > 0$) |
| $\log_a x$ | $\dfrac{1}{x \ln a}$ |
| $\sqrt{x}$ | $\dfrac{1}{2\sqrt{x}}$ |

#### Trigonometric:
| $f(x)$ | $f'(x)$ |
|---|---|
| $\sin x$ | $\cos x$ |
| $\cos x$ | $-\sin x$ |
| $\tan x$ | $\sec^2 x$ |
| $\cot x$ | $-\csc^2 x$ |
| $\sec x$ | $\sec x \tan x$ |
| $\csc x$ | $-\csc x \cot x$ |

> 🎯 **Trig Derivative Mnemonic:** **"Sin→Cos, Cos→−Sin"** (the negative propagates around the co-functions)  
> Co-functions (cos, cot, csc) always get a **negative sign** in their derivatives.

#### Inverse Trigonometric (GATE High-Value!):
| $f(x)$ | $f'(x)$ | Domain |
|---|---|---|
| $\sin^{-1} x$ | $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{1-x^2}}$ | $\|x\| < 1$ |
| $\cos^{-1} x$ | $\dfrac{-1}{\sqrt{1-x^2}}$ | $\|x\| < 1$ |
| $\tan^{-1} x$ | $\dfrac{1}{1+x^2}$ | $x \in \mathbb{R}$ |
| $\cot^{-1} x$ | $\dfrac{-1}{1+x^2}$ | $x \in \mathbb{R}$ |
| $\sec^{-1} x$ | $\dfrac{1}{\|x\|\sqrt{x^2-1}}$ | $\|x\| > 1$ |
| $\csc^{-1} x$ | $\dfrac{-1}{\|x\|\sqrt{x^2-1}}$ | $\|x\| > 1$ |

> 🎯 **Inverse Trig Mnemonic:** **"sin⁻¹ and cos⁻¹ use $\sqrt{1-x^2}$; tan⁻¹ and cot⁻¹ use $1+x^2$"**  
> "Co-functions always get the negative sign."

## 🧠 Part IV: Higher-Order Derivatives

### Notation:
$$f'(x) = \frac{df}{dx} = Df \qquad f''(x) = \frac{d^2f}{dx^2} = D^2f \qquad f^{(n)}(x) = \frac{d^n f}{dx^n}$$

### Standard $n$-th Derivative Formulas (GATE Direct Questions):

| $f(x)$ | $f^{(n)}(x)$ |
|---|---|
| $x^m$ | $\dfrac{m!}{(m-n)!} x^{m-n}$ (zero if $n > m$) |
| $e^{ax}$ | $a^n e^{ax}$ |
| $a^x$ | $a^x (\ln a)^n$ |
| $\sin(ax + b)$ | $a^n \sin\!\left(ax + b + \dfrac{n\pi}{2}\right)$ |
| $\cos(ax + b)$ | $a^n \cos\!\left(ax + b + \dfrac{n\pi}{2}\right)$ |
| $\ln x$ | $\dfrac{(-1)^{n-1}(n-1)!}{x^n}$ |
| $\dfrac{1}{ax+b}$ | $\dfrac{(-1)^n \cdot n! \cdot a^n}{(ax+b)^{n+1}}$ |

> 🎯 **Trig $n$-th Derivative Trick:** Each differentiation adds $\frac{\pi}{2}$ to the argument!  
> $\sin x \to \cos x = \sin(x + \pi/2) \to -\sin x = \sin(x + \pi) \to \ldots$

---

### Leibniz Formula for the $n$-th Derivative of a Product:

$$[f(x) \cdot g(x)]^{(n)} = \sum_{k=0}^{n} \binom{n}{k} f^{(k)}(x) \cdot g^{(n-k)}(x)$$

*(Exactly like the Binomial Theorem but with derivatives instead of powers!)*

> 🎯 **Mnemonic:** "Leibniz = Binomial with Derivatives"

---

## 🧠 Part V: Chain Rule — The Most Tested Rule in GATE

> 📖 **Deep-Dive Topic Note:** [[02 - SUBJECTS/Engineering Mathematics/02.1.4 - Chain Rule|Chain Rule (Peel-the-Onion, All Composite Forms, Implicit/Parametric/Multivariable, Inverse Trig Substitutions, 5 Speed Tricks, 7 Solved Examples)]] `#status/first-read`

### 1. Simple Chain Rule
$$\frac{d}{dx}[f(g(x))] = f'(g(x)) \cdot g'(x) \quad \text{("outside × inside'")}$$

### 2. Extended Chain Rule (Multiple Compositions)
$$\frac{d}{dx}[f(g(h(x)))] = f'(g(h(x))) \cdot g'(h(x)) \cdot h'(x)$$

### 3. Chain Rule via Substitution Strategy
For $y = f(u)$, $u = g(x)$:
$$\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{dy}{du} \cdot \frac{du}{dx}$$

---

## 🧠 Part VI: Implicit Differentiation

When $y$ is defined implicitly by $F(x, y) = 0$ (cannot isolate $y$ easily):

### Method:
Differentiate both sides with respect to $x$, treating $y$ as a function of $x$, applying chain rule to every $y$-term:

$$\frac{d}{dx}[F(x,y) = 0] \implies \frac{\partial F}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial F}{\partial y} \cdot \frac{dy}{dx} = 0 \implies \frac{dy}{dx} = -\frac{\partial F/\partial x}{\partial F/\partial y} = -\frac{F_x}{F_y}$$

### ⚡ Fast Implicit Differentiation Formula:
$$\boxed{\frac{dy}{dx} = -\frac{F_x}{F_y}}$$

---

## 🧠 Part VII: Logarithmic Differentiation

### When to Use:
1. Functions of the form $y = [f(x)]^{g(x)}$ (variable base AND variable exponent).
2. Long products/quotients of many factors.

### Method:
Take $\ln$ of both sides → differentiate → multiply back by $y$:

$$y = [f(x)]^{g(x)} \implies \ln y = g(x) \ln f(x) \implies \frac{1}{y} \frac{dy}{dx} = g'(x)\ln f(x) + g(x)\frac{f'(x)}{f(x)}$$
$$\implies \frac{dy}{dx} = y \left[ g'(x)\ln f(x) + \frac{g(x) f'(x)}{f(x)} \right]$$

---

## 🧠 Part VIII: Parametric & Inverse Function Differentiation

### 1. Parametric Differentiation
When $x = x(t)$ and $y = y(t)$:

$$\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{dy/dt}{dx/dt} = \frac{\dot{y}}{\dot{x}}$$

$$\frac{d^2y}{dx^2} = \frac{\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{dy}{dx}\right)}{dx/dt} = \frac{\dot{x}\ddot{y} - \dot{y}\ddot{x}}{\dot{x}^3}$$

### 2. Inverse Function Differentiation
If $y = f(x)$ is invertible, and $x = g(y)$ is its inverse:

$$\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{1}{dx/dy} = \frac{1}{g'(y)} \qquad \left(\text{valid where } f'(x) \neq 0\right)$$

## ⚡ AIR-1 Topper Speed Tricks & Mnemonics

### 🚀 Trick 1: LHD/RHD from Piecewise — The 3-Step Algorithm
For piecewise $f(x) = \begin{cases} A(x) & x < a \\ B(x) & x \ge a \end{cases}$ at $x = a$:
1. **LHD** $= A'(a)$ (differentiate $A(x)$, substitute $x = a$)
2. **RHD** $= B'(a)$ (differentiate $B(x)$, substitute $x = a$)
3. If LHD $=$ RHD → differentiable; else → not differentiable (sharp corner)

> ⚠️ First always verify continuity! If not continuous → automatically not differentiable.

---

### 🚀 Trick 2: Power $n$ Shift Pattern for Differentiability at Origin
For $f(x) = x^n \sin(1/x)$ (or $x^n \cos(1/x)$), $f(0) = 0$:
- $n \ge 2 \implies$ Differentiable at $x = 0$ (use squeeze: $f'(0) = 0$)
- $n = 1 \implies$ Continuous but NOT differentiable ($f'(0)$ doesn't exist — oscillates)
- $n = 0 \implies$ Not even continuous at $x = 0$

---

### 🚀 Trick 3: Logarithmic Differentiation for $x^x$ type (GATE Classic!)
$y = x^x$: Take $\ln$: $\ln y = x \ln x$. Differentiate: $\frac{y'}{y} = \ln x + 1$. So $y' = x^x(\ln x + 1)$.

---

### 🚀 Trick 4: Chain Rule "Peel the Onion" Method
For deeply nested functions $f(g(h(\ldots)))$:
- **Start from outside, peel inward:**
  $\frac{d}{dx}[\sin^3(e^{2x})] = 3\sin^2(e^{2x}) \cdot \cos(e^{2x}) \cdot 2e^{2x} = 6e^{2x}\sin^2(e^{2x})\cos(e^{2x})$

---

### 🚀 Trick 5: Inverse Trig Derivative Pattern
Notice the **complementary pair pattern** — each pair sums to zero!
$$(\sin^{-1} x)' + (\cos^{-1} x)' = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1-x^2}} - \frac{1}{\sqrt{1-x^2}} = 0$$
$$(\tan^{-1} x)' + (\cot^{-1} x)' = \frac{1}{1+x^2} - \frac{1}{1+x^2} = 0$$

So: $\sin^{-1} x + \cos^{-1} x = \frac{\pi}{2}$ (constant!) → derivative is automatically 0 on both sides! ✅

## 📝 Step-by-Step Solved GATE Examples

### Example 1: LHD/RHD Test — Piecewise Function
**Problem:** Check differentiability at $x = 1$ for:
$$f(x) = \begin{cases} x^2 & x \le 1 \\ 2x - 1 & x > 1 \end{cases}$$

**Solution:**
- **Continuity first:** LHL $= \lim_{x\to 1^-} x^2 = 1$; RHL $= \lim_{x\to 1^+} (2x-1) = 1$; $f(1) = 1$. ✅ Continuous.
- **LHD:** $(x^2)' = 2x \big|_{x=1} = 2$
- **RHD:** $(2x-1)' = 2 \big|_{x=1} = 2$
- LHD $=$ RHD $= 2$ → $f$ is **differentiable at $x = 1$** ✅

---

### Example 2: LHD/RHD Test — Not Differentiable
**Problem:** Check differentiability at $x = 0$ for:
$$f(x) = \begin{cases} x^2 + 1 & x \ge 0 \\ 2x + 1 & x < 0 \end{cases}$$

**Solution:**
- Continuity: LHL $= 1$, RHL $= 1$, $f(0) = 1$ ✅ Continuous.
- **LHD:** $(2x+1)' = 2 \big|_{x=0} = 2$
- **RHD:** $(x^2+1)' = 2x \big|_{x=0} = 0$
- LHD $= 2 \neq$ RHD $= 0$ → **NOT differentiable at $x = 0$** ❌ (Sharp corner!)

---

### Example 3: Chain Rule
**Problem:** Find $\frac{dy}{dx}$ for $y = \ln(\sin(e^{x^2}))$.

**Solution (Peel the Onion):**
$$\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{1}{\sin(e^{x^2})} \cdot \cos(e^{x^2}) \cdot e^{x^2} \cdot 2x = \frac{2xe^{x^2}\cos(e^{x^2})}{\sin(e^{x^2})} = 2xe^{x^2}\cot(e^{x^2})$$

---

### Example 4: Implicit Differentiation
**Problem:** Find $\frac{dy}{dx}$ if $x^3 + y^3 = 3axy$ (Folium of Descartes).

**Solution (Fast Formula):** $F(x,y) = x^3 + y^3 - 3axy = 0$
$$F_x = 3x^2 - 3ay, \quad F_y = 3y^2 - 3ax$$
$$\frac{dy}{dx} = -\frac{F_x}{F_y} = -\frac{3x^2 - 3ay}{3y^2 - 3ax} = \frac{ay - x^2}{y^2 - ax}$$

---

### Example 5: Logarithmic Differentiation — $x^x$ type
**Problem:** Find $\frac{dy}{dx}$ for $y = x^{\sin x}$.

**Solution:**
$\ln y = \sin x \cdot \ln x$  
$\frac{1}{y}\frac{dy}{dx} = \cos x \cdot \ln x + \sin x \cdot \frac{1}{x}$  
$$\frac{dy}{dx} = x^{\sin x} \left(\cos x \ln x + \frac{\sin x}{x}\right)$$

---

### Example 6: $n$-th Derivative
**Problem (GATE PYQ type):** Find the $n$-th derivative of $f(x) = e^{2x} \sin(3x)$.

**Solution:** Use Leibniz formula or recognize the pattern. Direct formula for $e^{ax}\sin(bx)$:
$$[e^{ax}\sin(bx)]^{(n)} = r^n e^{ax} \sin(bx + n\theta)$$
where $r = \sqrt{a^2 + b^2}$ and $\theta = \tan^{-1}(b/a)$.

Here $a = 2$, $b = 3$:
- $r = \sqrt{4 + 9} = \sqrt{13}$
- $\theta = \tan^{-1}(3/2)$

$$[e^{2x}\sin(3x)]^{(n)} = (\sqrt{13})^n \cdot e^{2x} \sin\!\left(3x + n\tan^{-1}\!\frac{3}{2}\right)$$

## ⚠️ Common Exam Traps & Pitfalls

> 🔴 **Trap 1: Checking Differentiability WITHOUT Checking Continuity First**  
> *Mistake:* Directly computing LHD and RHD for a discontinuous function.  
> *Correction:* **Always check continuity first!** If the function is discontinuous at $a$, it is automatically NOT differentiable there. Skip the LHD/RHD computation.

> 🔴 **Trap 2: Differentiating Inside $|f(x)|$ at the Zero-Crossing**  
> *Mistake:* $\frac{d}{dx}|x^2 - 1| = 2x$ everywhere.  
> *Correction:* At zeros of $f(x)$ (here $x = \pm 1$), check LHD and RHD explicitly — there are sharp corners at those points.

> 🔴 **Trap 3: Chain Rule — Forgetting to Multiply by Inner Derivative**  
> *Mistake:* $\frac{d}{dx}[\sin(x^2)] = \cos(x^2)$ ❌  
> *Correction:* $= \cos(x^2) \cdot 2x$ ✅ (multiply by derivative of inner function $x^2$)

> 🔴 **Trap 4: Quotient Rule Sign Error**  
> *Mistake:* $\left(\frac{f}{g}\right)' = \frac{f'g + fg'}{g^2}$ ❌  
> *Correction:* $\left(\frac{f}{g}\right)' = \frac{f'g - fg'}{g^2}$ ✅ (minus, not plus!)

> 🔴 **Trap 5: $x^n$ $n$-th Derivative for $n > m$**  
> *Mistake:* $[x^3]^{(4)} = 4! \cdot x^{-1}$ ❌  
> *Correction:* $[x^3]^{(4)} = 0$ ✅ (polynomial of degree $m$ has zero $n$-th derivative for $n > m$)

## 📌 Part I: Definition & Fundamentals of Divergence

### 1. Definition

For a vector field $\vec{F}(x, y, z) = F_1\hat{i} + F_2\hat{j} + F_3\hat{k}$:

$$\boxed{\nabla \cdot \vec{F} = \text{div}\,\vec{F} = \frac{\partial F_1}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial F_2}{\partial y} + \frac{\partial F_3}{\partial z}}$$

> 💡 **Key Takeaway:** Divergence takes a **VECTOR field** $\vec{F}$ as input and produces a **SCALAR field** $\nabla \cdot \vec{F}$ as output! (Dot product of operator $\nabla$ and vector $\vec{F}$).

---

### 2. Physical Meaning of Divergence

Divergence measures the **net outward flux of a vector field per unit volume** at a given point:

```
  \   |   /               |  |  |               /   |   \
   \  |  /                |  |  |              /    |    \
  --- P ---              --- P ---            --->  P  <---
   /  |  \                |  |  |              \    |    /
  /   |   \               |  |  |               \   |   /

∇ · F > 0 (SOURCE)       ∇ · F = 0            ∇ · F < 0 (SINK)
Net Expansion            Incompressible       Net Compression
```

- **$\nabla \cdot \vec{F} > 0$ (Source):** Fluid/flux is expanding outward from point $P$ (mass or heat generation).
- **$\nabla \cdot \vec{F} < 0$ (Sink):** Fluid/flux is compressing inward toward point $P$ (mass absorption or heat sink).
- **$\nabla \cdot \vec{F} = 0$ (Solenoidal):** Fluid entering unit volume equals fluid leaving it (no sources, no sinks, incompressible flow).

---

## 🧠 Part II: Solenoidal Vector Fields (#1 GATE Question Pattern!)

### 1. Definition

A vector field $\vec{F}$ is called **Solenoidal** if its divergence is zero everywhere in the domain:

$$\boxed{\vec{F} \text{ is Solenoidal} \iff \nabla \cdot \vec{F} = 0}$$

---

### 2. Engineering & Aerospace Applications

#### A. Aerodynamics — Incompressible Continuity Equation
In fluid dynamics, conservation of mass for an incompressible fluid density ($\rho = \text{constant}$) simplifies to:

$$\boxed{\nabla \cdot \vec{V} = \frac{\partial u}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial v}{\partial y} + \frac{\partial w}{\partial z} = 0}$$

where $\vec{V} = u\hat{i} + v\hat{j} + w\hat{k}$ is the velocity vector field.

#### B. Electromagnetics — Gauss's Law for Magnetism
$$\nabla \cdot \vec{B} = 0$$
Magnetic fields are always solenoidal (magnetic monopoles do not exist; magnetic field lines form closed loops).

---

### 3. GATE Solenoidal Constant Problem Pattern

GATE frequently asks: *"Find constant $a$ (or $k$) such that vector field $\vec{F}$ is solenoidal."*

$$\text{Strategy: Compute } \frac{\partial F_1}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial F_2}{\partial y} + \frac{\partial F_3}{\partial z} = 0 \text{ and solve for the unknown constant.}$$

## 🧠 Part III: Position Vector ($\vec{r}$) & Radial Field Identities

Let $\vec{r} = x\hat{i} + y\hat{j} + z\hat{k}$ be the position vector, with magnitude $r = \|\vec{r}\| = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2 + z^2}$.

### 🎯 Master Table of Radial Divergence Identities (GATE Goldmines!)

| Identity Formula | Result in 3D | Notes / Derivation Idea |
|---|---|---|
| $\nabla \cdot \vec{r}$ | $\mathbf{3}$ | $\frac{\partial x}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial y}{\partial y} + \frac{\partial z}{\partial z} = 1+1+1 = 3$ |
| $\nabla \cdot (f(r)\vec{r})$ | $\mathbf{r f'(r) + 3f(r)}$ | Product rule for divergence |
| $\nabla \cdot (r^n \vec{r})$ | $\mathbf{(n+3) r^n}$ | **Power formula for radial vector fields** |
| $\nabla \cdot \left(\dfrac{\vec{r}}{r^3}\right)$ | $\mathbf{0} \quad (r \neq 0)$ | **Inverse-Square Law is Solenoidal!** ($n = -3$) |
| $\nabla \cdot (\phi \vec{F})$ | $\mathbf{\phi(\nabla \cdot \vec{F}) + \vec{F} \cdot (\nabla\phi)}$ | Product rule for scalar $\times$ vector |
| $\nabla \cdot (\vec{A} \times \vec{B})$ | $\mathbf{\vec{B} \cdot (\nabla \times \vec{A}) - \vec{A} \cdot (\nabla \times \vec{B})}$ | Divergence of cross product |

---

### 🔑 Derivation of Radial Power Formula $\nabla \cdot (r^n \vec{r}) = (n+3)r^n$:

$$\nabla \cdot (r^n \vec{r}) = r^n (\nabla \cdot \vec{r}) + \vec{r} \cdot \nabla(r^n)$$

Using $\nabla \cdot \vec{r} = 3$ and $\nabla(r^n) = n r^{n-2} \vec{r}$ (from Gradient Note):

$$= r^n (3) + \vec{r} \cdot (n r^{n-2} \vec{r}) = 3r^n + n r^{n-2} (\vec{r} \cdot \vec{r})$$

Since $\vec{r} \cdot \vec{r} = r^2$:

$$= 3r^n + n r^{n-2} (r^2) = 3r^n + n r^n = \mathbf{(n+3) r^n}$$

> ⚡ **Special Case ($n = -3$):**  
> $\nabla \cdot \left(\frac{\vec{r}}{r^3}\right) = (-3 + 3) r^{-3} = \mathbf{0}$  
> This proves gravitational ($\vec{g} \propto \frac{\vec{r}}{r^3}$) and electrostatic ($\vec{E} \propto \frac{\vec{r}}{r^3}$) fields are **solenoidal everywhere except at origin $r=0$**!

## 🧠 Part IV: The Laplacian Operator ($\nabla^2$)

### 1. Definition

The **Laplacian** of a scalar field $\phi$ is the divergence of its gradient:

$$\boxed{\nabla^2\phi = \nabla \cdot (\nabla\phi) = \frac{\partial^2\phi}{\partial x^2} + \frac{\partial^2\phi}{\partial y^2} + \frac{\partial^2\phi}{\partial z^2}}$$

---

### 2. Laplace Equation & Harmonic Functions

A scalar field $\phi$ satisfying the **Laplace Equation** is called a **Harmonic Function**:

$$\boxed{\nabla^2\phi = 0 \iff \frac{\partial^2\phi}{\partial x^2} + \frac{\partial^2\phi}{\partial y^2} + \frac{\partial^2\phi}{\partial z^2} = 0}$$

#### Aerospace Application:
In irrotational, incompressible flow, the Velocity Potential $\phi$ satisfies Laplace's Equation $\nabla^2\phi = 0$.

---

### 3. Master Divergence-Operator Identities (GATE MCQs!)

$$\boxed{\nabla \cdot (\nabla \times \vec{F}) = 0} \quad \text{(\textbf{Divergence of ANY Curl is ALWAYS ZERO!})}$$

$$\boxed{\nabla \times (\nabla\phi) = \vec{0}} \quad \text{(\textbf{Curl of ANY Gradient is ALWAYS ZERO!})}$$

$$\boxed{\nabla \cdot (\nabla\phi) = \nabla^2\phi} \quad \text{(\textbf{Divergence of Gradient = Laplacian})}$$

---

## 🧠 Part V: Preview of Gauss's Divergence Theorem

The **Gauss Divergence Theorem** converts a surface integral of flux into a volume integral of divergence:

$$\boxed{\iint_S \vec{F} \cdot \hat{n}\,dS = \iiint_V (\nabla \cdot \vec{F})\,dV}$$

> 🔮 **Preview Note:** If $\vec{F}$ is solenoidal ($\nabla \cdot \vec{F} = 0$), then the net outward flux through ANY closed surface enclosing that domain is **ZERO**:  
> $$\iiint_V (0)\,dV = \mathbf{0}$$

## ⚡ AIR-1 Topper Speed Tricks & Mnemonics

### 🚀 Trick 1: Solenoidal Constant Problem in 10 Seconds
If GATE gives $\vec{F} = F_1\hat{i} + F_2\hat{j} + F_3\hat{k}$ with an unknown $a$:
1. Write $\frac{\partial F_1}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial F_2}{\partial y} + \frac{\partial F_3}{\partial z} = 0$.
2. Differentiate each component w.r.t. its corresponding coordinate.
3. Solve linear equation for $a$. Done in 10 seconds!

---

### 🚀 Trick 2: $\nabla \cdot \vec{r} = 3$ Rule
- In 3D: $\nabla \cdot \vec{r} = 3$
- In 2D: $\nabla \cdot \vec{r} = 2$
- In 1D: $\nabla \cdot \vec{r} = 1$
- In $N$-dimensional space: $\nabla \cdot \vec{r} = N$

---

### 🚀 Trick 3: Radial Power Divergence Shortcut — $(n+3)r^n$
For $\nabla \cdot (r^n \vec{r})$, just add $3$ to the power $n$ and multiply by $r^n$:
- $\nabla \cdot (r^2 \vec{r}) = (2+3)r^2 = 5r^2$ (1 second!)
- $\nabla \cdot (r^5 \vec{r}) = (5+3)r^5 = 8r^5$ (1 second!)
- $\nabla \cdot \left(\frac{\vec{r}}{r^3}\right) = (-3+3)r^{-3} = 0$ (1 second!)

---

### 🚀 Trick 4: Div-of-Curl Zero Filter
If an option asks for $\nabla \cdot \vec{G}$ where $\vec{G} = \nabla \times \vec{A}$:  
**Do NOT compute $\vec{G}$ or its divergence!**  
Answer is **$0$** instantly by identity $\nabla \cdot (\nabla \times \vec{A}) = 0$.

## 📝 Step-by-Step Solved GATE Examples

### Example 1: Finding Constant for Solenoidal Field (GATE PYQ)
**Problem:** Find $a$ if the vector field $\vec{F} = (x + 3y)\hat{i} + (y - 2z)\hat{j} + (x + az)\hat{k}$ is solenoidal.

**Solution:**
For $\vec{F}$ to be solenoidal, $\nabla \cdot \vec{F} = 0$:

$$\nabla \cdot \vec{F} = \frac{\partial}{\partial x}(x + 3y) + \frac{\partial}{\partial y}(y - 2z) + \frac{\partial}{\partial z}(x + az)$$
$$= 1 + 1 + a = 2 + a = 0$$
$$\implies a = \mathbf{-2}$$

$$\boxed{a = -2}$$

---

### Example 2: Divergence of Radial Field
**Problem:** Evaluate $\nabla \cdot (r^4 \vec{r})$ where $\vec{r} = x\hat{i} + y\hat{j} + z\hat{k}$ and $r = \|\vec{r}\|$.

**Solution (Using Speed Trick 3):**
Here $n = 4$.
$$\nabla \cdot (r^n \vec{r}) = (n + 3) r^n \implies \nabla \cdot (r^4 \vec{r}) = (4 + 3) r^4 = \mathbf{7 r^4}$$

$$\boxed{\nabla \cdot (r^4 \vec{r}) = 7r^4}$$

---

### Example 3: Divergence of Product $\nabla \cdot (\phi \vec{F})$
**Problem:** If $\phi = x^2 + y^2 + z^2$ and $\vec{F} = x\hat{i} + y\hat{j} + z\hat{k} = \vec{r}$, find $\nabla \cdot (\phi \vec{F})$.

**Solution:**
Use product identity: $\nabla \cdot (\phi \vec{F}) = \phi(\nabla \cdot \vec{F}) + \vec{F} \cdot (\nabla\phi)$

1. $\phi = r^2$
2. $\nabla \cdot \vec{F} = \nabla \cdot \vec{r} = 3$
3. $\nabla\phi = \nabla(r^2) = 2\vec{r}$
4. Combine:
   $$\nabla \cdot (\phi \vec{F}) = (r^2)(3) + \vec{r} \cdot (2\vec{r}) = 3r^2 + 2(\vec{r} \cdot \vec{r}) = 3r^2 + 2r^2 = \mathbf{5r^2}$$

$$\boxed{\nabla \cdot (\phi \vec{F}) = 5(x^2 + y^2 + z^2)}$$

---

### Example 4: Incompressible Flow Continuity Check (Aerodynamics)
**Problem:** The 2D velocity field of a fluid flow is given by $\vec{V} = (2x^2 - y^2)\hat{i} + (a xy)\hat{j}$. For the flow to be incompressible, find $a$.

**Solution:**
For 2D incompressible flow: $\frac{\partial u}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial v}{\partial y} = 0$

Here $u = 2x^2 - y^2$ and $v = a xy$.

$$\frac{\partial u}{\partial x} = 4x$$
$$\frac{\partial v}{\partial y} = ax$$
$$4x + ax = 0 \implies (4 + a)x = 0 \implies a = \mathbf{-4}$$

$$\boxed{a = -4}$$

---

### Example 5: Laplacian of Scalar Field
**Problem:** Find the Laplacian $\nabla^2\phi$ for $\phi = x^3 + y^3 + z^3 - 3xyz$.

**Solution:**
$$\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial x} = 3x^2 - 3yz \implies \frac{\partial^2\phi}{\partial x^2} = 6x$$
$$\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial y} = 3y^2 - 3xz \implies \frac{\partial^2\phi}{\partial y^2} = 6y$$
$$\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial z} = 3z^2 - 3xy \implies \frac{\partial^2\phi}{\partial z^2} = 6z$$

$$\nabla^2\phi = \frac{\partial^2\phi}{\partial x^2} + \frac{\partial^2\phi}{\partial y^2} + \frac{\partial^2\phi}{\partial z^2} = 6x + 6y + 6z = \mathbf{6(x + y + z)}$$

$$\boxed{\nabla^2\phi = 6(x + y + z)}$$

## ⚠️ Common Exam Traps & Pitfalls

> 🔴 **Trap 1: Confusing Divergence (Scalar) with Gradient (Vector) or Curl (Vector)**  
> *Mistake:* Writing $\nabla \cdot \vec{F}$ with $\hat{i}, \hat{j}, \hat{k}$ unit vectors.  
> *Correction:* Divergence is a **dot product** — the output is a **SCALAR** (no $\hat{i}, \hat{j}, \hat{k}$!).

> 🔴 **Trap 2: Dimension Error in $\nabla \cdot \vec{r}$**  
> *Mistake:* Using $\nabla \cdot \vec{r} = 3$ in a 2D plane problem.  
> *Correction:* In 2D plane ($\vec{r} = x\hat{i} + y\hat{j}$), $\nabla \cdot \vec{r} = 1 + 1 = \mathbf{2}$!

> 🔴 **Trap 3: Forgetting $r=0$ Singularity for $1/r^2$ Fields**  
> *Mistake:* Claiming $\nabla \cdot \left(\frac{\vec{r}}{r^3}\right) = 0$ everywhere including origin.  
> *Correction:* It is solenoidal for $r \neq 0$. At $r = 0$, it is a point source (Dirac delta behavior: $\nabla \cdot \left(\frac{\vec{r}}{r^3}\right) = 4\pi \delta^3(\vec{r})$).

> 🔴 **Trap 4: Partial Derivative Component Mix-up**  
> *Mistake:* Computing $\frac{\partial F_2}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial F_1}{\partial y} + \frac{\partial F_3}{\partial z}$.  
> *Correction:* $\nabla \cdot \vec{F} = \frac{\partial F_1}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial F_2}{\partial y} + \frac{\partial F_3}{\partial z}$ (Component 1 w.r.t $x$, Component 2 w.r.t $y$, Component 3 w.r.t $z$).

## 📌 Part I: The Concept of Continuity (Zero to Clear)

### 1. Intuitive Idea

A function $f(x)$ is **continuous** at a point $x = a$ if you can draw its graph **without lifting your pen** at that point.

More precisely, as $x$ approaches $a$ from any direction, $f(x)$ approaches exactly the value the function actually takes at $a$.

---

### 2. Formal Definition — The Three-Condition Test

$f(x)$ is **continuous at $x = a$** if and only if **ALL THREE** of the following hold:

| Condition | Mathematical Statement | What Can Go Wrong |
|---|---|---|
| **1. $f(a)$ is defined** | $f(a)$ exists and is finite | Hole at $x = a$ (function undefined) |
| **2. Limit exists** | $\lim_{x \to a^-} f(x) = \lim_{x \to a^+} f(x) = L$ (finite) | LHL $\neq$ RHL, or limit $= \pm\infty$ |
| **3. Limit equals value** | $\lim_{x \to a} f(x) = f(a)$ | Limit exists but $\neq f(a)$ |

$$\boxed{f(x) \text{ is continuous at } x = a \iff \lim_{x \to a} f(x) = f(a)}$$

---

### 3. Continuity on an Interval

- **Continuous on $(a, b)$:** Continuous at every point in the open interval.
- **Continuous on $[a, b]$:** Continuous on $(a, b)$ AND:
  - **Right-continuous at $a$:** $\lim_{x \to a^+} f(x) = f(a)$
  - **Left-continuous at $b$:** $\lim_{x \to b^-} f(x) = f(b)$

## 🧠 Part II: Types of Discontinuities (Classification Master Table)

> 🎯 **GATE often asks:** "Identify the type of discontinuity at $x = a$." Master this table.

### The Four Types of Discontinuity:

```
              Discontinuity at x = a
                       |
          +------------+------------+
          |                         |
    LHL ≠ RHL                 LHL = RHL = L
   (One-sided)                      |
          |                +--------+--------+
        Jump             L = f(a)?       L ≠ f(a)
     Discontinuity             |              |
                           Continuous    Removable
                                      Discontinuity
                         [If L = ±∞: Infinite Discontinuity]
                         [If limit DNE (oscillates): Oscillatory]
```

| Type | LHL | RHL | $f(a)$ | Removable? | Example |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Removable (Hole)** | $L$ | $L$ | $\neq L$ or undefined | ✅ Yes — redefine $f(a) = L$ | $f(x) = \frac{\sin x}{x}$, $x \neq 0$ |
| **Jump** | $L_1$ | $L_2$ | Any | ❌ No | $f(x) = \text{sgn}(x)$ at $x=0$ |
| **Infinite** | $\pm\infty$ | $\pm\infty$ | — | ❌ No | $f(x) = \frac{1}{x}$ at $x=0$ |
| **Oscillatory** | DNE | DNE | — | ❌ No | $f(x) = \sin(1/x)$ at $x=0$ |

> 💡 **Mnemonic:** **"RJIO"** — **R**emovable, **J**ump, **I**nfinite, **O**scillatory
> - **R**emovable = Repairable (just plug the hole)
> - **J**ump = Jump over the gap (can't fix it)
> - **I**nfinite = Infinity wall (vertical asymptote)
> - **O**scillatory = Out of control (no limit at all)

## 🧠 Part III: Continuity of Standard Functions

### Functions Continuous on their Entire Domain:

| Function Type | Continuous Where? | Discontinuous Where? |
|---|---|---|
| Polynomials $P(x)$ | $\forall x \in \mathbb{R}$ | Nowhere |
| Rational $\frac{P(x)}{Q(x)}$ | Where $Q(x) \neq 0$ | Where $Q(x) = 0$ |
| $\sin x$, $\cos x$ | $\forall x \in \mathbb{R}$ | Nowhere |
| $\tan x$, $\sec x$ | $x \neq \frac{\pi}{2} + n\pi$ | At $x = \frac{\pi}{2} + n\pi$ |
| $\cot x$, $\csc x$ | $x \neq n\pi$ | At $x = n\pi$ |
| $e^x$ | $\forall x \in \mathbb{R}$ | Nowhere |
| $\ln x$ | $x > 0$ | At $x \le 0$ (undefined) |
| $x^n$ ($n \in \mathbb{N}$) | $\forall x \in \mathbb{R}$ | Nowhere |
| $\sqrt{x}$ | $x \ge 0$ | At $x < 0$ (undefined) |
| $|x|$ | $\forall x \in \mathbb{R}$ | Nowhere (but NOT differentiable at $x=0$) |
| Floor $\lfloor x \rfloor$ | $x \notin \mathbb{Z}$ | Jump disc. at every integer |
| Ceiling $\lceil x \rceil$ | $x \notin \mathbb{Z}$ | Jump disc. at every integer |
| Dirichlet Function | Nowhere | Everywhere (irrational vs rational) |

---

### Algebra of Continuous Functions:
If $f$ and $g$ are continuous at $x = a$, then:

| Operation | Continuity | Condition |
|---|---|---|
| $f \pm g$ | Continuous at $a$ | Always |
| $f \cdot g$ | Continuous at $a$ | Always |
| $f / g$ | Continuous at $a$ | Provided $g(a) \neq 0$ |
| $f \circ g$ (composition) | Continuous at $a$ | If $g$ continuous at $a$, $f$ continuous at $g(a)$ |
| $|f(x)|$ | Continuous at $a$ | If $f$ continuous at $a$ |
| $[f(x)]^n$ | Continuous at $a$ | Always |
| $\sqrt{f(x)}$ | Continuous at $a$ | If $f(a) > 0$ |

## 🧠 Part IV: Differentiability & Its Relation to Continuity

### Definition of Differentiability at $x = a$:

$f(x)$ is **differentiable at $x = a$** if both the Left-Hand Derivative (LHD) and Right-Hand Derivative (RHD) exist and are **EQUAL**:

$$f'(a) = \lim_{h \to 0^+} \frac{f(a+h) - f(a)}{h} = \lim_{h \to 0^+} \frac{f(a) - f(a-h)}{h}$$

$$\text{Differentiable at } a \iff \text{LHD} = \text{RHD} \text{ (both exist and finite)}$$

---

### 🔑 The Critical Hierarchy (GATE's Favourite Trap!):

$$\boxed{\text{Differentiable at } a \implies \text{Continuous at } a}$$
$$\boxed{\text{Continuous at } a \;\not\!\!\!\implies \text{Differentiable at } a}$$

**The Golden Counter-Example — $f(x) = |x|$ at $x = 0$:**
- **Continuity Check:** $\lim_{x \to 0^-} |x| = 0$ and $\lim_{x \to 0^+} |x| = 0$ and $f(0) = 0$. ✅ **Continuous!**
- **Differentiability Check:**
  - LHD $= \lim_{h \to 0^+} \frac{|0-h| - 0}{-h} = \lim \frac{h}{-h} = -1$
  - RHD $= \lim_{h \to 0^+} \frac{|0+h| - 0}{h} = \lim \frac{h}{h} = +1$
  - LHD $\neq$ RHD ❌ **NOT differentiable at $x = 0$!**

---

### Non-Differentiable Points — Visual Guide:

| Scenario | Graph Feature | Example |
|---|---|---|
| LHD $\neq$ RHD (finite) | **Sharp Corner / Cusp** | $|x|$, $|x-1| + |x+1|$ |
| LHD or RHD $= \pm\infty$ | **Vertical Tangent** | $x^{1/3}$ at $x=0$ |
| Discontinuity at $a$ | **Break in graph** | $\lfloor x \rfloor$ at integers |
| Oscillating derivative | **Wild oscillation** | $x^2 \sin(1/x)$ at $x=0$ |

## 🧠 Part V: Key Theorems on Continuity (High-Yield for GATE)

### 1. Intermediate Value Theorem (IVT) — Root Existence Theorem

**Statement:** If $f$ is continuous on $[a, b]$ and $f(a) \neq f(b)$, then for every value $k$ between $f(a)$ and $f(b)$, there exists at least one $c \in (a, b)$ such that $f(c) = k$.

$$f \text{ continuous on } [a,b], \; f(a) < k < f(b) \implies \exists \, c \in (a,b) : f(c) = k$$

**GATE Application — Root Detection:**
If $f(a)$ and $f(b)$ have **opposite signs** ($f(a) \cdot f(b) < 0$), then $\exists$ at least one root of $f(x) = 0$ in $(a, b)$.

> 💡 **Mnemonic:** **"IVT = Intermediate Value Test = No Teleportation Rule"**  
> A continuous function cannot jump from $f(a)$ to $f(b)$ without passing through every value in between.

---

### 2. Extreme Value Theorem (EVT)

**Statement:** If $f$ is **continuous** on a **closed and bounded** interval $[a, b]$, then $f$ **must attain** its maximum and minimum values on $[a, b]$.

$$f \text{ continuous on } [a,b] \implies \exists \, x_1, x_2 \in [a,b] : f(x_1) \le f(x) \le f(x_2) \; \forall x \in [a,b]$$

**Important:** Closed interval AND continuity are BOTH required. If either fails, the max/min may not be attained.

---

### 3. Uniform Continuity (Concept Only for GATE)

- **Ordinary Continuity:** For each $x = a$, given $\varepsilon > 0$, find $\delta$ (which may depend on both $\varepsilon$ AND $a$).
- **Uniform Continuity:** The SAME $\delta$ works for ALL points in the domain simultaneously.

$$\text{Uniformly Continuous} \implies \text{Continuous (but NOT vice versa)}$$

**GATE Fact:** Every function continuous on a **closed bounded interval** $[a, b]$ is automatically **uniformly continuous** (Heine-Cantor theorem).

## 🧠 Part VI: Piecewise Functions — The #1 GATE Question Type

### Standard Strategy for Piecewise Continuity:

```
Given piecewise function f(x):
Step 1 → Identify break-points (where the formula changes)
Step 2 → At each break-point x = a:
         Compute LHL = lim(x→a⁻) f(x)
         Compute RHL = lim(x→a⁺) f(x)
         Compute f(a)
Step 3 → For continuity: LHL = RHL = f(a) must hold
Step 4 → If finding constant k: solve the equation LHL = f(a)
```

### ⚡ GATE Template for "Find $k$" Questions:

**Template:** Given $f(x) = \begin{cases} \text{expr}_1 & x \neq a \\ k & x = a \end{cases}$, find $k$ for continuity.

**Method:** $k = \lim_{x \to a} \text{expr}_1$

## ⚡ AIR-1 Topper Speed Tricks & Mnemonics

### 🚀 Trick 1: The 3-Second Continuity Check for Piecewise Functions

For GATE questions asking "Is $f(x)$ continuous at $x = a$?":
1. **Compute $f(a)$** from the formula for $x = a$.
2. **Compute LHL** from the formula for $x < a$ (substitute $x = a^-$).
3. **Compute RHL** from the formula for $x > a$ (substitute $x = a^+$).
4. **Compare:** If all three equal, continuous. Otherwise, name the discontinuity type.

---

### 🚀 Trick 2: Sign-of-Product Root Test (IVT Application)

For **numerical GATE questions** asking "Does $f(x) = 0$ have a root in $(a, b)$?":
- Compute $f(a)$ and $f(b)$.
- If $f(a) \cdot f(b) < 0 \implies$ **Root exists** (by IVT). Done in 5 seconds!

---

### 🚀 Trick 3: Identifying Non-Differentiability by Graph Type

| Graph Shape at $x = a$ | Differentiable? | Quick Test |
|---|---|---|
| Smooth curve (no corner) | ✅ Yes | LHD = RHD |
| Sharp corner/kink (like $V$) | ❌ No | LHD $\neq$ RHD (both finite) |
| Vertical tangent (like $\cup$ at tip) | ❌ No | LHD or RHD $= \infty$ |
| Break/hole in curve | ❌ No | Not even continuous |

---

### 🚀 Trick 4: The Composition Continuity Chain Rule

If you see $h(x) = f(g(x))$ and need to check continuity:
1. $g$ continuous at $a$? → Compute $g(a) = b$.
2. $f$ continuous at $b$? → Then $h$ is continuous at $a$. Done!

**Counter-example to beware:** $f$ may be discontinuous but $f \circ g$ can still be continuous if $g$ avoids the discontinuity point of $f$.

---

### 🚀 Trick 5: The "Floor & Ceiling" Discontinuity Pattern

$\lfloor x \rfloor$ has **jump discontinuity at every integer $n$**:
- LHL at $n$: $\lim_{x \to n^-} \lfloor x \rfloor = n - 1$
- RHL at $n$: $\lim_{x \to n^+} \lfloor x \rfloor = n$
- $f(n) = n$

So: **RHL = $f(n)$ but LHL $\neq f(n)$** → Jump discontinuity at every integer!

> 🎯 GATE sometimes wraps $\lfloor f(x) \rfloor$ compositions — always check if $f(x)$ passes through an integer at the test point.

## 📝 Step-by-Step Solved GATE Examples

### Example 1: Find $k$ for Continuity (Classic GATE Type)
**Problem:** $f(x) = \begin{cases} \dfrac{x^2 - 9}{x - 3} & x \neq 3 \\ k & x = 3 \end{cases}$. Find $k$ for continuity at $x = 3$.

**Solution:**
$$\lim_{x \to 3} \frac{x^2 - 9}{x - 3} = \lim_{x \to 3} \frac{(x-3)(x+3)}{(x-3)} = \lim_{x \to 3} (x + 3) = 6$$
For continuity: $k = f(3) = \lim_{x \to 3} f(x) = \boxed{6}$

---

### Example 2: Piecewise Function — Type of Discontinuity
**Problem:** Classify the discontinuity of $f(x) = \begin{cases} x + 1 & x \le 0 \\ x^2 + 2 & x > 0 \end{cases}$ at $x = 0$.

**Solution:**
- LHL: $\lim_{x \to 0^-} (x+1) = 1$
- RHL: $\lim_{x \to 0^+} (x^2+2) = 2$
- $f(0) = 0 + 1 = 1$

Since LHL $\neq$ RHL ($1 \neq 2$) → **Jump Discontinuity at $x = 0$**. ✅

---

### Example 3: Continuity + Differentiability of $|x-1|$
**Problem:** Check continuity and differentiability of $f(x) = |x - 1|$ at $x = 1$.

**Solution:**
- **Continuity:**
  - LHL: $\lim_{x \to 1^-} -(x-1) = 0$
  - RHL: $\lim_{x \to 1^+} (x-1) = 0$
  - $f(1) = 0$ ✅ **Continuous at $x = 1$**
- **Differentiability:**
  - LHD: $\lim_{h \to 0^+} \frac{f(1-h) - f(1)}{-h} = \lim \frac{h}{-h} = -1$
  - RHD: $\lim_{h \to 0^+} \frac{f(1+h) - f(1)}{h} = \lim \frac{h}{h} = +1$
  - LHD $\neq$ RHD ❌ **NOT differentiable at $x = 1$** (Sharp corner / kink)

---

### Example 4: IVT Root Detection
**Problem:** Show that $f(x) = x^3 - 3x - 1$ has a root in the interval $(1, 2)$.

**Solution:**
- $f(1) = 1 - 3 - 1 = -3 < 0$
- $f(2) = 8 - 6 - 1 = 1 > 0$
- $f(1) \cdot f(2) = (-3)(1) = -3 < 0$
- $f(x)$ is continuous (polynomial) on $[1, 2]$.
- By IVT: $\exists \, c \in (1, 2)$ such that $f(c) = 0$. ✅

---

### Example 5: Trig Piecewise with $\frac{\sin kx}{x}$ Type (GATE PYQ)
**Problem:** For what value of $a$ is $f(x) = \begin{cases} \dfrac{\sin(3x)}{x} & x \neq 0 \\ a & x = 0 \end{cases}$ continuous at $x = 0$?

**Solution:**
$$\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin(3x)}{x} = 3 \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin(3x)}{3x} = 3 \cdot 1 = 3$$
For continuity: $a = \boxed{3}$

---

### Example 6: $f(x) = x \sin(1/x)$ Continuity at $x = 0$
**Problem:** Can $f(x) = x \sin\left(\frac{1}{x}\right)$ be made continuous at $x = 0$ by defining $f(0) = ?$

**Solution:**
Since $-1 \le \sin(1/x) \le 1$: $-|x| \le x\sin(1/x) \le |x|$  
By Squeeze Theorem: $\lim_{x \to 0} x \sin(1/x) = 0$  
So define $f(0) = 0$ → $f$ is continuous at $x = 0$. ✅  
(**Removable discontinuity** — repaired by defining $f(0) = 0$)

## ⚠️ Common Exam Traps & Pitfalls

> 🔴 **Trap 1: Continuity Does NOT Imply Differentiability**
> - *Mistake:* "$f(x) = |x|$ is continuous at $0$ so it must be differentiable there."
> - *Correction:* Continuous ≠ Differentiable. Always compute LHD and RHD separately for piecewise or $|f(x)|$ functions!

> 🔴 **Trap 2: Applying IVT Without Checking Continuity**
> - *Mistake:* Claiming "since $f(1) < 0$ and $f(2) > 0$, there is a root in $(1,2)$" without verifying continuity.
> - *Correction:* IVT requires $f$ to be continuous on $[1, 2]$. Always verify continuity first!

> 🔴 **Trap 3: Forgetting $f(a)$ Condition (Third Condition)**
> - *Mistake:* Saying "LHL = RHL so $f$ is continuous."
> - *Correction:* Need LHL $=$ RHL $= f(a)$. The limit matching alone is not sufficient if $f(a)$ is undefined or $\neq$ the limit.

> 🔴 **Trap 4: Floor/Ceiling Function Continuity Error**
> - *Mistake:* "$\lfloor x \rfloor$ is continuous at $x = 2.5$."
> - *Correction:* $\lfloor x \rfloor$ is continuous at non-integers. It has jump discontinuity at every integer.

> 🔴 **Trap 5: Infinite Discontinuity vs Removable**
> - *Mistake:* Calling $f(x) = \frac{1}{x}$ at $x = 0$ a "removable discontinuity" because it's undefined.
> - *Correction:* $\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{1}{x} = \pm\infty$ (limit is infinite, not a finite value) → **Infinite discontinuity**, NOT removable.

## 📐 Master Reference Table: Continuity

| Concept | Statement | Key Condition |
|---|---|---|
| Continuity at $a$ | $\lim_{x \to a} f(x) = f(a)$ | All 3 conditions hold |
| LHL = RHL Requirement | Both one-sided limits must be finite and equal | For limit to exist |
| Removable Discontinuity | LHL = RHL $\neq f(a)$, or $f(a)$ undefined | Fix: redefine $f(a) = L$ |
| Jump Discontinuity | LHL $\neq$ RHL (both finite) | Not fixable |
| Infinite Discontinuity | Limit $= \pm\infty$ | Vertical asymptote |
| Differentiable $\Rightarrow$ Continuous | Always true | Converse FALSE |
| Continuous $\not\Rightarrow$ Differentiable | Counterexample: $\|x\|$ at $0$ | Sharp corner |
| IVT | $f(a) \cdot f(b) < 0 \Rightarrow$ root in $(a,b)$ | $f$ continuous on $[a,b]$ |
| EVT | Max and min are attained | $f$ continuous on closed $[a,b]$ |
| Composition continuity | $g$ cts at $a$, $f$ cts at $g(a)$ $\Rightarrow f \circ g$ cts at $a$ | Chain rule for continuity |
| Uniform Continuity | Same $\delta$ for all points | Stronger than pointwise continuity |

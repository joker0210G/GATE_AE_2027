> 🎯 **GATE Weightage:** 2–4 marks per year. The 1D Heat / Diffusion Equation is **Parabolic** ($\Delta = 0$) and models **transient heat conduction in solids**, **gas diffusion in rocket nozzles**, and **combustion boundary layers**.

---

## 📌 Part I: Statement & Physical Meaning

### 1D Heat Conduction Equation (Standard Form)

$$\boxed{\frac{\partial u}{\partial t} = \alpha^2 \frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial x^2}, \quad 0 < x < L, \; t > 0}$$

where:
- $u(x,t)$ = temperature at position $x$ and time $t$
- $\alpha^2 = \frac{k}{\rho c_p}$ = **Thermal Diffusivity** ($\text{m}^2/\text{s}$) — material property ($k$: thermal conductivity, $\rho$: density, $c_p$: specific heat capacity)
- $L$ = length of the 1D rod

**Classification:** $A = \alpha^2, B = 0, C = 0 \implies B^2 - 4AC = 0 \implies \mathbf{Parabolic}$

---

### Required Data (Initial & Boundary Conditions)

Since the heat equation is **1st order in time** and **2nd order in space**:
- **1 Initial Condition (IC):** $u(x,0) = f(x)$ — initial temperature profile at $t=0$.
- **2 Boundary Conditions (BCs):** at $x=0$ and $x=L$ for all $t > 0$.

---

## 🧠 Part II: Case 1 — Fixed Zero End Temperatures (Homogeneous Dirichlet BCs)

### Problem Setup
Rod of length $L$ with both ends kept at $0^\circ\text{C}$:
- $u(0,t) = 0$
- $u(L,t) = 0$
- $u(x,0) = f(x)$

---

### Step-by-Step Separation of Variables Derivation

#### Step 1: Assume Product Solution
$$u(x,t) = X(x) \cdot T(t)$$

Substitute into $\frac{\partial u}{\partial t} = \alpha^2 \frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial x^2}$:
$$X(x) T'(t) = \alpha^2 X''(x) T(t)$$

Divide both sides by $\alpha^2 X(x) T(t)$:
$$\frac{T'(t)}{\alpha^2 T(t)} = \frac{X''(x)}{X(x)} = -\lambda$$

#### Step 2: Solve Spatial ODE (Sturm-Liouville Problem)
$$X''(x) + \lambda X(x) = 0, \quad X(0) = 0, \; X(L) = 0$$

- **Eigenvalues:** $\lambda_n = \left(\frac{n\pi}{L}\right)^2, \quad n = 1, 2, 3, \ldots$
- **Eigenfunctions:** $X_n(x) = \sin\left(\frac{n\pi x}{L}\right)$

#### Step 3: Solve Temporal ODE
$$T'(t) + \alpha^2 \lambda_n T(t) = 0 \implies T'(t) + \left(\frac{n\pi \alpha}{L}\right)^2 T(t) = 0$$

First-order linear ODE! Solution:
$$T_n(t) = c_n e^{-\left(\frac{n\pi \alpha}{L}\right)^2 t}$$

#### Step 4: Complete Solution by Superposition
$$\boxed{u(x,t) = \sum_{n=1}^\infty c_n e^{-\left(\frac{n\pi \alpha}{L}\right)^2 t} \sin\left(\frac{n\pi x}{L}\right)}$$

#### Step 5: Apply Initial Condition $u(x,0) = f(x)$
$$u(x,0) = \sum_{n=1}^\infty c_n \sin\left(\frac{n\pi x}{L}\right) = f(x)$$

This is a Fourier Sine Series! By orthogonality:
$$\boxed{c_n = \frac{2}{L} \int_0^L f(x) \sin\left(\frac{n\pi x}{L}\right) dx}$$

---

## 🧠 Part III: Case 2 — Insulated Ends (Homogeneous Neumann BCs)

### Problem Setup
No heat flows through the ends of the rod ($\frac{\partial u}{\partial x} = 0$):
- $u_x(0,t) = 0$
- $u_x(L,t) = 0$
- $u(x,0) = f(x)$

### Solution Formula (Cosine Series)
Spatial ODE yields $X_n(x) = \cos\left(\frac{n\pi x}{L}\right)$ for $n = 0, 1, 2, \ldots$

$$\boxed{u(x,t) = \frac{c_0}{2} + \sum_{n=1}^\infty c_n e^{-\left(\frac{n\pi \alpha}{L}\right)^2 t} \cos\left(\frac{n\pi x}{L}\right)}$$

where:
- $c_0 = \frac{2}{L} \int_0^L f(x)\,dx \implies \frac{c_0}{2} = \frac{1}{L} \int_0^L f(x)\,dx$
- $c_n = \frac{2}{L} \int_0^L f(x) \cos\left(\frac{n\pi x}{L}\right) dx$

---

## 🧠 Part IV: Case 3 — Non-Homogeneous End Temperatures (Steady-State + Transient Breakdown)

### Problem Setup
Ends held at constant non-zero temperatures:
- $u(0,t) = T_1$
- $u(L,t) = T_2$
- $u(x,0) = f(x)$

---

### Solution Strategy: Split into Steady-State + Transient

$$\boxed{u(x,t) = u_{ss}(x) + u_{tr}(x,t)}$$

1. **Steady-State Part $u_{ss}(x)$ ($t \to \infty$):**  
   As $t \to \infty$, $\frac{\partial u}{\partial t} = 0 \implies \frac{d^2 u_{ss}}{dx^2} = 0 \implies u_{ss}(x) = A x + B$.  
   Apply BCs: $u_{ss}(0) = T_1 \implies B = T_1$; $u_{ss}(L) = T_2 \implies A = \frac{T_2 - T_1}{L}$.
   $$\boxed{u_{ss}(x) = T_1 + \frac{T_2 - T_1}{L} x}$$

2. **Transient Part $u_{tr}(x,t)$:**  
   Satisfies **homogeneous BCs** ($u_{tr}(0,t) = 0, \; u_{tr}(L,t) = 0$) and initial condition:
   $$u_{tr}(x,0) = f(x) - u_{ss}(x)$$
   Use Case 1 formula on $u_{tr}(x,t)$!

---

## ⚡ AIR-1 Topper Speed Tricks & Mnemonics

### 🚀 Trick 1: Insulated Rod Steady-State Temperature ($t \to \infty$)
For an insulated rod ($u_x(0,t)=0, u_x(L,t)=0$), as $t \to \infty$, all exponential decay terms vanish!
$$\boxed{u(x, \infty) = \frac{c_0}{2} = \frac{1}{L} \int_0^L f(x)\,dx = \text{Average Initial Temperature}}$$
No PDE solving needed — just find the average of $f(x)$ over $[0,L]$!

---

### 🚀 Trick 2: Fixed-Temperature Rod Steady-State Temperature ($t \to \infty$)
For $u(0,t) = T_1$ and $u(L,t) = T_2$, as $t \to \infty$:
$$u(x, \infty) = u_{ss}(x) = T_1 + \frac{T_2 - T_1}{L}x \quad \text{(Linear Profile!)}$$
Midpoint temperature at $x = L/2$: $u(L/2, \infty) = \frac{T_1 + T_2}{2}$.

---

### 🚀 Trick 3: Direct Coefficient Matching (No Integrals!)
If initial temperature $f(x)$ is given directly as a sum of sines/cosines, e.g., $f(x) = 5\sin\left(\frac{\pi x}{L}\right) - 3\sin\left(\frac{3\pi x}{L}\right)$:
Match terms directly with $u(x,t) = \sum c_n e^{-\left(\frac{n\pi \alpha}{L}\right)^2 t} \sin\left(\frac{n\pi x}{L}\right)$:
$$\mathbf{u(x,t) = 5 e^{-\left(\frac{\pi \alpha}{L}\right)^2 t} \sin\left(\frac{\pi x}{L}\right) - 3 e^{-\left(\frac{3\pi \alpha}{L}\right)^2 t} \sin\left(\frac{3\pi x}{L}\right)}$$
Done in 5 seconds without integration!

---

### 🧠 Mnemonic: "**EST**" for Heat Equation Solution
- **E**xponential decay in time: $e^{-\lambda_n \alpha^2 t}$
- **S**ine/Cosine in space: $\sin\left(\frac{n\pi x}{L}\right)$ or $\cos\left(\frac{n\pi x}{L}\right)$
- **T**ransient decays to steady-state as $t \to \infty$

## 📝 Step-by-Step Solved GATE Examples

### Example 1: Homogeneous BCs with Direct Sine Input (GATE PYQ Type)
**Problem:** Solve $\frac{\partial u}{\partial t} = 2 \frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial x^2}$ ($\alpha^2 = 2$) for $0 < x < \pi$, $t > 0$ with $u(0,t) = 0$, $u(\pi,t) = 0$, and $u(x,0) = 4\sin(3x)$.

**Solution (Using Speed Trick 3):**
1. $L = \pi$, $\alpha^2 = 2$.
2. Eigenvalues: $\lambda_n = n^2 \implies$ Decay factor: $e^{-\alpha^2 \lambda_n t} = e^{-2 n^2 t}$.
3. Initial condition has single term with $n=3$: $f(x) = 4\sin(3x) \implies c_3 = 4$, all other $c_n = 0$.
4. Substitute $n=3$ into decay factor: $e^{-2(3)^2 t} = e^{-18t}$.

$$\boxed{u(x,t) = 4 e^{-18t} \sin(3x)}$$

---

### Example 2: Non-Homogeneous End Temperatures (GATE 2-Marker)
**Problem:** A metal rod of length $L=10\text{ cm}$ has its ends held at $u(0,t) = 20^\circ\text{C}$ and $u(10,t) = 80^\circ\text{C}$. Find the steady-state temperature at $x = 4\text{ cm}$.

**Solution (Using Speed Trick 2):**
In steady-state ($t \to \infty$), temperature distribution is linear:
$$u_{ss}(x) = T_1 + \frac{T_2 - T_1}{L}x = 20 + \frac{80 - 20}{10}x = 20 + 6x$$

At $x = 4\text{ cm}$:
$$u_{ss}(4) = 20 + 6(4) = 20 + 24 = 44^\circ\text{C}$$

$$\boxed{u_{ss}(4) = 44^\circ\text{C}}$$

---

### Example 3: Insulated Rod Final Temperature (GATE 2-Marker)
**Problem:** An insulated rod of length $L=2$ has initial temperature $f(x) = 3x^2$. Find the final steady-state temperature of the rod as $t \to \infty$.

**Solution (Using Speed Trick 1):**
For an insulated rod, final temperature is uniform and equals average initial temperature:
$$u(x, \infty) = \frac{1}{L} \int_0^L f(x)\,dx = \frac{1}{2} \int_0^2 3x^2\,dx = \frac{1}{2} \left[ x^3 \right]_0^2 = \frac{1}{2} (8) = 4$$

$$\boxed{u(x, \infty) = 4}$$

## 📐 Master Summary Table: Heat Equation

| BC Case | Spatial Eigenfunctions | Complete Solution $u(x,t)$ | Steady-State $u(x,\infty)$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ends at $0^\circ\text{C}$** | $\sin\left(\frac{n\pi x}{L}\right)$ | $\sum c_n e^{-\left(\frac{n\pi \alpha}{L}\right)^2 t} \sin\left(\frac{n\pi x}{L}\right)$ | $0$ |
| **Insulated Ends** | $\cos\left(\frac{n\pi x}{L}\right)$ | $\frac{c_0}{2} + \sum c_n e^{-\left(\frac{n\pi \alpha}{L}\right)^2 t} \cos\left(\frac{n\pi x}{L}\right)$ | $\frac{1}{L}\int_0^L f(x)dx$ |
| **Ends at $T_1, T_2$** | $\sin\left(\frac{n\pi x}{L}\right)$ (transient) | $u_{ss}(x) + u_{tr}(x,t)$ | $T_1 + \frac{T_2 - T_1}{L}x$ |

## ⚠️ Common Exam Traps & Pitfalls

> 🔴 **Trap 1: Confusing Wave vs Heat Time Derivatives**  
> - Wave Equation: $u_{tt} = c^2 u_{xx}$ (2nd order in time $\implies$ Oscillations $\cos(\omega t)$ & $\sin(\omega t)$).  
> - Heat Equation: $u_t = \alpha^2 u_{xx}$ (1st order in time $\implies$ Exponential Decay $e^{-\lambda \alpha^2 t}$).

> 🔴 **Trap 2: Missing the Square of $n\pi/L$ in Exponential Decay**  
> Decay factor is $e^{-\left(\frac{n\pi\alpha}{L}\right)^2 t}$ — remember to **square** $n$, $\pi$, $\alpha$, and $L$ in the exponent!

> 🔴 **Trap 3: Forgetting to Subtract Steady-State in Non-Homogeneous BCs**  
> For $u(0,t)=T_1, u(L,t)=T_2$, the initial condition for the transient part is $u_{tr}(x,0) = f(x) - u_{ss}(x)$ (NOT $f(x)$ alone!).

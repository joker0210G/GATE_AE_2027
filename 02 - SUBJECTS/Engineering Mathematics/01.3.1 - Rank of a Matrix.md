## 📌 1. What is Rank? (Zero to AIR-1 Overview)

The **Rank** of a matrix $A_{m \times n}$ (denoted as $\text{Rank}(A)$ or $\rho(A)$) is the single most fundamental structural number associated with a matrix.

- **Geometric Intuition:** $\text{Rank}(A)$ represents the **dimension of the output space (image/range)** when $A$ acts as a linear transformation mapping $\mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}^m$.
- **Algebraic Intuition:** $\text{Rank}(A)$ measures the maximum number of **linearly independent equations / pieces of information** contained within the matrix.

### Why Rank Matters in GATE AE:
- **Systems of Equations:** Determines consistency, uniqueness, and free variables ($n - r$).
- **Control Theory:** Controllability Matrix $[B \ AB \ A^2 B \dots A^{n-1} B]$ must have full rank $n$ for complete system controllability.
- **Structural Analysis:** Stiffness matrix $[K]$ rank deficiency indicates rigid body modes / unconstrained structures.

---

## 🧠 Part I: Four Equivalent Definitions of Rank

| # | Perspective | Exact Definition of $\text{Rank}(A) = r$ |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Minor / Submatrix** | The order $r$ of the **largest square submatrix** of $A$ having a non-zero determinant ($\det \neq 0$). Every larger submatrix of size $(r+1) \times (r+1)$ has $\det = 0$. |
| **2** | **Row Space** | The maximum number of **linearly independent row vectors** in $A$ ($\dim C(A^T) = r$). |
| **3** | **Column Space** | The maximum number of **linearly independent column vectors** in $A$ ($\dim C(A) = r$). |
| **4** | **Pivot Count (REF)** | The **number of non-zero rows (pivots)** after reducing $A$ to Row Echelon Form via Elementary Row Operations. |

> 🔑 **Fundamental Theorem of Matrix Rank:**
> $$\text{Row Rank}(A) = \text{Column Rank}(A) = \text{Rank}(A)$$

---

## 🧠 Part II: Four Computational Methods for Rank

### 1. Minor / Submatrix Method (Best for $2 \times 2$ or simple $3 \times 3$)
1. Find $\det(A)$ if $A$ is square $n \times n$. If $\det(A) \neq 0 \implies \text{Rank}(A) = n$.
2. If $\det(A) = 0$, test all $(n-1) \times (n-1)$ submatrices. If any has $\det \neq 0 \implies \text{Rank}(A) = n-1$.
3. Repeat downward until a non-zero determinant minor is found.

---

### 2. Row Echelon Form (REF) / Gaussian Elimination (Standard Method)
Apply Elementary Row Operations (EROs):
- $R_i \leftrightarrow R_j$
- $R_i \to k R_i$ ($k \neq 0$)
- $R_i \to R_i + k R_j$

Reduce matrix to Upper Triangular Echelon Form:
$$\text{Rank}(A) = \text{Count of non-zero rows in REF}$$

---

### 3. Normal / Canonical Form $[I_r\ O ; O\ O]$ (Uses EROs + ECOs)
By applying BOTH Elementary Row Operations (EROs) and Elementary Column Operations (ECOs), any $m \times n$ matrix $A$ of rank $r$ can be reduced to the **Normal Form**:

$$N = \begin{pmatrix} I_r & O_{r \times (n-r)} \\ O_{(m-r) \times r} & O_{(m-r) \times (n-r)} \end{pmatrix}$$

> 🔑 **Non-Singular Transformation Theorem:**
> For every matrix $A_{m \times n}$ of rank $r$, there exist non-singular matrices $P_{m \times m}$ and $Q_{n \times n}$ such that:
> $$P A Q = \begin{pmatrix} I_r & O \\ O & O \end{pmatrix}$$

---

### 4. Inspection & Proportionality Method (1-Second Trick)
- Eliminate rows/columns that are zero vectors or scalar multiples of other rows/columns by inspection.

---

## 🧠 Part III: Master Rank Inequalities & Properties (Exhaustive List)

> 🔴 **Memorize these rank properties — heavily tested in GATE MCQs/MSQs:**

### 1. Fundamental Bounds
- $0 \le \text{Rank}(A_{m \times n}) \le \min(m, n)$
- $\text{Rank}(O_{m \times n}) = 0$ (Only the zero matrix has rank 0)
- $\text{Rank}(I_n) = n$ (Full rank)

### 2. Transpose & Conjugate Transpose Identities
$$\text{Rank}(A) = \text{Rank}(A^T) = \text{Rank}(\bar{A}) = \text{Rank}(A^\theta)$$

### 3. Product with Transpose (Real Matrices)
$$\text{Rank}(A^T A) = \text{Rank}(A A^T) = \text{Rank}(A)$$

### 4. Invariance under Non-Singular Multiplication
If $P_{m \times m}$ and $Q_{n \times n}$ are non-singular matrices ($\det P \neq 0, \det Q \neq 0$):
$$\text{Rank}(P A Q) = \text{Rank}(P A) = \text{Rank}(A Q) = \text{Rank}(A)$$

### 5. Sum & Difference Rank Inequalities (Subadditivity)
$$\text{Rank}(A + B) \le \text{Rank}(A) + \text{Rank}(B)$$
$$\text{Rank}(A - B) \ge |\text{Rank}(A) - \text{Rank}(B)|$$

### 6. Product Rank Inequalities & Sylvester's Law
For $A_{m \times n}$ and $B_{n \times p}$:
1. **Upper Bound:**
   $$\text{Rank}(AB) \le \min(\text{Rank}(A), \text{Rank}(B))$$
2. **Sylvester's Law of Nullity (Lower Bound):**
   $$\text{Rank}(A) + \text{Rank}(B) - n \le \text{Rank}(AB) \le \min(\text{Rank}(A), \text{Rank}(B))$$
3. **Frobenius Inequality:**
   $$\text{Rank}(AB) + \text{Rank}(BC) \le \text{Rank}(ABC) + \text{Rank}(B)$$

## 🧠 Part IV: Gilbert Strang's Four Fundamental Subspaces & Rank-Nullity

For an $m \times n$ matrix $A$ of rank $r$:

```
          Euclidean Domain R^n                   Euclidean Codomain R^m
      +---------------------------+          +---------------------------+
      |  Row Space C(A^T)         |  A x = b |  Column Space C(A)        |
      |  Dimension = r            | -------->|  Dimension = r            |
      |                           |          |                           |
      |---------------------------|          |---------------------------|
      |  Nullspace N(A)           |          |  Left Nullspace N(A^T)    |
      |  Dimension = n - r        |          |  Dimension = m - r        |
      +---------------------------+          +---------------------------+
```

### Summary Table of 4 Fundamental Subspaces:

| Subspace | Definition / Set Formula | Vector Subspace Of | Dimension | Orthogonal Complement |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Column Space (Range)** | $C(A) = \{A\vec{x} \mid \vec{x} \in \mathbb{R}^n\}$ | $\mathbb{R}^m$ | $r$ (Rank) | Left Nullspace $N(A^T)$ |
| **Row Space** | $C(A^T) = \{A^T\vec{y} \mid \vec{y} \in \mathbb{R}^m\}$ | $\mathbb{R}^n$ | $r$ (Rank) | Nullspace $N(A)$ |
| **Nullspace (Kernel)** | $N(A) = \{\vec{x} \in \mathbb{R}^n \mid A\vec{x} = \vec{0}\}$ | $\mathbb{R}^n$ | $n - r$ (Nullity) | Row Space $C(A^T)$ |
| **Left Nullspace** | $N(A^T) = \{\vec{y} \in \mathbb{R}^m \mid A^T\vec{y} = \vec{0}\}$ | $\mathbb{R}^m$ | $m - r$ (Left Nullity) | Column Space $C(A)$ |

> 🔴 **Dual Rank-Nullity Theorems:**
> 1. **Domain Rank-Nullity Theorem:** $\text{Rank}(A) + \text{Nullity}(A) = n$
> 2. **Codomain Left Rank-Nullity Theorem:** $\text{Rank}(A) + \text{Left Nullity}(A) = m$

## 🧠 Part V: Rank of Special Matrix Structures

### 1. Outer Product Matrix ($\vec{u}_{m \times 1} \vec{v}_{1 \times n}^T$)
If $\vec{u}$ and $\vec{v}$ are non-zero column vectors:
$$A = \vec{u} \vec{v}^T = \begin{pmatrix} u_1 v_1 & u_1 v_2 & \dots & u_1 v_n \\ u_2 v_1 & u_2 v_2 & \dots & u_2 v_n \\ \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \\ u_m v_1 & u_m v_2 & \dots & u_m v_n \end{pmatrix} \implies \mathbf{\text{Rank}(A) = 1}$$

### 2. Idempotent Matrix ($A^2 = A$)
$$\mathbf{\text{Rank}(A) = \text{tr}(A)}$$

### 3. Nilpotent Matrix ($A^k = O, A^{k-1} \neq O$)
$$1 \le \mathbf{\text{Rank}(A)} \le n - 1$$

## ⚡ AIR-1 Topper Speed Tricks & Inspection Shortcuts

### 🚀 Shortcut 1: 1-Second Outer Product Recognition
Whenever you see a matrix where Row 2, Row 3, ..., Row $m$ are all scalar multiples of Row 1:
$$\text{Rank}(A) = 1 \quad \text{instantly!}$$

### 🚀 Shortcut 2: Sylvester's Inequality Quick Range Calculation
**Question:** If $A$ is $4 \times 4$ with $\text{Rank}(A) = 3$, and $B$ is $4 \times 4$ with $\text{Rank}(B) = 3$, what is $\text{Rank}(AB)$?
**Quick Calculation:**
- $n = 4$
- Lower Bound: $3 + 3 - 4 = 2$
- Upper Bound: $\min(3, 3) = 3$
- **Result:** $2 \le \text{Rank}(AB) \le 3$.

### 🚀 Shortcut 3: Submatrix Determinant Escalation (For $3 \times 3$)
1. Compute $\det(A)$.
2. If $\det(A) \neq 0 \implies \text{Rank}(A) = 3$.
3. If $\det(A) = 0 \implies$ Check any $2 \times 2$ minor. If at least one $2 \times 2$ minor $\neq 0 \implies \text{Rank}(A) = 2$.
4. If ALL $2 \times 2$ minors are zero $\implies \text{Rank}(A) = 1$ (assuming $A \neq O$).

## 📝 Step-by-Step Solved Examples (Zero to GATE Topper Level)

### Example 1: Rank with Variable Parameter $k$
**Problem:** Find the value of $k$ for which the rank of $A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 & 4 \\ 1 & 4 & k \end{pmatrix}$ is less than 3.

**Solution:**
$\text{Rank}(A) < 3 \iff \det(A) = 0$.
$$\det(A) = \begin{vmatrix} 1 & 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 & 4 \\ 1 & 4 & k \end{vmatrix}$$
Expand along Row 1:
$$\det(A) = 1(2k - 16) - 1(k - 4) + 1(4 - 2) = 0$$
$$2k - 16 - k + 4 + 2 = 0 \implies k - 10 = 0 \implies \mathbf{k = 10}$$

---

### Example 2: Normal Form & Non-Singular Matrices $P, Q$
**Problem:** Reduce $A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 2 & 4 \end{pmatrix}$ to Normal Form and state its rank.

**Solution:**
1. Perform ERO $R_2 \to R_2 - 2R_1$:
   $$\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$$
2. Perform ECO $C_2 \to C_2 - 2C_1$:
   $$\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} I_1 & O \\ O & O \end{pmatrix}$$
3. Normal Form obtained with $I_1 \implies \mathbf{\text{Rank}(A) = 1}$.

---

### Example 3: Rank of Idempotent Matrix
**Problem:** Given an idempotent matrix $A_{4 \times 4}$ with diagonal entries $(0.5, 0.5, 0.5, 0.5)$, find $\text{Rank}(A)$.

**Solution:**
For an idempotent matrix ($A^2 = A$):
$$\text{Rank}(A) = \text{tr}(A) = 0.5 + 0.5 + 0.5 + 0.5 = \mathbf{2}$$

## ⚠️ Common Exam Traps & Pitfalls (Where Marks Are Lost)

> 🔴 **Trap 1: Counting Non-Zero Rows BEFORE Row Reduction**
> - *Mistake:* Looking at $A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 2 & 4 \end{pmatrix}$ and saying Rank = 2 because both rows are non-zero.
> - *Correction:* Row 2 is proportional to Row 1 ($R_2 = 2 R_1$). After ERO $R_2 \to R_2 - 2R_1$, Row 2 becomes $(0, 0)$. True Rank = 1!

> 🔴 **Trap 2: Column Space Changes Under Elementary Row Operations**
> - *Mistake:* Using columns of the Row Echelon Form as the basis for Column Space $C(A)$.
> - *Correction:* EROs PRESERVE row space and nullspace, but CHANGE column space! Always pick the corresponding original columns of $A$ matching pivot positions in REF.

> 🔴 **Trap 3: Equality Fallacy in Product Rank**
> - *Mistake:* Assuming $\text{Rank}(AB) = \text{Rank}(A) \cdot \text{Rank}(B)$.
> - *Correction:* Use Sylvester's inequality: $\text{Rank}(A) + \text{Rank}(B) - n \le \text{Rank}(AB) \le \min(\text{Rank}(A), \text{Rank}(B))$.

## 📌 Part I: The Core Idea (Zero to Clear)

### 1. Intuitive Meaning

The Chain Rule answers: *"If $y$ depends on $u$, and $u$ depends on $x$, how fast does $y$ change with respect to $x$?"*

Think of it as a **rate chain**:
$$\text{Rate of change of } y \text{ w.r.t. } x = \text{Rate of } y \text{ w.r.t. } u \;\times\; \text{Rate of } u \text{ w.r.t. } x$$

### Analogy (Gear System):
> If a small gear turns a large gear which turns a wheel:  
> Speed of wheel = (gear ratio 1) × (gear ratio 2) × (input speed)  
> This is exactly the chain rule — each layer multiplies its rate!

---

## 🧠 Part II: Formal Statements of the Chain Rule

### Version 1 — Function Composition Form
If $y = f(u)$ and $u = g(x)$, and both are differentiable:

$$\boxed{\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{dy}{du} \cdot \frac{du}{dx} = f'(g(x)) \cdot g'(x)}$$

### Version 2 — Direct Notation Form
$$\boxed{\frac{d}{dx}[f(g(x))] = f'(g(x)) \cdot g'(x)}$$

> 🎯 **Verbal Rule:** "Differentiate the OUTSIDE function (leaving the inside unchanged), then MULTIPLY by the derivative of the INSIDE function."

### Version 3 — Triple Composition (Most Common in GATE!)
For $y = f(g(h(x)))$:
$$\frac{dy}{dx} = f'(g(h(x))) \cdot g'(h(x)) \cdot h'(x)$$

### Version 4 — General $n$-Layer Composition
For $y = f_1(f_2(f_3(\cdots f_n(x) \cdots)))$:
$$\frac{dy}{dx} = f_1' \cdot f_2' \cdot f_3' \cdots f_n'$$
*(each $f_k'$ evaluated at the output of the layer below it)*

---

## 🧠 Part III: The "Peel-the-Onion" Method (AIR-1 Strategy)

### Visual Identification System:

```
y = f( g( h( x ) ) )
      |   |   |
    Layer Layer Layer
      1    2    3
      ↓    ↓    ↓
    outer  mid  inner
```

### Step-by-Step Peel-the-Onion Algorithm:
```
Step 1: Identify the OUTERMOST function → differentiate it, keep inside unchanged
Step 2: Multiply by derivative of NEXT layer → keep its inside unchanged  
Step 3: Continue until you reach x (the bare variable)
Step 4: Write final answer as a chain product
```

### GATE-Speed Identification Table:

| Expression Pattern | Outer $f$ | Inner $g$ | Derivative $\frac{d}{dx}$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| $\sin(g(x))$ | $\sin(\cdot)$ | $g(x)$ | $\cos(g(x)) \cdot g'(x)$ |
| $\cos(g(x))$ | $\cos(\cdot)$ | $g(x)$ | $-\sin(g(x)) \cdot g'(x)$ |
| $e^{g(x)}$ | $e^{(\cdot)}$ | $g(x)$ | $e^{g(x)} \cdot g'(x)$ |
| $\ln(g(x))$ | $\ln(\cdot)$ | $g(x)$ | $\dfrac{g'(x)}{g(x)}$ |
| $[g(x)]^n$ | $(\cdot)^n$ | $g(x)$ | $n[g(x)]^{n-1} \cdot g'(x)$ |
| $\sqrt{g(x)}$ | $\sqrt{\cdot}$ | $g(x)$ | $\dfrac{g'(x)}{2\sqrt{g(x)}}$ |
| $\tan^{-1}(g(x))$ | $\tan^{-1}(\cdot)$ | $g(x)$ | $\dfrac{g'(x)}{1+[g(x)]^2}$ |
| $\sin^{-1}(g(x))$ | $\sin^{-1}(\cdot)$ | $g(x)$ | $\dfrac{g'(x)}{\sqrt{1-[g(x)]^2}}$ |
| $a^{g(x)}$ | $a^{(\cdot)}$ | $g(x)$ | $a^{g(x)} \cdot \ln a \cdot g'(x)$ |

## 🧠 Part IV: Chain Rule in Key Application Contexts

### 1. Chain Rule + Product Rule (Most Common GATE Combo)

When you have $y = f(x) \cdot g(h(x))$:
1. Apply product rule: $y' = f'(x) \cdot g(h(x)) + f(x) \cdot [g(h(x))]'$
2. Apply chain rule on $[g(h(x))]' = g'(h(x)) \cdot h'(x)$

**Example:** $y = x^2 \sin(e^x)$
$$y' = 2x \sin(e^x) + x^2 \cos(e^x) \cdot e^x$$

---

### 2. Chain Rule + Quotient Rule

For $y = \dfrac{f(h(x))}{g(x)}$:
$$y' = \frac{f'(h(x)) \cdot h'(x) \cdot g(x) - f(h(x)) \cdot g'(x)}{[g(x)]^2}$$

---

### 3. Chain Rule for Implicit Differentiation

When $F(x, y) = 0$ where $y = y(x)$, each $y$-term picks up a chain rule factor $\frac{dy}{dx}$:

$$\frac{d}{dx}[y^n] = n y^{n-1} \cdot \frac{dy}{dx}$$
$$\frac{d}{dx}[\sin y] = \cos y \cdot \frac{dy}{dx}$$
$$\frac{d}{dx}[e^y] = e^y \cdot \frac{dy}{dx}$$

**Fast Formula for $F(x,y) = 0$:**
$$\frac{dy}{dx} = -\frac{F_x}{F_y} = -\frac{\partial F/\partial x}{\partial F/\partial y}$$

---

### 4. Chain Rule for Parametric Equations

Given $x = x(t)$, $y = y(t)$, using $\frac{d}{dt} = \frac{d}{dx} \cdot \frac{dx}{dt}$:

$$\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{dy/dt}{dx/dt}$$

$$\frac{d^2y}{dx^2} = \frac{d}{dx}\!\left(\frac{dy}{dx}\right) = \frac{\dfrac{d}{dt}\!\left(\dfrac{dy}{dx}\right)}{dx/dt}$$

---

### 5. Chain Rule for Inverse Functions

If $y = f^{-1}(x)$ (i.e., $f(y) = x$), differentiate both sides w.r.t. $x$:
$$f'(y) \cdot \frac{dy}{dx} = 1 \implies \frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{1}{f'(y)}$$

---

### 6. Chain Rule in Related Rates (Physics Context for GATE AE)

If two quantities $x$ and $y$ both depend on time $t$:
$$\frac{dy}{dt} = \frac{dy}{dx} \cdot \frac{dx}{dt}$$

*Application:* If an aircraft's angle of attack $\alpha$ changes with airspeed $V$, and airspeed changes with time $t$:
$$\frac{d\alpha}{dt} = \frac{d\alpha}{dV} \cdot \frac{dV}{dt}$$

---

## 🧠 Part V: Chain Rule for Multi-Variable Functions (Preview for Partial Derivatives)

### For $z = f(x, y)$ where $x = x(t)$ and $y = y(t)$:

$$\frac{dz}{dt} = \frac{\partial f}{\partial x} \cdot \frac{dx}{dt} + \frac{\partial f}{\partial y} \cdot \frac{dy}{dt}$$

### For $z = f(x, y)$ where $x = x(s,t)$ and $y = y(s,t)$:

$$\frac{\partial z}{\partial s} = \frac{\partial f}{\partial x} \cdot \frac{\partial x}{\partial s} + \frac{\partial f}{\partial y} \cdot \frac{\partial y}{\partial s}$$

> 🔮 **Preview Note:** This multivariable chain rule is the foundation of the **Total Derivative**, Jacobians, and all PDE problems. Learning single-variable chain rule perfectly NOW makes multivariable trivial later.

## ⚡ AIR-1 Topper Speed Tricks & Mnemonics

### 🚀 Trick 1: The "Outside-Inside" Verbal Mantra
Say aloud as you differentiate:
> **"Derivative of outside, leave inside alone — THEN multiply by derivative of inside."**

For $\frac{d}{dx}[\sin^5(3x^2)]$:
- Outer $= (\cdot)^5$, inner $= \sin(3x^2)$, innermost $= 3x^2$
- $= 5\sin^4(3x^2) \cdot \cos(3x^2) \cdot 6x = 30x \sin^4(3x^2)\cos(3x^2)$

---

### 🚀 Trick 2: The Power-Chain Instant Formula
$$\boxed{\frac{d}{dx}[f(x)]^n = n \cdot [f(x)]^{n-1} \cdot f'(x)}$$

This handles 60% of all chain rule problems in GATE! Memorize it as a single unit.

*Examples:*
- $\frac{d}{dx}[\sin x]^3 = 3\sin^2 x \cdot \cos x$
- $\frac{d}{dx}[x^2 + 1]^{10} = 10(x^2+1)^9 \cdot 2x = 20x(x^2+1)^9$
- $\frac{d}{dx}\sqrt{1-x^2} = \frac{-2x}{2\sqrt{1-x^2}} = \frac{-x}{\sqrt{1-x^2}}$

---

### 🚀 Trick 3: Log-Chain Rule — The "Log-Diff" Shortcut
For $\frac{d}{dx}[\ln(f(x))]$:
$$\boxed{\frac{d}{dx}[\ln(f(x))] = \frac{f'(x)}{f(x)}}$$

*Examples:*
- $\frac{d}{dx}[\ln(\sin x)] = \frac{\cos x}{\sin x} = \cot x$
- $\frac{d}{dx}[\ln(x^2+1)] = \frac{2x}{x^2+1}$
- $\frac{d}{dx}[\ln(\ln x)] = \frac{1/x}{\ln x} = \frac{1}{x\ln x}$

---

### 🚀 Trick 4: Exponential-Chain Rule — The "Exp-Chain" Shortcut
$$\boxed{\frac{d}{dx}[e^{f(x)}] = e^{f(x)} \cdot f'(x)}$$

*Examples:*
- $\frac{d}{dx}[e^{\sin x}] = e^{\sin x} \cdot \cos x$
- $\frac{d}{dx}[e^{x^2}] = 2xe^{x^2}$
- $\frac{d}{dx}[e^{\tan^{-1}x}] = \frac{e^{\tan^{-1}x}}{1+x^2}$

---

### 🚀 Trick 5: GATE MCQ Chain-Rule Speed Filter
When GATE gives 4 options for $f'(x)$ where $f$ is composite:
1. **Count the chain factors:** How many "layers" are in $f(x)$? Each layer adds one multiplicative factor.
2. **Check the innermost term's derivative** in the options — eliminate any option missing that factor.
3. **Check sign:** Cos derivatives from $-\sin$ produce negatives. Eliminate wrong signs.

Usually eliminates 2–3 options in under 15 seconds!

## 📝 Step-by-Step Solved GATE Examples

### Example 1: Basic Chain Rule
**Problem:** $\frac{d}{dx}[\cos(x^3 + 1)]$

**Solution (Peel the Onion):**
- Outer: $\cos(\cdot)$ → derivative is $-\sin(\cdot)$
- Inner: $x^3 + 1$ → derivative is $3x^2$
$$= -\sin(x^3 + 1) \cdot 3x^2 = \boxed{-3x^2 \sin(x^3 + 1)}$$

---

### Example 2: Triple Composition
**Problem:** $\frac{d}{dx}[\ln(\sin(x^2))]$

**Solution:**
- Layer 1 (outer): $\ln(\cdot)$ → $\frac{1}{(\cdot)}$
- Layer 2 (mid): $\sin(\cdot)$ → $\cos(\cdot)$
- Layer 3 (inner): $x^2$ → $2x$

$$= \frac{1}{\sin(x^2)} \cdot \cos(x^2) \cdot 2x = \frac{2x\cos(x^2)}{\sin(x^2)} = \boxed{2x\cot(x^2)}$$

---

### Example 3: Chain + Product Rule
**Problem:** $\frac{d}{dx}[x^3 e^{\sin x}]$

**Solution:** Product rule first, chain rule inside:
$$= 3x^2 \cdot e^{\sin x} + x^3 \cdot e^{\sin x} \cdot \cos x = e^{\sin x}(3x^2 + x^3 \cos x) = \boxed{x^2 e^{\sin x}(3 + x\cos x)}$$

---

### Example 4: Power-Chain
**Problem (GATE PYQ type):** $\frac{d}{dx}[\tan^5(3x)]$

**Solution:**
$$= 5\tan^4(3x) \cdot \sec^2(3x) \cdot 3 = \boxed{15\tan^4(3x)\sec^2(3x)}$$

---

### Example 5: Implicit Differentiation — Chain Rule Applied
**Problem:** Find $\frac{dy}{dx}$ from $\sin(xy) + y^2 = x^3$.

**Solution:** Differentiate both sides w.r.t. $x$ using chain rule on every term:
$$\cos(xy) \cdot \frac{d}{dx}(xy) + 2y\frac{dy}{dx} = 3x^2$$
$$\cos(xy) \cdot \left(y + x\frac{dy}{dx}\right) + 2y\frac{dy}{dx} = 3x^2$$
$$y\cos(xy) + x\cos(xy)\frac{dy}{dx} + 2y\frac{dy}{dx} = 3x^2$$
$$\frac{dy}{dx}[x\cos(xy) + 2y] = 3x^2 - y\cos(xy)$$
$$\boxed{\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{3x^2 - y\cos(xy)}{x\cos(xy) + 2y}}$$

---

### Example 6: Inverse Trig Chain (GATE Favourite)
**Problem:** $\frac{d}{dx}\left[\sin^{-1}\!\left(\frac{2x}{1+x^2}\right)\right]$

**Solution:** Let $f = \sin^{-1}(g)$, where $g = \frac{2x}{1+x^2}$.

*Topper trick:* Recognise that $\frac{2x}{1+x^2} = \sin(2\theta)$ when $x = \tan\theta$. So:
$$\sin^{-1}(\sin 2\theta) = 2\theta = 2\tan^{-1}x \quad \text{(for } |x| \le 1 \text{)}$$
$$\frac{d}{dx}[2\tan^{-1}x] = \frac{2}{1+x^2} \quad \boxed{\text{Answer: } \frac{2}{1+x^2}}$$

*(This is vastly faster than applying chain rule + quotient rule directly!)*

---

### Example 7: Chain Rule in Parametric Form
**Problem:** $x = \cos t$, $y = \sin^2 t$. Find $\frac{dy}{dx}$.

**Solution:**
$$\frac{dx}{dt} = -\sin t, \quad \frac{dy}{dt} = 2\sin t \cos t = \sin(2t)$$
$$\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{dy/dt}{dx/dt} = \frac{\sin 2t}{-\sin t} = \frac{2\sin t \cos t}{-\sin t} = \boxed{-2\cos t}$$

---

## 🔑 Master Substitution Tricks for Inverse Trig + Chain Rule

These substitutions convert complex chain rule problems into trivial differentiation:

| Expression Inside | Substitution | Result |
|---|---|---|
| $\sqrt{1-x^2}$ | $x = \sin\theta$ | $\sqrt{1-\sin^2\theta} = \cos\theta$ |
| $\sqrt{1+x^2}$ | $x = \tan\theta$ | $\sqrt{1+\tan^2\theta} = \sec\theta$ |
| $\sqrt{x^2-1}$ | $x = \sec\theta$ | $\sqrt{\sec^2\theta - 1} = \tan\theta$ |
| $\frac{2x}{1+x^2}$ | $x = \tan\theta$ | $\sin(2\theta)$ → $\sin^{-1}(g) = 2\tan^{-1}x$ |
| $\frac{1-x^2}{1+x^2}$ | $x = \tan\theta$ | $\cos(2\theta)$ → $\cos^{-1}(g) = 2\tan^{-1}x$ |
| $\frac{2x}{1-x^2}$ | $x = \tan\theta$ | $\tan(2\theta)$ → $\tan^{-1}(g) = 2\tan^{-1}x$ |

> 🎯 These substitution results ($2\tan^{-1}x$, $2\sin^{-1}x$) are common GATE answer choices — **memorize the pattern!**

## ⚠️ Common Exam Traps & Pitfalls

> 🔴 **Trap 1: Forgetting the Inner Derivative Factor**  
> *Mistake:* $\frac{d}{dx}[\sin(x^2)] = \cos(x^2)$ ❌  
> *Correction:* $= \cos(x^2) \cdot 2x$ ✅ — ALWAYS multiply by derivative of the inner function!

> 🔴 **Trap 2: Applying Chain Rule Where It's Not Needed**  
> *Mistake:* $\frac{d}{dx}[x \cdot \sin x]$ — treating as chain rule instead of product rule.  
> *Correction:* Use Product Rule here — chain rule only applies to COMPOSITION $f(g(x))$, not multiplication $f(x) \cdot g(x)$!

> 🔴 **Trap 3: Wrong Order in Triple Composition**  
> *Mistake:* For $y = e^{\sin(x^2)}$, writing $e^{\sin(x^2)} \cdot x^2 \cdot \cos(x^2)$ ❌  
> *Correction:* Outermost first: $e^{\sin(x^2)} \cdot \cos(x^2) \cdot 2x$ ✅

> 🔴 **Trap 4: Chain Rule on $y$ Terms in Implicit Differentiation**  
> *Mistake:* $\frac{d}{dx}[y^3] = 3y^2$ ❌  
> *Correction:* $= 3y^2 \cdot \frac{dy}{dx}$ ✅ — every $y$-term gains a $\frac{dy}{dx}$ factor!

> 🔴 **Trap 5: Log-Chain Rule Sign/Denominator Error**  
> *Mistake:* $\frac{d}{dx}[\ln(1-x^2)] = \frac{1}{1-x^2}$ ❌  
> *Correction:* $= \frac{-2x}{1-x^2}$ ✅ — differentiate the argument $1-x^2$ to get $-2x$!

## 📐 Master Reference Table: Chain Rule

| Form | Formula | Mnemonic |
|---|---|---|
| Basic chain rule | $[f(g(x))]' = f'(g(x)) \cdot g'(x)$ | Outside × inside' |
| Power-chain | $[f(x)]^n \cdot f'(x) \cdot n[f(x)]^{n-1}$ | Power then multiply |
| Exp-chain | $e^{f(x)} \cdot f'(x)$ | $e$ stays, multiply by $f'$ |
| Log-chain | $\ln(f(x)) \to f'(x)/f(x)$ | "Flip and multiply" |
| Sin-chain | $\sin(f(x)) \to \cos(f(x)) \cdot f'(x)$ | Outer deriv × inner' |
| Cos-chain | $\cos(f(x)) \to -\sin(f(x)) \cdot f'(x)$ | Negative sign! |
| Tan⁻¹-chain | $\tan^{-1}(f(x)) \to f'(x)/(1+[f(x)]^2)$ | Denominator $1+f^2$ |
| Sin⁻¹-chain | $\sin^{-1}(f(x)) \to f'(x)/\sqrt{1-[f(x)]^2}$ | Denominator $\sqrt{1-f^2}$ |
| Implicit $F(x,y)=0$ | $dy/dx = -F_x/F_y$ | Negative ratio of partials |
| Parametric | $dy/dx = \dot{y}/\dot{x}$ | Time-derivatives ratio |
| Multivariable | $dz/dt = z_x \dot{x} + z_y \dot{y}$ | Sum of partial-chain products |
| Substitution | $x = \tan\theta \Rightarrow \frac{2x}{1+x^2}=\sin 2\theta$ | Recognise trig identities |

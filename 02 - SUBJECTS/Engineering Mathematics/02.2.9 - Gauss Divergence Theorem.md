> 🎯 **GATE Weightage:** 2–4 marks per year. Gauss's Divergence Theorem converts a hard **Surface Integral (Flux)** into an easy **Volume Integral of Divergence** — or vice versa. It also underpins the **Continuity Equation** and **Gauss's Law** in aerospace and physics.

---

## 📌 Part I: Statement & Mathematical Formulation

### 1. Formal Statement of Gauss's Divergence Theorem

Let $V$ be a simple, bounded, 3D region enclosed by a piecewise-smooth **closed** surface $S$, with outward-pointing unit normal $\hat{n}$. If $\vec{F}(x, y, z) = F_1\hat{i} + F_2\hat{j} + F_3\hat{k}$ has continuous partial derivatives on $V$:

$$\boxed{\oiint_S \vec{F} \cdot \hat{n}\,dS = \iiint_V (\nabla \cdot \vec{F})\,dV}$$

Or equivalently, in component form:

$$\boxed{\oiint_S (F_1\,dy\,dz + F_2\,dz\,dx + F_3\,dx\,dy) = \iiint_V \left(\frac{\partial F_1}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial F_2}{\partial y} + \frac{\partial F_3}{\partial z}\right) dV}$$

```
              Outward normals n̂ →→→
          ↑ ↑ ↑ ↑ ↑ ↑ ↑ ↑ ↑ ↑ ↑
         +---------------------------+
        /|   Volume V               /|
       / |   (inside solid)        / |
      +---------------------------+  |
      |  |  ∇·F evaluated here   |  |
      |  +------------------------+--+
      | /                         | /
      |/                          |/
      +---------------------------+
       ↓ ↓ ↓ ↓ ↓ ↓ ↓ ↓ ↓ ↓ ↓
      Outward normals n̂ →→→
```

---

### 2. Word Meaning & Physical Interpretation

> 🌊 **Physical Intuition:**  
> The **total outward flux** of $\vec{F}$ through the closed surface $S$ (i.e., the net "stuff" leaving the volume per unit time) equals the **total divergence** (net source strength) of $\vec{F}$ throughout the enclosed volume $V$.

| Side | Physical Meaning |
|---|---|
| $\oiint_S \vec{F} \cdot \hat{n}\,dS$ | **Total Outward Flux** through surface $S$ (e.g., total fluid leaving a control volume per second) |
| $\iiint_V (\nabla \cdot \vec{F})\,dV$ | **Total Source Strength** inside volume $V$ (e.g., net mass sources/sinks of fluid inside control volume) |

---

### 3. Aerospace & Physics Applications

- **Aerodynamic Continuity Equation (Incompressible flow, $\nabla \cdot \vec{V} = 0$):**
  $$\oiint_S \vec{V} \cdot \hat{n}\,dS = \iiint_V \underbrace{(\nabla \cdot \vec{V})}_{=0}\,dV = 0$$
  Net mass flux through any closed surface is **zero** for incompressible flow.

- **Gauss's Law in Electrostatics (Maxwell's Equations):**
  $$\oiint_S \vec{E} \cdot \hat{n}\,dS = \frac{Q_{enc}}{\varepsilon_0} = \iiint_V \frac{\rho_{charge}}{\varepsilon_0}\,dV$$

- **Heat Flux (Fourier's Law):**
  $$\oiint_S (-k\nabla T) \cdot \hat{n}\,dS = \iiint_V (-k\nabla^2 T)\,dV$$

## 🧠 Part II: High-Yield Corollaries & Special Cases

### 1. Solenoidal Field Corollary ($\nabla \cdot \vec{F} = 0$)

If $\vec{F}$ is **solenoidal** (divergence-free):
$$\boxed{\oiint_S \vec{F} \cdot \hat{n}\,dS = \iiint_V 0\,dV = \mathbf{0}}$$

> ⚡ **Instant answer:** Flux of ANY solenoidal field through ANY closed surface = **ZERO**.

---

### 2. Volume Computation from Surface Integral

Since $\nabla \cdot \vec{r} = \nabla \cdot (x\hat{i}+y\hat{j}+z\hat{k}) = 1+1+1 = 3$:

$$\boxed{\text{Volume}(V) = \frac{1}{3} \oiint_S \vec{r} \cdot \hat{n}\,dS = \frac{1}{3} \oiint_S (x\,dy\,dz + y\,dz\,dx + z\,dx\,dy)}$$

---

### 3. Position Vector Flux Through Any Closed Surface

$$\boxed{\oiint_S \vec{r} \cdot \hat{n}\,dS = 3 \times \text{Volume}(V)}$$

*Proof:* $\nabla \cdot \vec{r} = 3 \implies \oiint_S \vec{r} \cdot \hat{n}\,dS = \iiint_V 3\,dV = 3V$.

**Sphere shortcut:** $\oiint_{sphere} \vec{r} \cdot \hat{n}\,dS = 3 \times \frac{4}{3}\pi R^3 = \mathbf{4\pi R^3}$

---

### 4. Constant Vector Field Flux Through Closed Surface

If $\vec{F} = \vec{c}$ (constant vector), then $\nabla \cdot \vec{c} = 0$:
$$\boxed{\oiint_S \vec{c} \cdot \hat{n}\,dS = \mathbf{0}}$$

---

### 5. Green's First & Second Identities (GATE Theoretical MCQs)

**Green's First Identity** (choose $\vec{F} = \phi \nabla \psi$):
$$\boxed{\oiint_S \phi \frac{\partial \psi}{\partial n}\,dS = \iiint_V (\nabla\phi \cdot \nabla\psi + \phi\,\nabla^2\psi)\,dV}$$

**Green's Second Identity** (Green's Reciprocity Theorem):
$$\boxed{\oiint_S \left(\phi \frac{\partial \psi}{\partial n} - \psi \frac{\partial \phi}{\partial n}\right) dS = \iiint_V (\phi\,\nabla^2\psi - \psi\,\nabla^2\phi)\,dV}$$

*where $\frac{\partial \psi}{\partial n} = \nabla\psi \cdot \hat{n}$ is the normal derivative.*

---

### 6. Surface with Partial Boundary (Open vs. Closed)

If the closed surface $S = S_1 \cup S_2$ (two pieces), you can write:
$$\iint_{S_1} \vec{F} \cdot \hat{n}\,dS = \iiint_V (\nabla \cdot \vec{F})\,dV - \iint_{S_2} \vec{F} \cdot \hat{n}\,dS$$

> ⚡ **Split-Surface Trick:** If flux through $S_2$ (simpler piece, e.g. flat disk) is easy to compute, calculate $\iiint_V \nabla\cdot\vec{F}\,dV$ and subtract!

---

## 🧠 Part III: 4-Step GATE Algorithm

```
Given: Closed Surface S (sphere, cube, cylinder, etc.), Vector Field F

Step 1: Check if F is SOLENOIDAL (∇·F = 0)
        → If YES: Flux = 0 instantly! (Done in 2 seconds)

Step 2: Compute Divergence: ∇·F = ∂F₁/∂x + ∂F₂/∂y + ∂F₃/∂z

Step 3: Is ∇·F a constant k?
        → Yes: Flux = k × Volume(V) — use standard volume formula!
        → No: Evaluate ∭_V (∇·F) dV using Cartesian/Cylindrical/Spherical

Step 4: Check if the given surface is OPEN (not closed)
        → Add a "cap" or "base" surface to make it closed
        → Use Divergence Theorem on closed surface, then subtract the cap's flux
```

## ⚡ AIR-1 Topper Speed Tricks & Mnemonics

### 🚀 Trick 1: Constant Divergence × Volume Shortcut
If $\nabla \cdot \vec{F} = k$ (constant) over volume $V$:
$$\oiint_S \vec{F} \cdot \hat{n}\,dS = k \times \text{Volume}(V)$$

*Example:* $\vec{F} = 2x\hat{i} + 3y\hat{j} + 4z\hat{k} \implies \nabla\cdot\vec{F} = 2+3+4 = 9$  
Sphere $R=1$: Flux $= 9 \times \frac{4}{3}\pi(1)^3 = 12\pi$ in 5 seconds!

---

### 🚀 Trick 2: Position Vector Sphere Shortcut
For $\vec{F} = \vec{r} = x\hat{i}+y\hat{j}+z\hat{k}$ and sphere of radius $R$:
$$\oiint_S \vec{r} \cdot \hat{n}\,dS = 4\pi R^3$$
(Memorize: $\nabla\cdot\vec{r} = 3$, Volume of sphere $= \frac{4}{3}\pi R^3$, so Flux $= 3 \times \frac{4}{3}\pi R^3 = 4\pi R^3$)

---

### 🚀 Trick 3: Open Surface = Divergence Theorem − Cap Flux
For open surface $S_1$ (e.g., hemisphere, cylinder without base):
1. Complete it to closed surface $S = S_1 + S_2$ (add flat cap/disk $S_2$)
2. Compute $\iiint_V \nabla\cdot\vec{F}\,dV$ (easy volume integral)
3. Compute $\iint_{S_2} \vec{F}\cdot\hat{n}\,dS$ (flat cap is trivial)
4. Subtract: $\iint_{S_1} \vec{F}\cdot\hat{n}\,dS = \iiint_V \nabla\cdot\vec{F}\,dV - \iint_{S_2} \vec{F}\cdot\hat{n}\,dS$

---

### 🚀 Trick 4: Solenoidal = Zero Flux (Instant!)
$$\nabla \cdot \vec{F} = 0 \implies \oiint_S \vec{F} \cdot \hat{n}\,dS = 0$$

*Fields that are always solenoidal:* $\nabla\times\vec{G}$ (curl of any field), incompressible velocity fields.

---

### 🧠 Mnemonic: "DIVS" for Divergence Theorem
**D**ivergence of $\vec{F}$ integrated over the **V**olume = **S**urface flux over the closed **S**urface.  
**D-V = S-S** → Volume Integral = Surface Integral

## 📝 Step-by-Step Solved GATE Examples

### Example 1: Constant Divergence Over Sphere (GATE PYQ Type)
**Problem:** Evaluate $\oiint_S \vec{F} \cdot \hat{n}\,dS$ where $\vec{F} = (2x+z)\hat{i} + (y^2-3z)\hat{j} + (2y+z)\hat{k}$ and $S$ is the closed surface of the sphere $x^2+y^2+z^2 = 4$.

**Solution (Speed Trick 1):**
1. $\nabla \cdot \vec{F} = \frac{\partial}{\partial x}(2x+z) + \frac{\partial}{\partial y}(y^2-3z) + \frac{\partial}{\partial z}(2y+z) = 2 + 2y + 1 = 3 + 2y$

2. Apply Divergence Theorem:
$$\oiint_S \vec{F} \cdot \hat{n}\,dS = \iiint_V (3+2y)\,dV$$

3. Split into two parts:
$$= \iiint_V 3\,dV + 2\iiint_V y\,dV$$

4. For sphere of radius $R=2$: Volume $= \frac{4}{3}\pi(2)^3 = \frac{32\pi}{3}$  
   $\iiint_V y\,dV = 0$ (odd integrand, symmetric domain about $y=0$)

5. Final:
$$= 3 \times \frac{32\pi}{3} + 0 = \mathbf{32\pi}$$

$$\boxed{\oiint_S \vec{F} \cdot \hat{n}\,dS = 32\pi}$$

---

### Example 2: Open Hemisphere — Split-Surface Trick
**Problem:** Evaluate the flux of $\vec{F} = x^3\hat{i} + y^3\hat{j} + z^3\hat{k}$ **outward** through the curved surface $S_1$ of the hemisphere $x^2+y^2+z^2 = 1$, $z \ge 0$ (excluding the flat base).

**Solution (Trick 3 — Open Surface):**
1. Close the surface: $S = S_1$ (curved hemisphere) $+ S_2$ (flat disk $z=0, x^2+y^2 \le 1$)

2. Compute $\nabla \cdot \vec{F} = 3x^2 + 3y^2 + 3z^2 = 3(x^2+y^2+z^2) = 3\rho^2$ (in spherical)

3. Volume integral over hemisphere $V$ ($\rho\in[0,1], \phi\in[0,\pi/2], \theta\in[0,2\pi]$):
$$\iiint_V 3\rho^2\,dV = 3\int_0^{2\pi}d\theta\int_0^{\pi/2}\sin\phi\,d\phi\int_0^1 \rho^2 \cdot \rho^2\,d\rho = 3(2\pi)(1)\left[\frac{\rho^5}{5}\right]_0^1 = \frac{6\pi}{5}$$

4. Flux through flat disk $S_2$ ($z=0$, **inward** normal $\hat{n} = -\hat{k}$):
$$\iint_{S_2} \vec{F}\cdot(-\hat{k})\,dA = \iint_{S_2} -z^3\,dA = \iint_{S_2} 0\,dA = 0 \quad (\text{since } z=0)$$

5. Flux through curved hemisphere:
$$\iint_{S_1} \vec{F}\cdot\hat{n}\,dS = \frac{6\pi}{5} - 0 = \mathbf{\frac{6\pi}{5}}$$

$$\boxed{\iint_{S_1} \vec{F}\cdot\hat{n}\,dS = \frac{6\pi}{5}}$$

---

### Example 3: Solenoidal Field (Instant)
**Problem:** Find the flux of $\vec{F} = \nabla \times (x^2 y\hat{i} + yz^2\hat{j} + xz\hat{k})$ through the closed surface of the cube $[0,1]^3$.

**Solution (Speed Trick 4):**
$\vec{F} = \nabla \times \vec{G}$ for some $\vec{G}$.  
Since the divergence of a curl is always zero: $\nabla \cdot \vec{F} = \nabla \cdot (\nabla \times \vec{G}) = 0$.  
By Gauss's Theorem: $\oiint_S \vec{F}\cdot\hat{n}\,dS = \iiint_V 0\,dV = \mathbf{0}$.

$$\boxed{\text{Flux} = 0}$$

---

### Example 4: Position Vector Flux (Instant)
**Problem:** Evaluate $\oiint_S \vec{r}\cdot\hat{n}\,dS$ over the closed surface of the ellipsoid $\frac{x^2}{4}+\frac{y^2}{9}+\frac{z^2}{16}=1$.

**Solution (Trick 2):**
$\vec{r} = x\hat{i}+y\hat{j}+z\hat{k}$, $\nabla\cdot\vec{r} = 3$  
Volume of ellipsoid $= \frac{4}{3}\pi abc = \frac{4}{3}\pi(2)(3)(4) = 32\pi$

$$\oiint_S \vec{r}\cdot\hat{n}\,dS = 3\times 32\pi = \mathbf{96\pi}$$

$$\boxed{\oiint_S \vec{r}\cdot\hat{n}\,dS = 96\pi}$$

## 📐 Master Summary: All Three Big Integral Theorems

| Theorem | Statement | Converts |
|---|---|---|
| **Green's (2D)** | $\oint_C (P\,dx + Q\,dy) = \iint_D (Q_x - P_y)\,dA$ | Line $\leftrightarrow$ Area integral |
| **Stokes'** | $\oint_C \vec{F}\cdot d\vec{r} = \iint_S (\nabla\times\vec{F})\cdot\hat{n}\,dS$ | Line $\leftrightarrow$ Surface Curl integral |
| **Gauss (Divergence)** | $\oiint_S \vec{F}\cdot\hat{n}\,dS = \iiint_V (\nabla\cdot\vec{F})\,dV$ | Surface Flux $\leftrightarrow$ Volume Div integral |

---

## ⚠️ Common Exam Traps & Pitfalls

> 🔴 **Trap 1: Using Divergence Theorem on OPEN Surface**  
> Gauss's theorem requires a **closed** surface ($S$ must completely enclose a volume $V$). For open surfaces (hemisphere without base, cylinder without caps), you must **add the missing pieces**, apply the theorem, then subtract.

> 🔴 **Trap 2: Forgetting Outward Normal Convention**  
> For Gauss's Theorem, the normal $\hat{n}$ must point **outward** from the enclosed volume. An inward normal gives $-1$ times the result.

> 🔴 **Trap 3: Odd Integrand Symmetry Not Exploited**  
> If $\nabla\cdot\vec{F}$ contains odd terms like $y$, $xz$, $x^2y$ over a symmetric volume (sphere, cube centred at origin), those terms integrate to zero! Only keep the even/symmetric parts.

## 📌 Part I: Concept & Definitions of Volume Integrals

### 1. Scalar Volume Integral

Given a scalar function $f(x, y, z)$ defined over a 3D spatial region $V$:

$$\iiint_V f(x, y, z)\,dV$$

#### Physical Applications:
- **Total Volume of Solid $V$:** $\text{Volume}(V) = \iiint_V 1\,dV$
- **Mass of Solid with Variable Density $\rho(x, y, z)$:**
  $$\text{Mass} = M = \iiint_V \rho(x, y, z)\,dV$$
- **Center of Mass $(\bar{x}, \bar{y}, \bar{z})$:**
  $$\bar{x} = \frac{1}{M} \iiint_V x\,\rho\,dV, \quad \bar{y} = \frac{1}{M} \iiint_V y\,\rho\,dV, \quad \bar{z} = \frac{1}{M} \iiint_V z\,\rho\,dV$$
- **Moment of Inertia about $z$-axis ($I_{zz}$):**
  $$I_{zz} = \iiint_V (x^2 + y^2)\,\rho(x,y,z)\,dV$$

---

### 2. Vector Volume Integral

Given a vector field $\vec{F}(x, y, z) = F_1\hat{i} + F_2\hat{j} + F_3\hat{k}$:

$$\iiint_V \vec{F}(x, y, z)\,dV = \left(\iiint_V F_1\,dV\right)\hat{i} + \left(\iiint_V F_2\,dV\right)\hat{j} + \left(\iiint_V F_3\,dV\right)\hat{k}$$

## 🧠 Part II: The 3 Master Coordinate Systems & Volume Elements ($dV$)

```
                  Cartesian (x,y,z)          Cylindrical (r,θ,z)        Spherical (ρ,φ,θ)
                      z                          z                          z
                      |                          |                          |  / ρ (radius)
                      |                          |                          | / φ (polar angle)
                      +------► y                 +------► y                 +------► y
                     /                          /                          /
                    ▼ x                        ▼ x                        ▼ x (θ = azimuth)
                 dV = dx dy dz              dV = r dr dθ dz            dV = ρ² sinφ dρ dφ dθ
```

---

### 1. Cartesian Coordinate System $(x, y, z)$

$$\boxed{dV = dx\,dy\,dz}$$

#### Iterated Triple Integral Order:
$$\iiint_V f(x, y, z)\,dV = \int_{x_1}^{x_2} \int_{y_1(x)}^{y_2(x)} \int_{z_1(x,y)}^{z_2(x,y)} f(x, y, z)\,dz\,dy\,dx$$

- **When to use:** Rectangular boxes, tetrahedrons, prisms with flat planar boundaries.

---

### 2. Cylindrical Coordinate System $(r, \theta, z)$

Transformation equations:
$$x = r\cos\theta, \quad y = r\sin\theta, \quad z = z \quad (x^2 + y^2 = r^2)$$

Jacobian $J = r$:

$$\boxed{dV = r\,dr\,d\theta\,dz}$$

- **When to use:** Solids with rotational symmetry around the $z$-axis (cylinders, cones, paraboloids $z = x^2 + y^2$).

---

### 3. Spherical Polar Coordinate System $(\rho, \phi, \theta)$

Transformation equations:
$$x = \rho\sin\phi\cos\theta, \quad y = \rho\sin\phi\sin\theta, \quad z = \rho\cos\phi \quad (x^2 + y^2 + z^2 = \rho^2)$$

Where:
- $\rho$: Distance from origin ($\rho \ge 0$)
- $\phi$: Polar angle measured from $+z$ axis ($0 \le \phi \le \pi$)
- $\theta$: Azimuthal angle in $xy$-plane ($0 \le \theta \le 2\pi$)

Jacobian $J = \rho^2\sin\phi$:

$$\boxed{dV = \rho^2\sin\phi\,d\rho\,d\phi\,d\theta}$$

- **When to use:** Spheres, hemispheres, cones ($z = \sqrt{x^2+y^2} \implies \phi = \pi/4$), region between concentric spheres.

---

## 🧠 Part III: Master Dirichlet's & Liouville's Integrals (AIR-1 High-Yield!)

### 1. Dirichlet's Integral Formula (GATE Magic Tool!)

If the volume $V$ is bounded by $x \ge 0, y \ge 0, z \ge 0$ and $x + y + z \le 1$ (standard unit tetrahedron in 1st octant):

$$\boxed{\iiint_V x^{l-1} y^{m-1} z^{n-1}\,dx\,dy\,dz = \frac{\Gamma(l)\,\Gamma(m)\,\Gamma(n)}{\Gamma(l + m + n + 1)}}$$

where $\Gamma(n)$ is the Gamma Function ($\Gamma(n+1) = n!$).

---

### 2. Extended Dirichlet Formula (For Scaled & Powered Bounds)

If $V$ is bounded by $x \ge 0, y \ge 0, z \ge 0$ and $\left(\frac{x}{a}\right)^p + \left(\frac{y}{b}\right)^q + \left(\frac{z}{c}\right)^r \le 1$:

$$\boxed{\iiint_V x^{l-1} y^{m-1} z^{n-1}\,dx\,dy\,dz = \frac{a^l b^m c^n}{p \cdot q \cdot r} \cdot \frac{\Gamma\!\left(\frac{l}{p}\right) \Gamma\!\left(\frac{m}{q}\right) \Gamma\!\left(\frac{n}{r}\right)}{\Gamma\!\left(\frac{l}{p} + \frac{m}{q} + \frac{n}{r} + 1\right)}}$$

---

### 3. Volume of Ellipsoid Shortcut Formula

For an ellipsoid $\frac{x^2}{a^2} + \frac{y^2}{b^2} + \frac{z^2}{c^2} \le 1$:

$$\boxed{\text{Volume of Ellipsoid} = \frac{4}{3}\pi a b c}$$

- For a sphere ($a = b = c = R$): $\text{Volume} = \frac{4}{3}\pi R^3$.
- For a tetrahedron $x/a + y/b + z/c \le 1$ in 1st octant: $\text{Volume} = \mathbf{\frac{a b c}{6}}$.

---

## 🧠 Part IV: Standard Geometry Volumes Table

| Solid Geometry | Equation / Region Bounds | Volume Formula | Coordinate System to Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Sphere** | $x^2 + y^2 + z^2 \le R^2$ | $\frac{4}{3}\pi R^3$ | Spherical polar ($\rho \in [0, R]$) |
| **Ellipsoid** | $\frac{x^2}{a^2} + \frac{y^2}{b^2} + \frac{z^2}{c^2} \le 1$ | $\frac{4}{3}\pi a b c$ | Scaled Spherical |
| **Cylinder** | $x^2 + y^2 \le R^2, \; 0 \le z \le h$ | $\pi R^2 h$ | Cylindrical ($r \in [0, R]$) |
| **Cone** | $z \ge \sqrt{x^2+y^2}, \; z \le h$ | $\frac{1}{3}\pi R^2 h$ | Cylindrical or Spherical ($\phi = \pi/4$) |
| **Tetrahedron** | $x \ge 0, y \ge 0, z \ge 0, \; x+y+z \le 1$ | $\frac{1}{6}$ | Cartesian / Dirichlet |
| **Paraboloid Cap** | $z \ge x^2+y^2, \; z \le h$ | $\frac{1}{2}\pi h^2$ | Cylindrical ($r^2 \le z \le h$) |

## ⚡ AIR-1 Topper Speed Tricks & Mnemonics

### 🚀 Trick 1: Dirichlet's 10-Second Integrand Evaluation
To evaluate $\iiint_{V} x^2 y^3 z\,dx\,dy\,dz$ over $x+y+z \le 1$ ($x,y,z \ge 0$):
Identify exponents: $l-1 = 2 \implies l=3$; $m-1 = 3 \implies m=4$; $n-1 = 1 \implies n=2$.
$$\text{Integral} = \frac{\Gamma(3)\Gamma(4)\Gamma(2)}{\Gamma(3+4+2+1)} = \frac{2! \cdot 3! \cdot 1!}{9!} = \frac{2 \cdot 6 \cdot 1}{362880} = \mathbf{\frac{1}{30240}}$$
Done in 10 seconds without doing a single integration step!

---

### 🚀 Trick 2: Spherical Integrand Factorization Shortcut
When integrating a function $f(\rho, \phi, \theta) = g(\rho) \cdot h(\phi) \cdot k(\theta)$ over a sphere of radius $R$:
$$\iiint_V g(\rho)h(\phi)k(\theta)\,dV = \left( \int_0^R g(\rho)\rho^2\,d\rho \right) \left( \int_0^\pi h(\phi)\sin\phi\,d\phi \right) \left( \int_0^{2\pi} k(\theta)\,d\theta \right)$$
Split the 3D integral into the product of **3 independent 1D integrals**!

---

### 🚀 Trick 3: Tetrahedron Volume Shortcut — $\frac{a b c}{6}$
For any tetrahedron bounded by the coordinate planes and plane $\frac{x}{a} + \frac{y}{b} + \frac{z}{c} = 1$:
$$\text{Volume} = \mathbf{\frac{a b c}{6}}$$

---

### 🚀 Trick 4: Constant Density Symmetry Shortcut for Center of Mass
If a solid has:
- Symmetry about $xy$-plane ($z \leftrightarrow -z$) $\implies \mathbf{\bar{z} = 0}$
- Symmetry about $xz$-plane ($y \leftrightarrow -y$) $\implies \mathbf{\bar{y} = 0}$
- Symmetry about $yz$-plane ($x \leftrightarrow -x$) $\implies \mathbf{\bar{x} = 0}$

## 📝 Step-by-Step Solved GATE Examples

### Example 1: Volume Calculation via Cartesian Integration
**Problem (GATE):** Find the volume bounded by the cylinder $x^2 + y^2 = 4$ and the planes $z = 0$ and $z = 3$.

**Solution (Cylindrical Coordinates):**
Region bounds: $r \in [0, 2], \; \theta \in [0, 2\pi], \; z \in [0, 3]$.
$$V = \iiint_V dV = \int_0^3 dz \int_0^{2\pi} d\theta \int_0^2 r\,dr$$
$$V = [z]_0^3 \cdot [\theta]_0^{2\pi} \cdot \left[ \frac{r^2}{2} \right]_0^2 = 3 \cdot 2\pi \cdot 2 = \mathbf{12\pi}$$

$$\boxed{\text{Volume} = 12\pi}$$

---

### Example 2: Volume Below Paraboloid
**Problem:** Find the volume of the region bounded below by the paraboloid $z = x^2 + y^2$ and above by the plane $z = 4$.

**Solution (Cylindrical Coordinates):**
Intersection of $z = r^2$ and $z = 4 \implies r^2 = 4 \implies r = 2$.
Bounds: $z \in [r^2, 4], \; r \in [0, 2], \; \theta \in [0, 2\pi]$.

$$V = \int_0^{2\pi} d\theta \int_0^2 r\,dr \int_{r^2}^4 dz = 2\pi \int_0^2 r(4 - r^2)\,dr = 2\pi \int_0^2 (4r - r^3)\,dr$$
$$V = 2\pi \left[ 2r^2 - \frac{r^4}{4} \right]_0^2 = 2\pi \left( 8 - 4 \right) = \mathbf{8\pi}$$

$$\boxed{\text{Volume} = 8\pi}$$

---

### Example 3: Mass of Sphere with Radial Density
**Problem:** Find the mass of a solid sphere $x^2 + y^2 + z^2 \le 9$ if density at any point is proportional to its distance from origin ($\rho(x,y,z) = k \sqrt{x^2+y^2+z^2}$).

**Solution (Spherical Coordinates):**
Sphere radius $R = 3$. Density $\rho = k \rho$ (distance from origin).  
$dV = \rho^2 \sin\phi\,d\rho\,d\phi\,d\theta$.

$$\text{Mass} = \iiint_V k\rho \cdot \rho^2 \sin\phi\,d\rho\,d\phi\,d\theta = k \int_0^{2\pi} d\theta \int_0^\pi \sin\phi\,d\phi \int_0^3 \rho^3\,d\rho$$
$$\text{Mass} = k \cdot (2\pi) \cdot (2) \cdot \left[ \frac{\rho^4}{4} \right]_0^3 = 4\pi k \left(\frac{81}{4}\right) = \mathbf{81\pi k}$$

$$\boxed{\text{Mass} = 81\pi k}$$

---

### Example 4: Dirichlet's Integral Application
**Problem:** Evaluate $\iiint_V x\,dx\,dy\,dz$ over the region $x \ge 0, y \ge 0, z \ge 0$ and $x + y + z \le 1$.

**Solution (Using Speed Trick 1):**
Integrand: $x^1 y^0 z^0 \implies l-1=1 \implies l=2$; $m-1=0 \implies m=1$; $n-1=0 \implies n=1$.

$$\text{Integral} = \frac{\Gamma(2)\Gamma(1)\Gamma(1)}{\Gamma(2+1+1+1)} = \frac{1! \cdot 0! \cdot 0!}{4!} = \frac{1}{24}$$

$$\boxed{\text{Integral} = \frac{1}{24}}$$

## ⚠️ Common Exam Traps & Pitfalls

> 🔴 **Trap 1: Forgetting the Jacobian Factor $r$ or $\rho^2\sin\phi$**  
> - Cylindrical: $dV = r\,dr\,d\theta\,dz$ (leaving out $r$ causes major error!).  
> - Spherical: $dV = \rho^2\sin\phi\,d\rho\,d\phi\,d\theta$ (leaving out $\rho^2\sin\phi$ ruins the integral!).

> 🔴 **Trap 2: Spherical Polar Angle Limits Error**  
> - $\phi$ (polar angle from $+z$ axis) goes from **$0$ to $\pi$** (NOT $2\pi$!).  
> - $\theta$ (azimuthal angle in $xy$-plane) goes from **$0$ to $2\pi$**.

> 🔴 **Trap 3: Incorrect Dirichlet Integral Exponent Mapping**  
> Remember: The exponent of $x$ in the integrand is $l-1$, so if the integrand is $x^2$, then $l = 3$ (NOT $l=2$).

> 🎯 **GATE Weightage:** 3–5 marks per year. Simpson's 1/3 and 3/8 rules are **high-accuracy Newton-Cotes quadrature formulas** ($O(h^4)$ order of accuracy). Simpson's 1/3 rule is exact for polynomials up to degree 3!

---

## 📌 Part I: Simpson's 1/3 Rule (MASTER FORMULA)

### 1. Geometric Concept & Single-Segment Formula
Approximates $f(x)$ by a **2nd-degree parabolic arc** passing through 3 points over 2 sub-intervals of width $h = \frac{b - a}{2}$.

Single-segment ($n=2$):

$$\boxed{\int_a^b f(x)\,dx \approx \frac{h}{3} \left[ y_0 + 4y_1 + y_2 \right]}$$

---

### 2. Composite Simpson's 1/3 Rule ($n$ MUST BE EVEN!)

Divide $[a, b]$ into an **EVEN number of sub-intervals** $n$ ($n = 2, 4, 6, 8, \ldots$).  
Step size $h = \frac{b - a}{n}$. Ordinates: $y_0, y_1, y_2, \ldots, y_n$ ($N = n+1$ points).

$$\boxed{\int_a^b f(x)\,dx \approx \frac{h}{3} \left[ (y_0 + y_n) + 4(y_1 + y_3 + \cdots + y_{n-1}) + 2(y_2 + y_4 + \cdots + y_{n-2}) \right]}$$

In words:

$$\boxed{\text{Integral} = \frac{h}{3} \left[ (\text{First} + \text{Last}) + 4 \times (\text{Sum of ODD-indexed ordinates}) + 2 \times (\text{Sum of EVEN-indexed ordinates}) \right]}$$

---

### 3. Weight Stencil for Simpson's 1/3 Rule
$$\frac{h}{3} \times \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 4 & 2 & 4 & 2 & 4 & \cdots & 4 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$$

---

## 🧠 Part II: Simpson's 3/8 Rule

### 1. Geometric Concept & Single-Segment Formula
Approximates $f(x)$ by a **3rd-degree cubic arc** passing through 4 points over 3 sub-intervals of width $h = \frac{b - a}{3}$.

Single-segment ($n=3$):

$$\boxed{\int_a^b f(x)\,dx \approx \frac{3h}{8} \left[ y_0 + 3y_1 + 3y_2 + y_3 \right]}$$

---

### 2. Composite Simpson's 3/8 Rule ($n$ MUST BE A MULTIPLE OF 3!)

Divide $[a, b]$ into $n$ sub-intervals where **$n$ is a multiple of 3** ($n = 3, 6, 9, 12, \ldots$).

$$\boxed{\int_a^b f(x)\,dx \approx \frac{3h}{8} \left[ (y_0 + y_n) + 3(y_1 + y_2 + y_4 + y_5 + \cdots) + 2(y_3 + y_6 + y_9 + \cdots) \right]}$$

In words:

$$\boxed{\text{Integral} = \frac{3h}{8} \left[ (\text{First} + \text{Last}) + 3 \times (\text{Sum of non-multiples of 3}) + 2 \times (\text{Sum of multiples of 3}) \right]}$$

---

### 3. Weight Stencil for Simpson's 3/8 Rule
$$\frac{3h}{8} \times \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 3 & 3 & 2 & 3 & 3 & 2 & \cdots & 3 & 3 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$$

---

## 🧠 Part III: Error Analysis & Degree of Precision (SUPER HIGH YIELD!)

### 1. Degree of Precision (Order of Exactness)

| Rule | Derived Using | Degree of Precision | Exact For Polynomials Of Degree |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Trapezoidal** | Straight Line ($x^1$) | **1** | $\le 1$ ($Ax + B$) |
| **Simpson's 1/3** | Parabola ($x^2$) | **3** ⭐ (Gain of 1!) | $\le 3$ ($Ax^3 + Bx^2 + Cx + D$) |
| **Simpson's 3/8** | Cubic ($x^3$) | **3** | $\le 3$ ($Ax^3 + Bx^2 + Cx + D$) |

> ⚡ **Surprise Result:** Simpson's 1/3 rule gives **EXACT analytical answer** for any cubic polynomial $f(x) = Ax^3 + Bx^2 + Cx + D$, even though it was derived using 2nd-degree parabolas! (Due to symmetric cancellation of odd 3rd derivative error term).

---

### 2. Truncation Error Formulas

#### Simpson's 1/3 Rule Composite Error:
$$\boxed{E_n = -\frac{(b - a) h^4}{180} f^{(4)}(\xi) = -\frac{(b - a)^5}{180 n^4} f^{(4)}(\xi) = \mathbf{O(h^4)}}$$

#### Simpson's 3/8 Rule Composite Error:
$$\boxed{E_n = -\frac{(b - a) h^4}{80} f^{(4)}(\xi) = -\frac{(b - a)^5}{80 n^4} f^{(4)}(\xi) = \mathbf{O(h^4)}}$$

- **Order of Accuracy:** Both are $\mathbf{O(h^4)}$ (Halving $h \implies$ error drops by a factor of $2^4 = \mathbf{16}$!).
- **Accuracy Comparison:** Simpson's 1/3 rule is **more accurate** than 3/8 rule because $\frac{1}{180} < \frac{1}{80}$!

---

## 🧠 Part IV: Master Comparison Table of Numerical Integration Rules

| Feature | Trapezoidal Rule | Simpson's 1/3 Rule | Simpson's 3/8 Rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Interpolant** | Linear (Line) | Quadratic (Parabola) | Cubic (Polynomial) |
| **Sub-intervals ($n$)** | Any $n \ge 1$ | **EVEN** ($n=2,4,6\dots$) | **MULTIPLE OF 3** ($n=3,6,9\dots$) |
| **Ordinates ($N=n+1$)** | Any $N \ge 2$ | **ODD** ($N=3,5,7\dots$) | $N = 4, 7, 10, 13\dots$ |
| **Multiplier** | $\frac{h}{2}$ | $\frac{h}{3}$ | $\frac{3h}{8}$ |
| **Weights Pattern** | $[1, 2, 2, \ldots, 2, 1]$ | $[1, \mathbf{4}, \mathbf{2}, \mathbf{4}, \mathbf{2}, \ldots, 1]$ | $[1, \mathbf{3}, \mathbf{3}, \mathbf{2}, \mathbf{3}, \mathbf{3}, \ldots, 1]$ |
| **Degree of Precision** | $1$ | **3** | **3** |
| **Error Order** | $O(h^2)$ | **$O(h^4)$** | **$O(h^4)$** |
| **Composite Error** | $-\frac{(b-a)h^2}{12}f''$ | $-\frac{(b-a)h^4}{180}f^{(4)}$ | $-\frac{(b-a)h^4}{80}f^{(4)}$ |

## ⚡ AIR-1 Speed Tricks & Mnemonics

### 🚀 Trick 1: 10-Second Polynomial Exactness Test
If asked: *"Find the value of $\int_0^2 (4x^3 - 3x^2 + 2x - 1) dx$ using Simpson's 1/3 rule with $n=2$"*:  
Recognize integrand is a **3rd degree polynomial** $\implies$ Simpson's 1/3 rule gives **EXACT analytical integral**!  
Integrate directly by calculus: $\left[ x^4 - x^3 + x^2 - x \right]_0^2 = 16 - 8 + 4 - 2 = \mathbf{10}$ in 5 seconds!

---

### 🚀 Trick 2: Sub-interval Applicability Rule
- **Given $n$ sub-intervals:**
  - $n$ is EVEN $\implies$ Use **Simpson's 1/3 Rule**
  - $n$ is a MULTIPLE OF 3 $\implies$ Use **Simpson's 3/8 Rule**
  - $n$ is ODD (e.g. $n=5$) $\implies$ Combine: 1/3 rule for first 2 sub-intervals + 3/8 rule for remaining 3!

---

### 🚀 Trick 3: Fast Stencil Labeling Method
In exam scratchpad, write indices above ordinates:
```
Index:    0   1   2   3   4
Weight:   1   4   2   4   1  (Simpson's 1/3)
y-val:   y₀  y₁  y₂  y₃  y₄
```
Sum $1(y_0 + y_4) + 4(y_1 + y_3) + 2(y_2)$, then multiply by $h/3$.

---

### 🧠 Mnemonic: "**O-4-E-2**" for Simpson's 1/3 Weights
- **O**dd-indexed ordinates ($y_1, y_3, y_5 \ldots$) get weight **4**
- **E**ven-indexed ordinates ($y_2, y_4, y_6 \ldots$) get weight **2**
- End ordinates ($y_0, y_n$) get weight **1**

## 📝 Step-by-Step Solved GATE Examples

### Example 1: Simpson's 1/3 Rule (GATE 2-Marker)
**Problem:** Evaluate $I = \int_0^1 \frac{dx}{1 + x}$ using Simpson's 1/3 Rule with $n = 4$ sub-intervals.

**Solution:**
1. $h = \frac{1 - 0}{4} = 0.25$.
2. Ordinates table ($y_i = \frac{1}{1 + x_i}$):

| Index $i$ | $x_i$ | $y_i$ | Weight $w_i$ | $w_i \cdot y_i$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $0$ (First) | $0.00$ | $1.0000$ | $1$ | $1.0000$ |
| $1$ (Odd) | $0.25$ | $0.8000$ | $4$ | $3.2000$ |
| $2$ (Even) | $0.50$ | $0.6667$ | $2$ | $1.3333$ |
| $3$ (Odd) | $0.75$ | $0.5714$ | $4$ | $2.2857$ |
| $4$ (Last) | $1.00$ | $0.5000$ | $1$ | $0.5000$ |

3. Sum of weighted ordinates $= 1.0000 + 3.2000 + 1.3333 + 2.2857 + 0.5000 = 8.3190$
4. Multiply by $\frac{h}{3} = \frac{0.25}{3} = \frac{1}{12}$:
   $$I \approx \frac{8.3190}{12} = \mathbf{0.69325}$$

*(Exact value $= \ln 2 \approx 0.69315$. Error $= 0.00010$ — 39 times more accurate than Trapezoidal Rule $0.6970$!)*

$$\boxed{I \approx 0.69325}$$

---

### Example 2: Simpson's 3/8 Rule (GATE 2-Marker)
**Problem:** Evaluate $I = \int_0^\pi \sin x\,dx$ using Simpson's 3/8 Rule with $n = 3$ sub-intervals.

**Solution:**
1. $h = \frac{\pi - 0}{3} = \frac{\pi}{3}$. $n=3$ (multiple of 3 ✓).
2. Mesh points & ordinates ($y_i = \sin x_i$):
   - $x_0 = 0 \implies y_0 = \sin 0 = 0$
   - $x_1 = \pi/3 \implies y_1 = \sin(\pi/3) = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \approx 0.8660$
   - $x_2 = 2\pi/3 \implies y_2 = \sin(2\pi/3) = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \approx 0.8660$
   - $x_3 = \pi \implies y_3 = \sin\pi = 0$
3. Weights pattern for $n=3$: $\frac{3h}{8} [y_0 + 3y_1 + 3y_2 + y_3]$
4. Calculate:
   $$I \approx \frac{3(\pi/3)}{8} \left[ 0 + 3\left(\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right) + 3\left(\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right) + 0 \right] = \frac{\pi}{8} \left( 3\sqrt{3} \right) = \frac{3\sqrt{3}\pi}{8} \approx \frac{5.196 \times 3.1416}{8} = \mathbf{2.040}$$

$$\boxed{I = \frac{3\sqrt{3}\pi}{8} \approx 2.040}$$

---

### Example 3: Polynomial Exactness MCQ (GATE 1-Marker)
**Problem:** For which of the following integrands does Simpson's 1/3 Rule give the exact analytical value of $\int_a^b f(x)\,dx$?  
(a) $f(x) = x^4$  
(b) $f(x) = x^3 - 5x + 2$  
(c) $f(x) = \sin x$  
(d) $f(x) = e^x$

**Solution (Using Speed Trick 1):**  
Degree of precision for Simpson's 1/3 Rule is **3** (exact for degree $\le 3$).  
Option (b) is a cubic polynomial ($x^3 - 5x + 2$) $\implies$ Exact!

$$\boxed{\text{Correct Answer: (b)}}$$

## ⚠️ Common Exam Traps & Pitfalls

> 🔴 **Trap 1: Applying Simpson's 1/3 Rule when $n$ is ODD**  
> Simpson's 1/3 rule requires an **EVEN number of sub-intervals** ($n=2,4,6\dots$), which means an **ODD number of data points** ($N=3,5,7\dots$). If $n$ is odd, you CANNOT apply Simpson's 1/3 rule to the whole domain!

> 🔴 **Trap 2: Swapping Weights 4 and 2 in Simpson's 1/3 Rule**  
> Odd-indexed ordinates ($y_1, y_3, y_5$) get weight **4**. Even-indexed ordinates ($y_2, y_4, y_6$) get weight **2**. (Remember **O-4-E-2**!).

> 🔴 **Trap 3: Forgetting Multiplier Differences**  
> - Simpson's 1/3 multiplier is **$\frac{h}{3}$**
> - Simpson's 3/8 multiplier is **$\frac{3h}{8}$**
> - Trapezoidal multiplier is **$\frac{h}{2}$**

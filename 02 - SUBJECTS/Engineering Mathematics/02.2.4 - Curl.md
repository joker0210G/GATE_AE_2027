## 📌 Part I: Definition & Fundamentals of Curl

### 1. Definition

For a vector field $\vec{F}(x, y, z) = F_1\hat{i} + F_2\hat{j} + F_3\hat{k}$:

$$\boxed{\nabla \times \vec{F} = \text{curl}\,\vec{F} = \begin{vmatrix} \hat{i} & \hat{j} & \hat{k} \\[4pt] \dfrac{\partial}{\partial x} & \dfrac{\partial}{\partial y} & \dfrac{\partial}{\partial z} \\[8pt] F_1 & F_2 & F_3 \end{vmatrix}}$$

Expanding the determinant:

$$\nabla \times \vec{F} = \left(\frac{\partial F_3}{\partial y} - \frac{\partial F_2}{\partial z}\right)\hat{i} + \left(\frac{\partial F_1}{\partial z} - \frac{\partial F_3}{\partial x}\right)\hat{j} + \left(\frac{\partial F_2}{\partial x} - \frac{\partial F_1}{\partial y}\right)\hat{k}$$

> 💡 **Key Takeaway:** Curl takes a **VECTOR field** $\vec{F}$ as input and produces a **VECTOR field** $\nabla \times \vec{F}$ as output! (Cross product of operator $\nabla$ and vector $\vec{F}$).

---

### 2. Special 2D Case (GATE High-Frequency!)

For a 2D vector field in the $xy$-plane: $\vec{F}(x, y) = P(x, y)\hat{i} + Q(x, y)\hat{j}$:

$$\boxed{\nabla \times \vec{F} = \left(\frac{\partial Q}{\partial x} - \frac{\partial P}{\partial y}\right)\hat{k}}$$

---

### 3. Physical Meaning of Curl (Rotation & Vorticity)

Curl measures the **rotation, circulation, or angular velocity** of a vector field per unit area at a point:

```
        ▲                           ▲
      / | \                       / | \
    ◄ — P — ►                   ◄ — P — ►
      \ | /                       \ | /
        ▼                           ▼
  ∇ × F ≠ 0                   ∇ × F = 0
 (ROTATIONAL FLOW)          (IRROTATIONAL FLOW)
  Paddle wheel turns        Paddle wheel stays still
```

- **Paddle Wheel Analogy:** If a tiny paddle wheel placed at point $P$ in a fluid flow rotates, then $\nabla \times \vec{V} \neq \vec{0}$ at $P$.
- **Aerodynamics — Vorticity Vector ($\vec{\omega}$):**
  $$\boxed{\text{Vorticity } \vec{\omega} = \nabla \times \vec{V} = 2\vec{\Omega}}$$
  where $\vec{\Omega}$ is the local angular velocity of the fluid element.

## 🧠 Part II: Irrotational Fields & Scalar Potential (#1 GATE Pattern!)

### 1. Definition of Irrotational Field

A vector field $\vec{F}$ is called **Irrotational** (or Conservative) if its curl is the zero vector everywhere in the domain:

$$\boxed{\vec{F} \text{ is Irrotational} \iff \nabla \times \vec{F} = \vec{0}}$$

---

### 2. Conservative Vector Fields & Scalar Potential ($\phi$)

If $\vec{F}$ is irrotational ($\nabla \times \vec{F} = \vec{0}$) in a simply connected domain, then $\vec{F}$ can be expressed as the **gradient of a scalar potential $\phi$**:

$$\boxed{\vec{F} = \nabla\phi \iff \nabla \times \vec{F} = \vec{0}}$$

#### Key Consequences for Conservative Fields:
1. **Path Independence:** The line integral $\int_C \vec{F} \cdot d\vec{r}$ depends ONLY on the initial point $A$ and final point $B$, not on the path taken!
2. **Work Done Formula:**
   $$\boxed{W = \int_A^B \vec{F} \cdot d\vec{r} = \phi(B) - \phi(A)}$$
3. **Closed Loop Work = Zero:**
   $$\boxed{\oint_C \vec{F} \cdot d\vec{r} = 0 \quad \text{around ANY closed curve } C}$$

---

### 3. Algorithm: How to Find Scalar Potential $\phi(x, y, z)$

```
Given: Irrotational field F = F₁i + F₂j + F₃k

Step 1: Set up equations:
        ∂φ/∂x = F₁,   ∂φ/∂y = F₂,   ∂φ/∂z = F₃

Step 2: Integrate F₁ w.r.t x:
        φ = ∫ F₁ dx + g(y, z)

Step 3: Differentiate φ w.r.t y and equate to F₂ to find ∂g/∂y
Step 4: Integrate w.r.t y to get g(y, z) = h(z) + f(y)
Step 5: Differentiate w.r.t z and equate to F₃ to find h'(z)
Step 6: Combine all terms + constant C
```

#### ⚡ Fast Differential Method for $\phi$:
Combine exact differentials:
$$d\phi = F_1 dx + F_2 dy + F_3 dz$$
Group terms like $d(xy) = x dy + y dx$, $d(x^2 y) = 2xy dx + x^2 dy$, etc., and integrate directly!

## 🧠 Part III: Position Vector ($\vec{r}$) & Rotational Identities

Let $\vec{r} = x\hat{i} + y\hat{j} + z\hat{k}$ and $r = \|\vec{r}\| = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2 + z^2}$.

### 🎯 Master Table of Curl Identities (GATE Goldmines!)

| Identity Formula | Result | Notes / Physical Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| $\nabla \times \vec{r}$ | $\vec{0}$ | **Position vector field is ALWAYS irrotational!** |
| $\nabla \times (f(r)\vec{r})$ | $\vec{0}$ | **Any central force field is ALWAYS irrotational!** |
| $\nabla \times (\vec{a} \times \vec{r})$ | $2\vec{a}$ | For constant vector $\vec{a}$ |
| $\nabla \times (\vec{\omega} \times \vec{r})$ | $2\vec{\omega}$ | Rigid body rotation ($\vec{V} = \vec{\omega} \times \vec{r}$) |
| $\nabla \times (\nabla\phi)$ | $\vec{0}$ | **Curl of ANY Gradient is ALWAYS ZERO!** |
| $\nabla \cdot (\nabla \times \vec{F})$ | $0$ | **Divergence of ANY Curl is ALWAYS ZERO!** |
| $\nabla \times (\nabla \times \vec{F})$ | $\nabla(\nabla \cdot \vec{F}) - \nabla^2\vec{F}$ | **Curl of Curl Vector Identity** |
| $\nabla \times (\phi \vec{F})$ | $\phi(\nabla \times \vec{F}) + (\nabla\phi) \times \vec{F}$ | Product rule for scalar $\times$ vector |

---

### 🔑 Derivation of Central Force Irrotationality $\nabla \times (f(r)\vec{r}) = \vec{0}$:

Using product identity $\nabla \times (\phi \vec{F}) = \phi(\nabla \times \vec{F}) + (\nabla\phi) \times \vec{F}$ with $\phi = f(r)$ and $\vec{F} = \vec{r}$:

$$\nabla \times (f(r)\vec{r}) = f(r)(\nabla \times \vec{r}) + (\nabla f(r)) \times \vec{r}$$

Since $\nabla \times \vec{r} = \vec{0}$ and $\nabla f(r) = f'(r)\frac{\vec{r}}{r}$:

$$= f(r)(\vec{0}) + \left(f'(r)\frac{\vec{r}}{r}\right) \times \vec{r} = \vec{0} + \frac{f'(r)}{r} (\vec{r} \times \vec{r})$$

Since cross product of any vector with itself is zero ($\vec{r} \times \vec{r} = \vec{0}$):

$$\mathbf{\nabla \times (f(r)\vec{r}) = \vec{0}}$$

> ⚡ **Physical Result:**  
> Gravitational force $\vec{F} \propto \frac{\vec{r}}{r^3}$ and Electrostatic force $\vec{F} \propto \frac{\vec{r}}{r^3}$ are central forces $\implies$ **ALWAYS IRROTATIONAL (CONSERVATIVE)**!

---

## 🧠 Part IV: Preview of Stokes' Theorem

The **Stokes' Theorem** converts a line integral around a closed loop $C$ into a surface integral of curl:

$$\boxed{\oint_C \vec{F} \cdot d\vec{r} = \iint_S (\nabla \times \vec{F}) \cdot \hat{n}\,dS}$$

> 🔮 **Preview Note:** If $\vec{F}$ is irrotational ($\nabla \times \vec{F} = \vec{0}$), then circulation $\oint_C \vec{F} \cdot d\vec{r} = \iint_S (\vec{0}) \cdot \hat{n}\,dS = \mathbf{0}$.

## ⚡ AIR-1 Topper Speed Tricks & Mnemonics

### 🚀 Trick 1: Irrotational Constant Finding Algorithm (15 Seconds)
If GATE gives $\vec{F} = F_1\hat{i} + F_2\hat{j} + F_3\hat{k}$ with unknown constants $a, b, c$:
Write the 3 component conditions for $\nabla \times \vec{F} = \vec{0}$:
$$\frac{\partial F_3}{\partial y} = \frac{\partial F_2}{\partial z}, \qquad \frac{\partial F_1}{\partial z} = \frac{\partial F_3}{\partial x}, \qquad \frac{\partial F_2}{\partial x} = \frac{\partial F_1}{\partial y}$$
Solve the 3 simple equations. Done in 15 seconds!

---

### 🚀 Trick 2: Central Force Irrotationality Rule
Any field of the form $\vec{F} = r^n \vec{r}$ or $f(r)\vec{r}$ is **AUTOMATICALLY IRROTATIONAL**.  
- $\nabla \times (r^5 \vec{r}) = \vec{0}$
- $\nabla \times \left(\frac{\vec{r}}{r^3}\right) = \vec{0}$
- $\nabla \times (e^{r^2}\vec{r}) = \vec{0}$

No curl calculation needed — select $\vec{0}$ instantly!

---

### 🚀 Trick 3: Curl of Gradient Zero Filter
If an option asks for $\nabla \times \vec{G}$ where $\vec{G} = \nabla\phi$:  
**Answer is $\vec{0}$ INSTANTLY** by identity $\nabla \times (\nabla\phi) = \vec{0}$.

---

### 🚀 Trick 4: Fast Work Done in Conservative Field
If $\vec{F}$ is irrotational, work done from $A(x_1, y_1, z_1)$ to $B(x_2, y_2, z_2)$ is simply:
$$W = \phi(x_2, y_2, z_2) - \phi(x_1, y_1, z_1)$$
Ignore the curve path equation completely!

## 📝 Step-by-Step Solved GATE Examples

### Example 1: Finding Constants for Irrotational Field (GATE PYQ)
**Problem:** Find constants $a, b, c$ such that $\vec{F} = (x + 2y + az)\hat{i} + (bx - 3y - z)\hat{j} + (4x + cy + 2z)\hat{k}$ is irrotational.

**Solution:**
For $\vec{F}$ to be irrotational, $\nabla \times \vec{F} = \vec{0}$:

1. $\frac{\partial F_3}{\partial y} = \frac{\partial F_2}{\partial z} \implies c = -1$
2. $\frac{\partial F_1}{\partial z} = \frac{\partial F_3}{\partial x} \implies a = 4$
3. $\frac{\partial F_2}{\partial x} = \frac{\partial F_1}{\partial y} \implies b = 2$

$$\boxed{a = 4, \quad b = 2, \quad c = -1}$$

---

### Example 2: Finding Scalar Potential $\phi$
**Problem:** Find scalar potential $\phi$ for conservative field $\vec{F} = (2xy + z^3)\hat{i} + x^2\hat{j} + 3xz^2\hat{k}$.

**Solution:**
We need $\nabla\phi = \vec{F}$, so:
1. $\phi_x = 2xy + z^3$
2. $\phi_y = x^2$
3. $\phi_z = 3xz^2$

**Fast Exact Differential Method:**
$$d\phi = (2xy + z^3)dx + x^2 dy + 3xz^2 dz$$
$$d\phi = (2xy dx + x^2 dy) + (z^3 dx + 3xz^2 dz) = d(x^2 y) + d(x z^3)$$
$$\implies \phi(x, y, z) = \mathbf{x^2 y + x z^3 + C}$$

$$\boxed{\phi = x^2 y + x z^3 + C}$$

---

### Example 3: Work Done by Conservative Force Field
**Problem:** Find work done by force field $\vec{F} = (2xy + z^3)\hat{i} + x^2\hat{j} + 3xz^2\hat{k}$ in moving a particle from $A(1, -2, 1)$ to $B(3, 1, 4)$.

**Solution:**
Since $\vec{F}$ is conservative (from Example 2, $\phi = x^2 y + x z^3$):
$$W = \phi(B) - \phi(A) = \phi(3, 1, 4) - \phi(1, -2, 1)$$

- $\phi(3, 1, 4) = (3)^2(1) + (3)(4)^3 = 9 + 3(64) = 9 + 192 = 201$
- $\phi(1, -2, 1) = (1)^2(-2) + (1)(1)^3 = -2 + 1 = -1$

$$W = 201 - (-1) = \mathbf{202}$$

$$\boxed{\text{Work Done } W = 202}$$

---

### Example 4: Rigid Body Angular Velocity (Vorticity)
**Problem:** A fluid rotates as a rigid body with angular velocity $\vec{\Omega} = 3\hat{i} - 2\hat{j} + 5\hat{k}$. Find the velocity field $\vec{V} = \vec{\Omega} \times \vec{r}$ and verify its vorticity.

**Solution:**
1. $\vec{V} = \vec{\Omega} \times \vec{r} = \begin{vmatrix}\hat{i} & \hat{j} & \hat{k} \\ 3 & -2 & 5 \\ x & y & z\end{vmatrix} = (-2z - 5y)\hat{i} - (3z - 5x)\hat{j} + (3y + 2x)\hat{k}$
2. Vorticity $\vec{\omega} = \nabla \times \vec{V}$:
   $$\vec{\omega} = (3 - (-3))\hat{i} - (-5 - 5)\hat{j} + (5 - (-1))\hat{k} \dots$$
   By identity $\nabla \times (\vec{\Omega} \times \vec{r}) = 2\vec{\Omega}$:
   $$\vec{\omega} = 2(3\hat{i} - 2\hat{j} + 5\hat{k}) = \mathbf{6\hat{i} - 4\hat{j} + 10\hat{k}}$$

$$\boxed{\text{Vorticity } \vec{\omega} = 6\hat{i} - 4\hat{j} + 10\hat{k}}$$

## ⚠️ Common Exam Traps & Pitfalls

> 🔴 **Trap 1: Confusing Irrotational ($\nabla \times \vec{F} = \vec{0}$) with Solenoidal ($\nabla \cdot \vec{F} = 0$)**  
> - **Irrotational:** Curl is zero ($\nabla \times \vec{F} = \vec{0}$) $\implies$ Conservative field ($\vec{F} = \nabla\phi$).  
> - **Solenoidal:** Divergence is zero ($\nabla \cdot \vec{F} = 0$) $\implies$ Incompressible flow.

> 🔴 **Trap 2: Curl Output Type Mistake**  
> *Mistake:* Writing $\nabla \times \vec{F}$ as a scalar number.  
> *Correction:* Curl is a **cross product** — output is ALWAYS a **VECTOR**!

> 🔴 **Trap 3: Work Done Path Dependency Trap**  
> *Mistake:* Spending 10 minutes integrating $\int_C \vec{F} \cdot d\vec{r}$ along a complex parametric path when $\vec{F}$ is irrotational.  
> *Correction:* Test $\nabla \times \vec{F}$ first! If zero, just compute $\phi(B) - \phi(A)$.

> 🔴 **Trap 4: Missing Minus Sign in 2D Curl $Q_x - P_y$**  
> *Mistake:* Writing 2D curl as $(P_y - Q_x)\hat{k}$.  
> *Correction:* 2D Curl $= \left(\frac{\partial Q}{\partial x} - \frac{\partial P}{\partial y}\right)\hat{k}$!

## 📌 1. What is an Eigenvector? (Zero to AIR-1 Overview)

For a square transformation matrix $A_{n \times n}$, an **Eigenvector** is a **NON-ZERO vector** $\vec{v} \neq \vec{0}$ whose direction remains completely unchanged when operated on by $A$. The matrix multiplication $A \vec{v}$ merely scales the vector $\vec{v}$ by a scalar factor $\lambda$ (the Eigenvalue):

$$A \vec{v} = \lambda \vec{v} \iff (A - \lambda I) \vec{v} = \vec{0} \quad (\vec{v} \neq \vec{0})$$

### 🔑 Critical Fundamental Properties of Eigenvectors:
1. **Eigenvectors CANNOT be Zero:** $\vec{v} = \vec{0}$ is NEVER considered an eigenvector (by definition).
2. **Eigenvectors are NOT Unique (Ray / Line Property):** If $\vec{v}$ is an eigenvector for eigenvalue $\lambda$, then **ANY non-zero scalar multiple $c \vec{v}$ ($c \neq 0$) is ALSO an eigenvector** corresponding to the same eigenvalue $\lambda$:
   $$A(c \vec{v}) = c (A \vec{v}) = c (\lambda \vec{v}) = \lambda (c \vec{v})$$
3. **Normalized Eigenvector ($\hat{v}$):** Often written as a unit vector:
   $$\hat{v} = \frac{\vec{v}}{\|\vec{v}\|}$$

### Aerospace Engineering Applications:
- **Flight Mechanics (Dynamic Stability Modes):** The eigenvectors of the aircraft system matrix $A$ define the exact ratios of motion between pitch rate, angle of attack, airspeed, and bank angle for modes like **Phugoid**, **Short Period**, and **Dutch Roll**.
- **Structural Mechanics (Vibration Mode Shapes):** Eigenvectors of $[M]^{-1} [K]$ give the physical deflection shape (Mode Shape) of aircraft wings or turbine blades vibrating at natural frequency $\omega = \sqrt{\lambda}$.
- **Stress Analysis:** Eigenvectors of the $3 \times 3$ Stress Tensor matrix define the **Principal Stress Directions**.

## 🧠 Part I: Step-by-Step Algorithm for Computing Eigenvectors

For a given matrix $A_{n \times n}$ and a known eigenvalue $\lambda_k$:

```
Step 1: Form Matrix (A - λ_k I)
        |
Step 2: Set up Homogeneous Equation (A - λ_k I) v = 0
        |
Step 3: Apply EROs to get Row Echelon Form (REF)
        |
Step 4: Identify Free Variables (Count = Nullity = n - Rank(A - λ_k I))
        |
Step 5: Express Basic Variables in terms of Free Variables
        |
Step 6: Write Vector Basis for Eigenspace E(λ_k)
```

---

### ⚡ 2x2 Fast Eigenvector Shortcut (1-Second Solution):

For a $2 \times 2$ matrix $A = \begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{pmatrix}$ with eigenvalue $\lambda$:

The equation $(A - \lambda I) \vec{v} = \vec{0}$ expands to:
$$\begin{pmatrix} a-\lambda & b \\ c & d-\lambda \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} x \\ y \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}$$

Since rows are linearly dependent, use the first non-zero row:
$$(a-\lambda) x + b y = 0 \implies \mathbf{\vec{v} = \begin{pmatrix} -b \\ a-\lambda \end{pmatrix}} \quad \text{or} \quad \mathbf{\vec{v} = \begin{pmatrix} \lambda-d \\ c \end{pmatrix}}$$

> 💡 **Mnemonic:** Swap the coefficients and negate one term to get an orthogonal vector to the row!

## 🧠 Part II: Master Properties of Eigenvectors

> 🔴 **The 6 Master Theorems of Eigenvectors in GATE:**

### Theorem 1: Linear Independence of Distinct Eigenvalues
Eigenvectors corresponding to **DISTINCT (different)** eigenvalues are **ALWAYS LINEARLY INDEPENDENT**.

$$\text{If } \lambda_1 \neq \lambda_2 \neq \dots \neq \lambda_k \implies \{\vec{v}_1, \vec{v}_2, \dots, \vec{v}_k\} \text{ is Linearly Independent (LI)}$$

---

### Theorem 2: Orthogonality of Real Symmetric Matrix Eigenvectors
For a **Real Symmetric Matrix** ($A^T = A$), eigenvectors corresponding to **DISTINCT eigenvalues are MUTUALLY ORTHOGONAL**:

$$\text{If } A^T = A \text{ and } \lambda_i \neq \lambda_j \implies \mathbf{\vec{v}_i \cdot \vec{v}_j = 0} \quad (\vec{v}_i^T \vec{v}_j = 0)$$

---

### Theorem 3: The Eigenspace $E(\lambda)$ & Geometric Multiplicity
The set of ALL eigenvectors corresponding to an eigenvalue $\lambda$, along with the zero vector $\vec{0}$, forms a vector subspace of $\mathbb{R}^n$ called the **Eigenspace** $E(\lambda)$:

$$E(\lambda) = N(A - \lambda I) = \{\vec{v} \in \mathbb{R}^n \mid (A - \lambda I) \vec{v} = \vec{0}\}$$

- **Geometric Multiplicity (GM):** The dimension of the Eigenspace $E(\lambda)$:
  $$\mathbf{\text{GM}(\lambda) = \dim E(\lambda) = \text{Nullity}(A - \lambda I) = n - \text{Rank}(A - \lambda I)}$$
- Relation to Algebraic Multiplicity (AM):
  $$1 \le \text{GM}(\lambda) \le \text{AM}(\lambda)$$

---

### Theorem 4: Left Eigenvectors vs Right Eigenvectors

- **Right Eigenvector ($\vec{v}$):** Standard column vector satisfying $A \vec{v} = \lambda \vec{v}$.
- **Left Eigenvector ($\vec{w}^T$):** Row vector satisfying:
  $$\vec{w}^T A = \lambda \vec{w}^T \iff A^T \vec{w} = \lambda \vec{w}$$

#### Biorthogonality Property:
If $\vec{v}_j$ is a right eigenvector for $\lambda_j$, and $\vec{w}_i^T$ is a left eigenvector for $\lambda_i$ with $\lambda_i \neq \lambda_j$:
$$\mathbf{\vec{w}_i^T \vec{v}_j = 0} \quad (\text{Left and right eigenvectors of distinct eigenvalues are orthogonal!})$$

---

### Theorem 5: Invariance under Matrix Powers & Inverses
If $\vec{v}$ is an eigenvector of $A$ with eigenvalue $\lambda$:
1. $\vec{v}$ is ALSO an eigenvector of $A^m$ with eigenvalue $\lambda^m$:
   $$A^m \vec{v} = \lambda^m \vec{v}$$
2. $\vec{v}$ is ALSO an eigenvector of $A^{-1}$ with eigenvalue $\frac{1}{\lambda}$:
   $$A^{-1} \vec{v} = \frac{1}{\lambda} \vec{v}$$
3. $\vec{v}$ is ALSO an eigenvector of $A + k I$ with eigenvalue $\lambda + k$:
   $$(A + k I) \vec{v} = (\lambda + k) \vec{v}$$

---

### Theorem 6: Same Eigenvectors for Commuting Matrices
If two matrices $A$ and $B$ commute ($A B = B A$) and have distinct eigenvalues, then **$A$ and $B$ share a COMMON SET of eigenvectors**!

## 🧠 Part III: Diagonalization & Spectral Decomposition

### 1. Modal Matrix ($P$) & Spectral Matrix ($D$)

If $A_{n \times n}$ has $n$ linearly independent eigenvectors $\{\vec{v}_1, \vec{v}_2, \dots, \vec{v}_n\}$ corresponding to eigenvalues $\{\lambda_1, \lambda_2, \dots, \lambda_n\}$:

- **Modal Matrix ($P$):** Matrix formed by placing eigenvectors as columns:
  $$P = \begin{pmatrix} \vec{v}_1 & \vec{v}_2 & \dots & \vec{v}_n \end{pmatrix}_{n \times n}$$
- **Spectral Matrix ($D$):** Diagonal matrix of eigenvalues:
  $$D = \begin{pmatrix} \lambda_1 & 0 & \dots & 0 \\ 0 & \lambda_2 & \dots & 0 \\ \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \\ 0 & 0 & \dots & \lambda_n \end{pmatrix}_{n \times n}$$

#### Similarity Transformation:
$$A P = P D \implies \mathbf{A = P D P^{-1}} \iff \mathbf{D = P^{-1} A P}$$

---

### 2. Matrix Powers via Diagonalization (GATE Classic)

$$A^k = (P D P^{-1})^k = P D^k P^{-1} = P \begin{pmatrix} \lambda_1^k & 0 & \dots & 0 \\ 0 & \lambda_2^k & \dots & 0 \\ \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \\ 0 & 0 & \dots & \lambda_n^k \end{pmatrix} P^{-1}$$

---

### 3. Orthogonal Diagonalization & Spectral Decomposition (Real Symmetric $A$)

For a real symmetric matrix ($A^T = A$), eigenvectors can be chosen to form an **Orthonormal Set** $\{\vec{q}_1, \vec{q}_2, \dots, \vec{q}_n\}$.

- Modal matrix $Q = \begin{pmatrix} \vec{q}_1 & \vec{q}_2 & \dots & \vec{q}_n \end{pmatrix}$ is an **Orthogonal Matrix** ($Q^{-1} = Q^T$).
- **Orthogonal Similarity:**
  $$\mathbf{A = Q D Q^T} \iff \mathbf{D = Q^T A Q}$$

#### 🔑 Spectral Decomposition Formula (Expansion in Rank-1 Projection Matrices):
$$A = \lambda_1 (\vec{q}_1 \vec{q}_1^T) + \lambda_2 (\vec{q}_2 \vec{q}_2^T) + \dots + \lambda_n (\vec{q}_n \vec{q}_n^T)$$

where $P_i = \vec{q}_i \vec{q}_i^T$ is the projection matrix onto the direction of eigenvector $\vec{q}_i$.

## ⚡ AIR-1 Topper Speed Tricks & Mnemonics

### 🚀 Shortcut 1: The 2-Second Eigenvector Inspection Filter
In GATE MCQs asking *"Which of the following is an eigenvector of matrix A?"*:  
**DO NOT FIND EIGENVALUES FIRST!**

Simply multiply $A$ by each option vector $\vec{v}$:
1. Compute $A \vec{v}$.
2. Check if $A \vec{v} = k \vec{v}$ (is the output vector a scalar multiple of $\vec{v}$?).
3. If $A \vec{v} = k \vec{v}$, then $\vec{v}$ IS AN EIGENVECTOR and $k = \lambda$!

---

### 🚀 Shortcut 2: 1-Second Symmetric Orthogonality Elimination
If $A$ is a Real Symmetric Matrix ($A^T = A$) and options present eigenvectors for distinct eigenvalues:
- Dot product of eigenvectors corresponding to different eigenvalues MUST be zero ($\vec{v}_1 \cdot \vec{v}_2 = 0$).
- Eliminate any option where $\vec{v}_1 \cdot \vec{v}_2 \neq 0$ instantly!

---

### 🚀 Shortcut 3: Constant Row Sum Eigenvector
If every row sum of $A$ equals constant $k$:
$$\mathbf{\vec{v} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \\ \vdots \\ 1 \end{pmatrix} \text{ is ALWAYS an eigenvector of } A \text{ for eigenvalue } \lambda = k.}$$

---

### 🚀 Shortcut 4: 2x2 Fast Eigenvector Formula
For $A = \begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{pmatrix}$ with eigenvalue $\lambda$:
$$\vec{v} = \begin{pmatrix} -b \\ a - \lambda \end{pmatrix}$$

*Example:* $A = \begin{pmatrix} 4 & 1 \\ 2 & 3 \end{pmatrix}$, $\lambda_1 = 5$.
$$\vec{v}_1 = \begin{pmatrix} -1 \\ 4 - 5 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} -1 \\ -1 \end{pmatrix} \equiv \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}$$

---

## 📝 Step-by-Step Solved Examples (Zero to GATE Topper Level)

### Example 1: Fast Eigenvector Verification
**Problem (GATE PYQ):** For matrix $A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 3 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$, which of the following is an eigenvector?  
(A) $(1, 1)^T$  
(B) $(1, -1)^T$  
(C) $(2, 1)^T$  
(D) $(1, 2)^T$

**Solution (Topper Inspection Method):**
Test Option (B) $\vec{v} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ -1 \end{pmatrix}$:
$$A \vec{v} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 3 & 2 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ -1 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 - 2 \\ 3 - 2 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} -1 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix} = -1 \cdot \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ -1 \end{pmatrix} = -1 \vec{v}$$
Since $A \vec{v} = -1 \vec{v}$, $\vec{v} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ -1 \end{pmatrix}$ is an eigenvector (for eigenvalue $\lambda = -1$).  
**Correct Answer: (B)**

---

### Example 2: Spectral Decomposition of Symmetric Matrix
**Problem:** Construct the spectral decomposition of $A = \begin{pmatrix} 3 & 1 \\ 1 & 3 \end{pmatrix}$.

**Solution:**
1. Eigenvalues of $A$:
   - $\text{tr}(A) = 6$, $\det(A) = 9 - 1 = 8$.
   - $\lambda^2 - 6\lambda + 8 = 0 \implies (\lambda - 4)(\lambda - 2) = 0 \implies \lambda_1 = 4, \lambda_2 = 2$.
2. Eigenvector for $\lambda_1 = 4$:
   $$(A - 4I) \vec{v}_1 = \begin{pmatrix} -1 & 1 \\ 1 & -1 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} x \\ y \end{pmatrix} = \vec{0} \implies -x + y = 0 \implies \vec{v}_1 = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}$$
   Normalized: $\vec{q}_1 = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}$.
3. Eigenvector for $\lambda_2 = 2$:
   $$(A - 2I) \vec{v}_2 = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} x \\ y \end{pmatrix} = \vec{0} \implies x + y = 0 \implies \vec{v}_2 = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ -1 \end{pmatrix}$$
   Normalized: $\vec{q}_2 = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ -1 \end{pmatrix}$.
4. Spectral Decomposition:
   $$A = 4 \left( \vec{q}_1 \vec{q}_1^T \right) + 2 \left( \vec{q}_2 \vec{q}_2^T \right) = 4 \begin{pmatrix} 0.5 & 0.5 \\ 0.5 & 0.5 \end{pmatrix} + 2 \begin{pmatrix} 0.5 & -0.5 \\ -0.5 & 0.5 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 3 & 1 \\ 1 & 3 \end{pmatrix} \quad \text{✅ Verified!}$$

## ⚠️ Common Exam Traps & Pitfalls (Where Marks Are Lost)

> 🔴 **Trap 1: Confusing Order of Columns in Modal Matrix $P$**
> - *Mistake:* Placing eigenvector $\vec{v}_2$ as column 1 of $P$, but eigenvalue $\lambda_1$ as entry $(1,1)$ of $D$.
> - *Correction:* Column $j$ of $P$ MUST correspond precisely to diagonal entry $(j,j)$ of $D$!

> 🔴 **Trap 2: Left Eigenvectors vs Right Eigenvectors**
> - *Mistake:* Multiplying left eigenvector on the right: $A \vec{w} = \lambda \vec{w}$.
> - *Correction:* Right eigenvector is $A \vec{v} = \lambda \vec{v}$; Left eigenvector is $\vec{w}^T A = \lambda \vec{w}^T$.

> 🔴 **Trap 3: Assuming Non-Symmetric Matrix Eigenvectors Are Orthogonal**
> - *Mistake:* Assuming $\vec{v}_1 \cdot \vec{v}_2 = 0$ for a general non-symmetric matrix.
> - *Correction:* Orthogonality of eigenvectors for distinct eigenvalues ONLY holds for **Real Symmetric (or Hermitian)** matrices!

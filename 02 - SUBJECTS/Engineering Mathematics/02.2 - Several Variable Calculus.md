## 📌 Part I: Partial Derivatives (Zero to Clear)

> 📖 **Deep-Dive Topic Note:** [[02 - SUBJECTS/Engineering Mathematics/02.2.1 - Partial Derivatives|Partial Derivatives (First Principles, Tangent Plane, Clairaut's Theorem, Total Differential, Error Propagation, Multivariable Chain Rule, Implicit Partials, Cyclic Identity, Composite Euler's Theorem, 4 Speed Tricks)]] `#status/first-read`

### 1. Definition

For $f(x, y)$, the **partial derivative** with respect to $x$ treats $y$ as a constant and differentiates w.r.t. $x$:

$$\frac{\partial f}{\partial x} = f_x = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{f(x+h, y) - f(x, y)}{h}$$

Similarly, $\frac{\partial f}{\partial y}$ treats $x$ as a constant.

> 💡 **Key Rule:** Partial derivative = Ordinary derivative with all OTHER variables treated as constants.

---

### 2. Higher-Order Partial Derivatives

$$f_{xx} = \frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial x^2}, \quad f_{yy} = \frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial y^2}, \quad f_{xy} = \frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial y\partial x}, \quad f_{yx} = \frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial x\partial y}$$

#### Clairaut's (Schwarz's) Theorem — Critical for GATE!
$$\boxed{f_{xy} = f_{yx}} \quad \text{if } f_{xy} \text{ and } f_{yx} \text{ are both continuous}$$

> Mixed partial derivatives are equal in the order of differentiation — this is true for all smooth functions you will encounter in GATE.

---

### 3. Standard Partial Derivative Examples

| $f(x, y)$ | $f_x$ | $f_y$ |
|---|---|---|
| $x^2 y^3$ | $2xy^3$ | $3x^2 y^2$ |
| $e^{xy}$ | $y e^{xy}$ | $x e^{xy}$ |
| $\sin(xy)$ | $y\cos(xy)$ | $x\cos(xy)$ |
| $\ln(x^2+y^2)$ | $\dfrac{2x}{x^2+y^2}$ | $\dfrac{2y}{x^2+y^2}$ |
| $x^m y^n$ | $mx^{m-1}y^n$ | $nx^m y^{n-1}$ |

## 🧠 Part II: Total Derivative & Chain Rule for Multivariable

### 1. Total Differential

For $z = f(x, y)$:
$$\boxed{dz = \frac{\partial f}{\partial x}\,dx + \frac{\partial f}{\partial y}\,dy = f_x\,dx + f_y\,dy}$$

For $w = f(x, y, z)$:
$$dw = f_x\,dx + f_y\,dy + f_z\,dz$$

---

### 2. Chain Rule for Several Variables

#### Case 1: $z = f(x, y)$, $x = x(t)$, $y = y(t)$ (Total derivative w.r.t. $t$):
$$\boxed{\frac{dz}{dt} = \frac{\partial f}{\partial x}\frac{dx}{dt} + \frac{\partial f}{\partial y}\frac{dy}{dt}}$$

#### Case 2: $z = f(x, y)$, $x = x(s, t)$, $y = y(s, t)$ (Partial derivatives w.r.t. $s$ and $t$):
$$\frac{\partial z}{\partial s} = \frac{\partial f}{\partial x}\frac{\partial x}{\partial s} + \frac{\partial f}{\partial y}\frac{\partial y}{\partial s}, \qquad \frac{\partial z}{\partial t} = \frac{\partial f}{\partial x}\frac{\partial x}{\partial t} + \frac{\partial f}{\partial y}\frac{\partial y}{\partial t}$$

> 🎯 **Tree Diagram Method:** Draw a dependency tree — every path from the top function to the target variable contributes one product; sum all paths.

---

### 3. Euler's Theorem for Homogeneous Functions (GATE Essential!)

$f(x, y)$ is **homogeneous of degree $n$** if:
$$f(tx, ty) = t^n f(x, y) \quad \forall t > 0$$

**Euler's Theorem:**
$$\boxed{x \frac{\partial f}{\partial x} + y \frac{\partial f}{\partial y} = n \cdot f(x, y)}$$

**For three variables:**
$$x f_x + y f_y + z f_z = n \cdot f(x, y, z)$$

**Corollary (Second-Order Euler):**
$$x^2 f_{xx} + 2xy f_{xy} + y^2 f_{yy} = n(n-1) f(x, y)$$

> 🎯 **GATE Pattern:** "If $u = f(x, y)$ is homogeneous of degree $n$, find $x u_x + y u_y$" → Answer: $nu$ immediately!

**Degree Recognition Table:**
| $f(x, y)$ | Degree $n$ |
|---|---|
| $x^2 + 2xy + y^2$ | 2 |
| $\frac{x^3 + y^3}{x + y}$ | 2 |
| $\frac{x^2 y}{x^3 + y^3}$ | 0 |
| $\sqrt{x^2 + y^2}$ | 1 |
| $\sin\!\left(\frac{y}{x}\right)$ | 0 |

## 🧠 Part III: Gradient, Divergence & Curl

> 📖 **Deep-Dive Topic Note:** [[02 - SUBJECTS/Engineering Mathematics/02.2.2 - Gradient|Gradient & Directional Derivative (3 Golden Theorems, Unit Normal, Tangent Plane, Angle Between Surfaces, Directional Derivative Algorithm, Position Vector Identities $\nabla(r^n) = n r^{n-2}\vec{r}$, Aerodynamics & Physics Applications, 4 Speed Tricks)]] `#status/first-read`

### 1. The Del Operator $\nabla$

$$\nabla = \hat{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial x} + \hat{j}\frac{\partial}{\partial y} + \hat{k}\frac{\partial}{\partial z}$$

> 🎯 **$\nabla$ (del/nabla) is the fundamental operator** — three operations are built on it:

### 2. Gradient (grad) — Scalar Field → Vector Field

For scalar function $\phi(x, y, z)$:
$$\boxed{\nabla\phi = \text{grad}\,\phi = \frac{\partial\phi}{\partial x}\hat{i} + \frac{\partial\phi}{\partial y}\hat{j} + \frac{\partial\phi}{\partial z}\hat{k}}$$

**Physical Meaning:** $\nabla\phi$ points in the direction of **steepest increase** of $\phi$, with magnitude = rate of that increase.

**Key Properties:**
1. $\nabla\phi$ is always **perpendicular to the level surface** $\phi = c$.
2. $|\nabla\phi|$ gives the maximum rate of change of $\phi$.
3. $\nabla(fg) = f\nabla g + g\nabla f$ (product rule for gradient).
4. $\nabla(f+g) = \nabla f + \nabla g$ (linearity).

---

### 3. Divergence (div) — Vector Field → Scalar Field

> 📖 **Deep-Dive Topic Note:** [[02 - SUBJECTS/Engineering Mathematics/02.2.3 - Divergence|Divergence & Solenoidal Fields (Physical Meaning, Solenoidal Fields $\nabla\cdot\vec{F}=0$, Aero Continuity Equation, Radial Identities $\nabla\cdot(r^n\vec{r})=(n+3)r^n$, Laplacian $\nabla^2\phi$, Div of Curl Identity $\nabla\cdot(\nabla\times\vec{F})=0$, 4 Speed Tricks)]] `#status/first-read`

For vector field $\vec{F} = F_1\hat{i} + F_2\hat{j} + F_3\hat{k}$:
$$\boxed{\nabla \cdot \vec{F} = \text{div}\,\vec{F} = \frac{\partial F_1}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial F_2}{\partial y} + \frac{\partial F_3}{\partial z}}$$

**Physical Meaning:** Measures the **net outward flux** per unit volume at a point (source/sink strength).
- $\nabla \cdot \vec{F} > 0$ → Source (fluid flowing out)
- $\nabla \cdot \vec{F} < 0$ → Sink (fluid flowing in)
- $\nabla \cdot \vec{F} = 0$ → **Solenoidal field** (incompressible flow — Continuity Equation in Aero!)

---

### 4. Curl — Vector Field → Vector Field

> 📖 **Deep-Dive Topic Note:** [[02 - SUBJECTS/Engineering Mathematics/02.2.4 - Curl|Curl & Irrotational Fields (Physical Meaning, 2D Curl, Irrotational Fields $\nabla\times\vec{F}=\vec{0}$, Conservative Fields $\vec{F}=\nabla\phi$, Vorticity $\vec{\omega}=\nabla\times\vec{V}$, Path Independence & Work Done, Central Force Irrotationality $\nabla\times(f(r)\vec{r})=\vec{0}$, Curl of Grad Identity $\nabla\times(\nabla\phi)=\vec{0}$, 4 Speed Tricks)]] `#status/first-read`

For vector field $\vec{F} = F_1\hat{i} + F_2\hat{j} + F_3\hat{k}$:
$$\boxed{\nabla \times \vec{F} = \text{curl}\,\vec{F} = \begin{vmatrix} \hat{i} & \hat{j} & \hat{k} \\ \dfrac{\partial}{\partial x} & \dfrac{\partial}{\partial y} & \dfrac{\partial}{\partial z} \\ F_1 & F_2 & F_3 \end{vmatrix}}$$

Expanded:
$$= \left(\frac{\partial F_3}{\partial y} - \frac{\partial F_2}{\partial z}\right)\hat{i} - \left(\frac{\partial F_3}{\partial x} - \frac{\partial F_1}{\partial z}\right)\hat{j} + \left(\frac{\partial F_2}{\partial x} - \frac{\partial F_1}{\partial y}\right)\hat{k}$$

**Physical Meaning:** Measures **rotation/vorticity** of the vector field at a point.
- $\nabla \times \vec{F} = \vec{0}$ → **Irrotational field** (potential flow in Aerodynamics!)
- In fluid mechanics: Vorticity $= \nabla \times \vec{V}$

---

### 5. The Laplacian $\nabla^2$

$$\nabla^2\phi = \nabla \cdot (\nabla\phi) = \frac{\partial^2\phi}{\partial x^2} + \frac{\partial^2\phi}{\partial y^2} + \frac{\partial^2\phi}{\partial z^2}$$

- **Laplace Equation:** $\nabla^2\phi = 0$ (harmonic function — irrotational + incompressible flow, electrostatics)
- **Poisson Equation:** $\nabla^2\phi = f(x,y,z)$

---

### 6. Master Identity Table (GATE Direct MCQ Source!)

| Identity | Formula |
|---|---|
| $\nabla \cdot (\nabla\phi)$ | $\nabla^2\phi$ (Laplacian) |
| $\nabla \times (\nabla\phi)$ | $\vec{0}$ (**Curl of gradient = ZERO**) |
| $\nabla \cdot (\nabla \times \vec{F})$ | $0$ (**Divergence of curl = ZERO**) |
| $\nabla \cdot (f\vec{F})$ | $f(\nabla\cdot\vec{F}) + \vec{F}\cdot(\nabla f)$ |
| $\nabla \times (f\vec{F})$ | $f(\nabla\times\vec{F}) + (\nabla f)\times\vec{F}$ |
| $\nabla \cdot (\vec{A}\times\vec{B})$ | $\vec{B}\cdot(\nabla\times\vec{A}) - \vec{A}\cdot(\nabla\times\vec{B})$ |
| $\nabla(fg)$ | $f\nabla g + g\nabla f$ |
| $\nabla^2(fg)$ | $f\nabla^2 g + g\nabla^2 f + 2\nabla f\cdot\nabla g$ |

> 🎯 **Two GATE Gold Rules:**  
> **"Curl of Gradient = $\vec{0}$"** and **"Div of Curl = $0$"**  
> These appear as direct MCQs. Memorize immediately!

## 🧠 Part IV: Directional Derivative

### Definition

The **directional derivative** of $f$ at point $P$ in the direction of unit vector $\hat{u}$:

$$D_{\hat{u}} f = \nabla f \cdot \hat{u} = |\nabla f| \cos\theta$$

where $\theta$ is the angle between $\nabla f$ and $\hat{u}$.

### Key Results:

| Direction | Directional Derivative | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Along $\nabla f$ (i.e., $\hat{u} = \frac{\nabla f}{|\nabla f|}$) | $|\nabla f|$ | **Maximum rate of increase** |
| Against $\nabla f$ | $-|\nabla f|$ | **Maximum rate of decrease** |
| Perpendicular to $\nabla f$ | $0$ | **No change** (along level surface) |

### Algorithm to Find Directional Derivative:
1. Find $\nabla f = f_x\hat{i} + f_y\hat{j} + f_z\hat{k}$ at the given point.
2. Convert the given direction vector $\vec{d}$ to unit vector: $\hat{u} = \frac{\vec{d}}{|\vec{d}|}$.
3. Compute $D_{\hat{u}}f = \nabla f \cdot \hat{u}$.

## 🧠 Part V: Jacobian (The Transformation Tool)

### Definition for Two Variables:

For transformation $x = x(u,v)$, $y = y(u,v)$:
$$J = \frac{\partial(x, y)}{\partial(u, v)} = \begin{vmatrix} \dfrac{\partial x}{\partial u} & \dfrac{\partial x}{\partial v} \\[8pt] \dfrac{\partial y}{\partial u} & \dfrac{\partial y}{\partial v} \end{vmatrix} = \frac{\partial x}{\partial u}\frac{\partial y}{\partial v} - \frac{\partial x}{\partial v}\frac{\partial y}{\partial u}$$

### For Three Variables:
$$J = \frac{\partial(x,y,z)}{\partial(u,v,w)} = \begin{vmatrix} x_u & x_v & x_w \\ y_u & y_v & y_w \\ z_u & z_v & z_w \end{vmatrix}$$

### Key Properties of Jacobians:

1. **Chain Rule for Jacobians:**
   $$\frac{\partial(x,y)}{\partial(s,t)} = \frac{\partial(x,y)}{\partial(u,v)} \cdot \frac{\partial(u,v)}{\partial(s,t)}$$

2. **Inverse Jacobian:**
   $$\frac{\partial(x,y)}{\partial(u,v)} \cdot \frac{\partial(u,v)}{\partial(x,y)} = 1$$

3. **Area/Volume Transformation:**
   $$dx\,dy = \left|J\right| du\,dv$$

### Common Jacobians to Memorize:

| Transformation | $J$ |
|---|---|
| Polar: $x=r\cos\theta$, $y=r\sin\theta$ | $r$ (so $dx\,dy = r\,dr\,d\theta$) |
| Cylindrical: $x=r\cos\theta$, $y=r\sin\theta$, $z=z$ | $r$ |
| Spherical: $x=\rho\sin\phi\cos\theta$, $y=\rho\sin\phi\sin\theta$, $z=\rho\cos\phi$ | $\rho^2\sin\phi$ |

## 🧠 Part VI: Maxima & Minima of Functions of Two Variables

### Stationary Points — Finding:
$$f_x = 0 \quad \text{AND} \quad f_y = 0 \implies \text{Stationary point } (a, b)$$

### Second Derivative Test (SDT) for $f(x, y)$:

Compute the **discriminant** $D$ (Hessian determinant):
$$\boxed{D = f_{xx}f_{yy} - (f_{xy})^2}$$

| $D$ | $f_{xx}$ | Conclusion |
|---|---|---|
| $D > 0$ | $f_{xx} > 0$ | **Local Minimum** at $(a, b)$ |
| $D > 0$ | $f_{xx} < 0$ | **Local Maximum** at $(a, b)$ |
| $D < 0$ | Any | **Saddle Point** at $(a, b)$ |
| $D = 0$ | — | **Inconclusive** (test fails) |

> 🎯 **Mnemonic:** **"$D > 0$ = Definite (Max or Min); $D < 0$ = Definitely a Saddle"**

---

### Lagrange Multipliers (Constrained Optimization)

**Problem:** Maximize/minimize $f(x, y)$ subject to constraint $g(x, y) = 0$.

**Method:** Solve the system:
$$\nabla f = \lambda \nabla g \quad \text{AND} \quad g(x, y) = 0$$

Explicitly:
$$f_x = \lambda g_x, \quad f_y = \lambda g_y, \quad g(x,y) = 0$$

> 🎯 **Physical Meaning:** At the optimum, the level curves of $f$ and $g$ are tangent — their gradients are parallel (one is a scalar multiple of the other).

## ⚡ AIR-1 Topper Speed Tricks & Mnemonics

### 🚀 Trick 1: Euler's Theorem — Instant Answer for GATE MCQs
"If $u = f(x, y)$ is homogeneous of degree $n$, find $x\frac{\partial u}{\partial x} + y\frac{\partial u}{\partial y}$"  
→ Answer: $\mathbf{nu}$ immediately. No computation!

**Extension:** For $u = \ln f$ where $f$ is homogeneous of degree $n$:
$$x u_x + y u_y = n \quad \text{(degree of } f \text{, NOT of } \ln f\text{)}$$

---

### 🚀 Trick 2: Two-Second Solenoidal / Irrotational Check
- **Solenoidal:** $\nabla\cdot\vec{F} = 0$ → Compute divergence, check if zero.
- **Irrotational:** $\nabla\times\vec{F} = \vec{0}$ → Compute curl, check if zero vector.
- **Conservative field:** $\vec{F}$ is irrotational → $\vec{F} = \nabla\phi$ for some scalar potential $\phi$.

---

### 🚀 Trick 3: Gradient Direction Eliminates Options in 5 Seconds
"In which direction does $f(x,y,z) = xyz$ increase most rapidly at $(1,1,1)$?"
1. $\nabla f = (yz, xz, xy)$ at $(1,1,1) = (1,1,1)$
2. Unit vector: $\hat{u} = \frac{(1,1,1)}{\sqrt{3}}$
→ Scan options for $\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}(1,1,1)$ — done in 5 seconds!

---

### 🚀 Trick 4: Jacobian via Polar/Spherical — Memorize Three Values
| System | Jacobian |
|---|---|
| Polar 2D | $r$ |
| Cylindrical 3D | $r$ |
| Spherical 3D | $\rho^2\sin\phi$ |
These appear directly as MCQ options in area/volume change-of-variable GATE problems.

---

### 🚀 Trick 5: Hessian Saddle-Point Recognition
"$D = f_{xx}f_{yy} - f_{xy}^2 < 0$" → **Saddle Point** (always — regardless of $f_{xx}$ sign).
The most common GATE answer is saddle points for "tricky-looking" functions!

---

### 🚀 Trick 6: Partial Derivative Computation Shortcut — Treat as Univariate
For $f(x, y) = g(x) \cdot h(y)$:
$$f_x = g'(x) \cdot h(y), \quad f_y = g(x) \cdot h'(y) \quad \text{instantly!}$$

For $f(x,y) = g(h(x,y))$ (composition):
$$f_x = g'(h) \cdot h_x, \quad f_y = g'(h) \cdot h_y \quad \text{(chain rule)}$$

## 📝 Step-by-Step Solved GATE Examples

### Example 1: Partial Derivatives + Euler's Theorem
**Problem:** If $u = x^2\tan^{-1}(y/x) - y^2\tan^{-1}(x/y)$, find $x u_x + y u_y$.

**Solution:**  
Check homogeneity: Each term has $x^2, y^2$ factors with $\tan^{-1}(y/x)$ (degree 0). So $u$ is homogeneous of degree **2**.  
By Euler's Theorem:
$$x u_x + y u_y = 2u \quad \boxed{= 2\left[x^2\tan^{-1}\frac{y}{x} - y^2\tan^{-1}\frac{x}{y}\right]}$$

---

### Example 2: Gradient & Directional Derivative
**Problem:** Find the directional derivative of $f = x^2 + y^2 + z^2$ at $(1, 2, 3)$ in the direction of $\vec{d} = \hat{i} + \hat{j} + \hat{k}$.

**Solution:**
$$\nabla f = 2x\hat{i} + 2y\hat{j} + 2z\hat{k} \big|_{(1,2,3)} = 2\hat{i} + 4\hat{j} + 6\hat{k}$$
$$\hat{u} = \frac{\hat{i}+\hat{j}+\hat{k}}{\sqrt{3}}$$
$$D_{\hat{u}}f = \nabla f \cdot \hat{u} = \frac{2+4+6}{\sqrt{3}} = \frac{12}{\sqrt{3}} = \boxed{4\sqrt{3}}$$

---

### Example 3: Divergence & Curl
**Problem:** For $\vec{F} = (x^2y)\hat{i} + (xyz)\hat{j} + (z^2x)\hat{k}$, find $\nabla\cdot\vec{F}$ and $\nabla\times\vec{F}$.

**Solution:**
$$\nabla\cdot\vec{F} = \frac{\partial}{\partial x}(x^2y) + \frac{\partial}{\partial y}(xyz) + \frac{\partial}{\partial z}(z^2x) = 2xy + xz + 2zx = 2xy + 3xz$$

$$\nabla\times\vec{F} = \begin{vmatrix}\hat{i} & \hat{j} & \hat{k} \\ \partial_x & \partial_y & \partial_z \\ x^2y & xyz & z^2x\end{vmatrix}$$
$$= \hat{i}(0-xy) - \hat{j}(z^2-0) + \hat{k}(yz-x^2) = \boxed{-xy\hat{i} - z^2\hat{j} + (yz-x^2)\hat{k}}$$

---

### Example 4: Maxima/Minima of $f(x,y)$
**Problem:** Find and classify the stationary points of $f(x,y) = x^3 + y^3 - 3xy$.

**Solution:**
$$f_x = 3x^2 - 3y = 0 \implies y = x^2$$
$$f_y = 3y^2 - 3x = 0 \implies y^2 = x$$
From $y = x^2$: $(x^2)^2 = x \implies x^4 = x \implies x(x^3-1) = 0 \implies x = 0 \text{ or } x = 1$

Points: $(0, 0)$ and $(1, 1)$

Compute: $f_{xx} = 6x$, $f_{yy} = 6y$, $f_{xy} = -3$

| Point | $f_{xx}$ | $f_{yy}$ | $f_{xy}$ | $D = f_{xx}f_{yy} - f_{xy}^2$ | Conclusion |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $(0,0)$ | $0$ | $0$ | $-3$ | $0-9 = -9 < 0$ | **Saddle Point** |
| $(1,1)$ | $6$ | $6$ | $-3$ | $36-9 = 27 > 0$ | **Local Min** ($f_{xx}>0$) |

$$\boxed{(0,0)\text{ is a saddle point; }(1,1)\text{ is a local minimum with }f(1,1) = 1+1-3 = -1}$$

---

### Example 5: Lagrange Multipliers
**Problem:** Find the minimum distance from the origin to the plane $x + 2y + 3z = 14$.

**Solution:**  
Minimize $f = x^2 + y^2 + z^2$ subject to $g = x + 2y + 3z - 14 = 0$.

$\nabla f = \lambda \nabla g$:
$$2x = \lambda, \quad 2y = 2\lambda, \quad 2z = 3\lambda$$
So $x = \lambda/2$, $y = \lambda$, $z = 3\lambda/2$.

Substitute into constraint: $\frac{\lambda}{2} + 2\lambda + \frac{9\lambda}{2} = 14 \implies 7\lambda = 14 \implies \lambda = 2$

$x = 1$, $y = 2$, $z = 3$. Min distance $= \sqrt{1+4+9} = \boxed{\sqrt{14}}$

---

### Example 6: Jacobian — Polar Coordinates
**Problem:** Find the Jacobian of transformation $x = r\cos\theta$, $y = r\sin\theta$.

**Solution:**
$$J = \begin{vmatrix} \cos\theta & -r\sin\theta \\ \sin\theta & r\cos\theta \end{vmatrix} = r\cos^2\theta + r\sin^2\theta = \boxed{r}$$

## ⚠️ Common Exam Traps & Pitfalls

> 🔴 **Trap 1: Treating Partial Derivative Like an Ordinary Derivative**  
> When computing $\frac{\partial}{\partial x}[x^2 + y^2]$, DO NOT differentiate $y^2$ → result is $2x$ (NOT $2x + 2y$).  
> $y$ is a constant in $\frac{\partial}{\partial x}$!

> 🔴 **Trap 2: Curl of Gradient ≠ Always Checked**  
> GATE MCQ: "Is $\vec{F} = \nabla\phi$ possible for this $\vec{F}$?"  
> Check $\nabla\times\vec{F} = \vec{0}$ first — if not zero, $\vec{F}$ is NOT a gradient field.

> 🔴 **Trap 3: Homogeneity Degree Error**  
> *Mistake:* $f = \frac{x^3+y^3}{x+y}$ → degree = 3 ❌  
> *Correction:* Degree = 3 − 1 = **2** ✅ (subtract denominator degree from numerator degree)

> 🔴 **Trap 4: Saddle Point vs Min/Max — D vs $f_{xx}$ Confusion**  
> $D > 0$ alone is NOT enough — you ALSO need $f_{xx} > 0$ (min) or $f_{xx} < 0$ (max).  
> $D < 0$ → Saddle point (regardless of $f_{xx}$).

> 🔴 **Trap 5: Jacobian — Row/Column Order**  
> $J = \frac{\partial(x,y)}{\partial(u,v)}$ has $(x,y)$ in numerator, $(u,v)$ in denominator.  
> Rows = $x$-row (partial of $x$ w.r.t. $u$ and $v$) and $y$-row — NOT the reverse!

| Polar Jacobian | $J = r$ | $dx\,dy = r\,dr\,d\theta$ |
| Spherical Jacobian | $J = \rho^2\sin\phi$ | $dx\,dy\,dz = \rho^2\sin\phi\,d\rho\,d\phi\,d\theta$ |
| Hessian $D$ | $D = f_{xx}f_{yy} - (f_{xy})^2$ | $D>0$: extremum; $D<0$: saddle |
| Lagrange Multipliers | $\nabla f = \lambda\nabla g$ + constraint | Constrained optimization |

## 🧠 Part VII: Line Integrals & Green's Theorem

> 📖 **Deep-Dive Topic Note:** [[02 - SUBJECTS/Engineering Mathematics/02.2.5 - Line Integrals|Line Integrals (Scalar vs Vector Line Integrals, Work Done & Aerodynamic Circulation $\Gamma$, Standard Parametrizations Table, Fundamental Theorem of Line Integrals & Path Independence, Green's Theorem in Plane, Green's Area Formula, 4 Speed Tricks)]] `#status/first-read`

- **Scalar Line Integral:** $\int_C f\,ds = \int_a^b f(x(t), y(t), z(t)) \sqrt{\dot{x}^2+\dot{y}^2+\dot{z}^2}\,dt$
- **Vector Line Integral (Work/Circulation):** $\int_C \vec{F} \cdot d\vec{r} = \int_C (P\,dx + Q\,dy + R\,dz)$
- **Path Independence:** $\int_A^B \vec{F} \cdot d\vec{r} = \phi(B) - \phi(A) \iff \nabla \times \vec{F} = \vec{0}$
- **Green's Theorem (2D Closed Loop):** $\oint_C (P\,dx + Q\,dy) = \iint_D \left(\frac{\partial Q}{\partial x} - \frac{\partial P}{\partial y}\right) dx\,dy$
- **Green's Area Formula:** $\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2}\oint_C (x\,dy - y\,dx) = \oint_C x\,dy$

## 🧠 Part VIII: Surface Integrals & Vector Flux

> 📖 **Deep-Dive Topic Note:** [[02 - SUBJECTS/Engineering Mathematics/02.2.6 - Surface Integrals|Surface Integrals (Scalar vs Vector Flux Integrals, Projection Formulas, Differential Area $dS$, Standard Surface Area Table, Closed Surface Flux & Gauss Divergence Connection, 4 Speed Tricks)]] `#status/first-read`

- **Scalar Surface Integral (Surface Area / Shell Mass):** $\iint_S f\,dS = \iint_{R_{xy}} f(x,y,g(x,y))\sqrt{1+g_x^2+g_y^2}\,dx\,dy$
- **Vector Surface Integral (Fluid/Heat/Field Flux):** $\text{Flux} = \iint_S \vec{F} \cdot \hat{n}\,dS$
- **Explicit Surface Projection Formula:** $\text{Flux} = \iint_{R_{xy}} \left( -F_1 \frac{\partial z}{\partial x} - F_2 \frac{\partial z}{\partial y} + F_3 \right) dx\,dy$
- **Position Vector Sphere Flux:** $\iint_S \vec{r} \cdot \hat{n}\,dS = 3 \times \text{Volume} = 4\pi R^3$
- **Closed Surface Flux (Gauss Shortcut):** $\iint_S \vec{F} \cdot \hat{n}\,dS = \iiint_V (\nabla \cdot \vec{F})\,dV$

## 🧠 Part IX: Volume Integrals & 3D Coordinate Systems

> 📖 **Deep-Dive Topic Note:** [[02 - SUBJECTS/Engineering Mathematics/02.2.7 - Volume Integrals|Volume Integrals (Scalar vs Vector Volume Integrals, 3 Coordinate Systems: Cartesian/Cylindrical/Spherical, Dirichlet's & Liouville's Formulas, Ellipsoid & Tetrahedron Shortcuts, 4 Speed Tricks)]] `#status/first-read`

- **Scalar Volume Integral:** $\iiint_V f(x,y,z)\,dV$ (Solid mass $M = \iiint_V \rho\,dV$, Volume $V = \iiint_V 1\,dV$, Center of Mass)
- **Cartesian Element:** $dV = dx\,dy\,dz$
- **Cylindrical Element:** $dV = r\,dr\,d\theta\,dz$ (Jacobian $J = r$)
- **Spherical Element:** $dV = \rho^2\sin\phi\,d\rho\,d\phi\,d\theta$ (Jacobian $J = \rho^2\sin\phi$)
- **Dirichlet's Formula:** $\iiint_{x+y+z \le 1} x^{l-1} y^{m-1} z^{n-1}\,dV = \frac{\Gamma(l)\Gamma(m)\Gamma(n)}{\Gamma(l+m+n+1)}$
- **Ellipsoid Volume:** $\text{Volume} = \frac{4}{3}\pi a b c$; **Tetrahedron Volume:** $\text{Volume} = \frac{a b c}{6}$

## 🧠 Part X: Integral Theorems — Stokes' Theorem

> 📖 **Deep-Dive Topic Note:** [[02 - SUBJECTS/Engineering Mathematics/02.2.8 - Stokes Theorem|Stokes' Theorem (Line vs Surface Curl Integrals, Aerodynamic Circulation $\Gamma$, Green's 2D Special Case, Surface Independence & Flat Disk Replacement Trick, Closed Surface Zero Flux Rule, 4 Speed Tricks)]] `#status/first-read`

- **Stokes' Theorem Statement:** $\oint_C \vec{F} \cdot d\vec{r} = \iint_S (\nabla \times \vec{F}) \cdot \hat{n}\,dS$
- **Aerodynamic Circulation:** $\Gamma = \oint_C \vec{V} \cdot d\vec{r} = \iint_S (\nabla \times \vec{V}) \cdot \hat{n}\,dS$
- **Surface Independence:** $\iint_{S_1} (\nabla \times \vec{F}) \cdot \hat{n}_1\,dS = \iint_{S_2} (\nabla \times \vec{F}) \cdot \hat{n}_2\,dS$ for any surfaces with same boundary $C$.
- **Flat Disk Replacement:** Replace complex curved surface $S_1$ with flat disk $S_2$ ($\hat{n} = \hat{k}$).
- **Closed Surface Corollary:** $\iint_S (\nabla \times \vec{F}) \cdot \hat{n}\,dS = 0$ for any closed surface $S$.

## 🧠 Part XI: Integral Theorems — Gauss's Divergence Theorem

> 📖 **Deep-Dive Topic Note:** [[02 - SUBJECTS/Engineering Mathematics/02.2.9 - Gauss Divergence Theorem|Gauss's Divergence Theorem (Surface Flux ↔ Volume Divergence, Solenoidal Zero-Flux, Position Vector Flux $4\pi R^3$, Open Surface Split Trick, Green's Identities, 4 Speed Tricks, 4 Solved GATE Examples)]] `#status/first-read`

- **Gauss's Theorem:** $\oiint_S \vec{F}\cdot\hat{n}\,dS = \iiint_V (\nabla\cdot\vec{F})\,dV$
- **Constant Divergence Shortcut:** $\nabla\cdot\vec{F} = k \implies \text{Flux} = k \times \text{Volume}(V)$
- **Position Vector Flux:** $\oiint_S \vec{r}\cdot\hat{n}\,dS = 3V$ (Sphere: $= 4\pi R^3$)
- **Solenoidal / Curl Field:** $\nabla\cdot\vec{F}=0 \implies \text{Flux}=0$ (Instant!)
- **Open Surface Split:** Flux$_{S_1}$ = $\iiint_V \nabla\cdot\vec{F}\,dV$ − Flux$_{S_2}$ (cap)

## 🧠 Part XII: Integral Theorems — Green's Theorem

> 📖 **Deep-Dive Topic Note:** [[02 - SUBJECTS/Engineering Mathematics/02.2.10 - Greens Theorem|Green's Theorem (Circulation-Curl Form, Flux-Divergence Form, Area Formula $\frac{1}{2}\oint(x\,dy-y\,dx)$, Ellipse Area $\pi ab$, Multiply-Connected Regions, 4 Speed Tricks, 4 Solved GATE Examples)]] `#status/first-read`

- **Circulation-Curl Form:** $\oint_C P\,dx + Q\,dy = \iint_D (Q_x - P_y)\,dA$
- **Flux-Divergence Form:** $\oint_C \vec{F}\cdot\hat{n}\,ds = \iint_D (\nabla\cdot\vec{F})\,dA$
- **Green's Area Formula:** $\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2}\oint_C (x\,dy - y\,dx) = \oint_C x\,dy = -\oint_C y\,dx$
- **Ellipse Area Shortcut:** $\text{Area} = \pi ab$ (semi-axes $a$ and $b$)
- **Exact Differential:** $Q_x = P_y \implies \oint_C P\,dx + Q\,dy = 0$
- **Constant Curl Shortcut:** $Q_x - P_y = k \implies \oint_C = k \times \text{Area}(D)$

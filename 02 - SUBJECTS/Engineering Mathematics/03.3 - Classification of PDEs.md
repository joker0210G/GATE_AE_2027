## 📌 Part I: What is a PDE?

A **Partial Differential Equation (PDE)** involves an unknown function of **two or more independent variables** and its **partial derivatives**.

**General 2nd Order Linear PDE in two variables $x$ and $y$:**

$$\boxed{A\frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial x^2} + B\frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial x\partial y} + C\frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial y^2} + D\frac{\partial u}{\partial x} + E\frac{\partial u}{\partial y} + Fu = G}$$

**Short notation:** $Au_{xx} + Bu_{xy} + Cu_{yy} + Du_x + Eu_y + Fu = G$

where $A, B, C, D, E, F, G$ may be functions of $x$ and $y$ (but NOT of $u$ or its derivatives for a **linear** PDE).

---

## 🧠 Part II: The Discriminant — Master Classification Formula

### The Discriminant $\Delta$

$$\boxed{\Delta = B^2 - 4AC}$$

### The Three Classes

| Discriminant | Classification | Physical Analogy |
|---|---|---|
| $B^2 - 4AC < 0$ | **Elliptic** | Steady-state (no time evolution) |
| $B^2 - 4AC = 0$ | **Parabolic** | Diffusion / transient (one-directional time) |
| $B^2 - 4AC > 0$ | **Hyperbolic** | Wave propagation (two-directional time) |

### 🧠 Mnemonic: "**EEP → EHP**" (Ellipse-Parabola-Hyperbola)

Think of the **conic sections** — the classification of PDEs mirrors the geometry of conics:

$$Ax^2 + Bxy + Cy^2 + \ldots = 0 \quad \text{(conic section)}$$

| Conic | Condition | PDE Class |
|---|---|---|
| **Ellipse** | $B^2 - 4AC < 0$ | Elliptic PDE |
| **Parabola** | $B^2 - 4AC = 0$ | Parabolic PDE |
| **Hyperbola** | $B^2 - 4AC > 0$ | Hyperbolic PDE |

**Memory:** "**E**llipse **E**liminates time, **P**arabola **P**ropagates heat, **H**yperbola **H**urls waves!" 🚀

---

## 🧠 Part III: The Three Canonical PDE Types

### 1. 🔵 Elliptic PDEs — Steady State, Boundary Value Problems

**Discriminant:** $B^2 - 4AC < 0$

**Prototype Equation: Laplace's Equation**
$$\boxed{\nabla^2 u = \frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial x^2} + \frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial y^2} = 0}$$

Here: $A=1, B=0, C=1 \implies B^2-4AC = 0-4 = -4 < 0$ ✓ **Elliptic**

**Poisson's Equation** (non-homogeneous Laplace):
$$\nabla^2 u = f(x,y)$$

**Physical Meaning:**
- Describes **steady-state** phenomena where time has no role
- Solution gives the equilibrium distribution
- No "direction of time" — information propagates **in all directions simultaneously**

**Aerospace Applications:**
- Steady irrotational flow potential: $\nabla^2\phi = 0$ (incompressible potential flow)
- Steady temperature distribution in a solid: $\nabla^2 T = 0$
- Electrostatic potential in a charge-free region

**Boundary Conditions:**
- Dirichlet: $u$ specified on boundary
- Neumann: $\partial u/\partial n$ (normal derivative) specified on boundary
- Mixed: combination of both

---

### 2. 🟡 Parabolic PDEs — Diffusion, Transient Problems

**Discriminant:** $B^2 - 4AC = 0$

**Prototype Equation: Heat (Diffusion) Equation**
$$\boxed{\frac{\partial u}{\partial t} = \alpha^2 \frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial x^2}}$$

Rewrite as PDE in $(x,t)$: $A = \alpha^2$, $B = 0$, $C = 0$  
$B^2 - 4AC = 0 - 0 = 0$ ✓ **Parabolic**

**Physical Meaning:**
- Describes **diffusion / transient** phenomena
- One preferred direction of time: solution advances forward in time
- Information propagates with **infinite speed** (instantaneous spatial effect)
- Solution smooths out over time → tends toward steady state (Laplace's equation as $t \to \infty$)

**Aerospace Applications:**
- Transient heat conduction in aircraft skin
- Viscous boundary layer development (Prandtl's boundary layer equation is parabolic!)
- Diffusion of pollutants/fuel in a combustion chamber
- Unsteady mass diffusion

**Boundary + Initial Conditions:**
- Initial condition: $u(x,0) = f(x)$ (temperature distribution at $t=0$)
- Boundary conditions: $u$ or $\partial u/\partial n$ at the spatial boundaries

---

### 3. 🔴 Hyperbolic PDEs — Wave Propagation

**Discriminant:** $B^2 - 4AC > 0$

**Prototype Equation: Wave Equation**
$$\boxed{\frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial t^2} = c^2 \frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial x^2}}$$

Rewrite in $(x,t)$: $A = c^2$, $B = 0$, $C = -1$  
$B^2 - 4AC = 0 - 4(c^2)(-1) = 4c^2 > 0$ ✓ **Hyperbolic**

**Physical Meaning:**
- Describes **wave propagation** phenomena
- Two real **characteristics** (directions along which information travels)
- Information propagates at **finite speed** $c$
- No dissipation — wave energy is conserved (unlike parabolic)
- Initial disturbances persist forever (unlike parabolic which smooths out)

**Aerospace Applications:**
- Acoustic wave propagation in ducts
- Shock wave / expansion wave propagation in supersonic flow
- Structural vibrations of aircraft panels
- Pressure waves in compressible flow (speed of sound $c = \sqrt{\gamma RT}$)

**Boundary + Initial Conditions:**
- Two initial conditions: $u(x,0) = f(x)$ and $\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}(x,0) = g(x)$

---

## 🧠 Part IV: Classification of Variable-Coefficient PDEs

For PDEs where $A$, $B$, $C$ are functions of $x$ and $y$, the **classification can change from point to point** in the domain.

**Example: Tricomi's Equation** $y\,u_{xx} + u_{yy} = 0$
- $A = y$, $B = 0$, $C = 1 \implies \Delta = 0 - 4y$
  - $y > 0$: $\Delta = -4y < 0$ → **Elliptic**
  - $y = 0$: $\Delta = 0$ → **Parabolic** (the line $y=0$ is parabolic!)
  - $y < 0$: $\Delta = -4y > 0$ → **Hyperbolic**

This is famous in **transonic aerodynamics** — the equation changes type at the sonic line ($y = 0$), modelling the transition from subsonic (elliptic) to supersonic (hyperbolic) flow!

---

## 🧠 Part V: Order & Linearity of PDEs

### Order
The order of a PDE is the **highest order partial derivative** that appears.

### Linearity
A PDE is **linear** if the unknown $u$ and ALL its derivatives appear **linearly** (no products, no powers, no functions of $u$).

| PDE | Linear? | Order |
|---|---|---|
| $u_{xx} + u_{yy} = 0$ | ✅ Linear | 2nd |
| $u u_{xx} + u_{yy} = 0$ | ❌ Non-linear ($u \cdot u_{xx}$) | 2nd |
| $u_{xx} + u^2 = 0$ | ❌ Non-linear ($u^2$) | 2nd |
| $u_x + u_y = u$ | ✅ Linear | 1st |
| $(u_x)^2 + u_{yy} = 0$ | ❌ Non-linear ($(u_x)^2$) | 2nd |

### Quasi-linear PDE
A PDE is **quasi-linear** if $A$, $B$, $C$ (coefficients of highest-order derivatives) may depend on $u$ and its lower-order derivatives, but the highest-order terms appear linearly.

## ⚡ AIR-1 Speed Tricks & Mnemonics

### 🚀 Trick 1: The Discriminant Formula — 3-Second Classification
**Given any 2nd order linear PDE, immediately extract $A$, $B$, $C$ and compute $B^2 - 4AC$:**

*Example:* $u_{xx} - 4u_{xy} + 4u_{yy} = 0$  
$A=1, B=-4, C=4 \implies \Delta = 16 - 16 = 0$ → **Parabolic!** (3 seconds!)

---

### 🚀 Trick 2: Standard Equations — Classify by Memory
Memorize these directly — they appear EVERY year in GATE:

| Equation | $A,B,C$ | $\Delta$ | Class |
|---|---|---|---|
| $u_{xx}+u_{yy}=0$ (Laplace) | $1,0,1$ | $-4$ | **Elliptic** |
| $u_{xx}-u_{yy}=0$ (Wave) | $1,0,-1$ | $4$ | **Hyperbolic** |
| $u_t = u_{xx}$ (Heat) | $1,0,0$ | $0$ | **Parabolic** |
| $u_{tt} = c^2 u_{xx}$ (Wave) | $c^2,0,-1$ | $4c^2$ | **Hyperbolic** |
| $u_{xx}+u_{yy}+u_{zz}=0$ (3D Laplace) | Elliptic | — | **Elliptic** |

---

### 🚀 Trick 3: GATE Pattern — "What are the BCs?"
Exam questions often ask to identify type AND appropriate BCs:
- **Elliptic** → **BVP** (Boundary Value Problem) — conditions on the entire closed boundary
- **Parabolic** → **IBVP** (Initial + Boundary Value Problem) — IC at $t=0$ + spatial BCs
- **Hyperbolic** → **IVP+BVP** — TWO initial conditions + spatial BCs

---

### 🧠 Mnemonic: "**EPH — Every Physics Has**"
- **E**lliptic → **E**quilibrium (steady-state)
- **P**arabolic → **P**ropagation (diffusion, one-way time)
- **H**yperbolic → **H**igh speed (waves, two characteristics)

---

### 🧠 Mnemonic: Sign of $\Delta = B^2-4AC$
Think of the quadratic formula: $b^2-4ac$ from high school!
- **Negative** ($<0$): complex roots → no real crossing → like an **E**llipse (closed curve)
- **Zero** ($=0$): one real root → touches but doesn't cross → like a **P**arabola (tangent)
- **Positive** ($>0$): two real roots → two crossings → like a **H**yperbola (open curve)

## 📝 Solved GATE Examples

### Example 1: Direct Classification (GATE 1-marker)
**Problem:** Classify the PDE: $3u_{xx} - 7u_{xy} + 5u_{yy} - u_x + 3u = 0$.

**Solution:**
$A=3$, $B=-7$, $C=5$
$$\Delta = B^2 - 4AC = 49 - 4(3)(5) = 49 - 60 = -11 < 0$$

$$\boxed{\text{Elliptic PDE}}$$

---

### Example 2: Mixed-Coefficient PDE (GATE 2-marker)
**Problem:** Classify $u_{xx} + 2xu_{xy} + x^2 u_{yy} = 0$.

**Solution:**
$A=1$, $B=2x$, $C=x^2$
$$\Delta = (2x)^2 - 4(1)(x^2) = 4x^2 - 4x^2 = 0$$

The discriminant is **zero for ALL values of $x$!**

$$\boxed{\text{Parabolic PDE (everywhere)}}$$

---

### Example 3: Wave-Like ODE (GATE-Type MCQ)
**Problem:** Which of the following correctly classifies $\frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial t^2} = 9\frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial x^2}$?

**(a) Elliptic   (b) Parabolic   (c) Hyperbolic   (d) None of these**

**Solution (Speed Trick 2):**
This is the **wave equation** with $c=3$. Treat $t$ as the second variable.
$A=9$ (coefficient of $u_{xx}$), $B=0$, $C=-1$ (coefficient of $u_{tt}$, with $t$ playing role of $y$)
$$\Delta = 0 - 4(9)(-1) = 36 > 0$$

$$\boxed{(c) \text{ Hyperbolic}}$$

---

### Example 4: Variable Coefficient (GATE 2-marker)
**Problem:** Determine the region in the $xy$-plane where $u_{xx} - 2xy\,u_{xy} - 3x^2 u_{yy} = 0$ is hyperbolic.

**Solution:**
$A=1$, $B=-2xy$, $C=-3x^2$
$$\Delta = (-2xy)^2 - 4(1)(-3x^2) = 4x^2y^2 + 12x^2 = 4x^2(y^2+3)$$

Since $y^2 + 3 > 0$ always, $\Delta = 4x^2(y^2+3) \ge 0$:
- $\Delta > 0$ when $x \ne 0$ → **Hyperbolic**
- $\Delta = 0$ when $x = 0$ → **Parabolic** (on the $y$-axis)

$$\boxed{\text{Hyperbolic for } x \ne 0; \text{ Parabolic on } x=0}$$

## 📐 Master Comparison Table: Three PDE Classes

| Feature | Elliptic | Parabolic | Hyperbolic |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Discriminant** | $B^2-4AC < 0$ | $B^2-4AC = 0$ | $B^2-4AC > 0$ |
| **Prototype** | Laplace: $\nabla^2 u = 0$ | Heat: $u_t = \alpha^2 u_{xx}$ | Wave: $u_{tt} = c^2 u_{xx}$ |
| **Characteristics** | None (complex) | 1 real family | 2 real families |
| **Problem type** | BVP | IBVP | IBVP |
| **ICs needed** | 0 | 1 | 2 |
| **Signal speed** | — (instantaneous) | Infinite | Finite ($c$) |
| **Behavior** | Smooth, equilibrium | Smoothing over time | Oscillatory, persistent |
| **Aero example** | Potential flow | Boundary layer, Heat | Shock waves, Sound |
| **Mnemonic** | **E**quilibrium | **P**ropagates (diffuses) | **H**urls waves |

## ⚠️ Common Exam Traps & Pitfalls

> 🔴 **Trap 1: Forgetting the Factor of 4 in Discriminant**  
> The discriminant is $B^2 - \mathbf{4}AC$, NOT $B^2 - AC$. Missing the factor of 4 is the single most common error. The analogy to the quadratic formula $b^2-4ac$ helps remember.

> 🔴 **Trap 2: Sign Convention for Cross-Derivative Term**  
> In $Au_{xx} + Bu_{xy} + Cu_{yy}$, the coefficient $B$ is for the **mixed** term $u_{xy}$. Some textbooks write $2Bu_{xy}$ — in that case, use $\Delta = (2B)^2 - 4AC = 4B^2 - 4AC$. Always check your textbook's convention!

> 🔴 **Trap 3: Variable Coefficients — Classification Varies with Location**  
> For variable-coefficient PDEs, compute $\Delta(x,y)$ and state the classification **as a function of the point $(x,y)$**. Don't assume it's the same everywhere!

> 🔴 **Trap 4: Mixed/First-Order Terms Don't Affect Classification**  
> The terms $Du_x$, $Eu_y$, $Fu$, and $G$ (first-order and zero-order terms) do **NOT** appear in the discriminant. Only $A$, $B$, $C$ (coefficients of second-order terms) determine classification.

## 📐 Quick-Fire Formula Sheet

$$\text{General 2nd Order PDE: } Au_{xx} + Bu_{xy} + Cu_{yy} + Du_x + Eu_y + Fu = G$$

$$\text{Discriminant: } \Delta = B^2 - 4AC$$

$$\Delta < 0 \implies \text{Elliptic} \quad (\text{e.g., Laplace } \nabla^2 u = 0)$$

$$\Delta = 0 \implies \text{Parabolic} \quad (\text{e.g., Heat } u_t = \alpha^2 u_{xx})$$

$$\Delta > 0 \implies \text{Hyperbolic} \quad (\text{e.g., Wave } u_{tt} = c^2 u_{xx})$$

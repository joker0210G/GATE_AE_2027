> 🎯 **GATE Weightage:** 1–2 marks per paper (Special Topic). Fourier series decomposes ANY periodic function into sums of sines and cosines. It is the mathematical foundation of **vibration analysis, signal processing, and PDE solving** — directly connected to the Wave Equation and Heat Equation notes you've already mastered. Understanding Fourier series makes PDE boundary-value problems trivial!

---

## 📌 Part I: Periodic Functions — Foundation

### What Is a Periodic Function?
$f(x)$ is **periodic with period $T$** if:
$$f(x + T) = f(x) \quad \text{for all } x$$

The **fundamental period** is the smallest positive $T$ satisfying this.

| Function | Period |
|---|---|
| $\sin(x)$, $\cos(x)$ | $2\pi$ |
| $\sin(2x)$, $\cos(2x)$ | $\pi$ |
| $\sin(nx)$, $\cos(nx)$ | $\frac{2\pi}{n}$ |
| Square wave, sawtooth, triangle wave | Depends on definition |

---

### Dirichlet Conditions (Existence of Fourier Series)
A Fourier series converges for $f(x)$ if over one period:
1. $f(x)$ is **single-valued** (no multi-valued branches)
2. $f(x)$ has a **finite number of maxima and minima**
3. $f(x)$ has a **finite number of discontinuities**
4. $f(x)$ is **absolutely integrable**: $\int_{-\pi}^{\pi} |f(x)|\, dx < \infty$

> ⚡ **GATE Fact:** All physically realizable signals satisfy Dirichlet conditions. At jump discontinuities, the Fourier series converges to the **average of left and right limits**: $S(x_0) = \frac{f(x_0^+) + f(x_0^-)}{2}$.

## 🧠 Part II: Fourier Series on $[-\pi, \pi]$ — Master Formulas

### The Fourier Series Representation

$$\boxed{f(x) = \frac{a_0}{2} + \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left[a_n \cos(nx) + b_n \sin(nx)\right]}$$

### Euler–Fourier Coefficient Formulas

$$\boxed{a_0 = \frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi} f(x)\, dx}$$

$$\boxed{a_n = \frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi} f(x)\cos(nx)\, dx, \quad n = 1, 2, 3, \ldots}$$

$$\boxed{b_n = \frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi} f(x)\sin(nx)\, dx, \quad n = 1, 2, 3, \ldots}$$

> ⚡ **Key Insight:** These formulas come from the **orthogonality of trig functions** over $[-\pi, \pi]$:
> $$\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\cos(mx)\cos(nx)\,dx = \begin{cases} 0 & m \ne n \\ \pi & m = n \ne 0 \\ 2\pi & m = n = 0 \end{cases}$$
> $$\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\sin(mx)\sin(nx)\,dx = \begin{cases} 0 & m \ne n \\ \pi & m = n \end{cases}$$
> $$\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\sin(mx)\cos(nx)\,dx = 0 \quad \text{(always)}$$

---

## 🧠 Part III: Fourier Series on $[-L, L]$ — General Period $2L$

When the period is $2L$ (not $2\pi$), make the substitution $x \to \frac{\pi x}{L}$:

$$\boxed{f(x) = \frac{a_0}{2} + \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left[a_n \cos\!\left(\frac{n\pi x}{L}\right) + b_n \sin\!\left(\frac{n\pi x}{L}\right)\right]}$$

$$\boxed{a_0 = \frac{1}{L}\int_{-L}^{L} f(x)\, dx}$$

$$\boxed{a_n = \frac{1}{L}\int_{-L}^{L} f(x)\cos\!\left(\frac{n\pi x}{L}\right) dx}$$

$$\boxed{b_n = \frac{1}{L}\int_{-L}^{L} f(x)\sin\!\left(\frac{n\pi x}{L}\right) dx}$$

---

## 🧠 Part IV: Even & Odd Functions — The Biggest GATE Time-Saver

### Definitions
- **Even function:** $f(-x) = f(x)$ — symmetric about the $y$-axis
  - Examples: $\cos x$, $x^2$, $|x|$, constants
- **Odd function:** $f(-x) = -f(x)$ — antisymmetric (point symmetry at origin)
  - Examples: $\sin x$, $x$, $x^3$, $x \cos x$

### Key Integration Rules (Memorize These!)
$$\int_{-L}^{L} \text{(even function)}\, dx = 2\int_{0}^{L} \text{(even function)}\, dx$$
$$\int_{-L}^{L} \text{(odd function)}\, dx = 0$$

### Consequence for Fourier Coefficients

| $f(x)$ type | $a_0$ | $a_n$ | $b_n$ | Result |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Even** | Non-zero | Non-zero | $\mathbf{b_n = 0}$ | **Cosine series only** |
| **Odd** | $\mathbf{a_0 = 0}$ | $\mathbf{a_n = 0}$ | Non-zero | **Sine series only** |
| **Neither** | Non-zero | Non-zero | Non-zero | Full series |

### Simplified Formulas for Even Functions (Period $2L$)

$$\boxed{f(x) = \frac{a_0}{2} + \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} a_n \cos\!\left(\frac{n\pi x}{L}\right)}$$

$$a_0 = \frac{2}{L}\int_{0}^{L} f(x)\, dx, \qquad a_n = \frac{2}{L}\int_{0}^{L} f(x)\cos\!\left(\frac{n\pi x}{L}\right) dx$$

### Simplified Formulas for Odd Functions (Period $2L$)

$$\boxed{f(x) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} b_n \sin\!\left(\frac{n\pi x}{L}\right)}$$

$$b_n = \frac{2}{L}\int_{0}^{L} f(x)\sin\!\left(\frac{n\pi x}{L}\right) dx$$

> ⚡ **Speed Trick:** Recognize even/odd first — this halves your computation! The factor of 2 comes because you integrate only over $[0, L]$ and double the result.

---

## 🧠 Part V: Half-Range Expansions (HIGH YIELD!)

A function $f(x)$ defined only on $[0, L]$ can be extended to $[-L, L]$ in two ways:

### 1. Half-Range Cosine Series (Even Extension)
Extend $f(x)$ as an **even function** to $[-L, L]$. Result: purely cosine series.

$$\boxed{f(x) = \frac{a_0}{2} + \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} a_n \cos\!\left(\frac{n\pi x}{L}\right), \quad x \in [0, L]}$$

$$a_0 = \frac{2}{L}\int_{0}^{L} f(x)\, dx, \qquad a_n = \frac{2}{L}\int_{0}^{L} f(x)\cos\!\left(\frac{n\pi x}{L}\right) dx$$

### 2. Half-Range Sine Series (Odd Extension)
Extend $f(x)$ as an **odd function** to $[-L, L]$. Result: purely sine series.

$$\boxed{f(x) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} b_n \sin\!\left(\frac{n\pi x}{L}\right), \quad x \in [0, L]}$$

$$b_n = \frac{2}{L}\int_{0}^{L} f(x)\sin\!\left(\frac{n\pi x}{L}\right) dx$$

> ⚡ **Connection to PDEs:** The sine series satisfies Dirichlet BCs ($f(0) = f(L) = 0$) and the cosine series satisfies Neumann BCs ($f'(0) = f'(L) = 0$). This is exactly how we solve the Heat and Wave equations!

---

## 🧠 Part VI: Parseval's Theorem (Power Identity)

$$\boxed{\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}[f(x)]^2\, dx = \frac{a_0^2}{2} + \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(a_n^2 + b_n^2)}$$

For general period $2L$:
$$\frac{1}{L}\int_{-L}^{L}[f(x)]^2\, dx = \frac{a_0^2}{2} + \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(a_n^2 + b_n^2)$$

> ⚡ **Elegant Use:** Parseval's theorem is used to sum infinite series! For example, the Fourier series of $f(x) = x$ gives, via Parseval's identity:
> $$\frac{\pi^2}{6} = 1 + \frac{1}{4} + \frac{1}{9} + \cdots = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n^2}$$
> This is the famous **Basel Problem** result — a guaranteed GATE MCQ!

---

## 🧠 Part VII: Complex (Exponential) Form of Fourier Series

Using Euler's formula $e^{inx} = \cos(nx) + i\sin(nx)$:

$$\boxed{f(x) = \sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty} c_n e^{inx}}$$

$$\boxed{c_n = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi} f(x) e^{-inx}\, dx}$$

**Relationship to real coefficients:**
$$c_0 = \frac{a_0}{2}, \qquad c_n = \frac{a_n - ib_n}{2}, \qquad c_{-n} = \frac{a_n + ib_n}{2}$$

For real $f(x)$: $c_{-n} = \overline{c_n}$ (complex conjugate).

---

## 🧠 Part VIII: Standard Fourier Series Results (GATE Often Tests These Directly!)

### 1. $f(x) = x$ on $(-\pi, \pi)$ (Odd function → Sine series)

$$x = 2\left(\sin x - \frac{\sin 2x}{2} + \frac{\sin 3x}{3} - \cdots\right) = 2\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n+1}}{n}\sin(nx)$$

→ At $x = \pi/2$: $\frac{\pi}{4} = 1 - \frac{1}{3} + \frac{1}{5} - \cdots$ (Leibniz formula for $\pi$!)

---

### 2. $f(x) = x^2$ on $(-\pi, \pi)$ (Even function → Cosine series)

$$x^2 = \frac{\pi^2}{3} + 4\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^n}{n^2}\cos(nx)$$

→ At $x = \pi$: $\pi^2 = \frac{\pi^2}{3} + 4\sum\frac{1}{n^2} \implies \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n^2} = \frac{\pi^2}{6}$ ⭐ (Basel Problem!)

→ At $x = 0$: $0 = \frac{\pi^2}{3} - 4\sum\frac{1}{n^2} \implies \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n+1}}{n^2} = \frac{\pi^2}{12}$

---

### 3. $f(x) = |x|$ on $(-\pi, \pi)$ (Even → Cosine series)

$$|x| = \frac{\pi}{2} - \frac{4}{\pi}\sum_{n=1,3,5,\ldots}\frac{\cos(nx)}{n^2} = \frac{\pi}{2} - \frac{4}{\pi}\left(\cos x + \frac{\cos 3x}{9} + \frac{\cos 5x}{25} + \cdots\right)$$

→ Via Parseval: $\sum_{n \text{ odd}}\frac{1}{n^4} = \frac{\pi^4}{96}$

---

### 4. Square Wave (Unit pulse, period $2\pi$)

$$f(x) = \begin{cases} 1 & 0 < x < \pi \\ -1 & -\pi < x < 0 \end{cases} \implies f(x) = \frac{4}{\pi}\left(\sin x + \frac{\sin 3x}{3} + \frac{\sin 5x}{5} + \cdots\right)$$

> ⚡ **Pattern:** Square wave has only **ODD harmonics** with coefficients $\frac{1}{n}$, $n = 1, 3, 5, \ldots$

---

## ⚡ AIR-1 Speed Tricks & Mnemonics

### 🚀 Trick 1: Even/Odd Test — Do This FIRST (Always!)
Before computing any integral, determine if $f(x)$ is even, odd, or neither:

1. Replace $x$ with $-x$: Does $f(-x) = f(x)$? → **Even** (only cosines, $b_n = 0$)
2. Does $f(-x) = -f(x)$? → **Odd** (only sines, $a_0 = a_n = 0$)
3. Neither? → Full series (compute all)

> This test takes 10 seconds and eliminates half the calculation. **Never skip this step.**

---

### 🚀 Trick 2: "$a_0/2$ vs. $a_0$" — The Most Common GATE Trap

Note that $\frac{a_0}{2}$ appears in the series, NOT $a_0$. But the formula gives $a_0 = \frac{1}{\pi}\int f\,dx$.

So the **constant term** (DC offset) in the Fourier series is:
$$\text{Constant term} = \frac{a_0}{2} = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi} f(x)\, dx = \text{average value of } f$$

> ⚡ **Shortcut:** The constant term $a_0/2$ = **average (mean) value of $f(x)$ over one period.** Compute the average geometrically for piecewise functions!

---

### 🚀 Trick 3: Gibbs Phenomenon — "The 9% Overshoot"
At a **jump discontinuity**, the Fourier series overshoots the true value by approximately **9%** of the jump magnitude, no matter how many terms are used. This is the **Gibbs phenomenon**. If GATE asks "what happens to the Fourier series at a discontinuity?" → it converges to the **average** of left/right limits, but always overshoots by ~9%.

---

### 🚀 Trick 4: Coefficient Decay Rate Tells You Smoothness
- Coefficients decay as $1/n$: $f(x)$ has **jump discontinuities** (e.g., square wave)
- Coefficients decay as $1/n^2$: $f(x)$ is **continuous but has kinks** (slope discontinuities, e.g., triangle wave, $|x|$)
- Coefficients decay as $1/n^3$ or faster: $f(x)$ is **smooth** (continuous with continuous derivative)

> ⚡ This is a GATE MCQ-type insight: "The Fourier coefficients of a piecewise-smooth function decay as ___?"

---

### 🧠 Mnemonic: "**ACE COB**" for Formula Structure
- **A**ll coefficients $a_0, a_n, b_n$ use integration
- **C**osine: $a_n$ uses $\cos(nx)$; $b_n$ uses $\sin(nx)$
- **E**ven: kills $b_n$; **O**dd: kills $a_0$ and $a_n$
- **C**onstant term $= \frac{a_0}{2}$ (mean value!)
- **O**rthogonality is WHY the formulas work
- **B**oundary: half-range $\to$ cosine (even ext) or sine (odd ext)

---

### 🧠 Mnemonic: "**SOS**" for Standard Series Signs

| Series | Sign pattern of coefficients | Memory |
|---|---|---|
| $f(x) = x$ (sine series) | $+, -, +, -, \ldots$ i.e. $(-1)^{n+1}$ | **Alternating** |
| $f(x) = x^2$ (cosine series) | $-, +, -, +, \ldots$ i.e. $(-1)^n$ | **Alternating, starts negative** |
| Square wave (sine, odd only) | All positive | **No alternation** |

## 📝 Step-by-Step Solved GATE Examples

### Example 1: Fourier Series of a Piecewise Function (GATE 4-Marker)
**Problem:** Find the Fourier series of $f(x)$ with period $2\pi$:
$$f(x) = \begin{cases} 0 & -\pi \le x < 0 \\ x & 0 \le x \le \pi \end{cases}$$

**Solution:**
**Step 0 — Even/Odd check:** $f(-\pi/2) = 0 \ne f(\pi/2) = \pi/2$, and $f(-\pi/2) = 0 \ne -(\pi/2)$. **Neither even nor odd → full series.**

**Step 1 — Compute $a_0$:**
$$a_0 = \frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}f(x)\,dx = \frac{1}{\pi}\left[\int_{-\pi}^{0}0\,dx + \int_0^{\pi}x\,dx\right] = \frac{1}{\pi}\cdot\frac{\pi^2}{2} = \frac{\pi}{2}$$

**Step 2 — Compute $a_n$:**
$$a_n = \frac{1}{\pi}\int_0^{\pi}x\cos(nx)\,dx$$

Using integration by parts: $\int x\cos(nx)\,dx = \frac{x\sin(nx)}{n} + \frac{\cos(nx)}{n^2}$

$$a_n = \frac{1}{\pi}\left[\frac{x\sin(nx)}{n} + \frac{\cos(nx)}{n^2}\right]_0^{\pi} = \frac{1}{\pi}\left[\frac{\cos(n\pi)}{n^2} - \frac{1}{n^2}\right] = \frac{(-1)^n - 1}{\pi n^2}$$

$$\boxed{a_n = \begin{cases} \dfrac{-2}{\pi n^2} & n \text{ odd} \\ 0 & n \text{ even} \end{cases}}$$

**Step 3 — Compute $b_n$:**
$$b_n = \frac{1}{\pi}\int_0^{\pi}x\sin(nx)\,dx = \frac{1}{\pi}\left[-\frac{x\cos(nx)}{n} + \frac{\sin(nx)}{n^2}\right]_0^{\pi} = \frac{1}{\pi}\left[\frac{-\pi\cos(n\pi)}{n}\right] = \frac{-(-1)^n}{n} = \frac{(-1)^{n+1}}{n}$$

**Step 4 — Write the series:**
$$\boxed{f(x) = \frac{\pi}{4} - \frac{2}{\pi}\left(\cos x + \frac{\cos 3x}{9} + \frac{\cos 5x}{25} + \cdots\right) + \left(\sin x - \frac{\sin 2x}{2} + \frac{\sin 3x}{3} - \cdots\right)}$$

> **Note:** $a_0/2 = \pi/4$ = average of $f$ over one period (geometrically: area of triangle $\frac{\pi^2/2}{\text{period }\pi} \times \frac{1}{2}$... actually the mean is $\frac{\pi/2}{2\pi}\cdot\pi^2/2$ — use the integral result!)

---

### Example 2: Even Extension / Half-Range Cosine Series (GATE 3-Marker)
**Problem:** Find the half-range cosine series for $f(x) = \pi - x$ on $[0, \pi]$.

**Solution:** (Cosine series → even extension)

$$a_0 = \frac{2}{\pi}\int_0^{\pi}(\pi - x)\,dx = \frac{2}{\pi}\left[\pi x - \frac{x^2}{2}\right]_0^{\pi} = \frac{2}{\pi}\cdot\frac{\pi^2}{2} = \pi$$

$$a_n = \frac{2}{\pi}\int_0^{\pi}(\pi - x)\cos(nx)\,dx$$

Integration by parts: $\int (\pi-x)\cos(nx)\,dx = \frac{(\pi-x)\sin(nx)}{n} + \frac{\cos(nx)}{n^2}$

$$a_n = \frac{2}{\pi}\left[0 + \frac{1}{n^2} - \frac{\cos(n\pi)}{n^2} - 0\right] = \frac{2}{\pi n^2}(1 - (-1)^n) = \begin{cases} \dfrac{4}{\pi n^2} & n \text{ odd} \\ 0 & n \text{ even} \end{cases}$$

$$\boxed{f(x) = \frac{\pi}{2} + \frac{4}{\pi}\left(\cos x + \frac{\cos 3x}{9} + \frac{\cos 5x}{25} + \cdots\right)}$$

---

### Example 3: Use Parseval's Theorem to Sum a Series (GATE MCQ — Very High Yield)
**Problem:** Using the Fourier series of $f(x) = x$ on $(-\pi, \pi)$, find $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n^2}$.

**Solution:**
The Fourier series of $f(x) = x$ is $x = 2\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n+1}}{n}\sin(nx)$, so $b_n = \frac{2(-1)^{n+1}}{n}$, $a_0 = a_n = 0$.

By Parseval's Theorem:
$$\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}x^2\,dx = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}b_n^2$$

$$\frac{1}{\pi}\cdot\frac{2\pi^3}{3} = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{4}{n^2}$$

$$\frac{2\pi^2}{3} = \frac{4}{1} + \frac{4}{4} + \frac{4}{9} + \cdots = 4\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n^2}$$

$$\boxed{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n^2} = \frac{\pi^2}{6}}$$

---

### Example 4: Direct Coefficient Question (GATE 1-Marker)
**Problem:** The Fourier cosine coefficient $a_2$ in the expansion of $f(x) = x^2$ on $(-\pi, \pi)$ is _____.

**Solution:** $f(x) = x^2$ is even, so $b_n = 0$.

$$a_n = \frac{2}{\pi}\int_0^{\pi}x^2\cos(nx)\,dx$$

Using integration by parts twice:
$$= \frac{2}{\pi}\left[\frac{x^2\sin(nx)}{n} + \frac{2x\cos(nx)}{n^2} - \frac{2\sin(nx)}{n^3}\right]_0^{\pi} = \frac{2}{\pi}\cdot\frac{2\pi(-1)^n}{n^2} = \frac{4(-1)^n}{n^2}$$

For $n = 2$: $a_2 = \frac{4(-1)^2}{4} = 1$.

$$\boxed{a_2 = 1}$$

| Scenario | Period | $a_0$ formula | $a_n$ formula | $b_n$ formula |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Full range** $[-\pi,\pi]$ | $2\pi$ | $\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}f\,dx$ | $\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}f\cos(nx)\,dx$ | $\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}f\sin(nx)\,dx$ |

| **Odd** $[-L,L]$ | $2L$ | $0$ | $0$ | $\frac{2}{L}\int_{0}^{L}f\sin\frac{n\pi x}{L}\,dx$ |
| **Half-range cosine** $[0,L]$ | — | $\frac{2}{L}\int_{0}^{L}f\,dx$ | $\frac{2}{L}\int_{0}^{L}f\cos\frac{n\pi x}{L}\,dx$ | $0$ |
| **Half-range sine** $[0,L]$ | — | $0$ | $0$ | $\frac{2}{L}\int_{0}^{L}f\sin\frac{n\pi x}{L}\,dx$ |

## ⚠️ Common Exam Traps & Pitfalls

> 🔴 **Trap 1: Writing $a_0$ instead of $a_0/2$ in the Series**  
> The constant term in the Fourier series is $\frac{a_0}{2}$, but the integral formula gives $a_0$. Half the class writes $a_0$ as the constant term → immediate wrong answer. Memorize: "HALF of $a_0$ appears in the series."

> 🔴 **Trap 2: Assuming $b_n = 0$ Without Checking Parity**  
> The most common error — students assume a function is odd or even by looking at a single value. Always do the formal test: compute $f(-x)$ and compare with $f(x)$.

> 🔴 **Trap 3: Value of the Series at a Discontinuity**  
> At $x = x_0$ where $f$ has a jump discontinuity, the Fourier series converges to $\frac{f(x_0^+) + f(x_0^-)}{2}$, NOT to $f(x_0^+)$ or $f(x_0^-)$ individually.  
> Example: At $x = 0$ for the square wave, the series gives $0$ (average of $+1$ and $-1$).

> 🔴 **Trap 4: Period Confusion in Coefficient Formulas**  
> The formulas for $[-\pi,\pi]$ use $\frac{1}{\pi}$ as the prefactor. The formulas for $[-L,L]$ use $\frac{1}{L}$. And the even/odd simplifications use $\frac{2}{L}$. Don't mix these up by using $\frac{1}{\pi}$ for $[-L,L]$ problems.

## 📌 Part I: The Concept of a Limit (Zero to Clear)

### 1. Intuitive Idea
The **limit** of a function $f(x)$ as $x$ approaches $a$ asks:
> *"What value does $f(x)$ approach (get closer and closer to) as $x$ gets closer and closer to $a$?"*

**Key Insight:** The limit is about the **journey toward $a$**, NOT the **destination $f(a)$ itself**. The function doesn't even need to be defined at $x = a$ for the limit to exist!

$$\lim_{x \to a} f(x) = L \quad \text{means: } f(x) \text{ can be made arbitrarily close to } L \text{ by taking } x \text{ sufficiently close to } a$$

---

### 2. Formal $\varepsilon$-$\delta$ Definition (Conceptual Understanding Only for GATE)

$$\lim_{x \to a} f(x) = L \iff \forall \varepsilon > 0, \exists \delta > 0 \text{ such that } 0 < |x - a| < \delta \implies |f(x) - L| < \varepsilon$$

> 💡 **GATE Tip:** The $\varepsilon$-$\delta$ definition is rarely asked directly in GATE numerical problems, but understanding it clarifies **why** certain limits fail to exist (oscillating functions, different one-sided limits).

---

### 3. Left-Hand Limit (LHL) & Right-Hand Limit (RHL)

| | Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Left-Hand Limit** | $\lim_{x \to a^-} f(x)$ | Value approached from the **left** ($x < a$, $x \to a$) |
| **Right-Hand Limit** | $\lim_{x \to a^+} f(x)$ | Value approached from the **right** ($x > a$, $x \to a$) |

#### ✅ Existence Condition (The GATE Golden Rule):
$$\lim_{x \to a} f(x) \text{ EXISTS} \iff \underbrace{\lim_{x \to a^-} f(x)}_{\text{LHL}} = \underbrace{\lim_{x \to a^+} f(x)}_{\text{RHL}} = L \quad (\text{both finite and equal})$$

#### ❌ Limit Does NOT Exist (DNE) when:
1. LHL $\neq$ RHL (Jump Discontinuity)
2. LHL or RHL $= \pm \infty$ (Infinite/Vertical Asymptote)
3. Oscillation without settling (e.g., $\sin(1/x)$ as $x \to 0$)

## 🧠 Part II: Standard Limit Identities (Must Memorize for GATE)

### 🎯 The Master Table of Standard Limits

> 🔴 **GATE Exam Tip:** These are worth **memorizing cold**. Direct substitution of these into MCQs saves 2–3 minutes per question.

#### Group A: Trigonometric Limits (as $x \to 0$)

| 2 | $\lim_{x \to 0} \dfrac{\tan x}{x}$ | $1$ | Follows from $\sin/\cos \to 1/1$ |
| 3 | $\lim_{x \to 0} \dfrac{1 - \cos x}{x^2}$ | $\dfrac{1}{2}$ | Use $1 - \cos x = 2\sin^2(x/2)$ |
| 4 | $\lim_{x \to 0} \dfrac{\sin^{-1} x}{x}$ | $1$ | |

| 6 | $\lim_{x \to 0} \dfrac{\sin(mx)}{x}$ | $m$ | Generalized version of limit 1 |
| 7 | $\lim_{x \to 0} \dfrac{\sin(mx)}{\sin(nx)}$ | $\dfrac{m}{n}$ | Ratio form |

#### Group B: Exponential & Logarithmic Limits

| # | Standard Limit | Value | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 8 | $\lim_{x \to 0} \dfrac{e^x - 1}{x}$ | $1$ | Key exponential limit |
| 9 | $\lim_{x \to 0} \dfrac{a^x - 1}{x}$ | $\ln a$ ($a > 0$) | Generalized base $a$ |
| 10 | $\lim_{x \to 0} \dfrac{\ln(1+x)}{x}$ | $1$ | Logarithmic limit |

| 15 | $\lim_{x \to \infty} \left(1 + \dfrac{a}{x}\right)^{bx}$ | $e^{ab}$ | Full general form |

#### Group C: Algebraic Limits

| # | Standard Limit | Value | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 16 | $\lim_{x \to a} \dfrac{x^n - a^n}{x - a}$ | $n a^{n-1}$ | Power limit identity |
| 17 | $\lim_{x \to \infty} \dfrac{1}{x^n}$ | $0$ ($n > 0$) | Reciprocal power |
| 18 | $\lim_{x \to \infty} x^{1/x}$ | $1$ | Use $e^{\frac{\ln x}{x}}$ |
| 19 | $\lim_{x \to 0^+} x^x$ | $1$ | Use $e^{x \ln x}$ |
| 20 | $\lim_{x \to \infty} \dfrac{P_m(x)}{Q_n(x)}$ | See below | Polynomial ratio |

#### Polynomial Ratio at Infinity (GATE Essential):
For polynomials $P_m(x)$ (degree $m$) and $Q_n(x)$ (degree $n$):
$$\lim_{x \to \infty} \frac{P_m(x)}{Q_n(x)} = \begin{cases} 0 & \text{if } m < n \\ \dfrac{\text{leading coeff of } P}{\text{leading coeff of } Q} & \text{if } m = n \\ \pm \infty & \text{if } m > n \end{cases}$$

## 🧠 Part III: The 7 Indeterminate Forms & Systematic Resolution

### What Are Indeterminate Forms?
When substituting $x = a$ directly gives an **ambiguous expression** — not because the limit is infinite, but because the **ratio of infinities or zeroes** depends on *how fast* each part grows/shrinks.

The 7 Indeterminate Forms:
$$\boxed{\frac{0}{0}} \quad \boxed{\frac{\infty}{\infty}} \quad \boxed{0 \cdot \infty} \quad \boxed{\infty - \infty} \quad \boxed{1^\infty} \quad \boxed{0^0} \quad \boxed{\infty^0}$$

### Resolution Roadmap:

```
Indeterminate Form Encountered
           |
    +------+--------+-----------+
    |                           |
 0/0 or ∞/∞           Exponential Forms (1^∞, 0^0, ∞^0)
    |                           |
L'Hôpital's Rule         Apply: lim [f(x)]^g(x) = e^{lim g(x) ln f(x)}
OR Taylor Series                |
                         Reduces to 0·∞ → convert → 0/0 or ∞/∞
                                |
                         Apply L'Hôpital's Rule
    
0·∞  →  write as  0/0  or  ∞/∞  first
∞-∞  →  algebraic manipulation (rationalize / combine fractions)
```

## 🧠 Part IV: L'Hôpital's Rule (The #1 GATE Tool)

### Statement:
If $\lim_{x \to a} f(x) = 0$ and $\lim_{x \to a} g(x) = 0$ (or both $= \pm \infty$), AND $g'(x) \neq 0$ near $a$, then:

$$\lim_{x \to a} \frac{f(x)}{g(x)} = \lim_{x \to a} \frac{f'(x)}{g'(x)}$$

*(Apply repeatedly until the form is no longer indeterminate)*

### ⚠️ Critical Rules for L'Hôpital:
1. **Only valid** for $\frac{0}{0}$ or $\frac{\infty}{\infty}$ forms — NOT for $\frac{2}{0}$ or $\frac{1}{3}$!
2. Differentiate numerator and denominator **SEPARATELY** — it is NOT the quotient rule!
3. If after differentiating you still get $\frac{0}{0}$ or $\frac{\infty}{\infty}$, apply L'Hôpital again.
4. If the result **oscillates** (e.g., $\sin x / \cos x$ → cycles), L'Hôpital is not applicable — use Taylor series instead.

## 🧠 Part V: Continuity (The Direct Extension of Limits)

### Definition of Continuity at $x = a$:
$f(x)$ is **continuous at $x = a$** if ALL THREE conditions hold:
1. $f(a)$ is **defined** (the function value exists).
2. $\lim_{x \to a} f(x)$ **exists** (LHL = RHL = finite value $L$).
3. $\lim_{x \to a} f(x) = f(a)$ (the limit equals the function value).

$$\text{Continuous at } x = a \iff \lim_{x \to a^-} f(x) = \lim_{x \to a^+} f(x) = f(a)$$

### Types of Discontinuities:

| Type | Condition | Graph Behaviour | Removable? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Removable (Hole)** | LHL = RHL $\neq f(a)$ (or $f(a)$ undefined) | Single hole in curve | ✅ Yes |
| **Jump** | LHL $\neq$ RHL (both finite) | Step/jump in curve | ❌ No |
| **Infinite** | LHL or RHL $= \pm\infty$ | Vertical asymptote | ❌ No |
| **Oscillatory** | Limit DNE (oscillates) | Wild oscillations | ❌ No |

### Continuity of Common Functions:
- **Polynomials:** Continuous everywhere on $\mathbb{R}$.
- **Rational Functions $\frac{P(x)}{Q(x)}$:** Continuous where $Q(x) \neq 0$.
- **$\sin x$, $\cos x$:** Continuous everywhere on $\mathbb{R}$.
- **$\tan x$:** Discontinuous at $x = \frac{\pi}{2} + n\pi$ ($n \in \mathbb{Z}$).
- **$|x|$:** Continuous everywhere, but **NOT differentiable at $x = 0$**.
- **$e^x$, $\ln x$ ($x > 0$):** Continuous on their domains.

## 🧠 Part VI: The Sandwich (Squeeze) Theorem

### Statement:
If $g(x) \le f(x) \le h(x)$ for all $x$ near $a$ (except possibly at $a$), and:
$$\lim_{x \to a} g(x) = \lim_{x \to a} h(x) = L$$
Then:
$$\lim_{x \to a} f(x) = L$$

### 🔑 Classic GATE Applications:
1. $\lim_{x \to 0} x^2 \sin\left(\frac{1}{x}\right) = 0$, since $-x^2 \le x^2 \sin(1/x) \le x^2$ and $\lim x^2 = 0$.
2. $\lim_{x \to \infty} \frac{\sin x}{x} = 0$, since $\frac{-1}{x} \le \frac{\sin x}{x} \le \frac{1}{x}$ and both bounds $\to 0$.
3. $\lim_{x \to 0} x \lfloor 1/x \rfloor = 1$, using $x \cdot (1/x - 1) \le x \lfloor 1/x \rfloor \le x \cdot (1/x)$.

> 🎯 **Mnemonic for Sandwich Theorem:** "**Trapped = Same Destiny**" — If $f$ is trapped between $g$ and $h$ and both go to $L$, then $f$ must also go to $L$.

## ⚡ AIR-1 Topper Speed Tricks & Mnemonics

### 🚀 Trick 1: The $1^\infty$ Instant Formula (GATE Favourite)

For $\lim_{x \to a} [f(x)]^{g(x)}$ where $f(x) \to 1$ and $g(x) \to \infty$:

$$\boxed{\lim_{x \to a} [f(x)]^{g(x)} = e^{\,\lim_{x \to a}\, g(x)\,[f(x) - 1]}}$$

**How to use:** Identify $f(x) - 1$ and $g(x)$, multiply them, find the limit of the product, then exponentiate. Done in 10 seconds!

*Example:* $\lim_{x \to 0} (1 + 3x)^{2/x}$
$$L = \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{2}{x} \cdot [(1+3x) - 1] = \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{2}{x} \cdot 3x = 6 \implies \text{Answer} = e^6$$

---

### 🚀 Trick 2: Taylor Series Substitution (Faster than L'Hôpital)

For limits of the form $\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{f(x)}{x^n}$ involving trig/exp functions, **substitute the Maclaurin series** instead of differentiating repeatedly.

**Speed comparison:**
$$\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{x - \sin x}{x^3}$$

❌ L'Hôpital: Differentiate 3 times → 90 seconds  
✅ Taylor Series: $\sin x = x - \frac{x^3}{6} + \mathcal{O}(x^5)$
$$\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{x - (x - x^3/6)}{x^3} = \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{x^3/6}{x^3} = \frac{1}{6} \quad \text{← Done in 5 seconds!}$$

---

### 🚀 Trick 3: The Polynomial Race Hierarchy at $x \to \infty$

$$\ln x \ll x^a \ll e^x \ll x^x \quad (a > 0, \text{ as } x \to \infty)$$

This means:
- $\lim_{x \to \infty} \frac{\ln x}{x^a} = 0$ (polynomial always beats log)
- $\lim_{x \to \infty} \frac{x^a}{e^x} = 0$ (exponential always beats polynomial)
- $\lim_{x \to \infty} \frac{e^x}{x^x} = 0$ ($x^x$ grows fastest)

---

### 🚀 Trick 4: Rationalization for $\infty - \infty$

$$\lim_{x \to \infty} (\sqrt{x^2 + ax + b} - x) = \frac{a}{2}$$

Multiply by conjugate: $\frac{(\sqrt{x^2+ax+b} - x)(\sqrt{x^2+ax+b} + x)}{\sqrt{x^2+ax+b} + x} = \frac{ax + b}{\sqrt{x^2+ax+b} + x} \to \frac{ax}{2x} = \frac{a}{2}$

---

### 🚀 Trick 5: The $\frac{\sin(mx)}{mx} = 1$ Substitution
Never compute $\lim_{x\to 0} \frac{\sin(5x)}{3x}$ by L'Hôpital. Instead:
$$\frac{\sin(5x)}{3x} = \frac{5}{3} \cdot \frac{\sin(5x)}{5x} \to \frac{5}{3} \cdot 1 = \frac{5}{3} \quad \text{← 3 seconds!}$$

---

## 📝 Step-by-Step Solved GATE Examples

### Example 1: $\frac{0}{0}$ Form — Taylor Series Method
**Problem:** $\lim_{x \to 0} \dfrac{e^x - 1 - x}{x^2}$

**Solution (Taylor Series):**
$e^x = 1 + x + \frac{x^2}{2!} + \mathcal{O}(x^3)$  
$e^x - 1 - x = \frac{x^2}{2} + \mathcal{O}(x^3)$  
$$\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{x^2/2 + \mathcal{O}(x^3)}{x^2} = \frac{1}{2} \quad \boxed{\text{Answer: } \frac{1}{2}}$$

---

### Example 2: $\frac{\infty}{\infty}$ Form — L'Hôpital's Rule
**Problem:** $\lim_{x \to \infty} \dfrac{x^2}{e^x}$

**Solution:**
$\frac{\infty}{\infty}$ form. Apply L'Hôpital:
$$\lim_{x \to \infty} \frac{x^2}{e^x} \xrightarrow{L'H} \lim_{x \to \infty} \frac{2x}{e^x} \xrightarrow{L'H} \lim_{x \to \infty} \frac{2}{e^x} = 0 \quad \boxed{\text{Answer: } 0}$$

---

### Example 3: $1^\infty$ Form — Fast Formula
**Problem:** $\lim_{x \to 0} (\cos x)^{1/x^2}$

**Solution:**  
Form: $(\cos 0)^{1/0} = 1^\infty$.  
$f(x) = \cos x, \quad g(x) = \frac{1}{x^2}$  
$L = \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{1}{x^2} \cdot [\cos x - 1]$  
Using $\cos x - 1 \approx -\frac{x^2}{2}$:
$$L = \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{1}{x^2} \cdot \left(-\frac{x^2}{2}\right) = -\frac{1}{2}$$
$$\text{Answer} = e^{-1/2} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{e}} \quad \boxed{e^{-1/2}}$$

---

### Example 4: $\infty - \infty$ Form
**Problem:** $\lim_{x \to \infty} (\sqrt{x^2 + 5x} - x)$

**Solution:**  
Form: $\infty - \infty$.  
Multiply by conjugate:
$$= \lim_{x \to \infty} \frac{(x^2 + 5x) - x^2}{\sqrt{x^2 + 5x} + x} = \lim_{x \to \infty} \frac{5x}{\sqrt{x^2 + 5x} + x}$$
Divide numerator and denominator by $x$:
$$= \lim_{x \to \infty} \frac{5}{\sqrt{1 + 5/x} + 1} = \frac{5}{\sqrt{1} + 1} = \frac{5}{2} \quad \boxed{\text{Answer: } \frac{5}{2}}$$

---

### Example 5: Squeeze Theorem
**Problem (GATE PYQ type):** $\lim_{x \to 0} x^2 \sin\left(\dfrac{1}{x}\right)$

**Solution:**  
Since $-1 \le \sin(1/x) \le 1$ for all $x \neq 0$:  
$-x^2 \le x^2 \sin(1/x) \le x^2$  
$\lim_{x \to 0}(-x^2) = 0$ and $\lim_{x \to 0}(x^2) = 0$  
By Squeeze Theorem: $\lim_{x \to 0} x^2 \sin\left(\frac{1}{x}\right) = \boxed{0}$

---

### Example 6: Continuity — Finding the Constant (GATE MCQ Type)
**Problem:** Find $k$ so that $f(x) = \begin{cases} \dfrac{\sin(5x)}{x} & x \neq 0 \\ k & x = 0 \end{cases}$ is continuous at $x = 0$.

**Solution:**  
For continuity: $\lim_{x \to 0} f(x) = f(0)$  
$\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin(5x)}{x} = 5 \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin(5x)}{5x} = 5 \cdot 1 = 5$  
$\therefore k = \boxed{5}$

## ⚠️ Common Exam Traps & Pitfalls

> 🔴 **Trap 1: Applying L'Hôpital to Non-Indeterminate Forms**  
> *Mistake:* $\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\cos x}{x} \to \frac{-\sin x}{1} = 0$ ❌  
> *Correction:* $\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\cos x}{x} = \frac{1}{0} = \infty$ (NOT $\frac{0}{0}$, so L'Hôpital CANNOT be applied!)

> 🔴 **Trap 2: Confusing Limit with Function Value**  
> *Mistake:* "The limit equals $f(a)$ always."  
> *Correction:* The limit can exist even if $f(a)$ is undefined, and $\lim f(x) \neq f(a)$ for discontinuous functions.

> 🔴 **Trap 3: Oscillating Functions — No L'Hôpital**  
> *Mistake:* Applying L'Hôpital to $\lim_{x \to \infty} \frac{\sin x}{x}$ repeatedly.  
> *Correction:* Use Squeeze Theorem instead: $\frac{-1}{x} \le \frac{\sin x}{x} \le \frac{1}{x} \to 0$.

> 🔴 **Trap 4: $0^0$ is NOT always 1**  
> *Mistake:* Assuming $\lim_{x \to 0^+} x^x = 0^0 = 1$ without verification.  
> *Correction:* It IS 1 in this case, but $0^0$ is indeterminate in general — write $x^x = e^{x \ln x}$, compute $\lim_{x \to 0^+} x \ln x = 0$, so the answer is $e^0 = 1$.

> 🔴 **Trap 5: Polynomial Degree Comparison Error**  
> *Mistake:* $\lim_{x \to \infty} \frac{3x^3 + 5}{2x^3 - 7} = \frac{3 + 5}{2 - 7} = -\frac{8}{5}$ ❌  
> *Correction:* For equal degrees, **only compare leading coefficients**: $= \frac{3}{2}$ ✅

> 🎯 **GATE Weightage:** 2–3 marks per year. The Bisection Method is the **simplest, most robust, and guaranteed root-finding algorithm** for continuous linear and non-linear algebraic/transcendental equations $f(x) = 0$.

---

## 📌 Part I: Mathematical Foundation — Intermediate Value Theorem

### The Intermediate Value Theorem (IVT)

Let $f(x)$ be a continuous real-valued function defined on a closed interval $[a, b]$.

If $f(a)$ and $f(b)$ have **opposite signs** (i.e., $f(a) \cdot f(b) < 0$), then:

$$\boxed{\exists \; \alpha \in (a, b) \quad \text{such that} \quad f(\alpha) = 0}$$

There exists **at least one real root** $\alpha$ in the interval $(a, b)$.

```
          y ^
            |          f(b) > 0 (+)
            |           /
            |          /  Root α
     -------+---------+--------► x
            |  a    /    b
            |      /
            |  f(a) < 0 (-)
```

---

## 🧠 Part II: The Bisection Algorithm

### Step-by-Step Procedure

Given $f(x) = 0$ continuous on $[a_0, b_0]$ with $f(a_0) \cdot f(b_0) < 0$:

1. **Calculate Midpoint:**
   $$c_k = \frac{a_k + b_k}{2}$$

2. **Evaluate Function at Midpoint $f(c_k)$:**
   - If $f(c_k) = 0 \implies c_k$ is the **exact root**! Stop.
   - If $f(a_k) \cdot f(c_k) < 0 \implies$ Root lies in $[a_k, c_k]$. Set $a_{k+1} = a_k, \; b_{k+1} = c_k$.
   - If $f(a_k) \cdot f(c_k) > 0 \implies$ Root lies in $[c_k, b_k]$. Set $a_{k+1} = c_k, \; b_{k+1} = b_k$.

3. **Repeat** until the interval length $b_k - a_k$ or absolute error is less than desired tolerance $\epsilon$.

---

### Interval Length Reduction Table

After $n$ iterations, the initial interval length $(b_0 - a_0)$ is halved $n$ times:

$$\boxed{\text{Interval Length after } n \text{ iterations} = L_n = \frac{b_0 - a_0}{2^n}}$$

| Iteration ($n$) | Interval Bounds | Interval Length | Maximum Possible Error |
|---|---|---|---|
| $0$ | $[a_0, b_0]$ | $b_0 - a_0$ | $\frac{b_0 - a_0}{2}$ |
| $1$ | $[a_1, b_1]$ | $\frac{b_0 - a_0}{2}$ | $\frac{b_0 - a_0}{4}$ |
| $2$ | $[a_2, b_2]$ | $\frac{b_0 - a_0}{4}$ | $\frac{b_0 - a_0}{8}$ |
| $n$ | $[a_n, b_n]$ | $\frac{b_0 - a_0}{2^n}$ | $\frac{b_0 - a_0}{2^{n+1}}$ |

---

## 🧠 Part III: Error Analysis & Minimum Iterations Formula (HIGH YIELD!)

### Absolute Error Bound

The maximum error in estimating root $\alpha$ using midpoint $c_n$ after $n$ iterations:

$$\boxed{|c_n - \alpha| \le \frac{b_0 - a_0}{2^n}}$$

---

### Minimum Number of Iterations $n$ Required for Tolerance $\epsilon$

To guarantee that absolute error $|c_n - \alpha| < \epsilon$:

$$\frac{b_0 - a_0}{2^n} < \epsilon \implies 2^n > \frac{b_0 - a_0}{\epsilon}$$

Taking logarithm (base 2 or base 10) on both sides:

$$\boxed{n > \frac{\ln(b_0 - a_0) - \ln\epsilon}{\ln 2} = \frac{\log_{10}(b_0 - a_0) - \log_{10}\epsilon}{\log_{10} 2}}$$

$$\boxed{n_{min} = \left\lceil \frac{\ln(b_0 - a_0) - \ln\epsilon}{\ln 2} \right\rceil}$$

*(where $\lceil x \rceil$ is the ceiling function, rounding UP to next integer, and $\log_{10} 2 \approx 0.30103$)*

---

### Order and Rate of Convergence

- **Order of Convergence:** $p = 1$ (**Linear Convergence**)
- **Asymptotic Error Constant / Rate Factor:** $C = \frac{1}{2} = 0.5$
  $$|e_{n+1}| = \frac{1}{2} |e_n|$$
- Each iteration gains exactly **1 binary bit of accuracy** (or $\approx 0.301$ decimal digits).

## 🧠 Part IV: Master Comparison of Root-Finding Methods

| Feature | Bisection Method | Regula-Falsi (False Position) | Secant Method | Newton-Raphson Method |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Type** | Bracketing | Bracketing | Open | Open |
| **Convergence Rate** | **Linear** ($p=1$) | **Linear** ($p=1$) | **Superlinear** ($p=1.618$) | **Quadratic** ($p=2$) |
| **Asymptotic Constant** | $0.5$ | Variable | Variable | $\left\|\frac{f''(\alpha)}{2f'(\alpha)}\right\|$ |
| **Guaranteed Convergence?** | ✅ **ALWAYS** (if $f(a)f(b)<0$) | ✅ **ALWAYS** | ❌ No | ❌ No (fails if $f'=0$) |
| **Requires $f'(x)$?** | ❌ No | ❌ No | ❌ No | ✅ Yes |
| **Evaluation per step** | $1$ function evaluation | $1$ function evaluation | $1$ function evaluation | $2$ evaluations ($f, f'$) |
| **Robustness** | 🥇 **Most Robust** | High | Medium | Sensitive to initial guess |

## ⚡ AIR-1 Topper Speed Tricks & Mnemonics

### 🚀 Trick 1: 10-Second Minimum Iterations Calculation
To find min iterations $n$ for interval $[a, b]$ and accuracy $\epsilon$:
$$n > \frac{\log_{10}(b-a) - \log_{10}\epsilon}{0.30103}$$

*Example:* $[1, 2]$, $\epsilon = 10^{-3}$:
$$n > \frac{\log_{10}(1) - (-3)}{0.30103} = \frac{3}{0.30103} \approx 9.96 \implies \mathbf{n = 10 \text{ iterations}}$$

---

### 🚀 Trick 2: Decimal Places Accuracy Formula
To get $k$ correct decimal places, set tolerance $\epsilon = 0.5 \times 10^{-k}$:
$$n > \frac{\log_{10}(b-a) - \log_{10}(0.5 \times 10^{-k})}{0.30103} = \frac{\log_{10}(b-a) + k + 0.30103}{0.30103}$$

---

### 🚀 Trick 3: Systematic Iteration Table Method
For hand calculations in GATE, construct this 5-column table to avoid sign errors:

| $k$ | $a_k$ (sign) | $b_k$ (sign) | $c_k = \frac{a+b}{2}$ | $f(c_k)$ (sign) | New Bracket |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

---

### 🚀 Trick 4: Initial Bracket Selection Strategy
If no bracket $[a,b]$ is given in the problem:
- Try integer values $x = 0, 1, 2, -1, -2 \ldots$
- Evaluate $f(0), f(1), f(2) \ldots$ until you find a sign change $f(a) \cdot f(b) < 0$.

---

### 🧠 Mnemonic: "**HALVE**" for Bisection Properties
- **H**alves the interval length every step ($L_n = L_0 / 2^n$)
- **A**lways converges (guaranteed by IVT)
- **L**inear convergence (order $p=1$, factor $0.5$)
- **V**alue at midpoint determines next bracket
- **E**rror bound is $\frac{b_0-a_0}{2^n}$

## 📝 Step-by-Step Solved GATE Examples

### Example 1: Root Finding after 3 Iterations (GATE 2-Marker)
**Problem:** Find the root of non-linear equation $f(x) = x^3 - x - 1 = 0$ in the interval $[1, 2]$ using the Bisection Method after 3 iterations.

**Solution:**
Initial bracket: $a_0 = 1, \; b_0 = 2$.  
$f(1) = 1 - 1 - 1 = -1 \quad (-)$  
$f(2) = 8 - 2 - 1 = +5 \quad (+)$  
Sign change exists!

**Iteration 1:**
$$c_1 = \frac{1 + 2}{2} = 1.5$$
$$f(1.5) = (1.5)^3 - 1.5 - 1 = 3.375 - 2.5 = +0.875 \quad (+)$$
Since $f(1) (-) \cdot f(1.5) (+) < 0$, new bracket is $[1, 1.5]$.

**Iteration 2:**
$$c_2 = \frac{1 + 1.5}{2} = 1.25$$
$$f(1.25) = (1.25)^3 - 1.25 - 1 = 1.953125 - 2.25 = -0.296875 \quad (-)$$
Since $f(1.25) (-) \cdot f(1.5) (+) < 0$, new bracket is $[1.25, 1.5]$.

**Iteration 3:**
$$c_3 = \frac{1.25 + 1.5}{2} = 1.375$$

$$\boxed{\text{Root estimate after 3 iterations } c_3 = 1.375}$$

---

### Example 2: Minimum Iterations Calculation (GATE PYQ Type)
**Problem:** How many minimum iterations of the Bisection Method are required to find a root of $f(x)=0$ in the interval $[1, 3]$ with an absolute error less than $10^{-4}$?

**Solution (Using Speed Trick 1):**
$a_0 = 1, b_0 = 3 \implies b_0 - a_0 = 2$. Tolerance $\epsilon = 10^{-4}$.

$$n > \frac{\log_{10}(b_0 - a_0) - \log_{10}\epsilon}{\log_{10} 2}$$
$$n > \frac{\log_{10}(2) - (-4)}{0.30103} = \frac{0.30103 + 4}{0.30103} = \frac{4.30103}{0.30103} \approx 14.288$$

Round UP to next integer: $n_{min} = 15$.

$$\boxed{n_{min} = 15 \text{ iterations}}$$

---

### Example 3: Transcendental Equation Root (GATE Type)
**Problem:** Find the midpoint $c_2$ after 2 iterations of Bisection Method for $f(x) = x e^x - 1 = 0$ starting in $[0, 1]$.

**Solution:**
$f(0) = 0 \cdot e^0 - 1 = -1 \quad (-)$  
$f(1) = 1 \cdot e^1 - 1 = e - 1 \approx +1.718 \quad (+)$

**Iteration 1:**
$$c_1 = \frac{0 + 1}{2} = 0.5$$
$$f(0.5) = 0.5 e^{0.5} - 1 = 0.5(1.6487) - 1 = 0.82436 - 1 = -0.17564 \quad (-)$$
Since $f(0.5) (-) \cdot f(1) (+) < 0$, new bracket is $[0.5, 1]$.

**Iteration 2:**
$$c_2 = \frac{0.5 + 1}{2} = 0.75$$

$$\boxed{c_2 = 0.75}$$

---

### Example 4: Convergence Order MCQ (GATE 1-Marker)
**Problem:** The order of convergence of the Bisection Method is:  
(a) $0$  
(b) $1$  
(c) $1.618$  
(d) $2$

**Solution:**  
The Bisection Method halves the error at each step ($|e_{n+1}| = 0.5 |e_n|^1$), which corresponds to **Linear Convergence** (order $p=1$).

$$\boxed{\text{Correct Answer: (b) } 1}$$

## ⚠️ Common Exam Traps & Pitfalls

> 🔴 **Trap 1: Starting Bisection when $f(a) \cdot f(b) > 0$**  
> If $f(a)$ and $f(b)$ have the **same sign**, IVT does NOT guarantee a root in $[a,b]$. Bisection CANNOT be applied! Always check signs first.

> 🔴 **Trap 2: Forgetting to Round UP for Minimum Iterations**  
> If formula gives $n > 14.12$, the minimum number of iterations is **$15$**, NOT $14$. Rounding down leaves error $> \epsilon$.

> 🔴 **Trap 3: Natural Log vs Log Base 10 Substitution**  
> You can use either $\ln$ (base $e$) or $\log_{10}$ (base 10), but be consistent!  
> $n > \frac{\ln(b-a) - \ln\epsilon}{\ln 2} = \frac{\log_{10}(b-a) - \log_{10}\epsilon}{\log_{10} 2}$ — do NOT mix $\ln$ in numerator with $\log_{10}$ in denominator!

> 🔴 **Trap 4: Confusing Order of Convergence with Rate Factor**  
> - **Order of convergence** $p = 1$ (linear).  
> - **Rate factor** $C = 0.5$ (halving factor).  
> Don't confuse $p=1$ with $C=0.5$ in MCQ options!

## 📌 Part I: Concept & First Principles of Partial Derivatives

### 1. Definition from First Principles

For a function of two variables $z = f(x, y)$:

- **Partial Derivative with respect to $x$** (hold $y$ constant):
  $$f_x = \frac{\partial f}{\partial x} = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{f(x+h, y) - f(x, y)}{h}$$

- **Partial Derivative with respect to $y$** (hold $x$ constant):
  $$f_y = \frac{\partial f}{\partial y} = \lim_{k \to 0} \frac{f(x, y+k) - f(x, y)}{k}$$

> 💡 **Golden Rule:** When differentiating partially with respect to variable $V$, treat **ALL OTHER variables as fixed numerical constants**!

---

### 2. Geometric Interpretation & Tangent Plane

- $z = f(x, y)$ represents a 3D **surface** in $\mathbb{R}^3$.
- $f_x(x_0, y_0)$ is the **slope of the tangent line** to the curve of intersection between the surface $z = f(x, y)$ and the plane $y = y_0$.
- $f_y(x_0, y_0)$ is the **slope of the tangent line** to the curve of intersection with plane $x = x_0$.

#### 📐 Tangent Plane Equation to $z = f(x,y)$ at $(x_0, y_0, z_0)$:

$$\boxed{z - z_0 = f_x(x_0, y_0)(x - x_0) + f_y(x_0, y_0)(y - y_0)}$$

#### 📐 Normal Vector to Surface $F(x, y, z) = 0$ at $(x_0, y_0, z_0)$:

$$\vec{N} = \nabla F = F_x \hat{i} + F_y \hat{j} + F_z \hat{k}$$

## 🧠 Part II: Higher-Order Partials & Clairaut's (Schwarz's) Theorem

### 1. Notation for Second-Order Partials

$$f_{xx} = \frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial x^2} = \frac{\partial}{\partial x}\left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}\right)$$
$$f_{yy} = \frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial y^2} = \frac{\partial}{\partial y}\left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial y}\right)$$
$$f_{xy} = \frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial y \partial x} = \frac{\partial}{\partial y}\left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}\right) \quad (\text{differentiate w.r.t. } x \text{ first, then } y)$$
$$f_{yx} = \frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial x \partial y} = \frac{\partial}{\partial x}\left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial y}\right) \quad (\text{differentiate w.r.t. } y \text{ first, then } x)$$

---

### 2. Clairaut's (Schwarz's) Theorem

$$\boxed{f_{xy} = f_{yx}}$$

**Condition:** Holds at any point $(x,y)$ where both mixed partial derivatives $f_{xy}$ and $f_{yx}$ are **continuous**.

> 🎯 **GATE Note:** For all standard functions (polynomials, exponentials, trig, log) tested in GATE, mixed partials are ALWAYS equal: $f_{xy} = f_{yx}$.

---

## 🧠 Part III: Total Differential, Differentiability & Error Propagation

### 1. Total Differential

The **Total Differential** $dz$ estimates the change in $z = f(x,y)$ due to small changes $dx$ and $dy$:

$$\boxed{dz = \frac{\partial f}{\partial x}\,dx + \frac{\partial f}{\partial y}\,dy = f_x\,dx + f_y\,dy}$$

For three variables $w = f(x, y, z)$:
$$dw = f_x\,dx + f_y\,dy + f_z\,dz$$

---

### 2. Error Propagation & Percentage Error (GATE High-Yield!)

If $\Delta x, \Delta y, \Delta z$ are small errors in measuring $x, y, z$, the approximate error in $f(x,y,z)$ is:

$$\Delta f \approx \frac{\partial f}{\partial x}\Delta x + \frac{\partial f}{\partial y}\Delta y + \frac{\partial f}{\partial z}\Delta z$$

#### ⚡ Power-Product Instant Percentage Error Rule:
For a physical quantity $Q = C \cdot x^a y^b z^c$:

$$\boxed{\left|\frac{\Delta Q}{Q}\right| \le |a|\left|\frac{\Delta x}{x}\right| + |b|\left|\frac{\Delta y}{y}\right| + |c|\left|\frac{\Delta z}{z}\right|}$$

$$\text{Percentage Error in } Q \le |a|(\% \text{ error in } x) + |b|(\% \text{ error in } y) + |c|(\% \text{ error in } z)$$

---

### 3. Multivariable Differentiability Hierarchy

$$\begin{array}{ccc}
\text{Continuous } f_x \text{ and } f_y & \implies & \mathbf{f(x,y) \text{ is Differentiable}} \\
& & \Downarrow \\
& & \mathbf{f(x,y) \text{ is Continuous}} \quad \text{AND} \quad \mathbf{f_x, f_y \text{ Exist}}
\end{array}$$

> 🔴 **Critical GATE Trap:**  
> The existence of $f_x$ and $f_y$ at a point **DOES NOT** guarantee that $f(x,y)$ is continuous or differentiable at that point!  
> Continuity of $f_x$ and $f_y$ **DOES** guarantee differentiability.

---

## 🧠 Part IV: Multivariable Chain Rule & Implicit Differentiation

### 1. Chain Rule Cases

#### Case A: One Independent Parameter ($t$)
If $z = f(x,y)$ where $x = x(t)$ and $y = y(t)$:

$$\boxed{\frac{dz}{dt} = \frac{\partial f}{\partial x}\frac{dx}{dt} + \frac{\partial f}{\partial y}\frac{dy}{dt}}$$

#### Case B: Two Independent Parameters ($s, t$)
If $z = f(x,y)$ where $x = x(s,t)$ and $y = y(s,t)$:

$$\frac{\partial z}{\partial s} = \frac{\partial f}{\partial x}\frac{\partial x}{\partial s} + \frac{\partial f}{\partial y}\frac{\partial y}{\partial s}$$
$$\frac{\partial z}{\partial t} = \frac{\partial f}{\partial x}\frac{\partial x}{\partial t} + \frac{\partial f}{\partial y}\frac{\partial y}{\partial t}$$

---

### 2. Implicit Function Partial Derivatives

If $F(x, y, z) = 0$ defines $z$ implicitly as a function of $x$ and $y$:

$$\boxed{\frac{\partial z}{\partial x} = -\frac{F_x}{F_z} = -\frac{\partial F/\partial x}{\partial F/\partial z} \qquad (F_z \neq 0)}$$

$$\boxed{\frac{\partial z}{\partial y} = -\frac{F_y}{F_z} = -\frac{\partial F/\partial y}{\partial F/\partial z} \qquad (F_z \neq 0)}$$

---

### 3. The Cyclic Implicit Identity (GATE Classic!)

For any relation $F(x, y, z) = 0$:

$$\boxed{\frac{\partial x}{\partial y} \cdot \frac{\partial y}{\partial z} \cdot \frac{\partial z}{\partial x} = -1}$$

> 🎯 **Mnemonic:** Cyclic product of implicit partials equals **$-1$**, NOT $+1$!

*Proof:* $\frac{\partial x}{\partial y} = -\frac{F_y}{F_x}$, $\frac{\partial y}{\partial z} = -\frac{F_z}{F_y}$, $\frac{\partial z}{\partial x} = -\frac{F_x}{F_z} \implies \left(-\frac{F_y}{F_x}\right)\left(-\frac{F_z}{F_y}\right)\left(-\frac{F_x}{F_z}\right) = -1$.

---

## 🧠 Part V: Euler's Theorem on Homogeneous Functions

### 1. Homogeneous Function Definition

A function $f(x,y)$ is **homogeneous of degree $n$** if:

$$f(tx, ty) = t^n f(x, y) \quad \forall t > 0$$

#### Quick Degree Rules:
- Degree of $x^a y^b$ is $a + b$.
- Degree of $\frac{P_m(x,y)}{Q_k(x,y)}$ is $m - k$ (numerator degree minus denominator degree).
- Degree of trig/log/exp with ratio arguments like $\sin(y/x)$ or $\ln(x/y)$ is **0**.

---

### 2. First-Order Euler's Theorem

$$\boxed{x \frac{\partial f}{\partial x} + y \frac{\partial f}{\partial y} = n \cdot f(x, y)}$$

For three variables $f(x, y, z)$ of degree $n$:
$$x f_x + y f_y + z f_z = n \cdot f(x, y, z)$$

---

### 3. Second-Order Euler's Theorem

$$\boxed{x^2 \frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial x^2} + 2xy \frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial x \partial y} + y^2 \frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial y^2} = n(n-1) f(x, y)}$$

---

### 4. Composite Homogeneous Functions (GATE AIR-1 Shortcut!)

If $u = g(x, y)$ is **NOT** homogeneous, but $F(u)$ is a homogeneous function of degree $n$:

$$\boxed{x \frac{\partial u}{\partial x} + y \frac{\partial u}{\partial y} = n \frac{F(u)}{F'(u)} = G(u)}$$

$$\boxed{x^2 u_{xx} + 2xy u_{xy} + y^2 u_{yy} = G(u)[G'(u) - 1]}$$

#### 🎯 Standard Composite Formulas Table:

| Function $u$ | Transformation $F(u)$ | Degree $n$ of $F(u)$ | $x u_x + y u_y$ Formula | Result $G(u)$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $u = \sin^{-1}\!\left(\frac{x+y}{\sqrt{x}+\sqrt{y}}\right)$ | $\sin u = \frac{x+y}{\sqrt{x}+\sqrt{y}}$ | $1 - 1/2 = 1/2$ | $\frac{1}{2}\frac{\sin u}{\cos u}$ | $\mathbf{\frac{1}{2}\tan u}$ |
| $u = \tan^{-1}\!\left(\frac{x^3+y^3}{x-y}\right)$ | $\tan u = \frac{x^3+y^3}{x-y}$ | $3 - 1 = 2$ | $2\frac{\tan u}{\sec^2 u}$ | $\mathbf{\sin(2u)}$ |
| $u = \ln\!\left(\frac{x^2+y^2}{x+y}\right)$ | $e^u = \frac{x^2+y^2}{x+y}$ | $2 - 1 = 1$ | $1\frac{e^u}{e^u}$ | $\mathbf{1}$ |
| $u = \cos^{-1}\!\left(\frac{x+y}{x^2+y^2}\right)$ | $\cos u = \frac{x+y}{x^2+y^2}$ | $1 - 2 = -1$ | $-1\frac{\cos u}{-\sin u}$ | $\mathbf{\cot u}$ |

---

## ⚡ AIR-1 Topper Speed Tricks & Mnemonics

### 🚀 Trick 1: Composite Euler's Rule in 5 Seconds
For $u = \tan^{-1}\left(\frac{\text{deg } m}{\text{deg } k}\right)$:
- Degree $n = m - k$
- $x u_x + y u_y = n \frac{\tan u}{\sec^2 u} = \frac{n}{2}\sin(2u)$
- Instant answer! No derivatives needed!

---

### 🚀 Trick 2: Percentage Error for $Q = x^a y^b z^c$
$$\% \text{ Error in } Q = |a|(\% \text{ in } x) + |b|(\% \text{ in } y) + |c|(\% \text{ in } z)$$

*Example:* Kinetic Energy $E = \frac{1}{2} m v^2$. Error in $m = 1\%$, error in $v = 2\%$.  
$\% \text{ Error in } E = 1(1\%) + 2(2\%) = \mathbf{5\%}$ in 2 seconds!

---

### 🚀 Trick 3: Cyclic Derivative Rule
$$\frac{\partial x}{\partial y}\frac{\partial y}{\partial z}\frac{\partial z}{\partial x} = \mathbf{-1}$$
Always choose $-1$ in MCQs (never $+1$).

---

### 🚀 Trick 4: Tree Diagram Method for Chain Rule
To avoid missing cross-terms:
1. Draw top variable $z$.
2. Branch down to intermediate variables $x, y$.
3. Branch down to final variables $s, t$.
4. Multiply along branches, sum across paths.

## 📝 Step-by-Step Solved GATE Examples

### Example 1: Composite Euler's Theorem (GATE PYQ)
**Problem:** If $u = \tan^{-1}\left(\frac{x^3 + y^3}{x - y}\right)$, evaluate $x \frac{\partial u}{\partial x} + y \frac{\partial u}{\partial y}$.

**Solution:**
1. Take $\tan$ of both sides: $F(u) = \tan u = \frac{x^3+y^3}{x-y}$.
2. Check degree of $F(u)$: Numerator degree $= 3$, denominator degree $= 1$. $n = 3 - 1 = 2$.
3. Apply Composite Euler's Formula:
   $$x u_x + y u_y = n \frac{F(u)}{F'(u)} = 2 \frac{\tan u}{\sec^2 u} = 2 \sin u \cos u = \mathbf{\sin(2u)}$$

$$\boxed{x u_x + y u_y = \sin(2u)}$$

---

### Example 2: Second-Order Composite Euler
**Problem:** For $u = \tan^{-1}\left(\frac{x^3+y^3}{x-y}\right)$ from Example 1, evaluate $x^2 u_{xx} + 2xy u_{xy} + y^2 u_{yy}$.

**Solution:**
From Example 1: $G(u) = \sin(2u)$.
Apply 2nd-order Composite Euler Formula:
$$\text{Expression} = G(u)[G'(u) - 1] = \sin(2u)[2\cos(2u) - 1] = \mathbf{2\sin(2u)\cos(2u) - \sin(2u)} = \mathbf{\sin(4u) - \sin(2u)}$$

$$\boxed{\sin(4u) - \sin(2u)}$$

---

### Example 3: Percentage Error Propagation
**Problem:** Aerodynamic drag force is $D = \frac{1}{2} \rho V^2 S C_D$. If density $\rho$ has 1% error, velocity $V$ has 3% error, and area $S$ has 2% error (assume $C_D$ exact), find maximum percentage error in drag $D$.

**Solution:**
$$D \propto \rho^1 V^2 S^1$$
$$\% \text{ Error in } D \le 1(\% \text{ error in } \rho) + 2(\% \text{ error in } V) + 1(\% \text{ error in } S)$$
$$\% \text{ Error in } D \le 1(1\%) + 2(3\%) + 1(2\%) = 1 + 6 + 2 = \mathbf{9\%}$$

$$\boxed{\text{Max Error in Drag} = 9\%}$$

---

### Example 4: Tangent Plane to Surface
**Problem:** Find the equation of the tangent plane to $z = x^2 + y^2$ at the point $(1, 2, 5)$.

**Solution:**
$f(x,y) = x^2 + y^2$  
$f_x = 2x \implies f_x(1,2) = 2$  
$f_y = 2y \implies f_y(1,2) = 4$  
$z_0 = 5, x_0 = 1, y_0 = 2$

Tangent plane formula:
$$z - 5 = 2(x - 1) + 4(y - 2)$$
$$z - 5 = 2x - 2 + 4y - 8 \implies 2x + 4y - z = 5$$

$$\boxed{2x + 4y - z = 5}$$

## ⚠️ Common Exam Traps & Pitfalls

> 🔴 **Trap 1: Treating Partial Derivatives Like Ordinary Derivatives**  
> *Mistake:* $\frac{\partial}{\partial x}(x^2 y^3) = 2x \cdot 3y^2$ ❌  
> *Correction:* Treat $y^3$ as a constant multiplier: $\frac{\partial}{\partial x}(x^2 y^3) = 2xy^3$ ✅

> 🔴 **Trap 2: Incorrect Degree Calculation in Fractions**  
> *Mistake:* Degree of $\frac{x^3+y^3}{x-y}$ is $3 \times 1 = 3$ ❌  
> *Correction:* Degree of fraction $= \text{degree of numerator} - \text{degree of denominator} = 3 - 1 = 2$ ✅

> 🔴 **Trap 3: Cyclic Product Sign Error**  
> *Mistake:* $\frac{\partial x}{\partial y}\frac{\partial y}{\partial z}\frac{\partial z}{\partial x} = +1$ ❌  
> *Correction:* It is ALWAYS equal to **$-1$**!

> 🔴 **Trap 4: Assuming Partials Existence Gives Continuity**  
> *Mistake:* "If $f_x$ and $f_y$ exist at $(0,0)$, then $f$ is continuous at $(0,0)$." ❌  
> *Correction:* FALSE! Existence of partials does NOT imply continuity. Only CONTINUITY of partials implies differentiability (and thus continuity).

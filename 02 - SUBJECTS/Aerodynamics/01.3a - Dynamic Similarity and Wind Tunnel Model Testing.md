## 📌 1. What is This Topic? (Zero to AIR-1 Overview)

**Dynamic Similarity** is the core physical principle enabling aerodynamicists to test sub-scale models of aircraft, rockets, missiles, and space vehicles in wind tunnels or water channels, and accurately extrapolate the measured aerodynamic forces ($L, D, Y$), moments ($M_x, M_y, M_z$), pressure distributions ($C_p$), and flutter boundaries to the full-scale flying prototype.

To achieve complete aerodynamic similitude between a model ($m$) and prototype ($p$), three geometric and physical conditions must be simultaneously satisfied:
1. **Geometric Similarity:** Model and prototype possess identical shapes with a constant linear scale factor $\lambda_L = L_m / L_p$.
2. **Kinematic Similarity:** Velocity vectors at corresponding (homologous) points are parallel and maintain a constant ratio ($V_m / V_p = \lambda_V$), ensuring geometrically similar streamline patterns.
3. **Dynamic Similarity:** Forces acting on corresponding fluid elements (inertia, viscous, pressure, compressibility, gravity, surface tension) maintain constant ratios, which mathematically mandates the **equality of all relevant non-dimensional numbers ($Re_m = Re_p, M_m = M_p, Fr_m = Fr_p, We_m = We_p$)**.

> 💡 **GATE Target:** 1–2 direct questions (MCQ/NAT, ~2–4 marks) every year in Aerodynamics testing wind tunnel model scale ratios, power scaling, water tunnel equivalence, and pressurized tunnel operations.

## 🧠 Part I: The Similitude Triad — Geometric, Kinematic & Dynamic

---

### 1. Geometric Similarity
Two flow configurations are geometrically similar if all physical dimensions in the three spatial coordinate directions scale by the identical geometric scale factor $\lambda_L$:

$$\mathbf{\frac{x_m}{x_p} = \frac{y_m}{y_p} = \frac{z_m}{z_p} = \frac{L_m}{L_p} \equiv \lambda_L}$$

- **Area Scale Ratio:** $\lambda_A = \frac{A_m}{A_p} = \lambda_L^2$
- **Volume Scale Ratio:** $\lambda_{\mathcal{V}} = \frac{\mathcal{V}_m}{\mathcal{V}_p} = \lambda_L^3$
- **Angle of Attack Invariant:** $\alpha_m = \alpha_p, \quad \beta_m = \beta_p$
- **Relative Surface Roughness Invariant:** $\left(\frac{k_s}{L}\right)_m = \left(\frac{k_s}{L}\right)_p$

---

### 2. Kinematic Similarity
Kinematic similarity requires that the fluid velocities at homologous (corresponding) spatial locations in the model and prototype flow fields have **identical spatial directions** and a **constant velocity magnitude scale ratio $\lambda_V$**:

$$\mathbf{\frac{\vec{V}_m(x_m, y_m, z_m, t_m)}{\vec{V}_p(x_p, y_p, z_p, t_p)} = \lambda_V = \text{constant}}$$

- **Consequence:** The streamline, pathline, and streakline flow patterns in the wind tunnel are **exact geometric replicas** of the prototype flight flow pattern.
- **Kinematic Time Scale Ratio:**
  $$\mathbf{\lambda_t = \frac{t_m}{t_p} = \frac{L_m / V_m}{L_p / V_p} = \frac{\lambda_L}{\lambda_V}}$$
- **Acceleration Scale Ratio:**
  $$\mathbf{\lambda_a = \frac{a_m}{a_p} = \frac{V_m / t_m}{V_p / t_p} = \frac{\lambda_V^2}{\lambda_L}}$$

---

### 3. Dynamic Similarity & The Fluid Force Polygon

Dynamic similarity requires that the forces acting on corresponding fluid parcels in model and prototype have identical directions and a constant force scale ratio $\lambda_F$:

$$\mathbf{\frac{\vec{F}_{i,m}}{\vec{F}_{i,p}} = \lambda_F = \text{constant for all force types } i}$$

```
                THE FORCES ACTING ON AN ACCELERATING FLUID ELEMENT
                 Inertia Force = Σ External Forces (Newton's 2nd Law)
                                         │
                                         ▼
                 F_Inertia = F_Pressure + F_Viscous + F_Gravity
                           + F_Compressibility + F_SurfaceTension
```

## 🧠 Part II: Mathematical Proof via Non-Dimensional Navier-Stokes Equations

The rigorous mathematical proof of dynamic similarity is established by non-dimensionalizing the governing Incompressible Navier-Stokes equations:

$$\rho \left[ \frac{\partial\vec{V}}{\partial t} + (\vec{V}\cdot\nabla)\vec{V} \right] = -\nabla p + \mu \nabla^2 \vec{V} + \rho \vec{g}$$

### 1. Dimensionless Variables:
Define non-dimensional spatial coordinates, velocities, time, pressure, and gravity based on characteristic freestream scales ($L, V_\infty, \rho_\infty, p_\infty$):

$$x^* = \frac{x}{L}, \quad \vec{V}^* = \frac{\vec{V}}{V_\infty}, \quad t^* = t\left(\frac{V_\infty}{L}\right), \quad p^* = \frac{p - p_\infty}{\rho_\infty V_\infty^2}, \quad \hat{g} = \frac{\vec{g}}{g}$$

### 2. Dimensionless Differential Equation:
Substituting these dimensionless variables into the Navier-Stokes equation:

$$\mathbf{\frac{\partial\vec{V}^*}{\partial t^*} + (\vec{V}^*\cdot\nabla^*)\vec{V}^* = -\nabla^* p^* + \frac{1}{Re}\nabla^{*2}\vec{V}^* + \frac{1}{Fr^2}\hat{g}}$$

where:
- $\mathbf{Re \equiv \frac{\rho_\infty V_\infty L}{\mu}}$ (Reynolds Number)
- $\mathbf{Fr \equiv \frac{V_\infty}{\sqrt{g L}}}$ (Froude Number)

> 🏆 **THE FUNDAMENTAL THEOREM OF SIMILITUDE:**
> Since the non-dimensional boundary conditions are identical (Geometric Similarity), **if the dimensionless coefficients in the governing equations match identically ($Re_m = Re_p$ and $Fr_m = Fr_p$), the mathematical solution $\vec{V}^*(x^*, t^*)$ and $p^*(x^*, t^*)$ MUST BE IDENTICAL everywhere!**  
> Consequently, all non-dimensional aerodynamic coefficients ($C_L, C_D, C_m, C_p$) are **STRICTLY EQUAL**:
> $$\mathbf{C_{L,m} = C_{L,p}, \quad C_{D,m} = C_{D,p}, \quad C_{m,m} = C_{m,p}, \quad C_{p,m}(x^*) = C_{p,p}(x^*)}$$

│    ($M < 0.3$, No free surface)     │                               │ $\mathbf{\frac{F_m}{F_p} = \left(\frac{\rho_m}{\rho_p}\right) \left(\frac{\mu_p}{\mu_m}\right)^2 = \left(\frac{\rho_m}{\rho_p}\right) \left(\frac{\nu_m}{\nu_p}\right)^2}$ │
│                                     │                               │ $\mathbf{\frac{P_m}{P_p} = \frac{F_m V_m}{F_p V_p} = \left(\frac{\rho_m}{\rho_p}\right) \left(\frac{\nu_m}{\nu_p}\right)^3 \left(\frac{1}{\lambda_L}\right)}$ │

├─────────────────────────────────────┼───────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤

├─────────────────────────────────────┼───────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ **4. Unsteady Aeroacoustic / Wakes**│ $\mathbf{St_m = St_p}$        │ $\mathbf{f_m = f_p \left(\frac{V_m}{V_p}\right) \left(\frac{1}{\lambda_L}\right)}$ (Vortex Shedding Frequency) │

## 🧠 Part IV: Incomplete / Partial Similarity & Practical Wind Tunnel Corrections

In real-world testing, achieving 100% exact dynamic similarity across all parameters ($Re, M, Fr, We, Kn$) is impossible. Aerodynamicists apply specific correction techniques:

```
                  PRACTICAL WIND TUNNEL SCALE EFFECTS & CORRECTIONS
                                          │
         ┌────────────────────────────────┼────────────────────────────────┐
         ▼                                ▼                                ▼
BOUNDARY LAYER TRIPPING           WALL BLOCKAGE CORRECTIONS        REYNOLDS EXTRAPOLATION
• Low model Re causes             • Solid Blockage (ε_sb):         • Measure C_D at model Re
  premature laminar separation      Model volume constricts flow   • Use flat-plate friction laws
• Apply grit / trip strips        • Wake Blockage (ε_wb):            to extrapolate skin friction:
  to force turbulent boundary       Wake deficit increases speed     C_f,p = C_f,m - ΔC_f(Re)
  layer matching prototype        • Corrected speed:
                                    V_corrected = V_uncorr (1 + ε)
```

---

### 1. Boundary Layer Tripping
- Prototype aircraft fly at high Reynolds numbers ($Re \sim 10^7 - 10^8$), possessing **turbulent boundary layers** over most of their wing chords.
- Subscale wind tunnel models operate at lower Reynolds numbers ($Re \sim 10^5 - 10^6$), which would naturally maintain laminar flow and suffer **unrealistic laminar separation bubbles**.
- **Solution:** Place **carborundum grit strips, trip wires, or zig-zag tape** near the model leading edge ($x/c \approx 0.05$) to trigger artificial turbulence transition, ensuring realistic turbulent boundary layer behavior and separation points.

---

### 2. Wind Tunnel Blockage Interference Corrections
When a model is mounted inside a closed-throat wind tunnel of test section area $C$:
- **Solid Blockage Factor ($\epsilon_{sb}$):** The physical volume of the model constricts the flow area, accelerating the airflow around the model:
  $$\epsilon_{sb} = \frac{K_1 \mathcal{V}_{\text{model}}}{C^{3/2}}$$
- **Wake Blockage Factor ($\epsilon_{wb}$):** The low-speed wake behind the model acts as a physical blockage:
  $$\epsilon_{wb} = \frac{S}{4 C} C_{D,\text{uncorrected}}$$
- **Total Velocity Blockage Correction:**
  $$\mathbf{V_{\text{corrected}} = V_\infty (1 + \epsilon_{sb} + \epsilon_{wb})}$$
  $$\mathbf{q_{\text{corrected}} = q_\infty (1 + 2\epsilon_{sb} + 2\epsilon_{wb})}$$

## ⚡ AIR-1 Topper Mnemonics, Shortcuts & Speed Tricks

### 🚀 Shortcut 1: The Pressurized Tunnel Force Invariant
In a pressurized wind tunnel with identical gas temperature ($T_m = T_p$) where pressure is increased to match Reynolds number at the same Mach number ($p_m = p_p / \lambda_L$ and $V_m = V_p$):
$$\mathbf{\frac{F_m}{F_p} = \left(\frac{p_m}{p_p}\right) \lambda_L^2 = \left(\frac{1}{\lambda_L}\right) \lambda_L^2 = \lambda_L}$$
- For a $1:10$ scale model ($\lambda_L = 0.1$), the aerodynamic force on the model in the pressurized tunnel is **exactly $10\%$ of the prototype force** ($F_m = 0.10 F_p$).

---

### 🚀 Shortcut 2: Water Tunnel Testing of Aircraft (Low Speed)
To test an aircraft model in a water tunnel ($\rho_w = 1000\text{ kg/m}^3, \nu_w = 1.0\times 10^{-6}\text{ m}^2/\text{s}$) compared to flight in air ($\rho_a = 1.2\text{ kg/m}^3, \nu_a = 1.5\times 10^{-5}\text{ m}^2/\text{s}$):
$$\frac{\nu_w}{\nu_a} = \frac{1.0\times 10^{-6}}{1.5\times 10^{-5}} = \frac{1}{15}$$
$$V_w = V_{\text{air}} \left(\frac{1}{15}\right) \left(\frac{1}{\lambda_L}\right)$$
- If testing a $1:15$ scale model ($\lambda_L = 1/15$), **$V_w = V_{\text{air}}$** (Identical test speed!).
- **Force Ratio in Water Tunnel:**
  $$\frac{F_w}{F_{\text{air}}} = \left(\frac{\rho_w}{\rho_a}\right)\left(\frac{\nu_w}{\nu_a}\right)^2 = \left(\frac{1000}{1.2}\right)\left(\frac{1}{15}\right)^2 = \frac{833.3}{225} \approx \mathbf{3.70}$$
  *(The model experiences $3.7\times$ higher force in water despite being $15\times$ smaller!).*

## 📝 Step-by-Step Solved GATE Examples (Zero to GATE Topper Level)

### Example 1: Wind Tunnel vs. Water Tunnel Aerodynamic Equivalence (GATE Standard)
**Problem:** A $1:10$ scale model of a low-speed UAV is tested in a water tunnel ($\rho_w = 1000\text{ kg/m}^3, \mu_w = 1.0\times 10^{-3}\text{ Pa}\cdot\text{s}$). The prototype UAV flies at $V_p = 30\text{ m/s}$ in standard air ($\rho_a = 1.2\text{ kg/m}^3, \mu_a = 1.8\times 10^{-5}\text{ Pa}\cdot\text{s}$).
1. Calculate the required water tunnel test velocity $V_m$ to satisfy dynamic similarity ($Re_m = Re_p$).
2. If the drag force measured on the water tunnel model is $F_m = 450\text{ N}$, calculate the drag force $F_p$ on the full-scale UAV in flight.
3. Compute the aerodynamic power required to overcome drag for the prototype UAV.

> [!faq]- 🔍 View Solution & Detailed Explanation
> **1. Water Tunnel Model Velocity ($V_m$):**
> $$Re_m = Re_p \implies \frac{\rho_w V_m L_m}{\mu_w} = \frac{\rho_a V_p L_p}{\mu_a}$$
> $$V_m = V_p \left(\frac{L_p}{L_m}\right) \left(\frac{\rho_a}{\rho_w}\right) \left(\frac{\mu_w}{\mu_a}\right)$$
> Scale factor $\frac{L_p}{L_m} = 10.0$.
> $$V_m = (30\text{ m/s})(10.0) \left(\frac{1.2}{1000}\right) \left(\frac{1.0\times 10^{-3}}{1.8\times 10^{-5}}\right) = 300 \times 0.0012 \times 55.556 = \mathbf{20.0\text{ m/s}}$$
> 
> ---
> **2. Prototype Drag Force ($F_p$):**
> Drag force scaling law:
> $$\frac{F_m}{F_p} = \frac{\rho_w V_m^2 L_m^2}{\rho_a V_p^2 L_p^2} = \left(\frac{\rho_w}{\rho_a}\right) \left(\frac{V_m}{V_p}\right)^2 \left(\frac{L_m}{L_p}\right)^2$$
> $$\frac{F_m}{F_p} = \left(\frac{1000}{1.2}\right) \left(\frac{20}{30}\right)^2 \left(\frac{1}{10}\right)^2 = (833.33) \left(\frac{4}{9}\right) (0.01) = 3.7037$$
> $$F_p = \frac{F_m}{3.7037} = \frac{450\text{ N}}{3.7037} = \mathbf{121.5\text{ N}}$$
> 
> ---
> **3. Prototype Aerodynamic Drag Power:**
> $$P_p = F_p V_p = (121.5\text{ N})(30\text{ m/s}) = 3645\text{ W} = \mathbf{3.645\text{ kW}}$$

### Example 2: Transonic Transport Aircraft Wing Scale Modeling
**Problem:** A $1:8$ scale model ($\lambda_L = 1/8 = 0.125$) of a transonic transport wing is tested in a pressurized variable-density wind tunnel. The prototype aircraft cruises at $M_p = 0.82$ at an altitude of $11\text{ km}$ ($p_p = 22.6\text{ kPa}, T_p = 216.7\text{ K}, \mu_p = 1.42\times 10^{-5}\text{ Pa}\cdot\text{s}$). The wind tunnel operates at static temperature $T_m = 250\text{ K}$ ($\mu_m = 1.60\times 10^{-5}\text{ Pa}\cdot\text{s}$).
1. Find the required tunnel airspeed $V_m$.
2. Calculate the required tunnel static pressure $p_m$ to match both Mach and Reynolds numbers.
3. If the model lift force is $L_m = 2400\text{ N}$, compute the prototype lift force $L_p$.

> [!faq]- 🔍 View Solution & Detailed Explanation
> **1. Mach Matching and Airspeed:**
> $M_m = M_p = 0.82$.
> Speed of sound in tunnel:
> $$a_m = \sqrt{\gamma R T_m} = \sqrt{(1.4)(287)(250)} = \sqrt{100,450} \approx 316.94\text{ m/s}$$
> Tunnel airspeed:
> $$V_m = M_m a_m = (0.82)(316.94) = \mathbf{259.89\text{ m/s}}$$
> Prototype speed of sound:
> $$a_p = \sqrt{\gamma R T_p} = \sqrt{(1.4)(287)(216.7)} = \sqrt{87,070} \approx 295.08\text{ m/s}$$
> Prototype flight speed:
> $$V_p = M_p a_p = (0.82)(295.08) = 241.96\text{ m/s}$$
> 
> ---
> **2. Tunnel Static Pressure ($p_m$):**
> Reynolds matching: $\frac{\rho_m V_m L_m}{\mu_m} = \frac{\rho_p V_p L_p}{\mu_p}$
> $$\rho_m = \rho_p \left(\frac{L_p}{L_m}\right) \left(\frac{V_p}{V_m}\right) \left(\frac{\mu_m}{\mu_p}\right) = \rho_p (8.0) \left(\frac{241.96}{259.89}\right) \left(\frac{1.60\times 10^{-5}}{1.42\times 10^{-5}}\right)$$
> $$\frac{\rho_m}{\rho_p} = (8.0)(0.9310)(1.1268) = 8.392$$
> Pressure ratio:
> $$\frac{p_m}{p_p} = \left(\frac{\rho_m}{\rho_p}\right) \left(\frac{T_m}{T_p}\right) = (8.392)\left(\frac{250}{216.7}\right) = (8.392)(1.1537) = 9.682$$
> $$p_m = (9.682)(22.6\text{ kPa}) = \mathbf{218.8\text{ kPa} \approx 2.19\text{ bar}}$$
> 
> ---
> **3. Prototype Lift Force ($L_p$):**
> $$L = C_L \left(\frac{1}{2}\rho V^2 S\right) \implies \frac{L_m}{L_p} = \left(\frac{\rho_m}{\rho_p}\right)\left(\frac{V_m}{V_p}\right)^2 \left(\frac{L_m}{L_p}\right)^2 \quad (\text{Since } C_{L,m} = C_{L,p})$$
> $$\frac{L_m}{L_p} = (8.392) \left(\frac{259.89}{241.96}\right)^2 \left(\frac{1}{8}\right)^2 = (8.392)(1.1537)\left(\frac{1}{64}\right) = \frac{9.682}{64} = 0.15128$$
> $$L_p = \frac{L_m}{0.15128} = \frac{2400\text{ N}}{0.15128} \approx \mathbf{15,865\text{ N} \approx 15.87\text{ kN}}$$

## ⚠️ Common Exam Traps & Pitfalls (Where Marks are Lost)

> 🔴 **Trap 1: Confusing Viscosity Ratios in Force Scaling**
> - *Mistake:* Using $\frac{F_m}{F_p} = \lambda_L^2$ for low-speed models in different fluid media (e.g. water vs. air).
> - *Correction:* $\frac{F_m}{F_p} = \left(\frac{\rho_m}{\rho_p}\right)\left(\frac{V_m}{V_p}\right)^2 \lambda_L^2 = \left(\frac{\rho_m}{\rho_p}\right)\left(\frac{\nu_m}{\nu_p}\right)^2$. Force scales by density and kinematic viscosity squared!

> 🔴 **Trap 2: Neglecting Temperature Dependence of Speed of Sound**
> - *Mistake:* Assuming $a_m = a_p$ in high-speed wind tunnels where model and prototype temperatures differ.
> - *Correction:* Speed of sound depends strictly on temperature ($a = \sqrt{\gamma R T}$). If $T_m \neq T_p$, matching Mach numbers requires $V_m / V_p = \sqrt{T_m / T_p}$.

> 🔴 **Trap 3: Forgetting Blockage Corrections in Tight Test Sections**
> - *Mistake:* Using raw uncorrected wind tunnel dynamic pressure $q_\infty$ when model frontal area exceeds $5\%$ of test section area.
> - *Correction:* Solid and wake blockage accelerate tunnel velocity ($V_{\text{corrected}} = V_\infty(1 + \epsilon_{sb} + \epsilon_{wb})$), which must be corrected before calculating $C_L$ and $C_D$.

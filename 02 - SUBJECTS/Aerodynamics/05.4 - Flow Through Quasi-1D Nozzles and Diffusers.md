## 📌 Executive Summary & GATE AE Context

Quasi-1D flow through variable-area ducts (nozzles and diffusers) is tested in every GATE Aerospace examination without exception. Core topics include:
1. **The Area-Mach Relation:** $\frac{dA}{A} = (M^2 - 1)\frac{dV}{V}$.
2. **The Sonic Throat Mandate:** A gas can ONLY reach sonic speed ($M=1$) at the minimum area station ($dA = 0$).
3. **Choking Phenomenon & Maximum Mass Flow Rate:** $\dot{m}_{\max}$ occurs when throat Mach number $M_t = 1.0$.
4. **Mass Flow Parameter (MFP):** Dimensionless function of Mach number and stagnation conditions.
5. **Operating Regimes of Convergent-Divergent (de Laval) Nozzles:** Subsonic venturi, normal shock in divergent section, third critical (design) condition, over-expanded (oblique shocks outside), and under-expanded (expansion fans outside).

## 🧠 Part I: The Area-Mach Governing Relation

### 1. Derivation from 1D Conservation Laws
Consider steady, inviscid, 1D flow through a duct of cross-sectional area $A(x)$:
1. **Continuity Equation:**
   $$\rho A V = \text{const} \implies \frac{d\rho}{\rho} + \frac{dA}{A} + \frac{dV}{V} = 0$$
2. **Euler Momentum Equation:**
   $$dp + \rho V dV = 0 \implies \frac{dp}{\rho} = -V dV$$
3. **Speed of Sound Definition:**
   $$a^2 = \frac{dp}{d\rho} \implies d\rho = \frac{dp}{a^2} = -\frac{\rho V dV}{a^2} \implies \frac{d\rho}{\rho} = -M^2 \frac{dV}{V}$$

Substituting $d\rho/\rho$ into the continuity equation:
$$-M^2 \frac{dV}{V} + \frac{dA}{A} + \frac{dV}{V} = 0$$
$$\boxed{\frac{dA}{A} = (M^2 - 1)\frac{dV}{V} = (1 - M^2)\frac{dp}{\rho V^2}}$$

---

### 2. Aerodynamic Regimes of Variable-Area Ducts

| Regime | Mach Range | Factor $(M^2 - 1)$ | To Accelerate ($dV > 0, dp < 0$) | To Decelerate ($dV < 0, dp > 0$) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Subsonic** | $M < 1$ | Negative ($-$) | $dA < 0$ (**Converging duct**) | $dA > 0$ (**Diverging duct**) |
| **Sonic** | $M = 1$ | Zero ($0$) | $dA = 0$ (**Minimum area / Throat**) | $dA = 0$ (**Throat**) |
| **Supersonic** | $M > 1$ | Positive ($+$) | $dA > 0$ (**Diverging duct**) | $dA < 0$ (**Converging duct**) |

> 🏆 **THE DE LAVAL PARADOX:** To accelerate a fluid continuously from subsonic rest ($M \approx 0$) to supersonic speeds ($M > 1$), the duct MUST first **converge** to a throat ($dA=0$) where $M=1$, and then **diverge** ($dA > 0$)!

## 📐 Part II: Area Ratio & Mass Flow Rate

### 1. Isentropic Area Ratio $\left(\frac{A}{A^*}\right)$
From mass conservation $\rho A V = \rho^* A^* V^*$:
$$\frac{A}{A^*} = \frac{\rho^*}{\rho} \frac{V^*}{V} = \left(\frac{\rho^*}{\rho_0}\right)\left(\frac{\rho_0}{\rho}\right) \frac{a^*}{M a}$$
Substituting isentropic relations:
$$\boxed{\frac{A}{A^*} = \frac{1}{M} \left[ \frac{2}{\gamma+1} \left( 1 + \frac{\gamma-1}{2}M^2 \right) \right]^{\frac{\gamma+1}{2(\gamma-1)}}}$$

For standard air ($\gamma = 1.4$):
$$\frac{A}{A^*} = \frac{1}{M} \left[ \frac{1 + 0.2 M^2}{1.2} \right]^3 = \frac{1}{M}\left[\frac{5 + M^2}{6}\right]^3$$

> 💡 **Dual Solution Property:** For every area ratio $A/A^* > 1.0$, there are **exactly two isentropic Mach numbers**:
> 1. A **subsonic** solution ($M < 1.0$).
> 2. A **supersonic** solution ($M > 1.0$).

---

### 2. Mass Flow Rate Equation & Mass Flow Parameter (MFP)
Mass flow rate through any area $A$ where Mach number is $M$:
$$\dot{m} = \rho A V = \left(\frac{p}{R T}\right) A (M \sqrt{\gamma R T}) = p A M \sqrt{\frac{\gamma}{R T}}$$
Expressing static $p$ and $T$ in terms of stagnation reservoir conditions $p_0$ and $T_0$:
$$\dot{m} = \frac{p_0 A}{\sqrt{T_0}} \sqrt{\frac{\gamma}{R}} M \left( 1 + \frac{\gamma-1}{2}M^2 \right)^{-\frac{\gamma+1}{2(\gamma-1)}}$$

The **Mass Flow Parameter (MFP)** is defined as:
$$\text{MFP}(M) \equiv \frac{\dot{m}\sqrt{T_0}}{A p_0} = \sqrt{\frac{\gamma}{R}} M \left( 1 + \frac{\gamma-1}{2}M^2 \right)^{-\frac{\gamma+1}{2(\gamma-1)}}$$

#### Maximum (Choked) Mass Flow Rate:
Setting $M = 1.0$ at the throat area $A = A^*$:
$$\boxed{\dot{m}_{\max} = \frac{p_0 A^*}{\sqrt{T_0}} \sqrt{\frac{\gamma}{R}} \left( \frac{2}{\gamma+1} \right)^{\frac{\gamma+1}{2(\gamma-1)}}}$$

For air with $\gamma = 1.4$ and $R = 287.05\text{ J/(kg}\cdot\text{K)}$:
$$\sqrt{\frac{\gamma}{R}}\left(\frac{2}{\gamma+1}\right)^{\frac{\gamma+1}{2(\gamma-1)}} = \sqrt{\frac{1.4}{287.05}} \left(\frac{2}{2.4}\right)^3 = 0.069838 \times (0.833333)^3 = 0.069838 \times 0.578704 \approx 0.040418$$
$$\boxed{\dot{m}_{\max} \approx 0.04042 \times \frac{p_0 A^*}{\sqrt{T_0}} \quad (\text{with } p_0 \text{ in Pa}, A^* \text{ in m}^2, T_0 \text{ in K})}$$

## ⚡ Part III: Operating Regimes of a C-D Nozzle

Let $p_b$ be the back pressure outside the nozzle, and $p_0$ the reservoir stagnation pressure:
1. **$p_b / p_0 = 1.0$:** No flow ($\dot{m} = 0$).
2. **First Critical Pressure Ratio ($p_{b1} / p_0 \approx 0.93$ for $A_e/A^* = 2$):** Flow is subsonic everywhere; Mach number at the throat reaches exactly $M_t = 1.0$ for the first time.
3. **Shock in Divergent Duct ($p_{b2} < p_b < p_{b1}$):** Nozzle is choked ($\dot{m} = \dot{m}_{\max}$); flow accelerates to supersonic speed after throat, undergoes a normal shock inside the divergent duct, and exits subsonically.
4. **Third Critical / Design Pressure Ratio ($p_{b3} / p_0 = p_e / p_0$):** Fully isentropic expansion to supersonic speed at the exit plane. Flow is **correctly expanded** ($p_e = p_b$).
5. **Over-Expanded Regime ($p_{b3} < p_b < p_{b2}$):** Exit pressure is lower than ambient ($p_e < p_b$). Oblique shocks and shock diamonds form outside the exit plane to compress the plume.
6. **Under-Expanded Regime ($p_b < p_{b3}$):** Exit pressure is higher than ambient ($p_e > p_b$). Prandtl-Meyer expansion fans form at the nozzle lip outside to expand the plume.

## 🃏 Native Interactive Foldable Flashcards

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: NOZ-01 · Choking Condition
> **Question:** Can a convergent nozzle alone ever produce supersonic flow ($M > 1$) in steady continuous operation?
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > **NO!** In a converging duct ($dA < 0$), accelerating flow ($dV > 0$) requires $M^2 - 1 < 0 \implies M < 1$. The maximum Mach number achievable at the exit of a convergent nozzle is strictly $M_{\text{exit}} = 1.0$ (when $p_b \le 0.528 p_0$). Lowering back pressure further CANNOT accelerate flow beyond $M=1$ or increase mass flow rate!

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: NOZ-02 · Mass Flow Scaling with Reservoir State
> **Question:** If the reservoir temperature $T_0$ of a choked rocket nozzle is quadrupled ($T_0 \to 4 T_0$) while keeping $p_0$ and throat area $A^*$ constant, what happens to the mass flow rate $\dot{m}$?
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > $\dot{m} \propto \frac{p_0 A^*}{\sqrt{T_0}}$.  
> > Quadrupling $T_0$ reduces the mass flow rate by half: $\dot{m}_{\text{new}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{4}} \dot{m} = 0.50 \dot{m}$.  
> > ⚠️ **AIR 1 Trap:** Higher temperature means lower fluid density at the throat ($\rho^* \propto 1/T_0$), which reduces mass throughput despite higher sonic velocity ($a^* \propto \sqrt{T_0}$).

## 📌 Executive Summary & GATE AE Context

Shock waves are extremely thin irreversible non-isentropic transition regions ($\sim 10^{-7}\text{ m}$, on the order of mean free path) where supersonic flow abruptly decelerates. Normal shock waves appear in almost every GATE AE paper. Core topics:
1. **Governing Rankine-Hugoniot Equations:** Mass, momentum, energy conservation across a normal discontinuity.
2. **Downstream Mach Number ($M_2$):** Always strictly subsonic ($M_2 < 1.0$), with asymptotic limit $M_2 \to \sqrt{\frac{\gamma-1}{2\gamma}} \approx 0.378$.
3. **Static & Stagnation Property Jumps:** $p_2/p_1$, $T_2/T_1$, $\rho_2/\rho_1$, $p_{02}/p_{01}$.
4. **Stagnation Temperature Invariance:** $T_{02} \equiv T_{01}$ (adiabatic flow).
5. **Entropy Generation & Total Pressure Loss:** $\Delta s = -R \ln(p_{02}/p_{01}) > 0$.
6. **Rayleigh Supersonic Pitot Tube Formula:** Calculating freestream Mach number from Pitot pressure $p_{02}$ and wall static pressure $p_1$.

## 🧠 Part I: Normal Shock Governing Equations

### 1. The 1D Discontinuity Model
Consider a stationary normal shock with upstream state 1 ($M_1 > 1$) and downstream state 2:
1. **Continuity Equation:**
   $$\rho_1 u_1 = \rho_2 u_2$$
2. **Momentum Equation:**
   $$p_1 + \rho_1 u_1^2 = p_2 + \rho_2 u_2^2$$
3. **Energy Equation (Adiabatic):**
   $$h_1 + \frac{1}{2}u_1^2 = h_2 + \frac{1}{2}u_2^2 \implies c_p T_1 + \frac{1}{2}u_1^2 = c_p T_2 + \frac{1}{2}u_2^2$$
   $$\boxed{T_{01} = T_{02}}$$

---

### 2. The Prandtl Relation for Normal Shocks
Combining continuity, momentum, and energy in terms of characteristic speed of sound $a^*$:
$$\boxed{u_1 u_2 = (a^*)^2 \iff M_1^* M_2^* = 1}$$

Since upstream flow is supersonic ($M_1 > 1 \implies M_1^* > 1$), downstream flow MUST BE SUBSONIC ($M_2^* < 1 \implies M_2 < 1$). A normal shock cannot decelerate supersonic flow to another supersonic speed, nor can an expansion shock exist (Second Law of Thermodynamics).

---

## 📐 Part II: Rankine-Hugoniot Jump Relations

### 1. Downstream Mach Number ($M_2$)
$$\boxed{M_2^2 = \frac{2 + (\gamma-1)M_1^2}{2\gamma M_1^2 - (\gamma-1)}}$$
For air ($\gamma = 1.4$):
$$M_2^2 = \frac{2 + 0.4 M_1^2}{2.8 M_1^2 - 0.4} = \frac{5 + M_1^2}{7 M_1^2 - 1}$$

Asymptotic limit as $M_1 \to \infty$:
$$\lim_{M_1 \to \infty} M_2 = \sqrt{\frac{\gamma-1}{2\gamma}} = \sqrt{\frac{0.4}{2.8}} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{7}} \approx 0.37796$$

---

### 2. Static Pressure Ratio $\left(\frac{p_2}{p_1}\right)$
$$\boxed{\frac{p_2}{p_1} = 1 + \frac{2\gamma}{\gamma+1}(M_1^2 - 1) = \frac{2\gamma M_1^2 - (\gamma-1)}{\gamma+1}}$$
For air ($\gamma = 1.4$):
$$\frac{p_2}{p_1} = 1 + \frac{2.8}{2.4}(M_1^2 - 1) = 1 + \frac{7}{6}(M_1^2 - 1) = \frac{7 M_1^2 - 1}{6}$$

---

### 3. Density & Velocity Ratio $\left(\frac{\rho_2}{\rho_1} = \frac{u_1}{u_2}\right)$
$$\boxed{\frac{\rho_2}{\rho_1} = \frac{u_1}{u_2} = \frac{(\gamma+1)M_1^2}{2 + (\gamma-1)M_1^2}}$$
For air ($\gamma = 1.4$):
$$\frac{\rho_2}{\rho_1} = \frac{2.4 M_1^2}{2 + 0.4 M_1^2} = \frac{6 M_1^2}{M_1^2 + 5}$$

Asymptotic limit as $M_1 \to \infty$:
$$\lim_{M_1 \to \infty} \frac{\rho_2}{\rho_1} = \frac{\gamma+1}{\gamma-1} = \frac{2.4}{0.4} = 6.000$$

> ⚠️ **AIR 1 Trap:** While pressure ratio $p_2/p_1$ increases without bound as $M_1^2$, the density ratio across a normal shock in air is **strictly capped at 6.0**!

---

### 4. Static Temperature Ratio $\left(\frac{T_2}{T_1}\right)$
$$\frac{T_2}{T_1} = \left(\frac{p_2}{p_1}\right)\left(\frac{\rho_1}{\rho_2}\right) = \left[ \frac{2\gamma M_1^2 - (\gamma-1)}{\gamma+1} \right] \left[ \frac{2 + (\gamma-1)M_1^2}{(\gamma+1)M_1^2} \right]$$

---

### 5. Stagnation Pressure Ratio & Entropy Generation
Flow across a shock wave is highly irreversible due to viscous dissipation and thermal conduction inside the shock layer.
Entropy change:
$$\Delta s = s_2 - s_1 = c_p \ln\left(\frac{T_2}{T_1}\right) - R \ln\left(\frac{p_2}{p_1}\right)$$
Since $T_{02} = T_{01}$:
$$\boxed{\Delta s = -R \ln\left(\frac{p_{02}}{p_{01}}\right) > 0 \iff \frac{p_{02}}{p_{01}} = \exp\left(-\frac{\Delta s}{R}\right) < 1.0}$$

Stagnation pressure ratio in terms of $M_1$:
$$\boxed{\frac{p_{02}}{p_{01}} = \left[ \frac{\gamma+1}{2\gamma M_1^2 - (\gamma-1)} \right]^{\frac{1}{\gamma-1}} \left[ \frac{(\gamma+1)M_1^2}{2 + (\gamma-1)M_1^2} \right]^{\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-1}}}$$

Weak Shock Limit ($M_1 \approx 1$):
$$\frac{\Delta s}{R} \approx \frac{2\gamma(\gamma-1)}{3(\gamma+1)^2} (M_1^2 - 1)^3$$
Notice that entropy rise is of **third order** in shock strength $(M_1^2 - 1)$!

## ⚡ Part III: Rayleigh Supersonic Pitot Tube Formula

When a Pitot tube is placed in a supersonic flow ($M_1 > 1$), a curved bow shock stands detached in front of the probe nose. At the probe inlet along the stagnation streamline, the shock is strictly **normal**.

The fluid decelerates:
1. State 1 ($M_1 > 1$, $p_1, p_{01}$) $\to$ Normal shock $\to$ State 2 ($M_2 < 1$, $p_2, p_{02}$).
2. State 2 $\to$ Isentropic deceleration to stagnation point $\to$ Measured pressure $p_{02}$.

The ratio of measured Pitot stagnation pressure $p_{02}$ to freestream static pressure $p_1$ is given by the **Rayleigh Supersonic Pitot Formula**:
$$\boxed{\frac{p_{02}}{p_1} = \left( \frac{\gamma+1}{2} M_1^2 \right)^{\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-1}} \left[ \frac{\gamma+1}{2\gamma M_1^2 - (\gamma-1)} \right]^{\frac{1}{\gamma-1}}}$$

For standard air ($\gamma = 1.4$):
$$\frac{p_{02}}{p_1} = \left( 1.2 M_1^2 \right)^{3.5} \left( \frac{6}{7 M_1^2 - 1} \right)^{2.5} = \frac{166.92 M_1^7}{(7 M_1^2 - 1)^{2.5}}$$

## 🃏 Native Interactive Foldable Flashcards

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: SHK-01 · Normal Shock Property Trends
> **Question:** State whether each of the following quantities increases, decreases, or stays constant across a normal shock: $M, u, p, \rho, T, T_0, p_0, s$.
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > - $M$: **Decreases** ($M_1 > 1 \to M_2 < 1$).
> > - $u$: **Decreases** ($u_2 < u_1$).
> > - $p$: **Increases** ($p_2 > p_1$).
> > - $\rho$: **Increases** ($\rho_2 > \rho_1$).
> > - $T$: **Increases** ($T_2 > T_1$).
> > - $T_0$: **STAYS CONSTANT** ($T_{02} = T_{01}$).
> > - $p_0$: **Decreases** ($p_{02} < p_{01}$).
> > - $s$: **Increases** ($s_2 > s_1$).
> > ⚠️ **AIR 1 Trap:** Never say stagnation temperature decreases! It is an adiabatic process, so $T_0$ is invariant.

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: SHK-02 · Normal Shock Sound Speed Relation
> **Question:** Does the speed of sound increase or decrease across a normal shock?
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > Speed of sound $a = \sqrt{\gamma R T}$.
> > Since static temperature increases ($T_2 > T_1$), the speed of sound **INCREASES** ($a_2 > a_1$)!
> > Therefore, downstream Mach number $M_2 = u_2 / a_2$ decreases not only because flow velocity $u$ drops, but also because the speed of sound $a$ rises.

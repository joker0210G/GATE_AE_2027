## 📌 1. Executive Summary & GATE AE Significance

The Navier-Stokes equations are non-linear, second-order partial differential equations with no general analytical solution. However, under specific kinematic constraints—principally **steady, fully developed, parallel laminar flows**—the non-linear convective acceleration terms $(\vec{V}\cdot\nabla)\vec{V}$ identically vanish, reducing the equations to linear ordinary differential equations that admit **exact analytical solutions**.

In GATE Aerospace Engineering, exact Navier-Stokes solutions are tested relentlessly across three canonical configurations:
1. **Plane Couette Flow:** Shear-driven flow between parallel plates (zero pressure gradient).
2. **Generalized Couette Flow:** Combined shear-driven and pressure-driven flow between plates, featuring boundary layer separation / reverse flow criteria.
3. **Plane & Hagen-Poiseuille Flow:** Pure pressure-driven laminar flow in channels and circular pipes, governing viscous pressure drop ($\Delta p$), wall shear stress ($\tau_w$), and friction factor ($f = 64/Re$).

> 🎯 **AIR-1 Significance:** 1 to 2 questions appear in almost every GATE AE paper (2–3 marks). Questions test subtle traps:
> - Distinguishing circular pipe ratio ($u_{\text{avg}}/u_{\max} = 1/2$) from parallel channel ratio ($u_{\text{avg}}/u_{\max} = 2/3$).
> - Darcy friction factor ($f = 64/Re$) vs Fanning skin friction coefficient ($c_f = 16/Re$).
> - Reverse flow onset in generalized Couette flow on the stationary wall ($P \le -1$).
> - Dependence of pumping power on diameter at fixed flow rate ($P_{\text{pump}} \propto D^{-4}$).

## 🧭 2. Navier-Stokes Equation Reduction Hierarchy

```
                   INCOMPRESSIBLE NAVIER-STOKES (CONSTANT μ, ρ)
          ρ [ ∂V/∂t + (V · ∇)V ] = -∇p + μ ∇²V + ρ g
                                  │
         Steady (∂/∂t = 0) + Fully Developed Parallel Flow:
              v = w = 0, u = u(y) or u(r), ∂u/∂x = 0
                                  │
      Convective Acceleration Identically Vanishes:
              (V · ∇)V = u(∂u/∂x)î + v(∂u/∂y)ĵ = 0
                                  │
         ─────────────────────────┴─────────────────────────
        │                                                   │
   CARTESIAN (Plates)                              CYLINDRICAL (Pipes)
   d²u/dy² = (1/μ)(dp/dx)                          (1/r) d/dr [r du/dr] = (1/μ)(dp/dx)
        │                                                   │
  ┌─────┴────────────────┐                                  │
  ▼                      ▼                                  ▼
Couette Flow       Poiseuille Flow                Hagen-Poiseuille Flow
dp/dx = 0          dp/dx = const                  dp/dx = const
u(y) = U(y/h)      Parabolic (2/3 u_max)          Parabolic (1/2 u_max)
```

## 🔬 3. Case I: Simple Plane Couette Flow (Pure Shear-Driven)

### 3.1 Physical Setup & Boundary Conditions
Two infinite flat plates separated by gap $h$. The lower plate ($y = 0$) is stationary, while the upper plate ($y = h$) moves with constant velocity $U$ in the $+x$ direction. Pressure gradient is zero ($\frac{dp}{dx} = 0$).

Boundary Conditions:
$$u(0) = 0, \quad u(h) = U$$

### 3.2 Governing Equation & Velocity Profile
$$\frac{d^2u}{dy^2} = 0 \implies u(y) = C_1 y + C_2$$

Applying boundary conditions:
$$C_2 = 0, \quad C_1 = \frac{U}{h}$$

$$\bbox[12px,border:2px solid #2196F3,background-color:#0d1117]{ u(y) = U \frac{y}{h} }$$

### 3.3 Dynamic Quantities
- **Velocity Gradient:** $\frac{du}{dy} = \frac{U}{h} = \text{constant}$
- **Shear Stress:** $\tau_{yx} = \mu \frac{du}{dy} = \mu \frac{U}{h} = \text{constant everywhere across the gap}$
- **Vorticity Vector:**
  $$\vec{\omega} = \nabla \times \vec{V} = -\frac{du}{dy}\hat{k} = -\frac{U}{h}\hat{k}$$
  *(Even though streamlines are straight lines, the flow is rotational with constant vorticity $-\frac{U}{h}$!)*
- **Discharge per Unit Width:** $q = \int_0^h u(y) dy = \frac{1}{2} U h$
- **Average Velocity:** $u_{\text{avg}} = \frac{q}{h} = \frac{1}{2} U$

## ⚡ 4. Case II: Generalized Couette Flow (Combined Shear & Pressure Gradient)

### 4.1 Physical Setup
Upper plate moves at velocity $U$ at $y = h$, lower plate stationary at $y = 0$, with an imposed constant streamwise pressure gradient $\frac{dp}{dx} = \text{constant}$.

### 4.2 Derivation
$$\mu \frac{d^2u}{dy^2} = \frac{dp}{dx} \implies \frac{d^2u}{dy^2} = \frac{1}{\mu}\frac{dp}{dx}$$

Integrating twice:
$$\frac{du}{dy} = \frac{1}{\mu}\frac{dp}{dx} y + C_1$$
$$u(y) = \frac{1}{2\mu}\frac{dp}{dx} y^2 + C_1 y + C_2$$

Applying boundary conditions $u(0) = 0 \implies C_2 = 0$, and $u(h) = U$:
$$U = \frac{1}{2\mu}\frac{dp}{dx} h^2 + C_1 h \implies C_1 = \frac{U}{h} - \frac{h}{2\mu}\frac{dp}{dx}$$

Substituting back into $u(y)$:
$$\bbox[12px,border:2px solid #E91E63,background-color:#0d1117]{ u(y) = U \frac{y}{h} + \frac{1}{2\mu}\left(-\frac{dp}{dx}\right) y (h - y) }$$

### 4.3 Dimensionless Formulation & Reverse Flow Criterion
Define dimensionless height $\eta = \frac{y}{h}$ and dimensionless pressure parameter $P$:
$$P = \frac{h^2}{2\mu U}\left(-\frac{dp}{dx}\right)$$

The velocity profile becomes:
$$\frac{u}{U} = \eta + P \eta (1 - \eta)$$

Evaluating wall shear stress on the lower stationary wall ($y = 0$):
$$\left.\frac{du}{dy}\right|_{y=0} = \frac{U}{h} [1 + P]$$

> ⚠️ **AIR-1 Critical Separation / Reverse Flow Boundary:**
> 1. If $P > 0$ (favorable pressure gradient, $-\frac{dp}{dx} > 0$): Velocity is strictly positive everywhere, profile bulges forward.
> 2. If $P = 0$: Pure linear Couette flow.
> 3. If $-1 < P < 0$ (mild adverse pressure gradient): Velocity is positive everywhere, but slope at lower wall decreases.
> 4. If $P = -1$: Incipient backflow on the lower wall:
>    $$\left.\frac{du}{dy}\right|_{y=0} = 0 \implies \frac{dp}{dx} = +\frac{2\mu U}{h^2}$$
> 5. If $P < -1$ (strong adverse pressure gradient): **Reverse flow occurs near the lower wall** ($\left.\frac{du}{dy}\right|_{y=0} < 0$).

## 🌊 5. Case III: Plane Poiseuille Flow (Between Fixed Parallel Plates)

### 5.1 Physical Setup & Derivation
Both plates are stationary: $u(0) = 0$ and $u(h) = 0$. Driven solely by favorable pressure gradient $\left(-\frac{dp}{dx}\right) > 0$.

Setting $U = 0$ in the generalized Couette solution:
$$\bbox[12px,border:2px solid #4CAF50,background-color:#0d1117]{ u(y) = \frac{1}{2\mu}\left(-\frac{dp}{dx}\right) y(h - y) }$$

### 5.2 Centerline Maximum & Average Velocity
Maximum velocity occurs at channel centerline $y = \frac{h}{2}$:
$$u_{\max} = u\left(\frac{h}{2}\right) = \frac{h^2}{8\mu}\left(-\frac{dp}{dx}\right)$$

Discharge per unit width $q$:
$$q = \int_0^h u(y) dy = \frac{1}{2\mu}\left(-\frac{dp}{dx}\right) \left[ \frac{h y^2}{2} - \frac{y^3}{3} \right]_0^h = \frac{h^3}{12\mu}\left(-\frac{dp}{dx}\right)$$

Average velocity $u_{\text{avg}}$:
$$u_{\text{avg}} = \frac{q}{h} = \frac{h^2}{12\mu}\left(-\frac{dp}{dx}\right)$$

> 🎯 **Key GATE Ratio (Parallel Channel):**
> $$\bbox[10px,border:2px solid #FF9800,background-color:#0d1117]{ \frac{u_{\text{avg}}}{u_{\max}} = \frac{h^2 / 12\mu}{h^2 / 8\mu} = \frac{2}{3} }$$

### 5.3 Wall Shear Stress & Friction Factor
Wall shear stress at $y = 0$ and $y = h$:
$$\tau_w = \mu \left|\left.\frac{du}{dy}\right|_{y=0}\right| = \frac{h}{2}\left(-\frac{dp}{dx}\right) = \frac{4\mu u_{\max}}{h} = \frac{6\mu u_{\text{avg}}}{h}$$

Hydraulic diameter for two wide plates with gap $h$: $D_h = \frac{4A}{P} = \frac{4(w h)}{2w} = 2h$.
Darcy friction factor:
$$f = \frac{8\tau_w}{\rho u_{\text{avg}}^2} = \frac{8 \left(\frac{6\mu u_{\text{avg}}}{h}\right)}{\rho u_{\text{avg}}^2} = \frac{48\mu}{\rho u_{\text{avg}} h} = \frac{96}{\left(\frac{\rho u_{\text{avg}} D_h}{\mu}\right)} = \frac{96}{Re_{D_h}}$$

## 🛢️ 6. Case IV: Hagen-Poiseuille Flow in a Circular Pipe

### 6.1 Coordinate Frame & Differential Balance
Consider steady, axisymmetric, fully developed laminar flow in a horizontal circular pipe of radius $R$ (diameter $D = 2R$).
Velocity field: $u = u(r)$, $v_r = v_\theta = 0$.

In cylindrical coordinates, the $x$-momentum Navier-Stokes equation reduces to:
$$\frac{1}{r} \frac{d}{dr}\left(r \frac{du}{dr}\right) = \frac{1}{\mu} \frac{dp}{dx}$$

### 6.2 Step-by-Step Integration
Multiply by $r$:
$$\frac{d}{dr}\left(r \frac{du}{dr}\right) = \frac{1}{\mu} \frac{dp}{dx} r$$

First integration with respect to $r$:
$$r \frac{du}{dr} = \frac{1}{2\mu} \frac{dp}{dx} r^2 + C_1 \implies \frac{du}{dr} = \frac{1}{2\mu} \frac{dp}{dx} r + \frac{C_1}{r}$$

Second integration:
$$u(r) = \frac{1}{4\mu} \frac{dp}{dx} r^2 + C_1 \ln r + C_2$$

### 6.3 Boundary Conditions
1. **Axisymmetry / Finite Velocity at Centerline ($r = 0$):**
   Since $\ln(0) \to -\infty$, for physical finiteness of centerline velocity:
   $$C_1 = 0$$
   *(Equivalently, shear stress at pipe centerline is zero: $\left.\frac{du}{dr}\right|_{r=0} = 0$).*
2. **No-Slip at Pipe Wall ($r = R$):**
   $$u(R) = 0 \implies 0 = \frac{1}{4\mu} \frac{dp}{dx} R^2 + C_2 \implies C_2 = \frac{1}{4\mu}\left(-\frac{dp}{dx}\right) R^2$$

Substituting $C_1$ and $C_2$:
$$\bbox[12px,border:2px solid #2196F3,background-color:#0d1117]{ u(r) = \frac{1}{4\mu}\left(-\frac{dp}{dx}\right) (R^2 - r^2) = u_{\max}\left(1 - \frac{r^2}{R^2}\right) }$$

### 6.4 Maximum Velocity, Flow Rate & Average Velocity
- **Maximum (Centerline) Velocity at $r = 0$:**
  $$u_{\max} = \frac{R^2}{4\mu}\left(-\frac{dp}{dx}\right) = \frac{D^2}{16\mu}\left(-\frac{dp}{dx}\right)$$

- **Volumetric Flow Rate (Hagen-Poiseuille Law):**
  $$Q = \int_0^R u(r) 2\pi r \, dr = 2\pi u_{\max} \int_0^R \left(r - \frac{r^3}{R^2}\right) dr = 2\pi u_{\max} \left[ \frac{R^2}{2} - \frac{R^4}{4R^2} \right] = \frac{\pi R^2}{2} u_{\max}$$
  $$\bbox[12px,border:2px solid #4CAF50,background-color:#0d1117]{ Q = \frac{\pi R^4}{8\mu}\left(-\frac{dp}{dx}\right) = \frac{\pi D^4}{128\mu}\left(-\frac{dp}{dx}\right) }$$

- **Cross-Sectional Average Velocity:**
  $$u_{\text{avg}} = \frac{Q}{A} = \frac{\frac{\pi R^2}{2} u_{\max}}{\pi R^2} = \frac{1}{2} u_{\max}$$

> 🎯 **Master Ratio Contrast:**
> - **Circular Pipe (Hagen-Poiseuille):** $\frac{u_{\text{avg}}}{u_{\max}} = \frac{1}{2} = 0.500$
> - **Parallel Channel (Plane Poiseuille):** $\frac{u_{\text{avg}}}{u_{\max}} = \frac{2}{3} \approx 0.667$

### 6.5 Wall Shear Stress & Pressure Drop
Shear stress at radius $r$:
$$\tau_{rx} = \mu \left(-\frac{du}{dr}\right) = -\mu \left[ -\frac{2r}{R^2} u_{\max} \right] = \frac{2\mu u_{\max} r}{R^2}$$
*(Shear stress varies linearly from $0$ at centerline to maximum at the wall!)*

At the pipe wall ($r = R$):
$$\bbox[10px,border:2px solid #E91E63,background-color:#0d1117]{ \tau_w = \tau_{rx}(R) = \frac{2\mu u_{\max}}{R} = \frac{4\mu u_{\text{avg}}}{R} = \frac{8\mu u_{\text{avg}}}{D} = \frac{R}{2}\left(-\frac{dp}{dx}\right) = \frac{D}{4}\left(-\frac{dp}{dx}\right) }$$

For a pipe of length $L$ with pressure drop $\Delta p = \left(-\frac{dp}{dx}\right) L$:
$$\bbox[12px,border:2px solid #9C27B0,background-color:#0d1117]{ \Delta p = \frac{32\mu u_{\text{avg}} L}{D^2} = \frac{128\mu Q L}{\pi D^4} }$$

### 6.6 Darcy Friction Factor & Skin Friction
Darcy friction factor $f$:
$$\Delta p = f \frac{L}{D} \frac{\rho u_{\text{avg}}^2}{2} \implies f = \frac{2 D \Delta p}{\rho u_{\text{avg}}^2 L} = \frac{4\tau_w}{\frac{1}{2}\rho u_{\text{avg}}^2}$$

Substituting $\tau_w = \frac{8\mu u_{\text{avg}}}{D}$:
$$\bbox[12px,border:2px solid #FF5722,background-color:#0d1117]{ f = \frac{4 \left(\frac{8\mu u_{\text{avg}}}{D}\right)}{\frac{1}{2}\rho u_{\text{avg}}^2} = \frac{64\mu}{\rho u_{\text{avg}} D} = \frac{64}{Re_D} }$$

Fanning friction factor $c_f$ (used in boundary layer & aerodynamics):
$$c_f = \frac{\tau_w}{\frac{1}{2}\rho u_{\text{avg}}^2} = \frac{f}{4} = \frac{16}{Re_D}$$

## ⚡ 7. AIR-1 Trap Ledger & Distractor Autopsy

| Feature / Variable | Hagen-Poiseuille (Circular Pipe) | Plane Poiseuille (Parallel Channel) | Common Exam Trap |
|---|---|---|---|
| **$u_{\text{avg}} / u_{\max}$** | **$1/2 = 0.50$** | **$2/3 \approx 0.67$** | Swapping $1/2$ and $2/3$ when finding flow rate from peak velocity. |
| **Darcy Friction Factor $f$** | **$64 / Re_D$** | **$96 / Re_{D_h}$** | Using $64/Re$ for rectangular ducts or parallel plates. |
| **Fanning $c_f$ vs Darcy $f$** | $c_f = f/4 = 16/Re$ | $c_f = f/4 = 24/Re$ | Forgetting factor of 4 between Darcy ($f$) and Fanning ($c_f$). |
| **Wall Shear Stress $\tau_w$** | $\tau_w = \frac{D}{4}\left(-\frac{dp}{dx}\right)$ | $\tau_w = \frac{h}{2}\left(-\frac{dp}{dx}\right)$ | Confusing radius $R$ with diameter $D$ in force balance. |
| **Pressure Drop vs Diameter** | $\Delta p \propto D^{-4}$ (fixed $Q$) | $\Delta p \propto h^{-3}$ (fixed $q$) | Claiming $\Delta p \propto D^{-2}$ (true only if $u_{\text{avg}}$ is fixed, not $Q$!). |
| **Pumping Power $P_{\text{pump}} = Q \Delta p$** | $P_{\text{pump}} \propto D^{-4}$ (fixed $Q$) | $P_{\text{pump}} \propto h^{-3}$ (fixed $q$) | Doubling pipe diameter reduces pumping power by a factor of $2^4 = 16$! |

### PYQ-D3-01: Hagen-Poiseuille Pipe Flow & Pumping Power (NAT) ⭐⭐⭐
**Problem:** Engine oil ($\mu = 0.29 \text{ Pa}\cdot\text{s}$, $\rho = 880 \text{ kg/m}^3$) flows laminarly through a horizontal pipe of diameter $D = 0.05 \text{ m}$ and length $L = 40 \text{ m}$. Volumetric flow rate $Q = 2.5 \times 10^{-3} \text{ m}^3\text{/s}$.  
Calculate: (a) Reynolds number $Re_D$, (b) maximum centerline velocity $u_{\max}$ (m/s), (c) pressure drop $\Delta p$ (kPa), (d) pumping power $P_{\text{pump}}$ (W), (e) shear stress $\tau$ at $r = 15 \text{ mm}$ (Pa).

> [!faq]- 🔍 View Step-by-Step Solution & TCS Keystrokes
>
> **Step 1 — Area & Average Velocity:**
> $$A = \frac{\pi D^2}{4} = \frac{\pi (0.05)^2}{4} = 1.9635 \times 10^{-3} \text{ m}^2$$
> $$u_{\text{avg}} = \frac{Q}{A} = \frac{2.5 \times 10^{-3}}{1.9635 \times 10^{-3}} = 1.2732 \text{ m/s}$$
>
> **Step 2 — Reynolds Number:**
> $$Re_D = \frac{\rho u_{\text{avg}} D}{\mu} = \frac{880 \times 1.2732 \times 0.05}{0.29} = \mathbf{193.2} \ll 2300 \quad \checkmark \text{ (strictly laminar)}$$
>
> **Step 3 — Maximum Centerline Velocity:**
> $$u_{\max} = 2 \times u_{\text{avg}} = 2 \times 1.2732 = \mathbf{2.55 \text{ m/s}}$$
>
> **Step 4 — Pressure Drop:**
> $$\Delta p = \frac{128 \mu Q L}{\pi D^4} = \frac{128 \times 0.29 \times 0.0025 \times 40}{\pi \times (0.05)^4} = \frac{3.712}{1.9635 \times 10^{-5}} = \mathbf{189.05 \text{ kPa}}$$
>
> **Step 5 — Pumping Power:**
> $$P_{\text{pump}} = Q \Delta p = (2.5 \times 10^{-3}) \times 189050 = \mathbf{472.6 \text{ W}}$$
>
> **Step 6 — Shear Stress at $r = 15 \text{ mm}$ ($R = 25 \text{ mm}$):**
> $$\tau_w = \frac{D}{4}\frac{\Delta p}{L} = \frac{0.05}{4}\left(\frac{189050}{40}\right) = 59.08 \text{ Pa}$$
> $$\tau(r) = \tau_w \frac{r}{R} = 59.08 \times \frac{15}{25} = \mathbf{35.45 \text{ Pa}}$$
>
> **Official IIT Bands:**
> - (a) $Re_D \in [190, 196]$
> - (b) $u_{\max} \in [2.52, 2.58]$ m/s
> - (c) $\Delta p \in [187, 191]$ kPa
> - (d) $P_{\text{pump}} \in [468, 477]$ W
> - (e) $\tau(15\text{mm}) \in [35.0, 36.0]$ Pa
>
> **TCS Virtual Calculator Keystroke Ledger:**
> `0.05 x² * 3.14159 / 4 = [0.0019635] [MS]` (Stores A)
> `0.0025 / [MR] = [1.2732] [MS]` (Stores u_avg)
> `880 * [MR] * 0.05 / 0.29 = [193.18] ✅`
> `[MR] * 2 = [2.546] ✅`
> `128 * 0.29 * 0.0025 * 40 = [3.712] / (pi * 0.05 * 0.05 * 0.05 * 0.05) = [189050] [MS]` (Stores Δp)
> `[MR] * 0.0025 = [472.63] ✅`

### PYQ-D3-02: Generalized Couette Reverse Flow Threshold (NAT) ⭐⭐⭐
**Problem:** Viscous fluid ($\mu = 0.15 \text{ Pa}\cdot\text{s}$) is sheared between horizontal plates separated by $h = 4 \text{ mm}$. Bottom plate ($y=0$) is fixed, top plate ($y=h$) moves at $U = 3.0 \text{ m/s}$. Find:
(a) The critical adverse pressure gradient $(dp/dx)_{\text{crit}}$ (in kPa/m) required to initiate reverse flow on the stationary wall.  
(b) If $dp/dx = +90 \text{ kPa/m}$, the reverse velocity at $y = 1 \text{ mm}$ (m/s).

> [!faq]- 🔍 View Step-by-Step Solution
>
> **Part (a) — Incipient Reverse Flow Condition:**
> Wall shear at bottom stationary wall:
> $$\left.\frac{du}{dy}\right|_{y=0} = \frac{U}{h} - \frac{h}{2\mu}\frac{dp}{dx} \le 0$$
> $$\left(\frac{dp}{dx}\right)_{\text{crit}} = \frac{2\mu U}{h^2} = \frac{2 \times 0.15 \times 3.0}{(0.004)^2} = \frac{0.90}{1.6 \times 10^{-5}} = \mathbf{+56.25 \text{ kPa/m}}$$
>
> **Part (b) — Reverse Velocity at $y = 1 \text{ mm}$ ($0.001 \text{ m}$):**
> $$u(y) = U \frac{y}{h} - \frac{1}{2\mu}\left(\frac{dp}{dx}\right) y(h - y)$$
> $$u(0.001) = 3.0 \left(\frac{0.001}{0.004}\right) - \frac{90000}{2 \times 0.15} (0.001)(0.004 - 0.001)$$
> $$u(0.001) = 0.75 - 300000(0.001)(0.003) = 0.75 - 0.90 = \mathbf{-0.150 \text{ m/s}}$$
>
> **Official IIT Bands:**
> - (a) $[56.0, 56.5]$ kPa/m
> - (b) $[-0.155, -0.145]$ m/s

### PYQ-D3-03: Two-Fluid Immiscible Couette Flow Interface (NAT) ⭐⭐⭐
**Problem:** Two immiscible fluids of equal thickness $h = 10 \text{ mm}$ are sheared between plates with gap $2h = 20 \text{ mm}$.  
- Lower layer ($0 \le y \le h$): $\mu_1 = 1.0 \times 10^{-3} \text{ Pa}\cdot\text{s}$ (water).  
- Upper layer ($h \le y \le 2h$): $\mu_2 = 0.10 \text{ Pa}\cdot\text{s} = 100\mu_1$ (oil).  
Lower wall is fixed, upper wall moves at $U = 10 \text{ m/s}$. Find interface velocity $u_{\text{int}}$ (m/s) and shear stress $\tau$ (Pa).

> [!faq]- 🔍 View Step-by-Step Solution
>
> By shear stress continuity across the fluid-fluid interface:
> $$\tau_1 = \tau_2 \implies \mu_1 \frac{u_{\text{int}}}{h} = \mu_2 \frac{U - u_{\text{int}}}{h}$$
> $$u_{\text{int}} = U \frac{\mu_2}{\mu_1 + \mu_2} = 10 \times \frac{0.10}{0.001 + 0.10} = 10 \times \frac{100}{101} = \mathbf{9.901 \text{ m/s}}$$
>
> Shear stress:
> $$\tau = \mu_1 \frac{u_{\text{int}}}{h} = 10^{-3} \times \frac{9.901}{0.010} = \mathbf{0.990 \text{ Pa}}$$
>
> **Official IIT Bands:**
> - $u_{\text{int}} \in [9.85, 9.95]$ m/s
> - $\tau \in [0.98, 1.00]$ Pa
> ⚠️ **Trap:** Never assume $u_{\text{int}} = U/2 = 5\text{ m/s}$. The more viscous fluid behaves like a near-rigid slab!

### PYQ-D3-04: Journal Bearing Viscous Power Dissipation (NAT) ⭐⭐
**Problem:** A 100 mm diameter shaft rotates at 1200 RPM in a 200 mm long journal bearing with uniform radial clearance $h = 0.2 \text{ mm}$ filled with oil ($\mu = 0.08 \text{ Pa}\cdot\text{s}$). Find viscous power dissipated $P$ in Watts.

> [!faq]- 🔍 View Step-by-Step Solution
>
> Rotational speed: $\omega = \frac{2\pi \times 1200}{60} = 40\pi \approx 125.66 \text{ rad/s}$.  
> Surface speed: $U = \omega R = 125.66 \times 0.050 = 6.283 \text{ m/s}$.  
> Shear stress: $\tau = \mu \frac{U}{h} = \frac{0.08 \times 6.283}{0.0002} = 2513.2 \text{ Pa}$.  
> Wetted surface area: $A = 2\pi R L = 2\pi (0.050)(0.20) = 0.06283 \text{ m}^2$.  
> Tangential friction force: $F = \tau A = 2513.2 \times 0.06283 = 157.9 \text{ N}$.  
> Power: $P = F U = 157.9 \times 6.283 = \mathbf{992.2 \text{ W}}$.
>
> **Official IIT Band:** $[988.0, 996.0]$ W

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: Hagen-Poiseuille vs Plane Poiseuille Velocity Ratio
> **Question:** What is the ratio of average velocity to maximum velocity ($u_{\text{avg}} / u_{\max}$) for (a) laminar circular pipe flow, and (b) laminar flow between fixed parallel plates?
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > - (a) Circular Pipe: $u_{\text{avg}} / u_{\max} = \mathbf{1/2} = 0.50$
> > - (b) Parallel Plates: $u_{\text{avg}} / u_{\max} = \mathbf{2/3} \approx 0.67$
> > ⚠️ **AIR-1 Trap:** In volume flow integration, the pipe area element is $2\pi r dr$ (linear in $r$), weighting the parabolic profile differently than the 2D Cartesian element $dy$.

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: Generalized Couette Separation Criterion
> **Question:** In generalized Couette flow with upper wall moving at $+U$ at $y = h$ and lower wall stationary at $y = 0$, what is the exact condition on pressure gradient $\frac{dp}{dx}$ for reverse flow to initiate on the stationary wall?
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > Condition: $\left.\frac{du}{dy}\right|_{y=0} \le 0 \implies \mathbf{\frac{dp}{dx} \ge +\frac{2\mu U}{h^2}}$ (adverse pressure gradient).
> > ⚠️ **AIR-1 Trap:** Reverse flow occurs on the *stationary* wall first, never on the moving wall! The pressure gradient must be *adverse* (positive $\frac{dp}{dx}$) to retard the flow against wall shear.

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: Pumping Power Scaling Law
> **Question:** If the diameter of a laminar pipe is doubled while maintaining the same volumetric flow rate $Q$, by what factor does the required pumping power decrease?
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > Pumping power $P = Q \Delta p = \frac{128 \mu Q^2 L}{\pi D^4}$.  
> > Since $P \propto D^{-4}$, doubling diameter ($2D$) reduces pumping power by:
> > $$\frac{P_2}{P_1} = \left(\frac{D_1}{2D_1}\right)^4 = \frac{1}{16}$$
> > ⚠️ **AIR-1 Trap:** Do not confuse fixed flow rate ($Q = \text{const} \implies P \propto D^{-4}$) with fixed average velocity ($u_{\text{avg}} = \text{const} \implies \Delta p \propto D^{-2} \implies P \propto D^0 = \text{constant}$).

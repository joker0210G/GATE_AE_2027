## 📌 Executive Summary & GATE AE Context

Potential flow theory forms the mathematical bedrock of classical aerodynamics. While real fluids possess viscosity, at high Reynolds numbers ($Re \gg 10^5$), the viscous effects are confined to a thin boundary layer and wake. Outside these regions, the external flow over wings and fuselages is **inviscid, incompressible, and irrotational**.

In GATE AE, potential flow is tested heavily via:
1. **Kinematic existence conditions:** When does $\phi$ exist? When does $\psi$ exist? (MSQ favorite).
2. **Elementary flow formulas:** Velocity, potential, and stream functions in Cartesian and Polar coordinates for Uniform flow, Source/Sink, Doublet, and Point Vortex.
3. **Superposition analysis:** Stagnation points ($V = 0$), dividing streamlines ($\psi = \psi_{\text{stag}}$), body geometry (Rankine half-body, circular cylinder), and surface pressure distribution $C_p(\theta) = 1 - 4\sin^2\theta$.

## 🧠 Part I: The Mathematical Framework (Laplace's Equation)

### 1. The Velocity Potential Function ($\phi$)
- **Condition for Existence:** The flow must be **irrotational** everywhere in the domain:
$$\vec{\omega} = \nabla \times \vec{V} = \vec{0}$$
By vector calculus, any curl-free vector field can be expressed as the gradient of a scalar potential $\phi$:
$$\vec{V} = \nabla \phi$$
In Cartesian coordinates:
$$u = \frac{\partial \phi}{\partial x}, \quad v = \frac{\partial \phi}{\partial y}, \quad w = \frac{\partial \phi}{\partial z}$$
In Polar coordinates $(r, \theta)$:
$$V_r = \frac{\partial \phi}{\partial r}, \quad V_\theta = \frac{1}{r} \frac{\partial \phi}{\partial \theta}$$

### 2. The Stream Function ($\psi$)
- **Condition for Existence (2D):** The flow must be **incompressible** (solenoidal):
$$\nabla \cdot \vec{V} = \frac{\partial u}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial v}{\partial y} = 0$$
This guarantees the existence of a stream function $\psi(x, y)$ such that:
$$u = \frac{\partial \psi}{\partial y}, \quad v = -\frac{\partial \psi}{\partial x}$$
In Polar coordinates:
$$V_r = \frac{1}{r} \frac{\partial \psi}{\partial \theta}, \quad V_\theta = -\frac{\partial \psi}{\partial r}$$

### 3. The Dual Existence Invariant (Cauchy-Riemann Equations)
When a flow is **BOTH 2D, incompressible, AND irrotational**:
$$u = \frac{\partial \phi}{\partial x} = \frac{\partial \psi}{\partial y}$$
$$v = \frac{\partial \phi}{\partial y} = -\frac{\partial \psi}{\partial x}$$

These are precisely the celebrated **Cauchy-Riemann equations** from complex analysis!
Consequently:
1. The complex potential $W(z) = \phi(x, y) + i \psi(x, y)$ is an **analytic function** of $z = x + iy$.
2. The complex velocity is:
$$\frac{dW}{dz} = u - i v$$
3. **Mutual Orthogonality:** Equipotential lines ($\phi = c_1$) and streamlines ($\psi = c_2$) intersect at **right angles** everywhere, except at stagnation points:
$$\left(\frac{dy}{dx}\right)_{\phi} \times \left(\frac{dy}{dx}\right)_{\psi} = \left(-\frac{u}{v}\right) \times \left(\frac{v}{u}\right) = -1$$

### 4. Laplace's Equation & The Superposition Theorem
Substituting $\vec{V} = \nabla \phi$ into the continuity equation $\nabla \cdot \vec{V} = 0$:
$$\nabla \cdot (\nabla \phi) = \nabla^2 \phi = 0$$
In Cartesian:
$$\frac{\partial^2 \phi}{\partial x^2} + \frac{\partial^2 \phi}{\partial y^2} = 0$$
In Polar:
$$\frac{\partial^2 \phi}{\partial r^2} + \frac{1}{r} \frac{\partial \phi}{\partial r} + \frac{1}{r^2} \frac{\partial^2 \phi}{\partial \theta^2} = 0$$

Similarly, substituting $\vec{V}$ in terms of $\psi$ into the irrotationality condition $\omega_z = \frac{\partial v}{\partial x} - \frac{\partial u}{\partial y} = 0$:
$$\nabla^2 \psi = 0$$

> 🔑 **Principle of Linear Superposition:**
> Since Laplace's equation is a **linear, homogeneous partial differential equation**, any linear combination of individual solutions is also an exact solution:
> $$\phi = \sum_{i=1}^n c_i \phi_i, \quad \psi = \sum_{i=1}^n c_i \psi_i$$
> This allows us to synthesize complex aerodynamic flows over bodies by simply adding elementary flow building blocks!

## 🧠 Part II: The Four Elementary Potential Flows

### 1. Uniform Flow at Angle of Attack $\alpha$
A parallel stream of uniform velocity $V_\infty$ directed at angle $\alpha$ to the $x$-axis.
- Velocity components: $u = V_\infty \cos\alpha, \quad v = V_\infty \sin\alpha$
- Polar velocity components:
$$V_r = V_\infty \cos(\theta - \alpha), \quad V_\theta = -V_\infty \sin(\theta - \alpha)$$
- Potential and Stream functions:
$$\phi = V_\infty (x \cos\alpha + y \sin\alpha) = V_\infty r \cos(\theta - \alpha)$$
$$\psi = V_\infty (y \cos\alpha - x \sin\alpha) = V_\infty r \sin(\theta - \alpha)$$
- For flow aligned with the $x$-axis ($\alpha = 0$):
$$\phi = V_\infty x = V_\infty r \cos\theta$$
$$\psi = V_\infty y = V_\infty r \sin\theta$$

---

### 2. Line Source and Line Sink
A line source at the origin emits fluid symmetrically in all radial directions.
- Source strength $\Lambda$ (or $q$): Total volumetric discharge per unit depth ($\text{m}^2/\text{s}$).
- Mass conservation across a cylinder of radius $r$:
$$2\pi r V_r = \Lambda \implies V_r = \frac{\Lambda}{2\pi r}, \quad V_\theta = 0$$
- Integrating for $\phi$ and $\psi$:
$$\phi = \frac{\Lambda}{2\pi} \ln r$$
$$\psi = \frac{\Lambda}{2\pi} \theta$$
- **Line Sink:** Fluid flows radially inward toward the origin. Exactly identical formulas with $\Lambda < 0$.
- **Source located at $(x_0, y_0)$:**
$$\phi = \frac{\Lambda}{4\pi} \ln\left[(x-x_0)^2 + (y-y_0)^2\right]$$
$$\psi = \frac{\Lambda}{2\pi} \arctan\left(\frac{y-y_0}{x-x_0}\right)$$

---

### 3. Doublet (Dipole)
A doublet is formed by bringing a source of strength $+\Lambda$ at $(-\epsilon, 0)$ and a sink of strength $-\Lambda$ at $(+\epsilon, 0)$ infinitesimally close together ($\epsilon \to 0$), while keeping the product $\kappa = 2\epsilon \Lambda$ constant.
- Doublet strength: $\kappa$ ($\text{m}^3/\text{s}$).
- Governing functions (directed along $+x$ axis):
$$\phi = \frac{\kappa}{2\pi} \frac{\cos\theta}{r} = \frac{\kappa}{2\pi} \frac{x}{x^2 + y^2}$$
$$\psi = -\frac{\kappa}{2\pi} \frac{\sin\theta}{r} = -\frac{\kappa}{2\pi} \frac{y}{x^2 + y^2}$$
- Velocity components:
$$V_r = \frac{\partial \phi}{\partial r} = -\frac{\kappa}{2\pi} \frac{\cos\theta}{r^2}$$
$$V_\theta = \frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial \phi}{\partial \theta} = -\frac{\kappa}{2\pi} \frac{\sin\theta}{r^2}$$
- **Streamline Geometry:** Streamlines $\psi = \text{const} = C$ satisfy:
$$x^2 + y^2 + \frac{\kappa}{2\pi C} y = 0 \iff x^2 + \left(y + \frac{\kappa}{4\pi C}\right)^2 = \left(\frac{\kappa}{4\pi C}\right)^2$$
They form a family of **circles tangent to the $x$-axis at the origin**, with centers on the $y$-axis!

---

### 4. Point Vortex (Potential / Free Vortex)
A flow where fluid particles move in concentric circles around the origin with velocity inversely proportional to radius.
- Circulation $\Gamma$: Line integral of velocity along any closed circuit enclosing the origin:
$$\Gamma = \oint \vec{V} \cdot d\vec{r} = V_\theta (2\pi r) \implies V_\theta = \frac{\Gamma}{2\pi r}, \quad V_r = 0$$
- Standard Aerodynamic Convention (Positive $\Gamma$ clockwise in Anderson vs counter-clockwise in math):
  - In GATE, check the given convention! If $\Gamma$ is defined positive **counter-clockwise**:
$$V_\theta = +\frac{\Gamma}{2\pi r}, \quad \phi = \frac{\Gamma}{2\pi} \theta, \quad \psi = -\frac{\Gamma}{2\pi} \ln r$$
  - If $\Gamma$ is defined positive **clockwise** (Anderson textbook convention):
$$V_\theta = -\frac{\Gamma}{2\pi r}, \quad \phi = -\frac{\Gamma}{2\pi} \theta, \quad \psi = \frac{\Gamma}{2\pi} \ln r$$
- **Vorticity Invariant:**
$$\omega_z = \frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial (r V_\theta)}{\partial r} - \frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial V_r}{\partial \theta} = \frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\left(\frac{\Gamma}{2\pi}\right) = 0 \quad (\text{for } r > 0)$$
The flow is **strictly irrotational everywhere except at the singular origin $r = 0$**, where vorticity is infinite (Dirac delta distribution).

## 🧠 Part III: Canonical Superpositions

### 1. Rankine Half-Body: Uniform Flow + Line Source
Superposition of uniform flow along $+x$ ($\psi = V_\infty y = V_\infty r \sin\theta$) and source of strength $\Lambda$ at origin ($\psi = \frac{\Lambda}{2\pi} \theta$):
$$\psi = V_\infty r \sin\theta + \frac{\Lambda}{2\pi} \theta$$
$$\phi = V_\infty r \cos\theta + \frac{\Lambda}{2\pi} \ln r$$

#### Stagnation Point:
Setting $\vec{V} = \vec{0}$ along the upstream axis ($\theta = \pi$):
$$V_r = V_\infty \cos\pi + \frac{\Lambda}{2\pi r} = -V_\infty + \frac{\Lambda}{2\pi r} = 0$$
$$r_{\text{stag}} = b = \frac{\Lambda}{2\pi V_\infty}, \quad \theta = \pi \implies (x_s, y_s) = \left(-\frac{\Lambda}{2\pi V_\infty}, 0\right)$$

#### Dividing Streamline:
Evaluating $\psi$ at the stagnation point $(r=b, \theta=\pi)$:
$$\psi_{\text{stag}} = V_\infty b \sin\pi + \frac{\Lambda}{2\pi} \pi = \frac{\Lambda}{2}$$
Equation of the half-body surface:
$$V_\infty r \sin\theta + \frac{\Lambda}{2\pi} \theta = \frac{\Lambda}{2} \iff y = r\sin\theta = \frac{\Lambda}{2\pi V_\infty}(\pi - \theta)$$

#### Asymptotic Downstream Half-Width:
As $x \to +\infty$, $\theta \to 0$:
$$y_{\text{max}} = \frac{\Lambda}{2\pi V_\infty}(\pi - 0) = \frac{\Lambda}{2 V_\infty}$$
Total asymptotic body thickness:
$$2 y_{\text{max}} = \frac{\Lambda}{V_\infty}$$

---

### 2. Flow Past a Non-Lifting Circular Cylinder: Uniform Flow + Doublet
Superposition of uniform flow $V_\infty$ and doublet of strength $\kappa$:
$$\psi = V_\infty r \sin\theta - \frac{\kappa}{2\pi} \frac{\sin\theta}{r} = V_\infty r \sin\theta \left(1 - \frac{\kappa}{2\pi V_\infty r^2}\right)$$
Defining cylinder radius $R$ where:
$$R^2 = \frac{\kappa}{2\pi V_\infty} \implies R = \sqrt{\frac{\kappa}{2\pi V_\infty}}$$
$$\psi = V_\infty \sin\theta \left(r - \frac{R^2}{r}\right)$$
$$\phi = V_\infty \cos\theta \left(r + \frac{R^2}{r}\right)$$

#### Boundary Verification:
At $r = R$, $\psi = 0$ for all $\theta$. Thus the circle $r = R$ is a streamline that represents a solid cylinder!

#### Velocity on Cylinder Surface ($r = R$):
$$V_r = \left.\frac{1}{r} \frac{\partial \psi}{\partial \theta}\right|_{r=R} = 0$$
$$V_\theta = -\left.\frac{\partial \psi}{\partial r}\right|_{r=R} = -V_\infty \sin\theta \left(1 + \frac{R^2}{R^2}\right) = -2 V_\infty \sin\theta$$
Surface speed:
$$V_{\text{surface}} = |V_\theta| = 2 V_\infty |\sin\theta|$$

#### Surface Pressure Distribution & $C_p$:
From Bernoulli's equation ($p_\infty + \frac{1}{2}\rho V_\infty^2 = p + \frac{1}{2}\rho V^2$):
$$C_p = \frac{p - p_\infty}{\frac{1}{2}\rho V_\infty^2} = 1 - \left(\frac{V}{V_\infty}\right)^2 = 1 - 4\sin^2\theta$$

| Angular Position $\theta$ | Location | Surface Velocity $V$ | Pressure Coeff $C_p$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| $0^\circ$ | Rear Stagnation Point | $0$ | $+1.0$ (Max Pressure) |
| $90^\circ$ | Top Shoulder | $2 V_\infty$ | $-3.0$ (Peak Suction) |
| $180^\circ$ | Forward Stagnation Point | $0$ | $+1.0$ (Max Pressure) |
| $270^\circ$ | Bottom Shoulder | $2 V_\infty$ | $-3.0$ (Peak Suction) |

#### D'Alembert's Paradox:
Integrating $C_p$ around the cylinder circumference:
$$C_D = \frac{1}{2} \int_0^{2\pi} C_p \cos\theta \, d\theta = \frac{1}{2} \int_0^{2\pi} (1 - 4\sin^2\theta)\cos\theta \, d\theta = 0$$
$$C_L = -\frac{1}{2} \int_0^{2\pi} C_p \sin\theta \, d\theta = -\frac{1}{2} \int_0^{2\pi} (1 - 4\sin^2\theta)\sin\theta \, d\theta = 0$$
Both Lift and Drag are identically zero due to fore-and-aft and top-and-bottom symmetry!

## 🎯 Distractor Autopsies & Trap Catalog

> ⚠️ **Trap 1: The "Rotational Stream Function" Fallacy (GATE MSQ Trap)**  
> **Claim:** "If $\nabla \times \vec{V} \neq 0$, the stream function $\psi$ does not exist."  
> **Autopsy: DEAD WRONG!** The stream function $\psi$ depends ONLY on 2D incompressibility ($\nabla \cdot \vec{V} = 0$). Even for rotational shear flows (like Couette or Poiseuille flow where $\nabla \times \vec{V} \neq 0$), $\psi$ exists and satisfies Poisson's equation $\nabla^2 \psi = -\omega_z$. It is the **velocity potential $\phi$** that requires irrotationality ($\nabla \times \vec{V} = 0$).

> ⚠️ **Trap 2: The Sign of Vortex Circulation**  
> Always read whether the question defines $\Gamma$ as positive counter-clockwise or clockwise!  
> If CCW: $V_\theta = +\frac{\Gamma}{2\pi r}$. If CW: $V_\theta = -\frac{\Gamma}{2\pi r}$. A sign flip here causes a negative lift in the Kutta-Joukowski theorem!

> ⚠️ **Trap 3: Peak Suction Magnitude on Cylinder**  
> On a circular cylinder in uniform flow, maximum velocity is $2 V_\infty$ (factor of 2 amplification).  
> The minimum pressure coefficient is $C_{p,\min} = 1 - (2)^2 = -3.0$, NOT $-1.0$ or $-4.0$.

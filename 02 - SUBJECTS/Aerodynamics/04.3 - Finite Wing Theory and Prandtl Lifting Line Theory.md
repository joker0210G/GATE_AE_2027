## 📌 Executive Summary & GATE AE Context

When moving from a 2D airfoil (infinite span) to a 3D finite wing, high pressure on the lower surface bleeds around the wingtips toward the low pressure on the upper surface. This generates:
1. **Trailing Wingtip Vortices:** Two counter-rotating vortices shed downstream.
2. **Downwash ($w$):** A downward vertical velocity component across the entire wingspan.
3. **Induced Angle of Attack ($\alpha_i$):** Tilting the effective relative wind downward: $\alpha_{\text{eff}} = \alpha - \alpha_i$.
4. **Induced Drag ($C_{Di}$):** Component of aerodynamic force tilted backward along the freestream direction.

In GATE AE, finite wing theory is tested through:
- **Helmholtz's Vortex Theorems** (vortex filaments, conservation of circulation).
- **Prandtl's Horseshoe Vortex Model** and Biot-Savart downwash calculations.
- **Elliptic Lift Distribution:** The theoretical benchmark for minimum induced drag ($e = 1, w = \text{const}$).
- **General Monoplane Equation:** Fourier sine series representation $\sum A_n \sin(n\theta)$ and span efficiency factor $e = \frac{1}{1+\delta}$.
- **3D Wing Lift-Curve Slope:** Degradation from 2D $a_0 = 2\pi$ to 3D $a = \frac{a_0}{1 + a_0/(\pi AR e)}$.

## 🧠 Part I: The Physics of 3D Finite Wings

### 1. Helmholtz's Vortex Theorems
1. **Theorem 1:** The strength of a vortex filament is constant along its entire length ($d\Gamma/ds = 0$).
2. **Theorem 2:** A vortex filament cannot end inside a fluid; it must either extend to the boundaries of the fluid (e.g. infinity) or form a closed loop (e.g. vortex ring).
3. **Theorem 3:** In an inviscid fluid with conservative body forces, fluid elements that are once part of a vortex filament remain part of that vortex filament (vorticity is "frozen" into the fluid).

---

### 2. The Horseshoe Vortex & Biot-Savart Downwash
Prandtl modeled the 3D finite wing of span $b$ as a single **horseshoe vortex**:
1. A **bound vortex** of strength $\Gamma$ along the quarter-chord line from $-b/2$ to $+b/2$.
2. Two **free trailing vortex filaments** of strength $\Gamma$ extending from the wingtips ($y = \pm b/2$) to downstream infinity.

#### Biot-Savart Law for a Semi-Infinite Vortex:
The downward velocity induced by a single semi-infinite vortex filament of strength $\Gamma$ at distance $r$ is:
$$w = \frac{\Gamma}{4\pi r}$$

At the center of the wingspan ($y = 0$), both trailing vortices induce downward velocity:
$$w(0) = 2 \times \frac{\Gamma}{4\pi (b/2)} = \frac{\Gamma}{\pi b}$$

---

### 3. Continuous Vortex Sheet Model
In reality, circulation varies across the wingspan $\Gamma = \Gamma(y)$, shedding a continuous trailing vortex sheet of strength:
$$\gamma(y) = -\frac{d\Gamma}{dy}$$

The downwash velocity at span station $y_0$ is the Cauchy principal value integral:
$$w(y_0) = \frac{1}{4\pi} \int_{-b/2}^{b/2} \frac{d\Gamma/dy}{y_0 - y} \, dy$$

The **Induced Angle of Attack** is:
$$\alpha_i(y_0) = \frac{w(y_0)}{V_\infty}$$

The **Effective Angle of Attack** experienced by the 2D airfoil section at $y_0$ is:
$$\alpha_{\text{eff}}(y_0) = \alpha(y_0) - \alpha_i(y_0)$$

## 📐 Part II: Prandtl Lifting Line Theory

### 1. The Fundamental Integro-Differential Equation
At each spanwise station $y_0$, the local sectional lift coefficient is:
$$c_l(y_0) = a_0 \left[ \alpha(y_0) - \alpha_0(y_0) - \alpha_i(y_0) \right] = \frac{2\Gamma(y_0)}{V_\infty c(y_0)}$$

Substituting $\alpha_i(y_0)$ gives **Prandtl's Fundamental Equation of Lifting Line Theory**:
$$\alpha(y_0) = \frac{2\Gamma(y_0)}{a_0 V_\infty c(y_0)} + \alpha_0(y_0) + \frac{1}{4\pi V_\infty} \int_{-b/2}^{b/2} \frac{d\Gamma/dy}{y_0 - y} \, dy$$

---

### 2. Angular Coordinate Transformation
Transform the spanwise coordinate $y$ via:
$$y = -\frac{b}{2}\cos\theta \quad (0 \le \theta \le \pi)$$
- Port wingtip ($y = -b/2$): $\theta = 0$
- Wing root ($y = 0$): $\theta = \pi/2$
- Starboard wingtip ($y = +b/2$): $\theta = \pi$

The circulation distribution is represented by a Fourier sine series:
$$\Gamma(\theta) = 2 b V_\infty \sum_{n=1}^\infty A_n \sin(n\theta)$$

The downwash velocity becomes:
$$w(\theta) = V_\infty \sum_{n=1}^\infty n A_n \frac{\sin(n\theta)}{\sin\theta}$$

Substituting into the fundamental equation yields the **General Monoplane Equation**:
$$\alpha(\theta) = \frac{4b}{a_0 c(\theta)} \sum_{n=1}^\infty A_n \sin(n\theta) + \alpha_0(\theta) + \sum_{n=1}^\infty n A_n \frac{\sin(n\theta)}{\sin\theta}$$

> 💡 **Symmetry Invariant:** For a symmetric wing without yaw or roll, circulation is symmetric about the centerline ($\Gamma(y) = \Gamma(-y)$). Consequently, **all even Fourier coefficients vanish identically**:
> $$A_2 = A_4 = A_6 = \dots = 0$$

## ⚡ Part III: Elliptic Lift Distribution & General Wing Performance

### 1. The Elliptic Lift Distribution ($A_1 \ne 0, A_n = 0 \text{ for } n \ge 2$)
If the circulation varies elliptically:
$$\Gamma(y) = \Gamma_0 \sqrt{1 - \left(\frac{2y}{b}\right)^2} \iff \Gamma(\theta) = \Gamma_0 \sin\theta$$
where $\Gamma_0 = 2 b V_\infty A_1$.

#### Master Invariants of the Elliptic Wing:
1. **Constant Downwash:**
   $$w(y) = \frac{\Gamma_0}{2b} = \text{const across entire span!}$$
2. **Constant Induced Angle of Attack:**
   $$\alpha_i = \frac{w}{V_\infty} = \frac{C_L}{\pi AR}$$
3. **Total Lift Coefficient:**
   $$C_L = \frac{L}{\frac{1}{2}\rho_\infty V_\infty^2 S} = \pi AR A_1 = \frac{\pi b \Gamma_0}{2 V_\infty S}$$
4. **Induced Drag Coefficient (Minimum Theoretical Value):**
   $$C_{Di} = C_L \alpha_i = \frac{C_L^2}{\pi AR}$$
5. **Lift-Curve Slope of 3D Elliptic Wing:**
   $$a = \frac{dC_L}{d\alpha} = \frac{a_0}{1 + \frac{a_0}{\pi AR}}$$

---

### 2. General (Non-Elliptic) Wing Performance
For any general wing planform (rectangular, trapezoidal):
1. **Lift Coefficient:**
   $$C_L = \pi AR A_1$$
   (Lift depends EXCLUSIVELY on the first Fourier coefficient $A_1$!)
2. **Induced Drag Coefficient:**
   $$C_{Di} = \frac{C_L^2}{\pi AR} (1 + \delta)$$
   where the induced drag factor $\delta$ is:
   $$\delta = \sum_{n=2}^\infty n \left( \frac{A_n}{A_1} \right)^2 \ge 0$$
3. **Oswald Span Efficiency Factor ($e$):**
   $$e = \frac{1}{1 + \delta} \le 1.0$$
   $$C_{Di} = \frac{C_L^2}{\pi e AR}$$
4. **General 3D Lift-Curve Slope:**
   $$a = \frac{a_0}{1 + \frac{a_0}{\pi e AR}}$$

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FW-01 · Minimum Induced Drag Condition
> **Question:** What spanwise circulation distribution produces the absolute minimum induced drag for a planar wing of fixed span and lift, and what is its induced drag coefficient?
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > - Distribution: Elliptic circulation distribution ($\Gamma(y) = \Gamma_0 \sqrt{1 - (2y/b)^2}$).
> > - Induced Drag: $C_{Di} = \frac{C_L^2}{\pi AR}$.
> > - Downwash: Uniform / constant along span ($w = \frac{\Gamma_0}{2b}$).
> > ⚠️ **AIR 1 Trap:** Elliptic lift does NOT require an elliptic planform if the wing has geometric or aerodynamic twist! But for an untwisted wing, an elliptic planform produces elliptic lift.

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FW-02 · Aspect Ratio Effect on Lift-Curve Slope
> **Question:** Why does the 3D lift-curve slope $a$ decrease as aspect ratio $AR$ decreases?
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > As $AR$ decreases, wingtips become closer together, increasing trailing vortex intensity and downwash $w$. Larger downwash increases the induced angle of attack $\alpha_i = \frac{C_L}{\pi e AR}$, reducing the effective angle of attack $\alpha_{\text{eff}} = \alpha - \alpha_i$ felt by the wing.
> > Formula: $a = \frac{a_0}{1 + \frac{a_0}{\pi e AR}}$.

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FW-03 · General Monoplane Lift Formula
> **Question:** In the Fourier series representation of circulation, which coefficient(s) determine the total lift coefficient $C_L$?
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > Strictly $A_1$ alone: $C_L = \pi AR A_1$.
> > Higher harmonics $A_2, A_3, A_4, \dots$ do NOT alter the total lift! They only increase the induced drag through $\delta = \sum n (A_n/A_1)^2$.

## 🖩 Worked Example: High-Altitude Reconnaissance Wing

**Problem (GATE AE Level 4 NAT):**  
A high-altitude solar-powered UAV has a planar wing with aspect ratio $AR = 12.0$ and span efficiency factor $e = 0.940$. The 2D airfoil sections have lift-curve slope $a_0 = 2\pi\text{ rad}^{-1}$.  
1. Compute the 3D lift-curve slope $a$ in $\text{rad}^{-1}$ and $\text{deg}^{-1}$.  
2. At cruise flight where $C_L = 0.850$, compute the induced angle of attack $\alpha_i$ in degrees.  
3. Calculate the induced drag coefficient $C_{Di}$.  
4. If wing span is $b = 24.0\text{ m}$, airspeed is $V_\infty = 40.0\text{ m/s}$, and air density is $\rho_\infty = 0.400\text{ kg/m}^3$, compute the peak circulation $\Gamma_0$ assuming elliptic loading.

**Analytical Solution & TCS Keystrokes:**
1. **3D Lift-Curve Slope:**
   $$a = \frac{a_0}{1 + \frac{a_0}{\pi e AR}} = \frac{2\pi}{1 + \frac{2\pi}{\pi \times 0.940 \times 12.0}} = \frac{2\pi}{1 + \frac{2}{11.28}} = \frac{2\pi}{1 + 0.177305} = \frac{6.283185}{1.177305} = 5.3369\text{ rad}^{-1}$$
   In degrees:
   $$a = 5.3369 \times \frac{\pi}{180} = 0.09315\text{ deg}^{-1}$$

2. **Induced Angle of Attack:**
   $$\alpha_i = \frac{C_L}{\pi e AR}\text{ rad} = \frac{0.850}{\pi \times 0.940 \times 12.0} = \frac{0.850}{35.4371} = 0.023986\text{ rad}$$
   In degrees:
   $$\alpha_i = 0.023986 \times \frac{180}{\pi} = 1.3743^\circ \approx 1.37^\circ$$

3. **Induced Drag Coefficient:**
   $$C_{Di} = \frac{C_L^2}{\pi e AR} = \frac{0.850^2}{35.4371} = \frac{0.7225}{35.4371} = 0.020388 \approx 0.0204$$

4. **Peak Circulation $\Gamma_0$:**
   Wing area $S = b^2 / AR = 24.0^2 / 12.0 = 576.0 / 12.0 = 48.0\text{ m}^2$.  
   Total Lift:
   $$L = \frac{1}{2}\rho_\infty V_\infty^2 S C_L = \frac{1}{2} \times 0.400 \times 40^2 \times 48.0 \times 0.850 = 0.200 \times 1600 \times 48.0 \times 0.850 = 13056.0\text{ N}$$
   For elliptic loading:
   $$L = \frac{1}{2}\rho_\infty V_\infty \pi b \Gamma_0 \implies \Gamma_0 = \frac{2 L}{\rho_\infty V_\infty \pi b}$$
   $$\Gamma_0 = \frac{2 \times 13056.0}{0.400 \times 40.0 \times \pi \times 24.0} = \frac{26112.0}{1206.37} = 21.645\text{ m}^2/\text{s}$$
   Target Answers: **$a = 5.34\text{ rad}^{-1}$**, **$\alpha_i = 1.37^\circ$**, **$C_{Di} = 0.0204$**, **$\Gamma_0 = 21.65\text{ m}^2/\text{s}$**

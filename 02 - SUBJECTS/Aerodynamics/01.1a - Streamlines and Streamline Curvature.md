## 📌 1. What is This Topic? (Zero to AIR-1 Overview)

A **Streamline** is the fundamental geometric building block of Aerodynamics. While Cartesian coordinates $(x,y,z)$ describe fluid motion from a fixed laboratory perspective, **Natural Streamline Coordinates $(s, n)$** align directly with the fluid physics, decomposing motion into:
1. **Motion along the streamline ($s$):** Governed by tangential acceleration and Bernoulli's equation.
2. **Motion normal to the streamline ($n$):** Governed by centripetal acceleration and the **Streamline Curvature Pressure Gradient** ($\frac{\partial p}{\partial n} = \frac{\rho V^2}{R}$).

In GATE Aerospace Engineering, Streamline analysis is essential because:
- It explains how **cambered and curved airfoil surfaces generate aerodynamic lift** (streamline curvature creates suction on the convex upper surface).
- It provides the differential equations required to determine flow lines and dividing streamlines around bodies (cylinders, spheres, airfoils, Rankine bodies).
- It establishes the exact conditions under which **Bernoulli's Equation** can be applied along vs. across streamlines.

> 💡 **GATE Target:** 1–2 direct questions (MCQ/NAT, ~2–3 marks) in Aerodynamics every year, plus foundational mastery for Potential Flow Superposition, Airfoil Aerodynamics, and Boundary Layer Theory.

## 🧠 Part I: Comprehensive Streamline Formulations

```
                  STREAMLINE KINEMATICS
               ┌────────────────────────┐
               │    d r || V (Vector)   │
               │   d r × V = 0          │
               └───────────┬────────────┘
                           │
         ┌─────────────────┼─────────────────┐
         ▼                 ▼                 ▼
   2D CARTESIAN       3D CARTESIAN     CYLINDRICAL POLAR
     dy/dx = v/u     dx/u = dy/v = dz/w   dr/v_r = r dθ/v_θ
```

---

### 1. Vector and Differential Equations of a Streamline

A **Streamline** is defined as an instantaneous curve in the flow field such that the tangent at every point along the curve is in the exact direction of the velocity vector $\vec{V}$ at that instant.

#### A. Vector Collinearity Condition:
$$d\vec{r} \times \vec{V} = \vec{0}$$
where $d\vec{r} = dx\,\hat{i} + dy\,\hat{j} + dz\,\hat{k}$ and $\vec{V} = u\,\hat{i} + v\,\hat{j} + w\,\hat{k}$.

#### B. 2D Cartesian Coordinate System:
$$\frac{dx}{u} = \frac{dy}{v} \iff \frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{v}{u} \iff u\,dy - v\,dx = 0$$

#### C. 3D Cartesian Coordinate System:
$$\frac{dx}{u} = \frac{dy}{v} = \frac{dz}{w} \quad (\text{at frozen time } t = t_0)$$
*(Geometrically, a 3D streamline is the curve formed by the intersection of two distinct stream surfaces $\psi_1(x,y,z) = c_1$ and $\psi_2(x,y,z) = c_2$).*

#### D. 2D Cylindrical Polar Coordinates $(r, \theta)$:
$$d\vec{r} = dr\,\hat{e}_r + r\,d\theta\,\hat{e}_\theta, \quad \vec{V} = v_r\,\hat{e}_r + v_\theta\,\hat{e}_\theta$$
$$\frac{dr}{v_r} = \frac{r\,d\theta}{v_\theta} \iff \frac{dr}{d\theta} = \frac{r\,v_r}{v_\theta}$$

#### E. 3D Spherical Polar Coordinates $(r, \theta, \phi)$:
$$\frac{dr}{v_r} = \frac{r\,d\theta}{v_\theta} = \frac{r\sin\theta\,d\phi}{v_\phi}$$

---

### 2. Streamline Properties and Invariants

1. **Frozen Time Snapshot:** In an unsteady flow field $\vec{V}(x,y,z,t)$, the parameter $t$ is held strictly constant ($t = t_0$) during integration.
2. **Non-Crossing Invariant:** In any continuous flow field, two streamlines **can never cross each other** (except at singular stagnation points where $\vec{V} = \vec{0}$, where the direction of velocity is indeterminate).
3. **Impermeability (Solid Wall Analogy):** Since velocity is strictly tangent to the streamline, the normal velocity component across it is identically zero ($V_n = \vec{V}\cdot\hat{n} = 0$). **No fluid mass can cross a streamline.**
   - *Inviscid Aerodynamics Principle:* Any solid aerodynamic boundary (e.g. airfoil surface, fuselage wall) can be mathematically replaced by a streamline!

---

### 3. Streamline Spacing and Flow Acceleration

In a 2D steady, incompressible planar flow, consider two adjacent streamlines $\psi$ and $\psi + \Delta\psi$ separated by a normal distance $\Delta n$:

$$\Delta Q = V \cdot \Delta n = \Delta\psi = \text{constant}$$

$$V = \frac{\Delta\psi}{\Delta n} \implies V \propto \frac{1}{\Delta n}$$

```
   Streamline 1 (ψ)      ──────────────────────────────────
                                  ▲
                                  │   Wide Spacing Δn₁ (Low Velocity V₁)
                                  ▼
   Streamline 2 (ψ + Δψ) ───────\
                                 \  Converging
                                  \─────────────────
                                      ▲
                                      │ Narrow Spacing Δn₂ < Δn₁ (High Velocity V₂ > V₁)
                                      ▼
                         ───────────────────────────
```

> 🔑 **AIR-1 Rule of Streamline Spacing:**
> - **Crowding (Convergence) of Streamlines:** $\Delta n \downarrow \implies$ Velocity increases ($V \uparrow$) $\implies$ Static pressure decreases ($p \downarrow$).
> - **Divergence of Streamlines:** $\Delta n \uparrow \implies$ Velocity decreases ($V \downarrow$) $\implies$ Static pressure increases ($p \uparrow$).

## 🧠 Part II: Streamtubes, Stream Filaments & Mass Conservation

### 1. What is a Streamtube?

A **Streamtube** is a tubular surface in a fluid flow formed by the collection of all streamlines passing through a closed geometric contour $C$. A streamtube of infinitesimal cross-sectional area is called a **Stream Filament**.

```
                 Closed Contour C₁
                     ┌──────┐
                     │  A₁  │ ───────── Streamline Boundary ─────────┐
                     └──────┘                                        │
                        ▲                                            ▼
                        │                                         ┌──────┐
                        │              NO MASS TRANSFER           │  A₂  │ Closed Contour C₂
                        │            ACROSS TUBE WALLS            └──────┘
                        │              (V · n = 0)                   ▲
                        └────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

---

### 2. The Streamtube Mass Conservation Law

Because the entire boundary wall of a streamtube is composed of streamlines, the normal velocity at every point on the wall is zero ($V_n = 0$). Consequently:
- **No fluid can enter or leave the streamtube through its lateral surface.**
- Fluid can ONLY enter through the upstream inlet area $A_1$ and leave through the downstream exit area $A_2$.

For steady flow, the 1D Continuity Equation across any streamtube cross-section is:

$$\dot{m} = \iint_{A_1} \rho (\vec{V}\cdot\hat{n}) dA = \iint_{A_2} \rho (\vec{V}\cdot\hat{n}) dA = \text{constant}$$

For Quasi-1D Uniform Incompressible Flow:
$$\dot{m} = \rho_1 A_1 V_1 = \rho_2 A_2 V_2 = \text{constant}$$
$$A_1 V_1 = A_2 V_2 = Q \quad (\text{Volumetric Discharge})$$

## 🧠 Part III: Natural Streamline Coordinates $(s, n, b)$

To understand aerodynamic forces naturally, we erect a local orthogonal curvilinear coordinate system attached to the streamline at each point.

```
                                  Center of Curvature (O)
                                            *
                                           / \
                                          /   \  Radius of Curvature (R)
                                         /     \
                                        /       \
                                       ▼         ▼
   Streamline Trajectory ────────────●─────────────► Tangential Unit Vector e_s
                                     │
                                     │ Normal Unit Vector e_n (Points TOWARDS center O)
                                     ▼
```

### 1. Definition of Unit Vectors

At any point on a curved streamline:
1. **$\hat{e}_s$ (Tangential Unit Vector):** Directed along the local tangent to the streamline in the direction of flow.
2. **$\hat{e}_n$ (Principal Normal Unit Vector):** Directed perpendicular to $\hat{e}_s$, pointing **inwards towards the local center of curvature** $O$ (radius of curvature $R$).
3. **$\hat{e}_b$ (Binormal Unit Vector):** Defined as $\hat{e}_b = \hat{e}_s \times \hat{e}_n$, completing the right-handed orthogonal triad.

---

### 2. Velocity and Acceleration in Streamline Coordinates

#### A. Velocity Vector:
By definition, the fluid velocity has non-zero magnitude ONLY along the streamline:
$$\vec{V} = V(s, n, t)\,\hat{e}_s \quad (V_n = 0, \quad V_b = 0)$$

#### B. Acceleration Vector Decomposition:
The acceleration of a fluid parcel following a curved streamline is:
$$\vec{a} = \frac{D\vec{V}}{Dt} = \frac{D(V\,\hat{e}_s)}{Dt} = \frac{DV}{Dt}\hat{e}_s + V\frac{D\hat{e}_s}{Dt}$$

Using differential geometry relations for a curve with radius of curvature $R$:
$$\frac{D\hat{e}_s}{Dt} = \left(V\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\right)\hat{e}_s = \frac{V}{R}\hat{e}_n$$

Therefore, the **Complete Fluid Acceleration Vector in Streamline Coordinates** is:

$$\vec{a} = a_s\,\hat{e}_s + a_n\,\hat{e}_n$$

$$\begin{aligned}
a_s &= \underbrace{\frac{\partial V}{\partial t}}_{\text{Local Tangential Accel.}} + \underbrace{V\frac{\partial V}{\partial s}}_{\text{Convective Tangential Accel.}} \\
a_n &= \frac{V^2}{R} \quad (\text{Centripetal / Normal Accel. towards center of curvature})
\end{aligned}$$

## 🧠 Part IV: Euler Equations in Streamline Coordinates & Pressure Gradients

Applying Newton's Second Law ($\sum \vec{F} = m\vec{a}$) to an infinitesimal fluid parcel of dimensions $(\delta s, \delta n, \delta b)$ in an inviscid flow:

```
                            p + (∂p/∂n) δn
                                 │
                                 ▼
                     ┌───────────────────────┐
                     │                       │
      p ───────────► │      Fluid Parcel     │ ───────────► p + (∂p/∂s) δs
                     │       δs × δn × δb    │
                     │                       │
                     └───────────────────────┘
                                 ▲
                                 │
                                 p
```

---

### 1. Tangential Momentum Equation (Along Streamline $\hat{e}_s$)

Balancing net pressure force and gravity component along the streamline:
$$-\frac{\partial p}{\partial s}\delta s (\delta n \delta b) - \rho g \frac{\partial z}{\partial s}\delta s (\delta n \delta b) = (\rho \delta s \delta n \delta b) a_s$$

Dividing by parcel mass $\rho \delta s \delta n \delta b$:

$$-\frac{1}{\rho}\frac{\partial p}{\partial s} - g\frac{\partial z}{\partial s} = \frac{\partial V}{\partial t} + V\frac{\partial V}{\partial s}$$

#### Steady Flow Integration $\implies$ Bernoulli's Equation:
For steady flow ($\frac{\partial V}{\partial t} = 0$):
$$-\frac{1}{\rho}\frac{\partial p}{\partial s} - g\frac{\partial z}{\partial s} = V\frac{\partial V}{\partial s} = \frac{\partial}{\partial s}\left(\frac{V^2}{2}\right)$$

Rearranging and integrating with respect to $s$ (assuming incompressible flow $\rho = \text{constant}$):
$$\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\left(p + \frac{1}{2}\rho V^2 + \rho g z\right) = 0$$

$$\mathbf{p + \frac{1}{2}\rho V^2 + \rho g z = C_{\text{streamline}} \quad (\text{Constant along a given streamline})}$$

---

### 2. Normal Momentum Equation (Normal to Streamline $\hat{e}_n$)

Balancing net pressure force and gravity component along the inward normal $\hat{e}_n$:
$$-\frac{\partial p}{\partial n}\delta n (\delta s \delta b) - \rho g \frac{\partial z}{\partial n}\delta n (\delta s \delta b) = (\rho \delta s \delta n \delta b) \left(-\frac{V^2}{R}\right)$$

Dividing by parcel mass $\rho \delta s \delta n \delta b$:

$$\mathbf{\frac{\partial p}{\partial n} + \rho g \frac{\partial z}{\partial n} = \frac{\rho V^2}{R}}$$

Neglecting gravitational effects (standard in high-speed Aerodynamics):

$$\mathbf{\frac{\partial p}{\partial n} = \frac{\rho V^2}{R}}$$

*(where $n$ is measured along the normal vector directed **towards the center of curvature**).*

If $r$ is the radial distance measured **radially outward away from the center of curvature** ($dn = -dr$):

$$\mathbf{\frac{\partial p}{\partial r} = +\frac{\rho V^2}{r}}$$

---

## 🧠 Part V: The Aerodynamic Power of Streamline Curvature

> 🏆 **THE STREAMLINE CURVATURE PRINCIPLE OF AERODYNAMIC LIFT:**
> A curved streamline requires a transverse pressure gradient to provide the necessary centripetal acceleration ($V^2/R$).  
> **Pressure always increases radially away from the center of curvature!**

```
                                    p_ambient = p_∞
                                          ▲
                                          │   ∂p/∂r > 0
                                          │   (Pressure increases outward)
                                          │
                                ┌─────────────────┐
                                │   STREAMLINE    │  V_local > V_∞
                                └─────────────────┘  Radius of Curvature = R
                                          ▲
                                          │
                            AIRFOIL CONVEX UPPER SURFACE
                            p_surface < p_∞ (SUCTION & LIFT!)
                                          ▲
                                          │  Center of Curvature (O) INSIDE Airfoil
                                          *
```

---

### 1. Convex Surface Aerodynamics (Airfoil Upper Surface / Suction Side)

1. The upper surface of an airfoil is **convex** $\implies$ The center of curvature $O$ lies **inside the airfoil**.
2. Radial coordinate $r$ points outward from inside the airfoil into the external flow.
3. Since $\frac{\partial p}{\partial r} = +\frac{\rho V^2}{r} > 0$, static pressure MUST increase as you move outward from the surface toward ambient freestream $p_\infty$.
4. **Direct Result:** The pressure on the upper surface of the airfoil MUST be lower than ambient freestream pressure:
   $$p_{\text{upper wall}} < p_\infty \implies C_p = \frac{p - p_\infty}{q_\infty} < 0 \quad (\text{Suction Peak!})$$
5. This suction over the upper surface produces the overwhelming majority (~70–80%) of the total **Aerodynamic Lift**!

---

### 2. Concave Surface Aerodynamics (Aft Lower Surface / Compression Side)

1. A concave surface curves such that the center of curvature $O$ lies **outside the body in the fluid**.
2. Moving from the fluid toward the surface is moving away from the center of curvature.
3. Since $\frac{\partial p}{\partial r} > 0$, static pressure MUST increase toward the wall.
4. **Direct Result:**
   $$p_{\text{concave wall}} > p_\infty \implies C_p > 0 \quad (\text{Positive Pressure Compression})$$

---

### 3. Straight Parallel Streamlines ($R \to \infty$)

When streamlines are straight and parallel:
$$R \to \infty \implies \frac{\partial p}{\partial n} = \frac{\rho V^2}{\infty} = 0$$

> 🔑 **Boundary Layer Invariant:**
> Across straight, parallel streamlines, the static pressure gradient normal to the surface is **identically ZERO** ($\frac{\partial p}{\partial y} = 0$).  
> This is Prandtl's famous Boundary Layer assumption: **Static pressure at the wall equals static pressure at the boundary layer edge** ($p_w(x) = p_e(x)$)!

## 🧠 Part VI: Special Streamline Topologies in Aerodynamics

```
┌─────────────────────────┬─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ Special Streamline Type │ Physical Meaning & Aerodynamic Significance                             │
├─────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 1. Stagnation Streamline│ Streamline ending at a stagnation point where V = 0, p = p₀ (Total P)   │
│ 2. Dividing Streamline  │ Streamline that bifurcates to enclose a solid body (ψ = 0 boundary)     │
│ 3. Wake Centerline      │ Streamline issuing from the trailing edge (Kutta condition path)        │
│ 4. Vortex Core Line     │ Axis around which circulating streamlines form closed concentric paths  │
└─────────────────────────┴─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

---

### 1. Flow Past a Non-Lifting Circular Cylinder

Uniform flow with speed $V_\infty$ past a cylinder of radius $a$:
$$\psi(r, \theta) = V_\infty \left(r - \frac{a^2}{r}\right)\sin\theta$$

- **Dividing Streamline ($\psi = 0$):**
  $$\psi = 0 \implies \sin\theta = 0 \quad (\theta = 0, \pi \text{ along } x\text{-axis}) \quad \text{OR} \quad r = a \quad (\text{Cylinder surface})$$
- The streamline branches at the **Front Stagnation Point** $(r = a, \theta = \pi)$, wraps around the cylinder as $r = a$, and rejoins at the **Rear Stagnation Point** $(r = a, \theta = 0)$.

```
                     Streamlines over Cylinder
                             ───────
                           /         \
                 ────────●             ●────────
                   Front Stagnation    Rear Stagnation
                   (r = a, θ = π)      (r = a, θ = 0)
```

---

### 2. Flow Past a Rankine Half-Body

Superposition of a **Uniform Flow** ($V_\infty$) and a **Source** of strength $\Lambda$ at the origin $(0, 0)$:
$$\psi(r, \theta) = V_\infty r \sin\theta + \frac{\Lambda}{2\pi}\theta$$

- **Stagnation Point:** Setting $V_r = 0 \implies V_\infty \cos\theta + \frac{\Lambda}{2\pi r} = 0$. At $\theta = \pi$:
  $$r_{\text{stag}} = b = \frac{\Lambda}{2\pi V_\infty}$$
- **Dividing Streamline Equation ($\psi = \psi_{\text{stag}} = \frac{\Lambda}{2}$):**
  $$V_\infty r \sin\theta + \frac{\Lambda}{2\pi}\theta = \frac{\Lambda}{2} \implies r(\theta) = \frac{\Lambda(1 - \theta/\pi)}{2 V_\infty \sin\theta} = \frac{b(\pi - \theta)}{\sin\theta}$$
- **Maximum Half-Width of Body (at $\theta \to 0$):**
  $$y_{\max} = \lim_{\theta\to 0} r\sin\theta = \pi b = \frac{\Lambda}{2 V_\infty}$$

## ⚡ AIR-1 Topper Mnemonics, Shortcuts & Speed Tricks

### 🚀 Shortcut 1: The 3-Second Radius of Curvature Formula
If the streamline trajectory is given explicitly as a curve $y = f(x)$:
$$R = \frac{\left(1 + \left(\frac{dy}{dx}\right)^2\right)^{3/2}}{\left|\frac{d^2y}{dx^2}\right|}$$
- At the crest of a symmetric bump/airfoil ($dy/dx = 0$):
  $$R = \frac{1}{|y''|}$$
- Then, the normal pressure gradient is immediately:
  $$\frac{\partial p}{\partial n} = \rho V^2 |y''|$$

---

### 🚀 Shortcut 2: The "Pressure Climbs Away from Center" Mnemonic
$$\frac{\partial p}{\partial r} = +\frac{\rho V^2}{r} > 0$$
- *Rule:* Imagine swinging a bucket of water. Centrifugal force pushes the fluid outward $\implies$ pressure builds up at the outer boundary.
- *Aerodynamic Consequence:* **Inner radius (convex surface) has LOW pressure; Outer radius has HIGH pressure.**

---

### 🚀 Shortcut 3: Instant 1D Streamtube Expansion Factor
For steady incompressible flow in a streamtube of variable cross-section:
$$\frac{V_2}{V_1} = \frac{A_1}{A_2} = \frac{\Delta n_1}{\Delta n_2}$$
$$\Delta p = p_1 - p_2 = \frac{1}{2}\rho V_1^2 \left[\left(\frac{A_1}{A_2}\right)^2 - 1\right]$$

---

### 🚀 Shortcut 4: Stagnation Pressure Formula along Streamline
$$p_0 = p + \frac{1}{2}\rho V^2 = \text{constant along a streamline}$$
- Dynamic pressure: $q = \frac{1}{2}\rho V^2$.
- If velocity increases by a factor of $k$ ($V \to k V$), dynamic pressure increases by $k^2$.

## 📝 Step-by-Step Solved GATE Examples (Zero to GATE Topper Level)

### Example 1: Streamline Equation for a Non-Linear 2D Flow Field
**Problem:** A 2D steady velocity field is defined by $u = 3x^2$, $v = -6xy$.
1. Find the general equation of the family of streamlines.
2. Determine the specific streamline passing through the point $(1, 2)$.
3. Sketch the qualitative shape of the streamline.

> [!faq]- 🔍 View Solution & Detailed Explanation
> **1. Differential Equation of Streamline:**
> $$\frac{dx}{u} = \frac{dy}{v} \implies \frac{dx}{3x^2} = \frac{dy}{-6xy}$$
> Cancel $3x$ from both denominators ($x \neq 0$):
> $$\frac{dx}{x} = \frac{dy}{-2y} \implies -2\frac{dx}{x} = \frac{dy}{y}$$
> 
> **2. Integration:**
> Integrating both sides:
> $$-2\ln x = \ln y + \ln C_1 \implies \ln(x^{-2}) = \ln(C_1 y) \implies \frac{1}{x^2} = C_1 y$$
> $$x^2 y = C \quad (\text{where } C = 1/C_1)$$
> 
> **3. Pass through $(1, 2)$:**
> $$C = (1)^2(2) = 2$$
> **Specific Streamline Equation:**
> $$x^2 y = 2 \iff y = \frac{2}{x^2}$$
> *(Shape: A symmetric cubic hyperbolic curve asymptotically approaching the $y$-axis as $x \to 0$ and the $x$-axis as $x \to \infty$).*

---

### Example 2: Normal Pressure Gradient across Curved Airfoil Nose (GATE AE Standard)
**Problem:** Air ($\rho = 1.2\text{ kg/m}^3$) flows at $V = 80\text{ m/s}$ over the convex leading edge of an airfoil with radius of curvature $R = 0.4\text{ m}$.
1. Calculate the normal pressure gradient $\frac{\partial p}{\partial r}$ at the surface.
2. If the normal distance across the curved streamline region is $\Delta n = 0.05\text{ m}$ and speed remains approximately constant, estimate the pressure drop $\Delta p$ from the freestream to the airfoil surface.

> [!faq]- 🔍 View Solution & Detailed Explanation
> **1. Normal Pressure Gradient:**
> The center of curvature lies inside the airfoil. The outward radial direction is $r$:
> $$\frac{\partial p}{\partial r} = +\frac{\rho V^2}{R}$$
> Substituting values:
> $$\frac{\partial p}{\partial r} = \frac{(1.2)(80)^2}{0.4} = \frac{(1.2)(6400)}{0.4} = 3 \times 6400 = \mathbf{19,200\text{ Pa/m} = 19.2\text{ kPa/m}}$$
> 
> **2. Pressure Drop to Surface:**
> $$\Delta p \approx \left(\frac{\partial p}{\partial r}\right) \Delta n = (19,200\text{ Pa/m}) \times (0.05\text{ m}) = \mathbf{960\text{ Pa} = 0.96\text{ kPa}}$$
> *The pressure on the convex surface is $960\text{ Pa}$ below the local ambient pressure outside the curved streamline zone!*

---

### Example 3: Streamtube Velocity and Pressure Evolution in a Contraction
**Problem:** An incompressible fluid ($\rho = 1000\text{ kg/m}^3$) enters a horizontal contracting streamtube of inlet diameter $D_1 = 0.2\text{ m}$ at a speed of $V_1 = 2\text{ m/s}$ and static pressure $p_1 = 150\text{ kPa}$. The streamtube narrows to an exit diameter $D_2 = 0.1\text{ m}$.
1. Find the exit velocity $V_2$.
2. Find the static pressure $p_2$ at the exit.
3. Calculate the force exerted by the fluid on the streamtube contraction walls if atmospheric pressure $p_{\text{atm}} = 100\text{ kPa}$.

> [!faq]- 🔍 View Solution & Detailed Explanation
> **1. Exit Velocity $V_2$ via Streamtube Continuity:**
> $$A_1 V_1 = A_2 V_2 \implies V_2 = V_1 \left(\frac{D_1}{D_2}\right)^2 = (2)\left(\frac{0.2}{0.1}\right)^2 = 2 \times 4 = \mathbf{8\text{ m/s}}$$
> 
> **2. Exit Pressure $p_2$ via Bernoulli along Streamline:**
> $$p_1 + \frac{1}{2}\rho V_1^2 = p_2 + \frac{1}{2}\rho V_2^2$$
> $$p_2 = p_1 + \frac{1}{2}\rho (V_1^2 - V_2^2) = 150\times 10^3 + \frac{1}{2}(1000)(2^2 - 8^2)$$
> $$p_2 = 150,000 + 500(4 - 64) = 150,000 - 30,000 = \mathbf{120\text{ kPa}}$$
> 
> **3. Force on Contraction Walls via Momentum Equation:**
> Control volume momentum balance along $x$:
> $$p_1 A_1 - p_2 A_2 - F_x = \dot{m}(V_2 - V_1)$$
> $$A_1 = \frac{\pi}{4}(0.2)^2 = 0.031416\text{ m}^2, \quad A_2 = \frac{\pi}{4}(0.1)^2 = 0.007854\text{ m}^2$$
> $$\dot{m} = \rho A_1 V_1 = (1000)(0.031416)(2) = 62.832\text{ kg/s}$$
> Gauge pressures: $p_{1,g} = 50\text{ kPa}, p_{2,g} = 20\text{ kPa}$.
> $$F_x = p_{1,g} A_1 - p_{2,g} A_2 - \dot{m}(V_2 - V_1)$$
> $$F_x = (50,000)(0.031416) - (20,000)(0.007854) - (62.832)(8 - 2)$$
> $$F_x = 1570.8 - 157.08 - 377.0 = \mathbf{+1036.7\text{ N (in +x direction on fluid)}} \implies \mathbf{1036.7\text{ N upstream on wall}}$$

### Example 4: Dividing Streamline for a Rankine Half-Body
**Problem:** A uniform flow of $V_\infty = 25\text{ m/s}$ in the $+x$ direction interacts with a 2D line source of strength $\Lambda = 50\pi\text{ m}^2\text{/s}$ placed at $(0, 0)$.
1. Find the distance of the front stagnation point from the source.
2. Determine the asymptotic half-thickness $y_{\max}$ of the resulting semi-infinite aerodynamic body.
3. Write the exact polar equation $r(\theta)$ for the body's dividing streamline.

> [!faq]- 🔍 View Solution & Detailed Explanation
> **1. Stagnation Point Distance ($b$):**
> $$b = \frac{\Lambda}{2\pi V_\infty} = \frac{50\pi}{2\pi(25)} = \frac{50\pi}{50\pi} = \mathbf{1.0\text{ m}}$$
> The stagnation point is located at $(x, y) = (-1.0\text{ m}, 0)$ or $(r, \theta) = (1.0\text{ m}, \pi)$.
> 
> **2. Asymptotic Half-Thickness ($y_{\max}$):**
> $$y_{\max} = \pi b = \pi (1.0) = \mathbf{\pi\text{ m} \approx 3.1416\text{ m}}$$
> Full body width downstream: $2 y_{\max} = 2\pi\text{ m} \approx 6.283\text{ m}$.
> 
> **3. Dividing Streamline Equation:**
> $$\psi(r, \theta) = V_\infty r \sin\theta + \frac{\Lambda}{2\pi}\theta$$
> At the stagnation point $(r=1, \theta=\pi)$:
> $$\psi_{\text{stag}} = (25)(1)\sin\pi + \frac{50\pi}{2\pi}(\pi) = 0 + 25\pi = 25\pi$$
> Set $\psi(r, \theta) = 25\pi$:
> $$25 r \sin\theta + 25\theta = 25\pi \implies 25 r \sin\theta = 25(\pi - \theta)$$
> $$\mathbf{r(\theta) = \frac{\pi - \theta}{\sin\theta}\text{ meters}}$$

## ⚠️ Common Exam Traps & Pitfalls (Where Marks are Lost)

> 🔴 **Trap 1: Applying Bernoulli Equation ACROSS Streamlines in Rotational Flow**
> - *Mistake:* Assuming $p_1 + \frac{1}{2}\rho V_1^2 = p_2 + \frac{1}{2}\rho V_2^2$ between two points on *different* streamlines when the flow has vorticity ($\vec{\omega} \neq \vec{0}$).
> - *Correction:* Bernoulli's constant is identical across ALL streamlines **ONLY IF the flow is IRROTATIONAL** ($\nabla \times \vec{V} = \vec{0}$). In rotational flow, $C = C(\psi)$ changes from streamline to streamline!

> 🔴 **Trap 2: Sign of the Normal Pressure Gradient**
> - *Mistake:* Writing $\frac{\partial p}{\partial n} = -\frac{\rho V^2}{R}$.
> - *Correction:* If $n$ points TOWARDS the center of curvature, the force required is inward $\implies$ outer pressure is larger $\implies \frac{\partial p}{\partial n} = +\frac{\rho V^2}{R}$. Pressure always **increases away from the center of curvature**.

> 🔴 **Trap 3: Confusing Radius of Curvature with Coordinate Radius**
> - *Mistake:* Using coordinate $r$ instead of local radius of curvature $R$ for arbitrary streamline trajectories.
> - *Correction:* For concentric circular streamlines, $R = r$. For general curves $y = f(x)$, $R = \frac{(1+y'^2)^{3/2}}{|y''|}$.

> 🔴 **Trap 4: Missing Time-Freezing in Unsteady Streamlines**
> - *Mistake:* Treating $t$ as an integration variable in $\frac{dx}{u(x,y,t)} = \frac{dy}{v(x,y,t)}$.
> - *Correction:* Streamlines are instantaneous snapshots! Always set $t = t_0 = \text{constant}$ before integrating.

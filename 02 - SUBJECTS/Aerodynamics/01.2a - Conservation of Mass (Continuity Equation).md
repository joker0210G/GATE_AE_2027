## 📌 1. What is This Topic? (Zero to AIR-1 Overview)

The **Conservation of Mass** (expressed mathematically as the **Continuity Equation**) is the first fundamental governing law of fluid mechanics and aerodynamics. It states that **mass can neither be created nor destroyed within a closed fluid system**.

In GATE Aerospace Engineering, Conservation of Mass is tested in two complementary representations:
1. **Integral Form (Reynolds Transport Theorem):** Applied to macroscopic finite Control Volumes (CV) such as jet engine nacelles, wind tunnel test sections, rocket nozzles, and boundary layers to compute mass flow rates ($\dot{m} = \iint \rho \vec{V}\cdot d\vec{A}$), mean velocities ($\bar{V}$), and mass accumulation rates ($\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\iiint \rho\,d\mathcal{V}$).
2. **Differential Form (Pointwise Conservation Law):** Applied to infinitesimal fluid elements via the divergence theorem, yielding the partial differential equation $\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial t} + \nabla \cdot (\rho\vec{V}) = 0$ in Cartesian, Cylindrical Polar, and Spherical coordinate systems, as well as the universal incompressibility condition $\nabla \cdot \vec{V} = 0$.

> 💡 **GATE Target:** 2–3 direct questions (MCQ/MSQ/NAT, ~3–5 marks) every year, plus an absolute prerequisite for Navier-Stokes equations, Euler equations, Quasi-1D nozzle compressible flow, and boundary layer momentum integral equations.

## 🧠 Part I: Reynolds Transport Theorem (RTT) & The Integral Form

```
                       REYNOLDS TRANSPORT THEOREM (RTT)
               System (Lagrangian) ────────► Control Volume (Eulerian)
                                      │
               ┌──────────────────────┴──────────────────────┐
               ▼                                             ▼
     Rate of Storage / Accumulation               Net Outflow Rate (Flux)
      in Control Volume (CV)                     across Control Surface (CS)
     ∂/∂t ∭_CV (ρ β) dV                          ∬_CS ρ β (V · n) dA
```

---

### 1. General Reynolds Transport Theorem (RTT)

For any extensive fluid property $B$ (e.g. mass $m$, momentum $m\vec{V}$, energy $E$) and its corresponding intensive property $\beta = \frac{dB}{dm}$:

$$\frac{D B_{\text{sys}}}{Dt} = \frac{\partial}{\partial t}\iiint_{\text{CV}} \rho \beta \, d\mathcal{V} + \iint_{\text{CS}} \rho \beta (\vec{V} \cdot \hat{n}) \, dA$$

where:
- $\hat{n}$ is the **outward-pointing unit normal vector** to the control surface $dA$.
- $\vec{V} \cdot \hat{n} > 0$ represents mass **outflow (efflux)**.
- $\vec{V} \cdot \hat{n} < 0$ represents mass **inflow (influx)**.

---

### 2. Derivation of the Integral Continuity Equation

For the conservation of mass, we set the extensive property to mass:
$$B = m \implies \beta = \frac{dm}{dm} = 1$$

Since mass is strictly conserved for a closed physical system:
$$\frac{D m_{\text{sys}}}{Dt} \equiv 0$$

Substituting into the RTT gives the **Universal Integral Continuity Equation**:

$$\mathbf{\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\iiint_{\text{CV}} \rho \, d\mathcal{V} + \iint_{\text{CS}} \rho (\vec{V} \cdot \hat{n}) \, dA = 0}$$

$$\underbrace{\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\iiint_{\text{CV}} \rho \, d\mathcal{V}}_{\text{Rate of Mass Accumulation within CV}} + \underbrace{\iint_{\text{CS}} \rho (\vec{V} \cdot \hat{n}) \, dA}_{\text{Net Rate of Mass Outflow across CS}} = 0$$

---

### 3. Engineering Simplifications of the Integral Form

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────┬─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ Flow Condition                      │ Simplified Integral Form                                │
├─────────────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ **1. Steady Compressible Flow**     │ $\iint_{\text{CS}} \rho (\vec{V} \cdot \hat{n}) \, dA = 0$│
│    ($\partial/\partial t = 0$)      │ $\sum \dot{m}_{\text{out}} = \sum \dot{m}_{\text{in}}$  │
├─────────────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ **2. Steady Quasi-1D Flow**         │ $\rho_1 A_1 V_1 = \rho_2 A_2 V_2 = \dot{m} = \text{const}$│
│    (Single inlet, single outlet)    │                                                         │
├─────────────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ **3. Steady Incompressible Flow**   │ $\iint_{\text{CS}} (\vec{V} \cdot \hat{n}) \, dA = 0$   │
│    ($\rho = \text{constant}$)       │ $A_1 V_1 = A_2 V_2 = Q = \text{constant (Discharge)}$   │
├─────────────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ **4. Multi-Port Junction**          │ $\sum_{i=1}^{N_{\text{in}}} \rho_i A_i V_i = \sum_{j=1}^{N_{\text{out}}} \rho_j A_j V_j$ │
└─────────────────────────────────────┴─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

---

### 4. Area-Averaged Mean Velocity ($\bar{V}$)

When velocity varies across a cross-section (e.g. viscous boundary layer, pipe flow, duct shear profile), the **true mass flow rate** is evaluated by surface integration:

$$\dot{m} = \iint_A \rho u(y, z) \, dA = \rho \bar{V} A$$

Thus, the **Area-Averaged Mean Velocity** $\bar{V}$ is defined as:

$$\mathbf{\bar{V} \equiv \frac{1}{A} \iint_A u(y, z) \, dA}$$

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────┬───────────────────────────────┬─────────────────────────┐
│ Flow Configuration                  │ Velocity Profile $u(y)$       │ Mean Velocity $\bar{V}$ │
├─────────────────────────────────────┼───────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────┤
│ **Laminar Flow between Plates**     │ $u(y) = U_{\max}\left(1 - \frac{y^2}{h^2}\right)$ │ $\mathbf{\bar{V} = \frac{2}{3}U_{\max}}$│
│ **Laminar Pipe Flow (Poiseuille)**  │ $u(r) = U_{\max}\left(1 - \frac{r^2}{R^2}\right)$ │ $\mathbf{\bar{V} = \frac{1}{2}U_{\max}}$│
│ **Turbulent Pipe Flow (1/7th Law)** │ $u(r) = U_{\max}\left(1 - \frac{r}{R}\right)^{1/7}$│ $\mathbf{\bar{V} = \frac{49}{60}U_{\max} \approx 0.817 U_{\max}}$│
└─────────────────────────────────────┴───────────────────────────────┴─────────────────────────┘
```

## 🧠 Part II: Derivation of the Differential Continuity Equation

```
                   DERIVATION VIA DIVERGENCE THEOREM
           Integral Form: ∂/∂t ∭_CV ρ dV + ∬_CS ρ (V · n) dA = 0
                                      │
                   Apply Gauss's Divergence Theorem:
                       ∬_CS ρ (V · n) dA = ∭_CV ∇ · (ρ V) dV
                                      │
                                      ▼
           ∭_CV [ ∂ρ/∂t + ∇ · (ρ V) ] dV = 0  (For arbitrary CV)
                                      │
                                      ▼
             POINTWISE CONSERVATIVE CONTINUITY EQUATION:
                        ∂ρ/∂t + ∇ · (ρ V) = 0
```

---

### 1. Vector Divergence Form (Conservative Form)

Applying **Gauss's Divergence Theorem** to convert the surface flux integral into a volume integral:
$$\iint_{\text{CS}} \rho (\vec{V} \cdot \hat{n}) \, dA = \iiint_{\text{CV}} \nabla \cdot (\rho \vec{V}) \, d\mathcal{V}$$

Substituting into the integral continuity equation:
$$\iiint_{\text{CV}} \left[ \frac{\partial \rho}{\partial t} + \nabla \cdot (\rho \vec{V}) \right] d\mathcal{V} = 0$$

Since this equation must hold identically for **any arbitrary, infinitesimal control volume $\mathcal{V}$**, the integrand itself must be identically zero everywhere in the continuum:

$$\mathbf{\frac{\partial \rho}{\partial t} + \nabla \cdot (\rho \vec{V}) = 0}$$

This is the celebrated **Differential Continuity Equation in Conservative (Divergence) Form**.

---

### 2. Convective Form (Non-Conservative Material Derivative Form)

Expanding the divergence of the scalar-vector product $\nabla \cdot (\rho \vec{V})$ using vector identities:
$$\nabla \cdot (\rho \vec{V}) = \rho (\nabla \cdot \vec{V}) + \vec{V} \cdot \nabla \rho$$

Substituting into the conservative equation:
$$\frac{\partial \rho}{\partial t} + \vec{V} \cdot \nabla \rho + \rho (\nabla \cdot \vec{V}) = 0$$

Recognizing the **Material Derivative of Density** $\frac{D\rho}{Dt} = \frac{\partial\rho}{\partial t} + \vec{V}\cdot\nabla\rho$:

$$\mathbf{\frac{D\rho}{Dt} + \rho (\nabla \cdot \vec{V}) = 0 \iff \frac{1}{\rho}\frac{D\rho}{Dt} + \nabla \cdot \vec{V} = 0}$$

> 🔑 **Physical Significance of $\nabla \cdot \vec{V}$:**
> $$\nabla \cdot \vec{V} = -\frac{1}{\rho}\frac{D\rho}{Dt} = \frac{1}{\delta\mathcal{V}}\frac{D(\delta\mathcal{V})}{Dt} \equiv \dot{\Delta} \quad (\text{Volumetric Dilatation Rate})$$
> The divergence of velocity represents the fractional rate of volume expansion of a fluid parcel!

## 🧠 Part III: Coordinate Systems Breakdown (High-Yield GATE Formulations)

```
┌───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                      THE CONTINUITY EQUATION IN ALL COORDINATE SYSTEMS                        │
├───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ **1. 3D Cartesian $(x, y, z)$:**                                                              │
│ $$\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial t} + \frac{\partial(\rho u)}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial(\rho v)}{\partial y} + \frac{\partial(\rho w)}{\partial z} = 0$$                                     │
├───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ **2. 3D Cylindrical Polar $(r, \theta, z)$:**                                                 │
│ $$\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial t} + \frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial(r \rho v_r)}{\partial r} + \frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial(\rho v_\theta)}{\partial \theta} + \frac{\partial(\rho v_z)}{\partial z} = 0$$│
├───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ **3. 3D Spherical Polar $(r, \theta, \phi)$:**                                                 │
│ $$\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial t} + \frac{1}{r^2}\frac{\partial(r^2 \rho v_r)}{\partial r} + \frac{1}{r\sin\theta}\frac{\partial(\rho v_\theta \sin\theta)}{\partial \theta} + \frac{1}{r\sin\theta}\frac{\partial(\rho v_\phi)}{\partial \phi} = 0$$│
└───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

---

### 1. Incompressible Flow Conditions ($\frac{D\rho}{Dt} = 0$)

When the density of every fluid particle remains constant along its trajectory ($\frac{D\rho}{Dt} = 0$):

$$\mathbf{\nabla \cdot \vec{V} = 0 \quad (\text{Solenoidal Velocity Field})}$$

#### Component Equations for Incompressible Flow:

1. **3D Cartesian:**
   $$\frac{\partial u}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial v}{\partial y} + \frac{\partial w}{\partial z} = 0$$
   *(In 2D: $\frac{\partial u}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial v}{\partial y} = 0 \implies \frac{\partial u}{\partial x} = -\frac{\partial v}{\partial y}$).*

2. **2D/3D Cylindrical Polar:**
   $$\frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial(r v_r)}{\partial r} + \frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial v_\theta}{\partial \theta} + \frac{\partial v_z}{\partial z} = 0 \iff \frac{\partial v_r}{\partial r} + \frac{v_r}{r} + \frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial v_\theta}{\partial \theta} + \frac{\partial v_z}{\partial z} = 0$$

3. **Spherical Polar (Symmetric Radial Flow):**
   $$\frac{1}{r^2}\frac{\partial(r^2 v_r)}{\partial r} = 0 \implies r^2 v_r = \text{constant} \implies v_r \propto \frac{1}{r^2} \quad (\text{3D Point Source/Sink})$$

---

## 🧠 Part IV: Logarithmic Differential Form for Quasi-1D Compressible Nozzle Flow

For 1D steady compressible flow through a duct or nozzle of variable cross-sectional area $A(x)$:

$$\dot{m} = \rho(x) A(x) V(x) = \text{constant}$$

Taking the natural logarithm ($\ln$) on both sides:
$$\ln(\dot{m}) = \ln(\rho) + \ln(A) + \ln(V) = \ln(\text{constant})$$

Differentiating both sides:

$$\mathbf{\frac{d\rho}{\rho} + \frac{dA}{A} + \frac{dV}{V} = 0}$$

```
                   AREA-VELOCITY-MACH COUPLING
       Combine with Euler's Equation: dp = -ρ V dV
       and Speed of Sound: a² = dp/dρ ⟹ dρ/ρ = -M² (dV/V)
                                │
                                ▼
               THE MASTER NOZZLE EQUATION:
                 dA/A = (M² - 1) (dV/V)
```

```
┌─────────────────┬──────────────────────┬──────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ Flow Regime     │ Mach Condition       │ Area-Velocity Response                               │
├─────────────────┼──────────────────────┼──────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ **Subsonic**    │ $M < 1 \implies M^2-1 < 0$│ $dA < 0$ (Converging) $\implies dV > 0$ (Accelerates)│
│                 │                      │ $dA > 0$ (Diverging) $\implies dV < 0$ (Decelerates) │
├─────────────────┼──────────────────────┼──────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ **Sonic Throat**│ $M = 1 \implies M^2-1 = 0$│ $dA = 0 \implies$ Area minimum (Throat station!)     │
├─────────────────┼──────────────────────┼──────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ **Supersonic**  │ $M > 1 \implies M^2-1 > 0$│ $dA > 0$ (Diverging) $\implies dV > 0$ (Accelerates) │
│                 │                      │ $dA < 0$ (Converging) $\implies dV < 0$ (Decelerates)│
└─────────────────┴──────────────────────┴──────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

## ⚡ AIR-1 Topper Mnemonics, Shortcuts & Speed Tricks

### 🚀 Shortcut 1: The 5-Second Incompressible Velocity Component Finder
To find the missing velocity component $w(x,y,z)$ for an incompressible flow:
$$w(x,y,z) = -\int \left(\frac{\partial u}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial v}{\partial y}\right) dz + f(x, y)$$
- Always determine the integration function $f(x, y)$ from the given wall/stagnation boundary condition (e.g. $w(x,y,0) = 0$).

---

### 🚀 Shortcut 2: Instant Radial Velocity Decay Rule from Continuity
- **2D Cylindrical Line Source/Sink:** $\frac{1}{r}\frac{d(r v_r)}{dr} = 0 \implies v_r \propto \mathbf{\frac{1}{r}}$.
- **3D Spherical Point Source/Sink:** $\frac{1}{r^2}\frac{d(r^2 v_r)}{dr} = 0 \implies v_r \propto \mathbf{\frac{1}{r^2}}$.

---

### 🚀 Shortcut 3: Laminar & Turbulent Mean Velocity Factor Memorization
- **Laminar Pipe Flow:** $\bar{V} = \mathbf{0.500 \, U_{\max}}$ (Exact factor $\frac{1}{2}$).
- **Laminar 2D Channel:** $\bar{V} = \mathbf{0.667 \, U_{\max}}$ (Exact factor $\frac{2}{3}$).
- **Turbulent Pipe Flow ($1/7$ law):** $\bar{V} = \mathbf{0.817 \, U_{\max}}$ (Exact factor $\frac{49}{60}$).

---

### 🚀 Shortcut 4: Unsteady Tank Filling / Draining Time Formula
For a tank of surface area $A_{\text{tank}}$ with liquid level $h(t)$ drained through an orifice of area $A_e$:
$$\frac{dh}{dt} = \frac{Q_{\text{in}} - Q_{\text{out}}}{A_{\text{tank}}} = \frac{Q_{\text{in}} - C_d A_e \sqrt{2gh}}{A_{\text{tank}}}$$

## 📝 Step-by-Step Solved GATE Examples (Zero to GATE Topper Level)

### Example 1: Determining an Unknown 3D Velocity Component (GATE AE Classic)
**Problem:** In an incompressible 3D flow field, the $x$ and $y$ velocity components are given by:
$$u = 2x^2 + y^2, \qquad v = -4xy + 3z$$
If $w = 0$ on the plane $z = 0$, find the complete analytical expression for $w(x, y, z)$.

> [!faq]- 🔍 View Solution & Detailed Explanation
> **Step 1: Check Incompressible Continuity Equation:**
> $$\frac{\partial u}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial v}{\partial y} + \frac{\partial w}{\partial z} = 0$$
> 
> **Step 2: Calculate Partial Derivatives of $u$ and $v$:**
> $$\frac{\partial u}{\partial x} = \frac{\partial}{\partial x}(2x^2 + y^2) = 4x$$
> $$\frac{\partial v}{\partial y} = \frac{\partial}{\partial y}(-4xy + 3z) = -4x$$
> 
> **Step 3: Solve for $\frac{\partial w}{\partial z}$:**
> $$\frac{\partial w}{\partial z} = -\left(\frac{\partial u}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial v}{\partial y}\right) = -(4x - 4x) = 0$$
> 
> **Step 4: Integrate with respect to $z$:**
> $$w(x, y, z) = \int 0 \, dz + f(x, y) = f(x, y)$$
> 
> **Step 5: Apply Boundary Condition:**
> Given that $w(x, y, 0) = 0 \implies f(x, y) = 0$.
> Therefore:
> $$\mathbf{w(x, y, z) = 0 \quad (\text{Identically zero everywhere!})}$$

### Example 2: Boundary Layer Mass Flow Rate & Deficit (GATE AE Core)
**Problem:** Air ($\rho = 1.2\text{ kg/m}^3$) flows over a flat plate of width $b = 1.0\text{ m}$. At station $x_1$, the laminar boundary layer thickness is $\delta = 10\text{ mm}$ and freestream velocity is $U_\infty = 30\text{ m/s}$. The velocity profile inside the boundary layer is given by:
$$\frac{u(y)}{U_\infty} = \frac{3}{2}\left(\frac{y}{\delta}\right) - \frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{y}{\delta}\right)^3 \quad (0 \le y \le \delta)$$
1. Calculate the total mass flow rate $\dot{m}_{\text{BL}}$ passing through the boundary layer.
2. Determine the mass flow deficit $\Delta \dot{m}$ due to boundary layer retardation compared to uniform inviscid flow.
3. Compute the displacement thickness $\delta^*$.

> [!faq]- 🔍 View Solution & Detailed Explanation
> **1. Mass Flow Rate through Boundary Layer ($\dot{m}_{\text{BL}}$):**
> $$\dot{m}_{\text{BL}} = \int_0^\delta \rho b u(y) \, dy = \rho b U_\infty \int_0^\delta \left[\frac{3}{2}\left(\frac{y}{\delta}\right) - \frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{y}{\delta}\right)^3\right] dy$$
> Let $\eta = y/\delta \implies dy = \delta\,d\eta$:
> $$\dot{m}_{\text{BL}} = \rho b U_\infty \delta \int_0^1 \left(\frac{3}{2}\eta - \frac{1}{2}\eta^3\right) d\eta = \rho b U_\infty \delta \left[\frac{3}{4}\eta^2 - \frac{1}{8}\eta^4\right]_0^1$$
> $$\dot{m}_{\text{BL}} = \rho b U_\infty \delta \left(\frac{3}{4} - \frac{1}{8}\right) = \rho b U_\infty \delta \left(\frac{5}{8}\right) = \frac{5}{8} \rho b U_\infty \delta$$
> Substitute values ($\rho = 1.2, b = 1.0, U_\infty = 30, \delta = 0.01\text{ m}$):
> $$\dot{m}_{\text{BL}} = \frac{5}{8}(1.2)(1.0)(30)(0.01) = \frac{5}{8}(0.36) = \mathbf{0.225\text{ kg/s}}$$
> 
> ---
> **2. Displacement Thickness $\delta^*$:**
> By definition:
> $$\delta^* = \int_0^\delta \left(1 - \frac{u}{U_\infty}\right) dy = \delta \int_0^1 \left(1 - \frac{3}{2}\eta + \frac{1}{2}\eta^3\right) d\eta = \delta \left[1 - \frac{3}{4} + \frac{1}{8}\right] = \delta \left(\frac{3}{8}\right) = \frac{3}{8}\delta$$
> $$\delta^* = \frac{3}{8}(10\text{ mm}) = \mathbf{3.75\text{ mm} = 0.00375\text{ m}}$$
> 
> ---
> **3. Mass Flow Deficit ($\Delta \dot{m}$):**
> $$\Delta \dot{m} = \rho b U_\infty \delta^* = (1.2)(1.0)(30)(0.00375) = \mathbf{0.135\text{ kg/s}}$$
> *(Check: $\dot{m}_{\text{inviscid}} - \dot{m}_{\text{BL}} = \rho b U_\infty \delta - \dot{m}_{\text{BL}} = (1.2)(1)(30)(0.01) - 0.225 = 0.360 - 0.225 = 0.135\text{ kg/s}$ ✅ Verified!).*

### Example 3: Cylindrical Polar Continuity for an Axisymmetric Radial Diffuser
**Problem:** An incompressible fluid flows radially outwards between two parallel circular disks of spacing $h$. The radial velocity is given by $v_r = \frac{C}{r}$, $v_\theta = 0$, $v_z = 0$.
1. Verify whether this velocity field satisfies the incompressible continuity equation.
2. Determine the volume flow rate $Q$ passing through any cylindrical surface of radius $r$.

> [!faq]- 🔍 View Solution & Detailed Explanation
> **1. Incompressible Continuity in Cylindrical Polar Coordinates:**
> $$\frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial(r v_r)}{\partial r} + \frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial v_\theta}{\partial \theta} + \frac{\partial v_z}{\partial z} = 0$$
> Substitute $v_r = \frac{C}{r}$, $v_\theta = 0$, $v_z = 0$:
> $$r v_r = r\left(\frac{C}{r}\right) = C = \text{constant}$$
> $$\frac{\partial(r v_r)}{\partial r} = \frac{\partial(C)}{\partial r} = 0$$
> Since $v_\theta = 0$ and $v_z = 0$:
> $$\frac{1}{r}(0) + 0 + 0 = 0 \quad \text{✅ Satisfied! Incompressible flow!}$$
> 
> ---
> **2. Volumetric Flow Rate $Q$:**
> The flow crosses a cylindrical surface of area $A(r) = 2\pi r h$:
> $$Q = A(r) v_r(r) = (2\pi r h)\left(\frac{C}{r}\right) = \mathbf{2\pi h C = \text{constant}}$$
> *Discharge $Q$ is completely independent of radius $r$, satisfying global mass conservation.*

### Example 4: Unsteady Draining of an Aircraft Pressurized Fuel Tank (RTT)
**Problem:** A cylindrical aircraft fuel tank of cross-sectional area $A_T = 2.0\text{ m}^2$ contains aviation fuel ($\rho = 800\text{ kg/m}^3$). Fuel is pumped into the tank at a steady mass flow rate $\dot{m}_{\text{in}} = 40\text{ kg/s}$ while simultaneously draining from a bottom nozzle of area $A_e = 0.005\text{ m}^2$ at exit velocity $V_e = 12\text{ m/s}$. Find the rate of change of liquid fuel height $\frac{dh}{dt}$ in the tank.

> [!faq]- 🔍 View Solution & Detailed Explanation
> Apply the Integral Continuity Equation to the control volume bounding the fuel tank:
> $$\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\iiint_{\text{CV}} \rho \, d\mathcal{V} + \sum \dot{m}_{\text{out}} - \sum \dot{m}_{\text{in}} = 0$$
> Mass inside tank: $m_{\text{CV}} = \rho A_T h(t)$.
> $$\frac{d m_{\text{CV}}}{dt} = \rho A_T \frac{dh}{dt}$$
> Exit mass flow rate:
> $$\dot{m}_{\text{out}} = \rho A_e V_e = (800\text{ kg/m}^3)(0.005\text{ m}^2)(12\text{ m/s}) = 48\text{ kg/s}$$
> Substitute into RTT equation:
> $$\rho A_T \frac{dh}{dt} + \dot{m}_{\text{out}} - \dot{m}_{\text{in}} = 0$$
> $$(800)(2.0)\frac{dh}{dt} + 48 - 40 = 0$$
> $$1600 \frac{dh}{dt} + 8 = 0 \implies \frac{dh}{dt} = -\frac{8}{1600} = \mathbf{-0.005\text{ m/s} = -5.0\text{ mm/s}}$$
> *The fuel height is dropping at a steady rate of $5.0\text{ mm/s}$.*

## ⚠️ Common Exam Traps & Pitfalls (Where Marks are Lost)

> 🔴 **Trap 1: Forgetting Density in Compressible Continuity**
> - *Mistake:* Using $\frac{\partial u}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial v}{\partial y} = 0$ for high-speed gas/nozzle flows.
> - *Correction:* For compressible flow ($\rho \neq \text{const}$), the continuity equation is $\mathbf{\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial t} + \nabla\cdot(\rho\vec{V}) = 0}$. The divergence of velocity $\nabla\cdot\vec{V}$ is non-zero in compressible flows!

> 🔴 **Trap 2: Coordinate Scale Factors in Cylindrical Continuity**
> - *Mistake:* Writing cylindrical continuity as $\frac{\partial v_r}{\partial r} + \frac{\partial v_\theta}{\partial \theta} = 0$.
> - *Correction:* You MUST include the metric scaling factor $r$: $\mathbf{\frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial(r v_r)}{\partial r} + \frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial v_\theta}{\partial \theta} = 0} \iff \frac{\partial v_r}{\partial r} + \frac{v_r}{r} + \frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial v_\theta}{\partial \theta} = 0$.

> 🔴 **Trap 3: Sign of Normal Vector in Reynolds Transport Theorem**
> - *Mistake:* Treating inflow $\vec{V}\cdot\hat{n}$ as positive.
> - *Correction:* $\hat{n}$ is the **OUTWARD** normal. Thus, inflow has $\vec{V}\cdot\hat{n} < 0$ (negative flux) and outflow has $\vec{V}\cdot\hat{n} > 0$ (positive flux).

> 🔴 **Trap 4: Mean Velocity Calculation for Non-Linear Profiles**
> - *Mistake:* Averaging maximum and minimum velocities ($\bar{V} = \frac{U_{\max} + 0}{2}$) for laminar boundary layers or pipes.
> - *Correction:* Mean velocity MUST be calculated via area integration: $\bar{V} = \frac{1}{A}\iint u\,dA$. For parabolic pipe flow, $\bar{V} = 0.5 U_{\max}$, and for 2D channel flow, $\bar{V} = \frac{2}{3} U_{\max}$.

### PYQ Sprint 1: Quasi-1D Compressible Nozzle Area-Velocity (NAT)
**Problem:** Air flows isentropically through a convergent-divergent nozzle. At the throat (station T), the cross-sectional area is $A_T = 0.01\text{ m}^2$, Mach number $M_T = 1.0$, total temperature $T_0 = 500\text{ K}$, and total pressure $p_0 = 200\text{ kPa}$. At exit station E, $M_E = 2.0$ with $\gamma = 1.4$. Find the area ratio $A_E / A_T$ using the isentropic area-Mach number relation.

**Isentropic Area-Mach Relation:**

$$\frac{A}{A^*} = \frac{1}{M}\left[\frac{2}{\gamma+1}\left(1 + \frac{\gamma-1}{2}M^2\right)\right]^{(\gamma+1)/[2(\gamma-1)]}$$

For $\gamma = 1.4$:

$$\frac{A}{A^*} = \frac{1}{M}\left[\frac{1}{6}\left(1 + 0.2M^2\right)\right]^{3}$$

> [!faq]- 🔍 View Solution
> At throat: $M = 1.0 \implies A_T = A^*$. Therefore $A_E/A_T = A_E/A^*$ at $M_E = 2.0$.
>
> $$\frac{A_E}{A^*} = \frac{1}{2.0}\left[\frac{1}{6}\left(1 + 0.2 \times 4\right)\right]^3 = \frac{1}{2}\left[\frac{1.8}{6}\right]^3 = \frac{1}{2}(0.3)^3 = \frac{1}{2}(0.027)$$
>
> Wait — let me use the standard form correctly:
>
> $$\frac{A_E}{A^*} = \frac{1}{2}\left[\frac{2}{2.4}\left(1 + 0.2 \times 4\right)\right]^{3} = \frac{1}{2}\left[\frac{2 \times 1.8}{2.4}\right]^3 = \frac{1}{2}\left[1.5\right]^3 = \frac{3.375}{2} = \mathbf{1.6875}$$
>
> **Accepted GATE range:** $[1.68, 1.70]$
>
> TCS Keystrokes: `1.8 × 2 ÷ 2.4 = [MS] [MR] × [MR] × [MR] ÷ 2 =` → `1.6875`

### PYQ Sprint 2: Unsteady RTT — Rising Tank Level (NAT)
**Problem:** A rocket propellant tank of diameter $D = 2\text{ m}$ is being filled at a mass flow rate $\dot{m}_{in} = 300\text{ kg/s}$. Liquid oxygen ($\rho = 1141\text{ kg/m}^3$) simultaneously drains through a launch vehicle feedline of diameter $d = 0.1\text{ m}$ at velocity $V_{drain} = 15\text{ m/s}$. Find the rate of rise of the LOX level $dh/dt$ in m/s.

> [!faq]- 🔍 View Solution
> Apply the integral continuity equation (unsteady RTT):
>
> $$\frac{d m_{CV}}{dt} + \dot{m}_{out} - \dot{m}_{in} = 0$$
>
> Rate of mass change in tank: $\frac{d m_{CV}}{dt} = \rho \cdot \frac{\pi D^2}{4} \cdot \frac{dh}{dt}$
>
> Exit mass flow: $\dot{m}_{out} = \rho \cdot \frac{\pi d^2}{4} \cdot V_{drain} = 1141 \times \frac{\pi \times 0.01}{4} \times 15$
>
> $$\dot{m}_{out} = 1141 \times 7.854 \times 10^{-3} \times 15 = 134.4\text{ kg/s}$$
>
> Substituting:
>
> $$1141 \times \frac{\pi \times 4}{4} \cdot \frac{dh}{dt} + 134.4 - 300 = 0$$
>
> $$1141 \times \pi \cdot \frac{dh}{dt} = 165.6$$
>
> $$\frac{dh}{dt} = \frac{165.6}{1141 \times \pi} = \frac{165.6}{3584.4} \approx \mathbf{0.0462\text{ m/s}}$$
>
> **Accepted GATE range:** $[0.044, 0.048]$ m/s

### PYQ Sprint 3: Cylindrical Vortex Continuity Check (MCQ)
**Problem:** A 2D velocity field in polar coordinates is given by:
$$v_r = 0, \quad v_\theta = \frac{C}{r}$$
where $C$ is a constant. This flow is:
- (A) Incompressible only
- (B) Irrotational only
- (C) Both incompressible and irrotational
- (D) Neither

> [!faq]- 🔍 View Solution — **Answer: (C)**
>
> **Incompressibility check** (cylindrical continuity $v_z = 0$, axisymmetric):
>
> $$\frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial(r v_r)}{\partial r} + \frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial v_\theta}{\partial \theta} = \frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial(0)}{\partial r} + \frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial (C/r)}{\partial \theta} = 0 + 0 = 0 \quad \checkmark$$
>
> **Irrotationality check** (vorticity $\omega_z$ in polar):
>
> $$\omega_z = \frac{1}{r}\left[\frac{\partial(r v_\theta)}{\partial r} - \frac{\partial v_r}{\partial \theta}\right] = \frac{1}{r}\left[\frac{\partial(C)}{\partial r} - 0\right] = \frac{1}{r}(0) = 0 \quad \checkmark$$
>
> *(Note: $r v_\theta = r \cdot C/r = C = \text{constant}$, so its $r$-derivative is zero.)*
>
> Both conditions satisfied $\implies$ **Free/Potential Vortex: incompressible AND irrotational**.
>
> ⚠️ **Trap:** A **forced vortex** ($v_\theta = \omega r$) has $\omega_z = 2\omega \neq 0$ (rotational). Free vortex $\neq$ forced vortex!

### PYQ Sprint 4: Boundary Layer Mass Flux Deficit — Momentum Thickness Link (NAT)
**Problem:** In an incompressible boundary layer on a flat plate at station $x$, the velocity profile is:
$$\frac{u}{U_\infty} = \sin\left(\frac{\pi y}{2\delta}\right) \quad (0 \le y \le \delta)$$
Find the displacement thickness $\delta^*$ and momentum thickness $\theta$ in terms of $\delta$.

> [!faq]- 🔍 View Solution
>
> **Displacement Thickness:**
>
> $$\frac{\delta^*}{\delta} = \int_0^1 \left(1 - \frac{u}{U_\infty}\right) d\eta = \int_0^1 \left(1 - \sin\frac{\pi\eta}{2}\right) d\eta$$
>
> $$= \left[\eta + \frac{2}{\pi}\cos\frac{\pi\eta}{2}\right]_0^1 = \left(1 + 0\right) - \left(0 + \frac{2}{\pi}\right) = 1 - \frac{2}{\pi}$$
>
> $$\delta^* = \delta\left(1 - \frac{2}{\pi}\right) \approx \mathbf{0.3634\,\delta}$$
>
> **Momentum Thickness:**
>
> $$\frac{\theta}{\delta} = \int_0^1 \frac{u}{U_\infty}\left(1 - \frac{u}{U_\infty}\right) d\eta = \int_0^1 \sin\frac{\pi\eta}{2}\left(1 - \sin\frac{\pi\eta}{2}\right) d\eta$$
>
> $$= \int_0^1 \sin\frac{\pi\eta}{2} d\eta - \int_0^1 \sin^2\frac{\pi\eta}{2} d\eta$$
>
> $$= \frac{2}{\pi} - \frac{1}{2} = \frac{4-\pi}{2\pi}$$
>
> $$\theta = \delta \cdot \frac{4-\pi}{2\pi} \approx \mathbf{0.1366\,\delta}$$
>
> **AIR-1 Memory Anchor:** For sinusoidal profile: $\delta^*/\delta \approx 0.363$, $\theta/\delta \approx 0.137$ (memorize these — they appear repeatedly in boundary layer drag questions).

### PYQ Sprint 5: Area-Velocity Paradox — Conceptual MCQ
**Problem:** In a supersonic nozzle ($M > 1$), increasing the exit area $A_e$ while maintaining constant reservoir conditions will:
- (A) Decrease exit Mach number
- (B) Increase exit Mach number
- (C) Not change the Mach number (choked throat controls it)
- (D) Create a normal shock at exit

> [!faq]- 🔍 View Solution — **Answer: (B)**
>
> From the master nozzle equation:
>
> $$\frac{dA}{A} = (M^2 - 1)\frac{dV}{V}$$
>
> At supersonic conditions ($M > 1$): $M^2 - 1 > 0$.
>
> $dA > 0 \implies dV > 0 \implies$ velocity increases $\implies$ **Mach number increases**.
>
> ⚠️ **Trap:** Option (A) is the subsonic intuition. In subsonic flow, area increase slows flow. At supersonic speeds, area increase accelerates flow — the **opposite** behavior!

## ⚡ Bernoulli Equation: Derivation from Euler's Equation (AIR-1 Standard)

### Why Bernoulli Needs Its Own Derivation Section Here

Bernoulli's equation is the single most used result in low-speed aerodynamics. However, it is **not** an independent law — it is the integral of Euler's (inviscid momentum) equation along a streamline. Understanding this derivation prevents the common mistake of applying Bernoulli where its assumptions are violated.

### The 4 Non-Negotiable Assumptions for Bernoulli

| # | Assumption | Consequence of Violation |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Inviscid** ($\mu = 0$) | Viscous dissipation → total pressure decreases → Bernoulli invalid |
| 2 | **Steady** ($\partial/\partial t = 0$) | Unsteady Bernoulli requires extra $\partial\phi/\partial t$ term |
| 3 | **Incompressible** ($\rho = \text{const}$) | Use isentropic relations for $M > 0.3$ |
| 4 | **Along a streamline** (or irrotational) | Bernoulli constant $H$ differs across streamlines unless $\nabla\times\vec{V}=0$ |

### Streamline-Coordinate Derivation (GATE-Level Rigor)

The steady, inviscid Euler momentum equation in streamline coordinates ($s$ = along streamline):

$$\rho V \frac{dV}{ds} = -\frac{dp}{ds} - \rho g \frac{dz}{ds}$$

Rearranging:

$$\frac{dp}{\rho} + V\,dV + g\,dz = 0$$

For constant density ($\rho = \text{const}$), integrate along the streamline:

$$\mathbf{p + \frac{1}{2}\rho V^2 + \rho g z = H = \text{constant (along streamline)}}$$

#### Forms of Bernoulli's Equation

$$p + \frac{1}{2}\rho V^2 + \rho g z = H \quad \text{(Pressure form, Pa)}$$

$$\frac{p}{\rho g} + \frac{V^2}{2g} + z = \frac{H}{\rho g} \quad \text{(Head form, meters)}$$

$$\frac{p}{\rho} + \frac{V^2}{2} + gz = h_0 \quad \text{(Energy form, J/kg)}$$

#### Irrotational Extension (Applicable Across Streamlines)
When the flow is **irrotational** ($\nabla \times \vec{V} = 0$) everywhere, the Bernoulli constant $H$ is **the same for ALL streamlines**:

$$p + \frac{1}{2}\rho V^2 = p_\infty + \frac{1}{2}\rho V_\infty^2 = \text{const}$$

This is why Bernoulli can be applied between the freestream and the cylinder surface in potential flow — the flow is irrotational everywhere (outside the vortex core).

> 🔴 **The Bernoulli Validity Fence — 4 Failure Modes:**
> 1. **Turbulent shear layers:** Viscosity is NOT zero — massive energy dissipation occurs.
> 2. **Boundary layers:** Rotational flow; $H$ varies across the layer.
> 3. **Through a normal shock:** Entropy jumps; total pressure $p_0$ drops dramatically.
> 4. **Unsteady flow:** The standard form requires adding $\rho \frac{\partial\phi}{\partial t}$ (Crocco's theorem).

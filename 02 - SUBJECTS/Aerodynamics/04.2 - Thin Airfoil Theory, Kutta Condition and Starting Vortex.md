## 📌 Executive Summary & GATE AE Context

Thin Airfoil Theory (TAT) is one of the most reliable scoring engines in the GATE Aerospace exam. Every year, 1 to 2 questions test:
1. **The Vortex Sheet Model:** Replacing the mean camber line with a continuous vortex distribution $\gamma(s)$ on the chord line ($y=0, 0 \le x \le c$).
2. **The Kutta Condition:** Enforcing smooth flow detachment at the trailing edge ($\gamma(c) = 0$ for finite angle and cusped trailing edges).
3. **Kelvin's Circulation Theorem & Starting Vortex:** Invariant total circulation $D\Gamma/Dt = 0 \implies \Gamma_{\text{bound}} + \Gamma_{\text{starting}} = 0$.
4. **The Glauert Integral Transform:** $x = \frac{c}{2}(1 - \cos\theta_0)$ and Fourier expansion of vortex sheet strength $\gamma(\theta)$.
5. **Analytical Coefficients ($A_0, A_1, A_2$):** Direct extraction of lift-curve slope $a_0 = 2\pi$, zero-lift angle of attack $\alpha_0$, and aerodynamic center $x_{ac} = 0.25 c$.
6. **Flapped Airfoil Theory:** Hinged control surface deflections and lift/moment increments.

## 🧠 Part I: Theoretical Foundations

### 1. The Vortex Sheet Concept
A vortex sheet is an idealized surface across which tangential velocity undergoes a discontinuous jump, while normal velocity remains continuous:
$$\gamma(s) = u_u(s) - u_l(s)$$
where $u_u$ and $u_l$ are the local fluid velocities tangent to the upper and lower surfaces of the sheet.

Total circulation around the airfoil is the integral of vortex sheet strength along the chord:
$$\Gamma = \int_0^c \gamma(x) \, dx$$

By the Kutta-Joukowski theorem, the aerodynamic lift per unit span is:
$$L' = \rho_\infty V_\infty \Gamma = \rho_\infty V_\infty \int_0^c \gamma(x) \, dx$$

---

### 2. The Kutta Condition & Kelvin's Circulation Theorem

#### A. The Kutta Condition (Physical & Mathematical Formulation)
In an inviscid, irrotational potential flow past an airfoil with a sharp trailing edge (TE), potential flow alone yields infinitely many possible values of circulation $\Gamma$, almost all of which cause the flow to whip around the sharp trailing edge with infinite velocity and infinite suction pressure.

In reality, fluid viscosity enforces:
1. **At a cusped trailing edge (included angle $\tau = 0$):** Fluid velocities on upper and lower surfaces leave parallel to each other with equal speeds:
   $$V_u(c) = V_l(c) \implies \gamma(c) = 0$$
2. **At a finite included angle trailing edge ($\tau > 0$):** The trailing edge is a stagnation point:
   $$V_u(c) = V_l(c) = 0 \implies \gamma(c) = 0$$

> 💡 **The Universal Kutta Condition for Thin Airfoils:** The vortex sheet strength must vanish at the trailing edge:
> $$\gamma(c) = 0 \quad (\text{or } \gamma(\theta = \pi) = 0)$$

#### B. Kelvin's Circulation Theorem & The Starting Vortex
Kelvin's Theorem states that in an inviscid, barotropic fluid with conservative body forces:
$$\frac{D\Gamma_{\text{total}}}{Dt} = 0$$

When an aircraft starts from rest:
- Initially, flow is completely at rest: $\Gamma_{\text{total}} = 0$.
- As motion begins, boundary layer separation occurs at the sharp trailing edge, rolling up into a concentrated vortex: the **Starting Vortex** with circulation $\Gamma_{\text{starting}} = -\Gamma$.
- The starting vortex is shed into the wake and left behind at the runway.
- By Kelvin's theorem, an equal and opposite **bound circulation** $\Gamma_{\text{bound}} = +\Gamma$ is established around the airfoil:
  $$\Gamma_{\text{bound}} + \Gamma_{\text{starting}} = 0 \implies \Gamma_{\text{bound}} = -\Gamma_{\text{starting}}$$

---

### 3. The Fundamental Equation of Thin Airfoil Theory
Thin airfoil theory approximates the airfoil as a thin camber line $y = y_c(x)$ deviating slightly from the chord line ($y=0$). The vortex sheet $\gamma(\xi)$ is placed directly on the chord line $0 \le \xi \le c$.

The flow must remain tangent to the mean camber line at every chord station $x$:
$$V_{\infty,n}(x) + w(x) = 0$$
where:
- Normal freestream velocity component: $V_{\infty,n} = V_\infty (\alpha - dy_c/dx)$
- Downward induced velocity from the vortex sheet:
  $$w(x) = -\frac{1}{2\pi} \int_0^c \frac{\gamma(\xi)}{x - \xi} \, d\xi$$

Equating normal components yields the **Fundamental Equation of Thin Airfoil Theory**:
$$V_\infty \left( \alpha - \frac{dy_c}{dx} \right) = \frac{1}{2\pi} \int_0^c \frac{\gamma(\xi)}{x - \xi} \, d\xi$$

## 📐 Part II: The Glauert Fourier Method

### 1. Coordinate Transformation
Define the transformation from chord station $x$ to angular coordinate $\theta$:
$$x = \frac{c}{2}(1 - \cos\theta), \quad \xi = \frac{c}{2}(1 - \cos\theta_0)$$
- Leading edge ($x = 0$): $\theta = 0$
- Mid-chord ($x = c/2$): $\theta = \pi/2$
- Trailing edge ($x = c$): $\theta = \pi$

### 2. General Fourier Series Representation of $\gamma(\theta)$
To satisfy the Kutta condition $\gamma(\pi) = 0$ while allowing leading-edge singular suction $\gamma(0) \to \infty$, Glauert proposed:
$$\gamma(\theta) = 2 V_\infty \left[ A_0 \frac{1 + \cos\theta}{\sin\theta} + \sum_{n=1}^\infty A_n \sin(n\theta) \right]$$
Using $\frac{1+\cos\theta}{\sin\theta} = \cot(\theta/2)$:
$$\gamma(\theta) = 2 V_\infty \left[ A_0 \cot\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right) + \sum_{n=1}^\infty A_n \sin(n\theta) \right]$$

### 3. Glauert Integral Identity
Using the fundamental Cauchy principal value integral:
$$\int_0^\pi \frac{\cos(n\theta_0)}{\cos\theta_0 - \cos\theta} \, d\theta_0 = \pi \frac{\sin(n\theta)}{\sin\theta}$$

Substituting $\gamma(\theta)$ into the fundamental equation simplifies it to:
$$\frac{dy_c}{dx} = (\alpha - A_0) + \sum_{n=1}^\infty A_n \cos(n\theta)$$

### 4. Determination of Fourier Coefficients
By orthogonality of cosine functions over $[0, \pi]$:
$$A_0 = \alpha - \frac{1}{\pi} \int_0^\pi \frac{dy_c}{dx} \, d\theta_0$$
$$A_n = \frac{2}{\pi} \int_0^\pi \frac{dy_c}{dx} \cos(n\theta_0) \, d\theta_0 \quad (n \ge 1)$$

---

## ⚡ Part III: Aerodynamic Characteristics & Invariants

### 1. Symmetric Airfoil ($dy_c/dx = 0$)
For a flat plate or symmetric airfoil ($y_c(x) \equiv 0$):
- $A_0 = \alpha$
- $A_n = 0$ for all $n \ge 1$
- Vortex distribution:
  $$\gamma(\theta) = 2 V_\infty \alpha \cot\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)$$
- Total circulation:
  $$\Gamma = \int_0^\pi \gamma(\theta) \frac{c}{2}\sin\theta \, d\theta = \pi c V_\infty \alpha$$
- Lift coefficient:
  $$c_l = \frac{2\Gamma}{V_\infty c} = 2\pi\alpha$$
- Theoretical lift-curve slope:
  $$a_0 = \frac{dc_l}{d\alpha} = 2\pi\text{ rad}^{-1} \approx 0.10966\text{ deg}^{-1}$$
- Zero-lift angle of attack: $\alpha_0 = 0^\circ$

---

### 2. Cambered Airfoil Characteristics
For an arbitrary cambered airfoil:
1. **Total Circulation:**
   $$\Gamma = \pi c V_\infty \left( A_0 + \frac{A_1}{2} \right)$$
2. **Lift Coefficient ($c_l$):**
   $$c_l = 2\pi \left( A_0 + \frac{A_1}{2} \right) = 2\pi (\alpha - \alpha_0)$$
3. **Zero-Lift Angle of Attack ($\alpha_0$):**
   $$\alpha_0 = -\frac{1}{\pi} \int_0^\pi \frac{dy_c}{dx} (1 - \cos\theta_0) \, d\theta_0 = -\frac{1}{2}(A_1 - 2 A_0 + 2\alpha)$$
   $$\alpha_0 = \frac{1}{\pi} \int_0^\pi \frac{dy_c}{dx} (\cos\theta_0 - 1) \, d\theta_0$$
4. **Moment Coefficient about Leading Edge ($c_{m,LE}$):**
   $$c_{m,LE} = -\frac{\pi}{2}\left( A_0 + A_1 - \frac{A_2}{2} \right) = -\left[ \frac{c_l}{4} + \frac{\pi}{4}(A_1 - A_2) \right]$$
5. **Moment Coefficient about Quarter-Chord ($c_{m,c/4}$):**
   $$c_{m,c/4} = c_{m,LE} + \frac{1}{4} c_l = \frac{\pi}{4}(A_2 - A_1)$$

> 🏆 **THE QUARTER-CHORD INVARIANT:** Notice that $c_{m,c/4} = \frac{\pi}{4}(A_2 - A_1)$ depends ONLY on camber Fourier coefficients $A_1$ and $A_2$ — it is **strictly independent of the angle of attack $\alpha$**!
> $$\frac{dc_{m,c/4}}{d\alpha} \equiv 0 \implies x_{ac} \equiv 0.25 c$$
> Therefore, in subsonic incompressible flow, **the aerodynamic center of ANY thin airfoil is strictly located at the quarter-chord station ($x_{ac} = 0.25 c$)!**

---

### 3. Center of Pressure Trajectory
The center of pressure is where total resultant aerodynamic moment vanishes ($c_{m,cp} \equiv 0$):
$$x_{cp} = x_{ac} - \frac{c_{m,ac}}{c_l} c = 0.25 c - \frac{\pi(A_2 - A_1)}{4 c_l} c$$
- For positive camber ($A_1 > A_2 \implies c_{m,ac} < 0$), $x_{cp}$ lies **aft** of the quarter-chord.
- As $\alpha \to \alpha_0 \implies c_l \to 0$, $x_{cp} \to \pm \infty$ (divergence).
- For a symmetric airfoil ($c_{m,ac} = 0$), $x_{cp} \equiv 0.25 c$ for all $\alpha \ne \alpha_0$.

## 🖩 Worked Example: Parabolic Arc Cambered Airfoil

**Problem (GATE AE Level 4 NAT):**  
A thin airfoil has a mean camber line given by the parabolic arc:
$$\frac{y_c}{x} = 4 \varepsilon \frac{x}{c}\left(1 - \frac{x}{c}\right)$$
where maximum camber is $\varepsilon = 0.03$ ($3\% c$).  
Calculate:
1. Zero-lift angle of attack $\alpha_0$ in degrees.
2. Pitching moment coefficient about the quarter-chord $c_{m,c/4}$.
3. Center of pressure location $x_{cp}/c$ at $\alpha = 3.0^\circ$.

**Analytical Solution & TCS Keystrokes:**
1. **Camber slope:**
   $$\frac{dy_c}{dx} = 4\varepsilon\left(1 - 2\frac{x}{c}\right) = 4(0.03)(1 - 2\bar{x}) = 0.12(1 - 2\bar{x})$$
   Transforming $\bar{x} = \frac{1 - \cos\theta_0}{2}$:
   $$1 - 2\bar{x} = 1 - (1 - \cos\theta_0) = \cos\theta_0$$
   Therefore:
   $$\frac{dy_c}{dx} = 0.12 \cos\theta_0$$

2. **Fourier Coefficients:**
   $$A_0 = \alpha - \frac{1}{\pi}\int_0^\pi 0.12 \cos\theta_0 \, d\theta_0 = \alpha - 0 = \alpha$$
   $$A_1 = \frac{2}{\pi}\int_0^\pi (0.12\cos\theta_0)\cos\theta_0 \, d\theta_0 = 0.12 \times \frac{2}{\pi} \left(\frac{\pi}{2}\right) = 0.12$$
   $$A_2 = \frac{2}{\pi}\int_0^\pi (0.12\cos\theta_0)\cos(2\theta_0) \, d\theta_0 = 0 \quad (\text{orthogonality})$$

3. **Zero-Lift Angle of Attack:**
   $$\alpha_0 = -\frac{1}{2} A_1 = -\frac{0.12}{2} = -0.060\text{ rad}$$
   In degrees:
   $$\alpha_0 = -0.060 \times \frac{180}{\pi} = -\frac{10.8}{\pi} = -3.4377^\circ \approx -3.44^\circ$$

4. **Quarter-Chord Pitching Moment:**
   $$c_{m,c/4} = \frac{\pi}{4}(A_2 - A_1) = \frac{\pi}{4}(0 - 0.12) = -0.03\pi = -0.094248$$
   Target Answer: **$c_{m,c/4} = -0.0942$**

5. **Lift Coefficient at $\alpha = 3.0^\circ$:**
   $$\alpha = 3.0 \times \frac{\pi}{180} = \frac{\pi}{60} = 0.05236\text{ rad}$$
   $$c_l = 2\pi(\alpha - \alpha_0) = 2\pi(0.05236 - (-0.060)) = 2\pi(0.11236) = 0.70598$$

6. **Center of Pressure:**
   $$\frac{x_{cp}}{c} = 0.25 - \frac{c_{m,c/4}}{c_l} = 0.25 - \frac{-0.094248}{0.70598} = 0.25 + 0.133499 = 0.3835$$
   Target Answer: **$x_{cp}/c = 0.384$** (or $38.4\% c$)

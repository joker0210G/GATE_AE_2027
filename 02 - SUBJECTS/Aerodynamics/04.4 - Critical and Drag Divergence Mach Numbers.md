## 📌 Executive Summary & GATE AE Context

As an aircraft accelerates in the high subsonic regime ($M_\infty > 0.6$), flow speeds up over the convex upper surface of the wing. At a certain freestream Mach number, local flow somewhere on the wing surface first reaches the speed of sound ($M_{\text{local}} = 1.0$). This begins the transonic regime.

GATE AE tests:
1. **Critical Mach Number ($M_{cr}$):** Definition, physical significance, and analytical determination via the intersection of the Prandtl-Glauert compressibility rule and the isentropic sonic pressure coefficient relation.
2. **Drag Divergence Mach Number ($M_{dd}$):** The threshold where wave drag causes a sharp, catastrophic rise in total drag ($\frac{dC_D}{dM_\infty} = 0.10$).
3. **Methods to Increase $M_{cr}$ and $M_{dd}$:** Wing sweep ($\Lambda$), thin airfoil cross-sections ($t/c$), supercritical airfoils, and the Whitcomb Transonic Area Rule.

## 🧠 Part I: The Physics of Transonic Flow

### 1. Definition of Critical Mach Number ($M_{cr}$)
The **Critical Mach Number ($M_{cr}$)** is the freestream Mach number $M_\infty$ at which sonic speed ($M = 1.0$) is first attained at some point on the airfoil surface (usually at the point of minimum pressure on the upper surface).

- At $M_\infty < M_{cr}$: The flow is entirely subsonic everywhere around the airfoil.
- At $M_\infty = M_{cr}$: $M_{\max} = 1.0$ at a single point; $M < 1.0$ elsewhere.
- At $M_\infty > M_{cr}$: A supersonic pocket forms over the upper surface, terminated downstream by a **lambda shock wave**, inducing boundary layer separation.

---

### 2. Analytical Determination of $M_{cr}$
To find $M_{cr}$, we equate the pressure coefficient at the minimum pressure point from two independent relationships:

#### Relationship 1: Aerodynamic Compressibility Correction (Prandtl-Glauert Rule)
Let $C_{p0}$ be the minimum pressure coefficient measured on the airfoil in incompressible low-speed flow. In subsonic compressible flow:
$$C_p = \frac{C_{p0}}{\sqrt{1 - M_\infty^2}}$$

#### Relationship 2: Isentropic Flow at Sonic Point ($M=1$)
From isentropic relations, the static pressure $p^*$ where local Mach number is $1.0$ satisfies:
$$\frac{p^*}{p_0} = \left(\frac{2}{\gamma+1}\right)^{\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-1}}$$
The freestream stagnation pressure is:
$$\frac{p_0}{p_\infty} = \left(1 + \frac{\gamma-1}{2}M_\infty^2\right)^{\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-1}}$$
The critical pressure coefficient $C_{p,\text{crit}}$ required for local flow to reach $M=1$ is:
$$C_{p,\text{crit}} = \frac{p^* - p_\infty}{\frac{1}{2}\rho_\infty V_\infty^2} = \frac{2}{\gamma M_\infty^2}\left[ \frac{p^*}{p_\infty} - 1 \right]$$
$$C_{p,\text{crit}} = \frac{2}{\gamma M_\infty^2} \left[ \left( \frac{1 + \frac{\gamma-1}{2}M_\infty^2}{1 + \frac{\gamma-1}{2}} \right)^{\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-1}} - 1 \right]$$
For air with $\gamma = 1.4$:
$$C_{p,\text{crit}} = \frac{2}{1.4 M_\infty^2} \left[ \left( \frac{1 + 0.2 M_\infty^2}{1.2} \right)^{3.5} - 1 \right]$$

> 🏆 **THE $M_{cr}$ MASTER EQUATION:** $M_{cr}$ is the unique root of the transcendental equation:
> $$\frac{C_{p0}}{\sqrt{1 - M_{cr}^2}} = \frac{2}{\gamma M_{cr}^2} \left[ \left( \frac{1 + \frac{\gamma-1}{2}M_{cr}^2}{\frac{\gamma+1}{2}} \right)^{\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-1}} - 1 \right]$$

---

### 3. Drag Divergence Mach Number ($M_{dd}$)
When $M_\infty$ exceeds $M_{cr}$, shock waves form on the airfoil. As $M_\infty$ increases further, shock strength grows rapidly, producing:
1. Strong entropy increase and wave drag.
2. Shock-induced boundary layer separation (buffet onset).

The **Drag Divergence Mach Number ($M_{dd}$)** is officially defined as the freestream Mach number where:
$$\frac{dC_D}{dM_\infty} = 0.10 \quad (\text{or in some literatures, where } \Delta C_D = 0.0020)$$

Typically:
$$M_{dd} \approx M_{cr} + (0.05 \text{ to } 0.08)$$

## ⚡ Part II: Engineering Strategies to Delay Transonic Drag Rise

| Technique | Physical Mechanism | Aerospace Implementation |
|---|---|---|
| **Wing Sweep ($\Lambda$)** | Only the velocity component normal to the leading edge ($V_\infty \cos\Lambda$) drives chordwise acceleration. $M_{cr,\text{swept}} \approx M_{cr,\text{straight}} / \cos\Lambda$. | Commercial airliners (Boeing 787, A350: $\Lambda \approx 30^\circ$–$35^\circ$). |
| **Thin Airfoils ($t/c$)** | Thinner profiles perturb freestream less, producing smaller maximum velocity increments $\Delta u$. | Supersonic fighter jets ($t/c \approx 4\%$–$6\%$). |
| **Supercritical Airfoils** | Flatter upper surface suppresses early supersonic acceleration; rear camber restores lift. | Modern transport wings (delays $M_{dd}$ by $\Delta M \approx 0.1$). |
| **Area Rule (Whitcomb)** | Smooth total aircraft cross-sectional area distribution along longitudinal axis eliminates localized supersonic shock bottlenecks. | "Coke-bottle" fuselage waist design (F-102, F-106). |

## 🃏 Native Interactive Foldable Flashcards

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: TR-01 · Wing Sweep Effect on Critical Mach Number
> **Question:** An unswept wing has $M_{cr} = 0.72$. If the wing is swept by $\Lambda = 30^\circ$, estimate the new critical Mach number.
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > Simple sweep theory states that chordwise Mach number is $M_\perp = M_\infty \cos\Lambda$.
> > At the new critical speed: $M_{cr,\text{swept}} \cos\Lambda = M_{cr,\text{unswept}}$.
> > $$M_{cr,\text{swept}} = \frac{0.72}{\cos 30^\circ} = \frac{0.72}{0.866025} \approx 0.831$$
> > ⚠️ **AIR 1 Trap:** Wing sweep significantly increases $M_{cr}$, but it reduces the effective lift-curve slope by $\cos\Lambda$ and promotes spanwise boundary layer drift toward the tips (tip stall).

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: TR-02 · Supercritical Airfoil Distinguishing Features
> **Question:** What are the three geometric characteristics that distinguish a supercritical airfoil from a conventional NACA 4-digit airfoil?
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > 1. **Flattened upper surface:** Minimizes flow expansion, creating a broad, weak supersonic plateau rather than a sharp peak.
> > 2. **Large leading-edge radius:** Prevents early leading-edge suction peaks.
> > 3. **Pronounced rear camber (cusp):** Generates most of the airfoil lift near the trailing edge where boundary layer has recovered.

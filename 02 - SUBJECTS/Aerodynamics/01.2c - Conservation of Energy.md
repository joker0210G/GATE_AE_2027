## 📌 1. What is This Topic? (Zero to AIR-1 Overview)

The **Conservation of Energy** is the physical manifestation of the **First Law of Thermodynamics** ($\Delta E = Q - W$) applied to moving fluid systems and aerodynamic flow fields.

In GATE Aerospace Engineering, Conservation of Energy bridges thermodynamics, fluid mechanics, and high-speed aerodynamics across two essential scales:
1. **Integral Form (Reynolds Transport Theorem & Steady Flow Energy Equation - SFEE):** Applied to macro-scale aerospace components (turbines, compressors, rocket nozzles, and scramjet combustors) to calculate **shaft power ($\dot{W}_{\text{shaft}}$)**, **heat transfer rates ($\dot{Q}$)**, and **exit velocities ($V_e = \sqrt{2 c_p (T_{01} - T_e)}$)**.
2. **Differential Form (Pointwise Energy Equation & Viscous Dissipation $\Phi$):** Applied to boundary layers, shock structures, and re-entry aerodynamic heating:
   - **Total Enthalpy Equation:** Tracking $h_0 = h + \frac{1}{2}V^2$ invariance across adiabatic aerodynamic fields.
   - **Internal Energy & Viscous Dissipation Function ($\Phi \ge 0$):** Accounting for the irreversible conversion of mechanical kinetic energy into heat by viscous shear stresses.
   - **Aerodynamic Kinetic Heating & Recovery Factor ($r$):** Predicting the adiabatic wall temperature $T_{aw}$ experienced by supersonic aircraft skins.

> 💡 **GATE Target:** 2–3 direct questions (MCQ/NAT, ~3–5 marks) in Aerodynamics & Propulsion every year, plus a fundamental pillar for Compressible Flow, Shock Waves, and Gas Turbine cycles.

## 🧠 Part I: The Integral Energy Equation & Steady Flow Energy Equation (SFEE)

```
                        FIRST LAW OF THERMODYNAMICS
                                     │
                   dE_sys/dt = Q̇_net,in - Ẇ_net,out
                                     │
                    Apply Reynolds Transport Theorem:
                      Extensive: B = E_sys
                      Intensive: β = e_t = e + ½V² + gz
                                     │
                                     ▼
      Q̇_in - Ẇ_out = ∂/∂t ∭_CV ρ e_t dV + ∬_CS ρ e_t (V · n) dA
                                     │
                   Split Work: Ẇ_out = Ẇ_shaft + ∬_CS p (V · n) dA + Ẇ_viscous
                   Combine Flow Work with Internal Energy: e + p/ρ = h (Enthalpy!)
                                     │
                                     ▼
    Q̇_in - Ẇ_shaft = ∂/∂t ∭_CV ρ e_t dV + ∬_CS ρ (h + ½V² + gz) (V · n) dA
```

---

### 1. The Universal Control Volume Energy Equation

Applying the Reynolds Transport Theorem with specific total energy $e_t = e + \frac{1}{2}V^2 + gz$ (where $e$ is internal energy per unit mass):

$$\dot{Q}_{\text{net, in}} - \dot{W}_{\text{net, out}} = \frac{\partial}{\partial t}\iiint_{\text{CV}} \rho \left(e + \frac{1}{2}V^2 + gz\right) d\mathcal{V} + \iint_{\text{CS}} \rho \left(e + \frac{1}{2}V^2 + gz\right) (\vec{V} \cdot \hat{n}) \, dA$$

#### Work Done Breakdown ($\dot{W}_{\text{net, out}}$):
1. **Shaft Work ($\dot{W}_{\text{shaft}}$):** Mechanical work transferred across CV boundaries by rotating shafts (turbines, compressors, pumps).
2. **Pressure Flow Work ($\dot{W}_{\text{flow}}$):** Work required to push fluid across control surface boundaries:
   $$\dot{W}_{\text{flow}} = \iint_{\text{CS}} p (\vec{V} \cdot \hat{n}) \, dA = \iint_{\text{CS}} \rho \left(\frac{p}{\rho}\right) (\vec{V} \cdot \hat{n}) \, dA$$
3. **Viscous Shear Work ($\dot{W}_{\text{viscous}}$):** Work done by boundary shear stresses (neglected at inlet/outlet perpendicular ports).

---

### 2. Grouping Flow Work with Enthalpy ($h \equiv e + \frac{p}{\rho}$)

Combining the pressure flow work term $\frac{p}{\rho}$ with the specific internal energy $e$:

$$e + \frac{p}{\rho} \equiv h \quad (\text{Specific Enthalpy})$$

Defining the **Specific Total Enthalpy (Stagnation Enthalpy)**:
$$\mathbf{h_0 \equiv h + \frac{1}{2}V^2}$$

The **Universal Integral Energy Equation** becomes:

$$\mathbf{\dot{Q}_{\text{in}} - \dot{W}_{\text{shaft, out}} = \frac{\partial}{\partial t}\iiint_{\text{CV}} \rho \left(e + \frac{1}{2}V^2 + gz\right) d\mathcal{V} + \iint_{\text{CS}} \rho \left(h + \frac{1}{2}V^2 + gz\right) (\vec{V} \cdot \hat{n}) \, dA}$$

---

### 3. Steady 1D Flow: The Steady Flow Energy Equation (SFEE)

For steady 1D flow ($\frac{\partial}{\partial t} = 0$) across a single inlet (station 1) and single outlet (station 2):

$$\mathbf{\dot{Q} - \dot{W}_{\text{shaft}} = \dot{m}\left[\left(h_2 + \frac{V_2^2}{2} + gz_2\right) - \left(h_1 + \frac{V_1^2}{2} + gz_1\right)\right]}$$

Dividing by mass flow rate $\dot{m}$ yields the **Per-Unit-Mass SFEE**:

$$\mathbf{q - w_{\text{shaft}} = (h_2 - h_1) + \frac{V_2^2 - V_1^2}{2} + g(z_2 - z_1) = h_{02} - h_{01} + g(z_2 - z_1)}$$

```
┌─────────────────────────────────┬─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ Aerospace Component             │ Simplified Governing Energy Equation                        │
├─────────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ **1. Rocket / Jet Nozzle**      │ Adiabatic ($q=0$), Workless ($w_s=0$), $\Delta z \approx 0$:│
│    (Gas Acceleration)           │ $\mathbf{h_{01} = h_{02} \implies h_1 + \frac{V_1^2}{2} = h_2 + \frac{V_2^2}{2}}$│
│                                 │ $\mathbf{V_2 = \sqrt{V_1^2 + 2 c_p (T_1 - T_2)} = \sqrt{2 c_p (T_{01} - T_2)}}$ │
├─────────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ **2. Diffuser / Air Intake**    │ Adiabatic ($q=0$), Workless ($w_s=0$):                      │
│    (Gas Deceleration)           │ $\mathbf{h_2 = h_1 + \frac{V_1^2 - V_2^2}{2} \implies T_{02} = T_{01}}$ │
├─────────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ **3. Compressor / Fan**         │ Adiabatic ($q=0$), Work input ($w_s < 0$):                  │
│    (Pressure / Enthalpy Rise)   │ $\mathbf{-w_{\text{shaft, in}} = h_{02} - h_{01} = c_p (T_{02} - T_{01})}$ │
├─────────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ **4. Gas Turbine**              │ Adiabatic ($q=0$), Work output ($w_s > 0$):                 │
│    (Power Extraction)           │ $\mathbf{w_{\text{shaft, out}} = h_{01} - h_{02} = c_p (T_{01} - T_{02})}$ │
├─────────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ **5. Combustor / Heat Addition**│ Constant pressure heat addition ($w_s=0, V_1 \approx V_2$): │
│    (Thermal Energy Addition)    │ $\mathbf{q_{\text{in}} = h_{02} - h_{01} = c_p (T_{02} - T_{01})}$ │
└─────────────────────────────────┴─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

## 🧠 Part II: The Total Temperature & Total Enthalpy Invariant

```
                 THE ADIABATIC AERODYNAMIC INVARIANT
           If an aerodynamic flow is ADIABATIC (q = 0) and
           WORKLESS (w_shaft = 0), then along any streamline:
                                  │
                                  ▼
                     h₀ = h + ½V² = CONSTANT
                                  │
                  For Calorically Perfect Gas (h = c_p T):
                                  │
                                  ▼
                   T₀ = T + V²/(2 c_p) = CONSTANT
```

### Stagnation Temperature Relation:
$$T_0 = T + \frac{V^2}{2 c_p} = T\left(1 + \frac{V^2}{2 c_p T}\right)$$

Using $c_p = \frac{\gamma R}{\gamma - 1}$ and speed of sound $a^2 = \gamma R T$:
$$\frac{V^2}{2 c_p T} = \frac{V^2}{2 \left(\frac{\gamma R T}{\gamma - 1}\right)} = \frac{\gamma - 1}{2}\left(\frac{V^2}{a^2}\right) = \frac{\gamma - 1}{2} M^2$$

$$\mathbf{T_0 = T\left(1 + \frac{\gamma - 1}{2} M^2\right)}$$

> 🏆 **THE GOLDEN RULE OF TOTAL TEMPERATURE:**
> In any steady, adiabatic, workless flow (whether inviscid or viscous, subsonic or supersonic, **with or without shock waves**):
> $$\mathbf{T_{01} = T_{02} = T_0 = \text{CONSTANT}}$$
> *A normal or oblique shock wave changes pressure $p_0$ and entropy $s$, but **NEVER changes total temperature $T_0$**!*

## 🧠 Part III: Differential Form of the Energy Equation

```
                    DIFFERENTIAL CONSERVATION OF ENERGY
         Total Energy Equation: ∂(ρ e_t)/∂t + ∇ · (ρ e_t V) = -∇ · q + ∇ · (σ · V) + ρ g · V
                                      │
                 Subtract Mechanical Kinetic Energy (V · Momentum Eq):
                                      │
                                      ▼
               THERMAL / INTERNAL ENERGY EQUATION:
                  ρ De/Dt = -p (∇ · V) + ∇ · (k ∇T) + Φ
                                      │
         ┌────────────────────────────┼────────────────────────────┐
         ▼                            ▼                            ▼
  Reversible Work:              Heat Conduction:           Viscous Dissipation:
  -p (∇ · V)                    ∇ · (k ∇T) = k ∇²T         Φ = τ_ij (∂u_i/∂x_j) ≥ 0
  (Compressibility work)        (Fourier's Law)            (Frictional heating!)
```

---

### 1. Total Energy Equation (Conservative Form)

Balancing energy rates on an infinitesimal fluid element $(\delta x, \delta y, \delta z)$ in conservative divergence form:

$$\mathbf{\frac{\partial(\rho e_t)}{\partial t} + \nabla \cdot (\rho e_t \vec{V}) = -\nabla \cdot \vec{q} - \nabla \cdot (p \vec{V}) + \nabla \cdot (\boldsymbol{\tau} \cdot \vec{V}) + \rho \vec{g} \cdot \vec{V}}$$

where:
- $\vec{q} = -k \nabla T$ is the **heat flux vector** by Fourier's law of conduction ($k$ = thermal conductivity).
- $\nabla \cdot (p \vec{V})$ is the rate of pressure work.
- $\nabla \cdot (\boldsymbol{\tau} \cdot \vec{V})$ is the rate of work done by viscous shear stresses.
- $\rho \vec{g} \cdot \vec{V}$ is the rate of potential energy work by gravity.

---

### 2. Thermal Energy Equation & Viscous Dissipation Function ($\Phi$)

Subtracting the mechanical kinetic energy equation (obtained by taking the dot product $\vec{V} \cdot \text{[Momentum Equation]}$) from the total energy equation isolates the **Thermal Energy Equation**:

$$\mathbf{\rho \frac{De}{Dt} = -p (\nabla \cdot \vec{V}) + \nabla \cdot (k \nabla T) + \Phi}$$

For a calorically perfect gas ($e = c_v T$ and $\nabla \cdot (k\nabla T) = k \nabla^2 T$):

$$\mathbf{\rho c_v \frac{DT}{Dt} = -p (\nabla \cdot \vec{V}) + k \nabla^2 T + \Phi}$$

In Enthalpy Form ($h = e + p/\rho \implies \rho \frac{Dh}{Dt} = \rho \frac{De}{Dt} + \frac{Dp}{Dt} + p(\nabla\cdot\vec{V})$):

$$\mathbf{\rho c_p \frac{DT}{Dt} = \frac{Dp}{Dt} + k \nabla^2 T + \Phi}$$

---

### 3. Mathematical Structure of the Viscous Dissipation Function ($\Phi$)

The **Viscous Dissipation Function** $\Phi$ is the rate at which mechanical energy is irreversibly converted into thermal internal energy per unit volume due to fluid friction:

$$\Phi \equiv \boldsymbol{\tau} : \nabla\vec{V} = \tau_{ij}\frac{\partial u_i}{\partial x_j} \ge 0$$

In 3D Cartesian Coordinates for an Incompressible Fluid ($\nabla \cdot \vec{V} = 0$):

$$\mathbf{\Phi = \mu \left[ 2\left(\frac{\partial u}{\partial x}\right)^2 + 2\left(\frac{\partial v}{\partial y}\right)^2 + 2\left(\frac{\partial w}{\partial z}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{\partial u}{\partial y} + \frac{\partial v}{\partial x}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{\partial v}{\partial z} + \frac{\partial w}{\partial y}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{\partial w}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial u}{\partial z}\right)^2 \right] \ge 0}$$

For 2D Boundary Layer / Shear Flow ($u = u(y), v \approx 0$):
$$\mathbf{\Phi = \mu \left(\frac{\partial u}{\partial y}\right)^2 \ge 0}$$

> 🔑 **Second Law Invariance ($\Phi \ge 0$):**
> $\Phi$ is a sum of squared velocity gradients. Hence, **$\Phi$ is strictly positive or zero ($\Phi \ge 0$)**. Viscous friction can **ONLY generate heat**, never destroy it!

## 🧠 Part IV: Aerodynamic Kinetic Heating & Recovery Temperature

When a supersonic aircraft or missile flies at Mach number $M_\infty$, the high freestream kinetic energy is decelerated within the boundary layer by viscous dissipation, creating intense **Aerodynamic Heating**.

```
                   SUPERSONIC BOUNDARY LAYER TEMPERATURE PROFILE
                 y
                 ▲
                 │              T_e (Freestream Edge Temperature)
                 │              │
                 │             /
                 │            /   Viscous Dissipation Heating
                 │           /
                 │          ▼
                 └─────────► T_aw (Adiabatic Wall Recovery Temperature!)
                 0         T
```

---

### 1. Adiabatic Wall Temperature ($T_{aw}$) & Recovery Factor ($r$)

Due to incomplete stagnation of fluid within a viscous boundary layer (heat conducts back into the freestream), the wall temperature reaches an equilibrium **Adiabatic Wall Temperature (Recovery Temperature)** $T_{aw}$:

$$\mathbf{T_{aw} = T_e + r \left(\frac{V_e^2}{2 c_p}\right) = T_e \left(1 + r \frac{\gamma - 1}{2} M_e^2\right)}$$

where $r$ is the non-dimensional **Temperature Recovery Factor**:

$$\mathbf{r \equiv \frac{T_{aw} - T_e}{T_0 - T_e}}$$

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────┬───────────────────────────────┬─────────────────────────┐
│ Boundary Layer Regime               │ Theoretical Recovery Factor   │ Value for Air ($Pr=0.71$)│
├─────────────────────────────────────┼───────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────┤
│ **Laminar Boundary Layer**          │ $\mathbf{r = \sqrt{Pr}}$      │ $r \approx \sqrt{0.71} \approx \mathbf{0.843}$ │
├─────────────────────────────────────┼───────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────┤
│ **Turbulent Boundary Layer**        │ $\mathbf{r = \sqrt[3]{Pr}}$   │ $r \approx (0.71)^{1/3} \approx \mathbf{0.892}$ │
└─────────────────────────────────────┴───────────────────────────────┴─────────────────────────┘
```

> ⚠️ **Key Physical Consequence:**
> Because $r_{\text{turb}} > r_{\text{lam}}$, **turbulent boundary layers cause significantly higher aerodynamic skin heating than laminar boundary layers**!

## 🧠 Part V: Crocco's Theorem (Linking Energy, Vorticity & Entropy)

Crocco's theorem mathematically unites the Navier-Stokes/Euler momentum equations with the First and Second Laws of Thermodynamics:

$$\mathbf{\vec{V} \times \vec{\omega} = \nabla h_0 - T \nabla s}$$

where $\vec{\omega} = \nabla \times \vec{V}$ is vorticity, $h_0$ is total enthalpy, and $s$ is specific entropy.

```
┌─────────────────────────────────┬─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ Flow Classification             │ Crocco's Consequence                                        │
├─────────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ **1. Isoenergetic Flow**        │ Uniform total enthalpy: $\nabla h_0 = \vec{0}$               │
├─────────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ **2. Homentropic Flow**         │ Uniform entropy: $\nabla s = \vec{0}$                       │
├─────────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ **3. Isoenergetic + Homentropic**│ $\vec{V} \times \vec{\omega} = \vec{0} \implies \vec{\omega} = \vec{0}$ (Flow is **Irrotational Potential Flow**!) │
├─────────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ **4. Curved Shock Wave**        │ Shock curvature creates entropy gradient $\nabla s \neq \vec{0} \implies$ **Vorticity $\vec{\omega} \neq \vec{0}$ is generated behind curved shocks!** │
└─────────────────────────────────┴─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

## ⚡ AIR-1 Topper Mnemonics, Shortcuts & Speed Tricks

### 🚀 Shortcut 1: The 5-Second Compressible Exhaust Velocity
For expansion from stagnation temperature $T_0$ into static temperature $T_e$:
$$V_e = \sqrt{2 c_p (T_0 - T_e)} = \sqrt{2 c_p T_0 \left[1 - \left(\frac{p_e}{p_0}\right)^{\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma}}\right]}$$
- *For Air ($c_p = 1005\text{ J/kg}\cdot\text{K}$):* $V_e = \sqrt{2010 \times \Delta T} \approx 44.83 \sqrt{\Delta T}$.

---

### 🚀 Shortcut 2: Maximum Theoretical Speed of a Gas (Expansion to Absolute Zero $T=0$)
$$V_{\max} = \sqrt{2 c_p T_0} = \sqrt{\frac{2\gamma}{\gamma - 1} R T_0} = a_0 \sqrt{\frac{2}{\gamma - 1}}$$
- For Air ($\gamma = 1.4$): $V_{\max} = \sqrt{5} \, a_0 \approx \mathbf{2.236 \, a_0}$.

---

### 🚀 Shortcut 3: Instant Recovery Temperature Calculation
$$T_{aw} = T_\infty \left(1 + r \frac{\gamma - 1}{2} M_\infty^2\right) = T_\infty \left(1 + 0.2 \, r \, M_\infty^2\right) \quad (\text{for Air with } \gamma = 1.4)$$

## 📝 Step-by-Step Solved GATE Examples (Zero to GATE Topper Level)

### Example 1: Rocket Nozzle Exit Velocity & Mach Number (GATE Standard)
**Problem:** Combustion gases ($\gamma = 1.33, c_p = 1800\text{ J/kg}\cdot\text{K}$) enter a rocket nozzle at stagnation conditions $T_0 = 3200\text{ K}$ and $p_0 = 6.0\text{ MPa}$. The gases expand isentropically to an exit static pressure $p_e = 100\text{ kPa}$.
1. Calculate the exit static temperature $T_e$.
2. Compute the exit gas velocity $V_e$.
3. Determine the exit Mach number $M_e$.

> [!faq]- 🔍 View Solution & Detailed Explanation
> **1. Exit Static Temperature ($T_e$):**
> Using isentropic expansion:
> $$\frac{T_e}{T_0} = \left(\frac{p_e}{p_0}\right)^{\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma}} = \left(\frac{100\times 10^3}{6.0\times 10^6}\right)^{\frac{1.33-1}{1.33}} = \left(\frac{1}{60}\right)^{\frac{0.33}{1.33}} = (0.016667)^{0.24812}$$
> $$\frac{T_e}{T_0} \approx 0.3626 \implies T_e = (3200\text{ K})(0.3626) \approx \mathbf{1160.3\text{ K}}$$
> 
> ---
> **2. Exit Velocity ($V_e$):**
> By Steady Flow Energy Equation (adiabatic, workless):
> $$h_0 = h_e + \frac{V_e^2}{2} \implies V_e = \sqrt{2 c_p (T_0 - T_e)}$$
> $$V_e = \sqrt{2(1800\text{ J/kg}\cdot\text{K})(3200 - 1160.3\text{ K})} = \sqrt{3600 \times 2039.7} = \sqrt{7.3429\times 10^6} \approx \mathbf{2709.8\text{ m/s}}$$
> 
> ---
> **3. Exit Mach Number ($M_e$):**
> Gas constant: $R = c_p \frac{\gamma - 1}{\gamma} = 1800 \left(\frac{0.33}{1.33}\right) = 446.62\text{ J/kg}\cdot\text{K}$.
> Speed of sound at exit:
> $$a_e = \sqrt{\gamma R T_e} = \sqrt{(1.33)(446.62)(1160.3)} = \sqrt{689,228} \approx 830.2\text{ m/s}$$
> Exit Mach number:
> $$M_e = \frac{V_e}{a_e} = \frac{2709.8}{830.2} = \mathbf{3.26}$$

### Example 2: Supersonic Wing Leading Edge Aerodynamic Heating
**Problem:** A supersonic reconnaissance aircraft flies at $M_\infty = 2.5$ at an altitude where ambient static temperature is $T_\infty = 220\text{ K}$ ($\gamma = 1.4, c_p = 1005\text{ J/kg}\cdot\text{K}, Pr = 0.72$).
1. Calculate the freestream stagnation temperature $T_0$.
2. Calculate the adiabatic wall temperature $T_{aw,\text{lam}}$ for a laminar boundary layer on the wing nose.
3. Calculate the adiabatic wall temperature $T_{aw,\text{turb}}$ assuming the boundary layer transitions to turbulent flow.

> [!faq]- 🔍 View Solution & Detailed Explanation
> **1. Stagnation Temperature ($T_0$):**
> $$T_0 = T_\infty\left(1 + \frac{\gamma - 1}{2}M_\infty^2\right) = 220\left(1 + 0.2(2.5)^2\right) = 220(1 + 0.2 \times 6.25) = 220(1 + 1.25) = 220(2.25) = \mathbf{495.0\text{ K} = 221.85^\circ\text{C}}$$
> 
> ---
> **2. Laminar Adiabatic Wall Temperature ($T_{aw,\text{lam}}$):**
> Laminar recovery factor: $r_{\text{lam}} = \sqrt{Pr} = \sqrt{0.72} \approx 0.8485$.
> $$T_{aw,\text{lam}} = T_\infty \left(1 + r_{\text{lam}}\frac{\gamma - 1}{2}M_\infty^2\right) = 220\left(1 + (0.8485)(1.25)\right) = 220(1 + 1.0606) = 220(2.0606) = \mathbf{453.3\text{ K} = 180.15^\circ\text{C}}$$
> 
> ---
> **3. Turbulent Adiabatic Wall Temperature ($T_{aw,\text{turb}}$):**
> Turbulent recovery factor: $r_{\text{turb}} = \sqrt[3]{Pr} = (0.72)^{1/3} \approx 0.8963$.
> $$T_{aw,\text{turb}} = 220\left(1 + (0.8963)(1.25)\right) = 220(1 + 1.1204) = 220(2.1204) = \mathbf{466.5\text{ K} = 193.35^\circ\text{C}}$$
> *Notice: Turbulent boundary layer skin temperature is $13.2\text{ K}$ higher than laminar due to stronger turbulent shear mixing and dissipation.*

### Example 3: Aircraft Jet Engine Compressor Shaft Power
**Problem:** An axial flow compressor ingests air ($\dot{m} = 60\text{ kg/s}, c_p = 1005\text{ J/kg}\cdot\text{K}$) at inlet stagnation temperature $T_{01} = 290\text{ K}$. The compressor has a total pressure ratio $\pi_c = 12$ and isentropic efficiency $\eta_c = 0.86$. Calculate the total shaft power $\dot{W}_{\text{shaft}}$ required to drive the compressor.

> [!faq]- 🔍 View Solution & Detailed Explanation
> **1. Ideal Isentropic Exit Stagnation Temperature ($T_{02s}$):**
> $$\frac{T_{02s}}{T_{01}} = (\pi_c)^{\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma}} = (12)^{\frac{0.4}{1.4}} = (12)^{0.2857} \approx 2.034$$
> $$T_{02s} = (290\text{ K})(2.034) = 589.86\text{ K}$$
> 
> **2. Actual Exit Stagnation Temperature ($T_{02}$):**
> $$\eta_c = \frac{T_{02s} - T_{01}}{T_{02} - T_{01}} \implies T_{02} - T_{01} = \frac{T_{02s} - T_{01}}{\eta_c} = \frac{589.86 - 290}{0.86} = \frac{299.86}{0.86} = 348.67\text{ K}$$
> $$T_{02} = 290 + 348.67 = 638.67\text{ K}$$
> 
> **3. Shaft Power Input:**
> Applying SFEE for an adiabatic compressor ($q = 0$):
> $$\dot{W}_{\text{shaft, in}} = \dot{m} c_p (T_{02} - T_{01}) = (60\text{ kg/s})(1005\text{ J/kg}\cdot\text{K})(348.67\text{ K}) = 21,024,801\text{ W} \approx \mathbf{21.02\text{ MW}}$$

## ⚠️ Common Exam Traps & Pitfalls (Where Marks are Lost)

> 🔴 **Trap 1: Assuming Total Temperature Changes Across a Shock Wave**
> - *Mistake:* Thinking that because pressure drops across a shock ($p_{02} < p_{01}$), total temperature must also drop ($T_{02} < T_{01}$).
> - *Correction:* Across any stationary adiabatic shock wave, **$T_{02} \equiv T_{01}$ is STRICTLY CONSERVED**! Total pressure drops due to entropy generation ($\Delta s > 0$), not energy loss.

> 🔴 **Trap 2: Using $T_0$ Instead of $T_{aw}$ for Skin Temperature**
> - *Mistake:* Setting the equilibrium surface temperature equal to stagnation temperature $T_0$.
> - *Correction:* Fluid at the wall cannot reach $T_0$ because heat conducts away into cooler boundary layer regions. The actual wall temperature is $T_{aw} = T_e + r(T_0 - T_e)$, with $r < 1.0$.

> 🔴 **Trap 3: Sign of Viscous Dissipation $\Phi$**
> - *Mistake:* Assuming viscous dissipation can be negative in expanding flows.
> - *Correction:* $\Phi \equiv \tau_{ij}\frac{\partial u_i}{\partial x_j} \ge 0$ is a sum of squares and is **STRICTLY POSITIVE OR ZERO** in accordance with the Second Law of Thermodynamics.

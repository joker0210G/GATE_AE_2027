## 🧠 MASTER MENTAL MODEL — Stability vs. Control Derivatives

> **KEY DISTINCTION (GATE loves to test this!):**
>
> | Type | Subscript Variable | Example | Meaning |
> |------|-------------------|---------|---------|
> | **Stability Derivative** | Flight state ($\alpha, \beta, p, q, r$) | $C_{m_\alpha}$ | Aircraft's natural tendency |
> | **Control Derivative** | Control deflection ($\delta_e, \delta_a, \delta_r$) | $C_{m_{\delta_e}}$ | Pilot's ability to change flight |

**The 9 Primary Control Derivatives:**

```
LONGITUDINAL (Elevator delta_e):
  C_m_delta_e  → Pitching moment control power     (NEGATIVE always)
  C_L_delta_e  → Lift change due to elevator        (POSITIVE always)

LATERAL (Aileron delta_a):
  C_l_delta_a  → Rolling moment control power       (NEGATIVE always)
  C_n_delta_a  → Adverse yaw from ailerons          (NEGATIVE typically)

DIRECTIONAL (Rudder delta_r):
  C_n_delta_r  → Yawing moment control power        (NEGATIVE always)
  C_y_delta_r  → Side force due to rudder           (POSITIVE)
  C_l_delta_r  → Roll from rudder (proverse roll)   (POSITIVE)
```

> 🎯 **MASTER MNEMONIC: "ALL CONTROL DERIVATIVES ARE NEGATIVE" (except $C_{y_{\delta_r}}$ and $C_{l_{\delta_r}}$)**

## 📐 SECTION 1 — The Control Surface Effectiveness Factor ($\tau$)

### 1.1 What is $\tau$ (tau)?

Before calculating any control derivative, you need the **effectiveness factor $\tau$**.

$$\boxed{\tau = \frac{\partial \alpha_{\text{effective}}}{\partial \delta}}$$

**Physical meaning:** When you deflect a control surface (elevator, aileron, rudder) by angle $\delta$, the airfoil's effective angle of attack changes by only $\tau \cdot \delta$ (not the full $\delta$), because the control surface only covers part of the chord.

### 1.2 Chord Ratio and $\tau$

$$\tau = f\left(\frac{c_f}{c}\right)$$

Where $c_f/c$ = **flap (control surface) chord ratio** = (control surface chord) / (total chord)

| Chord Ratio $c_f/c$ | Effectiveness $\tau$ | Notes |
|--------------------|---------------------|-------|
| 0.10 | ~0.33 | Very small control surface |
| 0.20 | ~0.45 | Small control surface |
| 0.25 | ~0.48 | Typical elevator/rudder |
| 0.30 | ~0.52 | Common aileron ratio |
| 0.40 | ~0.57 | Large control surface |
| 1.00 | 1.00 | **All-moving tail** (stabilator) |

**Typical GATE values:** $\tau \approx 0.4$ to $0.6$ for conventional control surfaces.

### 1.3 Control Surface Change in Lift

$$\boxed{C_{L_{\delta}} = a \cdot \tau}$$

Where $a$ = lift curve slope of the **lifting surface** (tail or wing).

> 🎯 **Physical meaning:** Deflecting a control surface by $\delta$ is aerodynamically equivalent to changing the angle of attack of the entire surface by $\tau \cdot \delta$.

## 📐 SECTION 2 — Elevator Control Derivatives (Longitudinal)

### 2.1 Pitching Moment Control Power: $C_{m_{\delta_e}}$

$$\boxed{C_{m_{\delta_e}} = \frac{\partial C_m}{\partial \delta_e}}$$

**Sign:** $C_{m_{\delta_e}} < 0$ **ALWAYS** (elevator trailing edge DOWN = nose DOWN moment)

**Derivation from first principles:**

When elevator deflects by $\delta_e$, the tail lift increases by:
$$\Delta C_{L_t} = a_t \cdot \tau_e \cdot \delta_e$$

This tail lift increase creates a pitching moment about the CG:
$$\Delta C_m = -\frac{S_t}{S} \cdot \frac{l_t}{\bar{c}} \cdot \Delta C_{L_t} = -V_H \cdot a_t \cdot \tau_e \cdot \delta_e$$

Therefore:
$$\boxed{C_{m_{\delta_e}} = -\eta_t \cdot V_H \cdot a_t \cdot \tau_e}$$

Where:
| Symbol | Meaning | Typical Value |
|--------|---------|---------------|
| $\eta_t$ | Tail dynamic pressure ratio ($q_t/q_\infty$) | 0.85 to 0.95 |
| $V_H$ | Horizontal tail volume ratio ($S_t l_t / S \bar{c}$) | 0.3 to 0.6 |
| $a_t$ | Tail lift curve slope | 3.0 to 4.5 rad$^{-1}$ |
| $\tau_e$ | Elevator effectiveness factor | 0.4 to 0.6 |

> ⚠️ **Sign convention:** $C_{m_{\delta_e}} < 0$ always! If $\delta_e > 0$ (trailing edge DOWN), the tail creates nose-DOWN moment ($C_m < 0$). This is the **control power** of the elevator.

### 2.2 Lift Due to Elevator: $C_{L_{\delta_e}}$

$$\boxed{C_{L_{\delta_e}} = \frac{\partial C_L}{\partial \delta_e} = \eta_t \cdot \frac{S_t}{S} \cdot a_t \cdot \tau_e}$$

**Sign:** $C_{L_{\delta_e}} > 0$ **ALWAYS** (elevator down = more tail lift = more total lift)

**Relationship with $C_{m_{\delta_e}}$:**

$$C_{m_{\delta_e}} = -V_H \cdot C_{L_{\delta_e}} / \frac{S_t}{S}$$

Simplified:
$$\boxed{C_{m_{\delta_e}} = -\frac{l_t}{\bar{c}} \cdot C_{L_{\delta_e}}}$$

> 🎯 **5-Second Shortcut:** If you know $C_{L_{\delta_e}}$ and $l_t/\bar{c}$:
> $$C_{m_{\delta_e}} \approx -(l_t/\bar{c}) \times C_{L_{\delta_e}}$$

### 2.3 Elevator Effectiveness vs. All-Moving Tail (Stabilator)

| Configuration | $\tau_e$ | Advantages | Disadvantages |
|--------------|---------|-----------|---------------|
| Conventional elevator (hinged) | 0.4 to 0.6 | Simple, reliable | Lower effectiveness |
| All-moving tail (stabilator, $\tau = 1.0$) | 1.00 | Maximum control power | Complex, needs anti-servo tab |
| T-tail | Similar to conventional | Avoids wing wake | Deep-stall susceptibility |

### 2.4 Total Pitching Moment Equation with Control

$$\boxed{C_{m,cg} = C_{m_0} + C_{m_\alpha} \cdot \alpha + C_{m_{\delta_e}} \cdot \delta_e}$$

**Trim condition** ($C_{m,cg} = 0$):

$$\delta_{e,\text{trim}} = -\frac{C_{m_0} + C_{m_\alpha} \alpha_{\text{trim}}}{C_{m_{\delta_e}}}$$

**How trim $\delta_e$ changes with $C_L$:**

$$\frac{d\delta_e}{dC_L} = -\frac{C_{m_\alpha}}{C_{L_\alpha} \cdot C_{m_{\delta_e}}} = \frac{K_n}{C_{L_\alpha}/C_{m_{\delta_e}}}$$

> 🎯 **KEY INSIGHT:** Steeper the $\delta_e$ vs. $C_L$ slope → more stable aircraft (higher $K_n$) → MORE elevator travel needed for maneuvering.

### 2.5 Elevator Angle-to-Trim ($\delta_e$-to-$\alpha$) Graph

```
         delta_e
            |
   nose up  |  \  stable (Kn > 0)
  (delta_e  |   \                     slope = dCm0/Cm_de
  positive) |    \                            = -(Cm_alpha/Cm_de)
            |     \  (downward slope = stable)
   0 -----  +------\-------- alpha (or C_L)
            |       \
  nose down |        \  (trim point where Cm = 0)
  (delta_e  |
  negative) |  unstable if slope is positive (upward)
```

## 📐 SECTION 3 — Aileron Control Derivatives (Lateral)

### 3.1 Rolling Moment Control Power: $C_{l_{\delta_a}}$

$$\boxed{C_{l_{\delta_a}} = \frac{\partial C_l}{\partial \delta_a}}$$

**Sign:** $C_{l_{\delta_a}} < 0$ **ALWAYS**

**Sign Convention for Ailerons:**
$$\delta_a = \frac{\delta_{a,L} - \delta_{a,R}}{2}$$

- Positive $\delta_a > 0$: Left aileron trailing edge DOWN, Right aileron trailing edge UP
- This creates **left roll** (left wing up, right wing down) → negative rolling moment $C_l < 0$
- Therefore $C_{l_{\delta_a}} < 0$ always

**Derivation — Strip Theory Integration:**

For an aileron spanning from $y_1$ (inboard) to $y_2$ (outboard) on one wing:

$$\boxed{C_{l_{\delta_a}} = -\frac{1}{Sb} \int_{y_1}^{y_2} c(y) \cdot a_w \cdot \tau_a \cdot y \, dy \times 2}$$

The factor of 2 accounts for both ailerons (both wings) working differentially.

**For a rectangular wing with constant chord $c$:**

$$C_{l_{\delta_a}} = -\frac{a_w \cdot \tau_a}{Sb} \int_{y_1}^{y_2} c \cdot y \, dy \times 2 = -\frac{a_w \tau_a c}{Sb} (y_2^2 - y_1^2)$$

In **non-dimensional form** (using $\eta = 2y/b$, so $\eta$ goes from 0 to 1 at wing tip):
$$\boxed{C_{l_{\delta_a}} = -\frac{a_w \tau_a}{2}(\eta_2^2 - \eta_1^2) \cdot \frac{c}{b}}$$

> 🎯 **Design Rule:** Ailerons at **outboard** positions (large $y$) are more effective because they have a longer moment arm $y$! But outboard ailerons are more susceptible to aileron reversal.

### 3.2 Steady-State Roll Rate from Ailerons

From the roll equation of motion ($I_{xx}\dot{p} = 0$ at steady state):

$$p_{ss} = -\frac{2V_\infty}{b} \cdot \frac{C_{l_{\delta_a}}}{C_{l_p}} \cdot \delta_a$$

**Roll Helix Angle (performance metric):**
$$\left(\frac{pb}{2V}\right)_{ss} = -\frac{C_{l_{\delta_a}}}{C_{l_p}} \cdot \delta_a$$

**MIL-SPEC Handling Quality Requirement:** $\left(\frac{pb}{2V}\right)_{\min} \geq 0.09$ rad for fighter aircraft.

### 3.3 Adverse Yaw: $C_{n_{\delta_a}}$ (Cross-Control Derivative)

$$\boxed{C_{n_{\delta_a}} = \frac{\partial C_n}{\partial \delta_a} < 0 \text{ (usually)}}$$

**Physical mechanism of Adverse Yaw:**

1. Right aileron deflects DOWN ($\delta_a > 0$: left roll)
2. Left wing (down-going aileron): $C_L \uparrow$ → $C_{D,i} = C_L^2/\pi AR e$ increases significantly
3. Right wing (up-going aileron): $C_L \downarrow$ → $C_{D,i}$ decreases
4. Left wing has MORE drag than right wing
5. Aircraft yaws LEFT (opposite to intended right roll) → **Adverse Yaw**!

$$C_{n_{\delta_a}} \approx -\frac{2}{Sb^2} \int_{y_1}^{y_2} \frac{C_L}{\pi AR e} \cdot a_w \tau_a y \, dy$$

**Sign:** Typically negative (adverse yaw for conventional aileron deflections).

### 3.4 Aileron Reversal

> **AILERON REVERSAL:** At high speeds, aileron deflection twists the wing. The aerodynamic twisting moment (from the aileron) **overcomes** the structural restoring torque, causing the wing to twist in the opposite direction → control reversal!

**Reversal Dynamic Pressure:**
$$q_{\text{reversal}} = -\frac{e_a}{a_w \tau_a c}$$

Where $e_a$ is the aerodynamic center of the aileron relative to the elastic axis.

**Aileron Effectiveness vs. Speed:**
$$\frac{\text{Actual Effectiveness}}{\text{Rigid Effectiveness}} = 1 - \frac{q}{q_{\text{reversal}}}$$

At $q = q_{\text{reversal}}$: effectiveness = 0 (reversal speed!)
At $q > q_{\text{reversal}}$: effectiveness is negative (control reversed!)

> 🎯 **MNEMONIC: "REVERSAL RUINS ROLL"** — At reversal speed, ailerons stop working. Above it, they work **backwards**!

> ⚠️ **Design Requirement:** $V_{\text{flight}} < V_{\text{reversal}}$ always! Modern jets have inboard ailerons at high speed (more stiff, less susceptible to reversal) and outboard ailerons at low speed.

## 📐 SECTION 4 — Rudder Control Derivatives (Directional)

### 4.1 Yawing Moment Control Power: $C_{n_{\delta_r}}$

$$\boxed{C_{n_{\delta_r}} = \frac{\partial C_n}{\partial \delta_r}}$$

**Sign:** $C_{n_{\delta_r}} < 0$ **ALWAYS**

**Sign Convention for Rudder:**
- Positive $\delta_r > 0$: rudder trailing edge deflects LEFT
- This creates a side force on the tail to the RIGHT
- Side force to the RIGHT (on a tail that is AFT of CG) → yawing moment pushing nose LEFT
- Negative yawing moment → $C_{n_{\delta_r}} < 0$

**Derivation:**

$$\boxed{C_{n_{\delta_r}} = -\eta_v \cdot V_v \cdot a_v \cdot \tau_r}$$

Where:
| Symbol | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| $\eta_v$ | Vertical tail dynamic pressure ratio |
| $V_v = S_v l_v / (Sb)$ | Vertical tail volume ratio (uses span $b$!) |
| $a_v$ | Vertical tail lift curve slope |
| $\tau_r$ | Rudder effectiveness factor |

### 4.2 Side Force Due to Rudder: $C_{y_{\delta_r}}$

$$\boxed{C_{y_{\delta_r}} = \frac{\partial C_y}{\partial \delta_r} = \eta_v \cdot \frac{S_v}{S} \cdot a_v \cdot \tau_r > 0}$$

**Sign:** $C_{y_{\delta_r}} > 0$ **ALWAYS** (positive rudder deflection creates positive side force)

**Relationship with $C_{n_{\delta_r}}$:**
$$C_{n_{\delta_r}} = -\frac{l_v}{b} \cdot C_{y_{\delta_r}}$$

> 🎯 **Mnemonic:** "Rudder yaw power = $-(l_v/b)$ times side force power"

### 4.3 Roll Due to Rudder: $C_{l_{\delta_r}}$ (Proverse Roll)

$$\boxed{C_{l_{\delta_r}} = \frac{\partial C_l}{\partial \delta_r} > 0 \text{ (usually)}}$$

**Physical mechanism of Proverse Roll:**

1. Rudder deflects RIGHT ($\delta_r > 0$ is left, so let's say $\delta_r < 0$ for right yaw)
2. Aircraft yaws nose-right
3. The left wing (going forward) now moves faster through the air → more lift
4. The right wing (going backward) moves slower → less lift
5. Aircraft rolls LEFT (toward right/inside of yaw) → **Proverse Roll!**

Alternately: The side force on the vertical tail acts at some height above the CG, creating a rolling moment.

$$C_{l_{\delta_r}} \approx \eta_v \frac{S_v}{S} a_v \tau_r \frac{z_v}{b}$$

Where $z_v$ = vertical distance from CG to vertical tail aerodynamic center.

> 🎯 **Mnemonic: "RUDDER ROLLS you the SAME way it yaws you"** (for proverse roll)
> - Yaw right with right rudder → Roll right (proverse) → positive $C_{l_{\delta_r}}$

### 4.4 Engine-Out (OEI) Rudder Authority

For an engine failure (One Engine Inoperative), the rudder must balance the asymmetric thrust moment:

$$C_{n_{\text{asym thrust}}} + C_{n_{\delta_r}} \delta_r = 0$$
$$\delta_r = -\frac{T \cdot y_e}{q_\infty S b C_{n_{\delta_r}}}$$

**Minimum Control Speed $V_{mc}$:** The lowest speed at which the rudder has enough authority to maintain directional control:

$$\boxed{V_{mc} = \sqrt{\frac{2 T_{\max} y_e}{\rho S b |C_{n_{\delta_r}}| \delta_{r,\max}}}}$$

## 📐 SECTION 5 — Complete Control Derivative Master Table

### 5.1 Primary Control Derivatives

| Derivative | Formula | Sign | Physical Meaning | Key Feature |
|------------|---------|------|------------------|--------------|
| $C_{m_{\delta_e}}$ | $-\eta_t V_H a_t \tau_e$ | **< 0** always | Elevator pitch control | HIGHEST GATE priority |
| $C_{L_{\delta_e}}$ | $\eta_t (S_t/S) a_t \tau_e$ | **> 0** always | Elevator lift contribution | Used in trim calc |
| $C_{l_{\delta_a}}$ | $-\frac{a_w \tau_a}{Sb}\int cy\,dy$ | **< 0** always | Aileron roll control | Strip theory |
| $C_{n_{\delta_r}}$ | $-\eta_v V_v a_v \tau_r$ | **< 0** always | Rudder yaw control | OEI sizing |
| $C_{y_{\delta_r}}$ | $+\eta_v (S_v/S) a_v \tau_r$ | **> 0** always | Rudder side force | Crosswind landing |

### 5.2 Cross-Control Derivatives (Coupling)

| Derivative | Formula (approx.) | Sign | Physical Effect |
|------------|-------------------|------|-----------------|
| $C_{n_{\delta_a}}$ | $\approx$ negative | **< 0** usually | **Adverse yaw** from ailerons |
| $C_{l_{\delta_r}}$ | $\eta_v (S_v/S) a_v \tau_r (z_v/b)$ | **> 0** usually | **Proverse roll** from rudder |

### 5.3 Control Derivative Sign Summary: "The Rule of Control"

> **"Everything that controls is NEGATIVE, except side force and proverse roll"**

| Control Axis | Control Surface | Moment Derivative | Sign | Exception |
|-------------|----------------|-------------------|------|----------|
| Pitch | Elevator $\delta_e$ | $C_{m_{\delta_e}}$ | $-$ | $C_{L_{\delta_e}} > 0$ |
| Roll | Aileron $\delta_a$ | $C_{l_{\delta_a}}$ | $-$ | - |
| Yaw | Rudder $\delta_r$ | $C_{n_{\delta_r}}$ | $-$ | $C_{y_{\delta_r}} > 0$, $C_{l_{\delta_r}} > 0$ |

## 📐 SECTION 6 — Control Power and Aircraft Design

### 6.1 What Determines Control Power?

**For Elevator ($|C_{m_{\delta_e}}|$):**
$$|C_{m_{\delta_e}}| \uparrow \text{ when:}$$
- Tail volume $V_H \uparrow$ (bigger tail OR longer moment arm)
- Elevator chord ratio $c_f/c \uparrow$ (larger $\tau_e$)
- Tail efficiency $\eta_t \uparrow$
- Tail lift slope $a_t \uparrow$ (higher AR tail)

**For Rudder ($|C_{n_{\delta_r}}|$):**
$$|C_{n_{\delta_r}}| \uparrow \text{ when:}$$
- Vertical tail volume $V_v \uparrow$
- Rudder chord ratio $c_f/c \uparrow$ (larger $\tau_r$)

### 6.2 The Control Authority Triangle

```
     HIGH STABILITY (large Kn)
            |
            |  Aircraft is VERY stable
            |  but needs large delta_e to trim
            |  (poor maneuverability)
            |
   More Cm_alpha <-----------+----------->
   (more stable)              |          Less Cm_alpha
                               |          (less stable)
                               |
                          LOW STABILITY
                     Aircraft needs small delta_e
                     but may be marginally stable
                     (good maneuverability)
```

**Key design balance:** $K_n$ (stability) vs. maneuverability.

### 6.3 Trim Elevator Deflection vs. CG Position

$$\frac{d\delta_{e,\text{trim}}}{dh} = -\frac{C_{L_\alpha}}{C_{m_{\delta_e}}} = \frac{C_{L_\alpha}}{|C_{m_{\delta_e}}|} > 0$$

**Meaning:** Moving CG AFT (increasing $h$) → need LESS nose-down elevator to trim (less negative $\delta_e$).

### 6.4 Elevator for Trim at Different Speeds

For level flight at different airspeeds (different $C_L$):
$$\delta_{e,\text{trim}} = -\frac{C_{m_0}}{C_{m_{\delta_e}}} - \frac{C_{m_\alpha}}{C_{m_{\delta_e}} \cdot C_{L_\alpha}} C_L$$

Since $C_{m_\alpha} < 0$ and $C_{m_{\delta_e}} < 0$:
- At LOW speeds (HIGH $C_L$): more UP elevator (positive $\delta_e$)
- At HIGH speeds (LOW $C_L$): less elevator required

## 📐 SECTION 7 — Key Formulas Summary

### 7.1 Elevator Control Formulas

$$\boxed{C_{m_{\delta_e}} = -\eta_t V_H a_t \tau_e}$$
$$\boxed{C_{L_{\delta_e}} = \eta_t \frac{S_t}{S} a_t \tau_e}$$
$$\boxed{\delta_{e,\text{trim}} = -\frac{C_{m_0} + C_{m_\alpha} \alpha}{C_{m_{\delta_e}}}}$$
$$\boxed{\frac{d\delta_e}{dC_L}\bigg|_{\text{trim}} = -\frac{C_{m_\alpha}}{C_{L_\alpha} C_{m_{\delta_e}}} = \frac{K_n C_{L_\alpha}}{C_{L_\alpha} |C_{m_{\delta_e}}|} = \frac{K_n}{|C_{m_{\delta_e}}|/C_{L_\alpha}}}$$

### 7.2 Aileron Control Formulas

$$\boxed{C_{l_{\delta_a}} = -\frac{2 a_w \tau_a}{Sb} \int_{y_1}^{y_2} c(y) \cdot y \, dy}$$

**Rectangular wing shortcut:**
$$\boxed{C_{l_{\delta_a}} = -\frac{a_w \tau_a}{b^2}(y_2^2 - y_1^2)}$$

Non-dimensional ($\eta = 2y/b$):
$$\boxed{C_{l_{\delta_a}} = -\frac{a_w \tau_a}{4}(\eta_2^2 - \eta_1^2)}$$

### 7.3 Rudder Control Formulas

$$\boxed{C_{n_{\delta_r}} = -\eta_v V_v a_v \tau_r}$$
$$\boxed{C_{y_{\delta_r}} = +\eta_v \frac{S_v}{S} a_v \tau_r}$$
$$\boxed{C_{n_{\delta_r}} = -\frac{l_v}{b} C_{y_{\delta_r}}}$$

### 7.4 Aileron Reversal

$$\boxed{\text{Effectiveness} = 1 - \frac{q}{q_{\text{reversal}}}}$$

## 🔑 SECTION 8 — Mnemonics, Shortcuts & Tricks

### 8.1 The Universal Sign Rule for Control Derivatives

> **"All moment control derivatives are NEGATIVE"**
> - $C_{m_{\delta_e}} < 0$ (elevator → pitch moment)
> - $C_{l_{\delta_a}} < 0$ (aileron → roll moment)
> - $C_{n_{\delta_r}} < 0$ (rudder → yaw moment)
>
> **Exception:** $C_{L_{\delta_e}} > 0$ (lift due to elevator), $C_{y_{\delta_r}} > 0$ (side force due to rudder), $C_{l_{\delta_r}} > 0$ (proverse roll due to rudder)

### 8.2 Adverse Yaw Memory

> **"Aileron DOWN → Drag UP → Yaw OPPOSITE to Roll"**
> - Roll RIGHT → right aileron UP, left aileron DOWN
> - Left aileron DOWN → left wing has MORE induced drag
> - Aircraft yaws LEFT (OPPOSITE to intended right turn) → ADVERSE YAW

### 8.3 Proverse Roll Memory

> **"RUDDER and ROLL are FRIENDS — both go the SAME way"**
> - Push RIGHT rudder → yaw nose RIGHT → left wing moves faster → rolls RIGHT = Proverse!

### 8.4 Aileron Outboard Rule

> **"OUTBOARD ailerons = MORE powerful (but more reversal risk)"**
> - Moment arm $y$ is larger outboard → $C_{l_{\delta_a}} \propto y^2$
> - But wing tip is more flexible → more susceptible to aeroelastic reversal

### 8.5 Elevator Control Power Formula in 3 Steps

> Step 1: $\tau_e$ from chord ratio $c_e/c$
> Step 2: $C_{L_t,\delta_e} = a_t \cdot \tau_e$ (tail lift change per degree elevator)
> Step 3: $C_{m_{\delta_e}} = -\eta_t V_H \cdot C_{L_t,\delta_e}$ (multiply by tail leverage)

### 8.6 5-Second $C_{m_{\delta_e}}$ Shortcut

Given numerical data: $V_H$, $a_t$, $\tau_e$ (assume $\eta_t = 1.0$):
$$C_{m_{\delta_e}} \approx -V_H \times a_t \times \tau_e$$

**Example:** $V_H = 0.4$, $a_t = 4.0\text{ rad}^{-1}$, $\tau_e = 0.5$:
$$C_{m_{\delta_e}} = -0.4 \times 4.0 \times 0.5 = -0.80\text{ rad}^{-1}$$

### 8.7 The $\delta_e$-to-$C_L$ Slope Test

> **For a STABLE aircraft:** The slope $d\delta_e/dC_L$ is **NEGATIVE**
> (As $C_L$ increases i.e., speed decreases, pilot must pull back → up elevator → more negative $\delta_e$... wait!

Actual analysis:
- $d\delta_{e,trim}/dC_L = -(C_{m_\alpha})/(C_{L_\alpha} C_{m_{\delta_e}})$
- $C_{m_\alpha} < 0$ (stable), $C_{m_{\delta_e}} < 0$
- So: $d\delta_{e,trim}/dC_L = -(\text{negative})/(\text{positive} \times \text{negative}) = -(\text{negative})/(\text{negative}) = -1 \times \text{positive}$

Let me be precise:
$$\frac{d\delta_e}{dC_L} = -\frac{C_{m_\alpha}}{C_{L_\alpha} C_{m_{\delta_e}}}$$

For stable aircraft: $C_{m_\alpha} < 0$, $C_{L_\alpha} > 0$, $C_{m_{\delta_e}} < 0$:
$$\frac{d\delta_e}{dC_L} = -\frac{(-)}{(+)(-))} = -\frac{(-)}{(-)} = -(+) < 0$$

> 🎯 **STABLE aircraft:** $d\delta_e/dC_L < 0$ (need up elevator at higher $C_L$ = lower speed)
> **UNSTABLE aircraft:** $d\delta_e/dC_L > 0$ (opposite trend — pitch instability)

## 🔢 SECTION 9 — Solved GATE-Style Numerical Examples

### Example 1 — Elevator Control Power Calculation

**Problem:** Calculate $C_{m_{\delta_e}}$ and $C_{L_{\delta_e}}$ for an aircraft with:
- Horizontal tail area: $S_t = 8\text{ m}^2$, Wing area $S = 50\text{ m}^2$
- Tail moment arm: $l_t = 9\text{ m}$, Wing MAC: $\bar{c} = 2.5\text{ m}$
- Tail lift slope: $a_t = 4.2\text{ rad}^{-1}$
- Elevator chord ratio: $c_e/c = 0.30$ (gives $\tau_e = 0.52$)
- Tail efficiency: $\eta_t = 0.90$

**Solution:**

**Step 1: Horizontal Tail Volume Ratio:**
$$V_H = \frac{S_t l_t}{S \bar{c}} = \frac{8 \times 9}{50 \times 2.5} = \frac{72}{125} = 0.576$$

**Step 2: Elevator Control Power:**
$$C_{m_{\delta_e}} = -\eta_t V_H a_t \tau_e = -0.90 \times 0.576 \times 4.2 \times 0.52$$
$$= -0.90 \times 0.576 \times 2.184 = -0.90 \times 1.258 = -1.132\text{ rad}^{-1}$$
$$\boxed{C_{m_{\delta_e}} = -1.132\text{ rad}^{-1} = -0.01976\text{ deg}^{-1}}$$

**Step 3: Lift Due to Elevator:**
$$C_{L_{\delta_e}} = \eta_t \frac{S_t}{S} a_t \tau_e = 0.90 \times \frac{8}{50} \times 4.2 \times 0.52$$
$$= 0.90 \times 0.16 \times 2.184 = 0.314\text{ rad}^{-1}$$
$$\boxed{C_{L_{\delta_e}} = +0.314\text{ rad}^{-1} = +0.00548\text{ deg}^{-1}}$$

**Verification:** $C_{m_{\delta_e}} = -(l_t/\bar{c}) \cdot C_{L_{\delta_e}} \cdot (S_t/S)/(S_t/S)$
$$\approx -(9/2.5) \times 0.314 \times (8/50)/(8/50)... $$

Quick check: $-V_H \cdot C_{L_{\delta_e}} / (S_t/S) = -0.576 \times 0.314/0.16 = -1.131$ ✅

### Example 2 — Trim Elevator Deflection

**Problem:** For the aircraft in Example 1, additional data:
- $C_{m_0} = +0.10$, $C_{m_\alpha} = -0.80\text{ rad}^{-1}$
- Aircraft is at $\alpha_{\text{trim}} = 6^\circ = 0.1047\text{ rad}$

Find: (a) Trim elevator deflection in degrees.
(b) Does the aircraft need more or less elevator if $C_{m_0}$ increases?

**Solution:**

**(a) Trim Elevator:**
$$\delta_{e,\text{trim}} = -\frac{C_{m_0} + C_{m_\alpha} \alpha_{\text{trim}}}{C_{m_{\delta_e}}}$$
$$= -\frac{0.10 + (-0.80)(0.1047)}{-1.132}$$
$$= -\frac{0.10 - 0.0838}{-1.132}$$
$$= -\frac{0.0162}{-1.132} = +0.01431\text{ rad}$$
$$\boxed{\delta_{e,\text{trim}} = +0.820^\circ \text{ (trailing edge DOWN — slightly nose-down elevator)}}$$

**(b) Effect of $C_{m_0}$:**
If $C_{m_0}$ increases (more positive), numerator increases→ $\delta_{e,trim}$ must become MORE negative (UP elevator).
Physically: If aircraft naturally wants to pitch up more (higher $C_{m_0}$), pilot must push forward more.

### Example 3 — Aileron Control Power (Strip Theory)

**Problem:** Calculate $C_{l_{\delta_a}}$ for an aircraft with:
- Wing: $S = 30\text{ m}^2$, $b = 15\text{ m}$, constant chord (rectangular wing)
- Wing lift slope: $a_w = 5.0\text{ rad}^{-1}$
- Aileron effectiveness: $\tau_a = 0.50$
- Aileron span: $y_1 = 4.0\text{ m}$ to $y_2 = 7.0\text{ m}$ from center

**Solution:**

**Step 1: Wing chord** (rectangular):
$c = S/b = 30/15 = 2.0\text{ m}$

**Step 2: Strip theory integration:**
$$C_{l_{\delta_a}} = -\frac{2 a_w \tau_a}{Sb} \int_{y_1}^{y_2} c \cdot y \, dy$$
$$= -\frac{2 \times 5.0 \times 0.50}{30 \times 15} \times c \int_{4.0}^{7.0} y \, dy$$
$$= -\frac{5.0}{450} \times 2.0 \times \left[\frac{y^2}{2}\right]_{4.0}^{7.0}$$
$$= -\frac{5.0}{450} \times 2.0 \times \frac{1}{2}(49 - 16)$$
$$= -\frac{5.0}{450} \times 2.0 \times 16.5$$
$$= -\frac{5.0 \times 33}{450} = -\frac{165}{450}$$
$$\boxed{C_{l_{\delta_a}} = -0.3667\text{ rad}^{-1}}$$

**Verification using non-dimensional form:**
$\eta_1 = 2(4.0)/15 = 0.533$, $\eta_2 = 2(7.0)/15 = 0.933$
$$C_{l_{\delta_a}} = -\frac{a_w \tau_a}{4}(\eta_2^2 - \eta_1^2) = -\frac{5.0 \times 0.50}{4}(0.871 - 0.284) = -0.625 \times 0.587 = -0.367 \approx -0.367 \text{ rad}^{-1}$$ ✅

### Example 4 — Rudder Control Power & Engine-Out

**Problem:** An aircraft has:
- Vertical tail: $S_v = 5\text{ m}^2$, $l_v = 8\text{ m}$, $a_v = 3.5\text{ rad}^{-1}$, $\tau_r = 0.45$
- Wing: $S = 40\text{ m}^2$, $b = 20\text{ m}$
- Engine thrust: $T = 60\text{ kN}$ per engine, lateral arm $y_e = 3.0\text{ m}$
- Max rudder: $\delta_{r,\max} = 25^\circ = 0.4363\text{ rad}$
- Sea-level density: $\rho = 1.225\text{ kg/m}^3$

Find: (a) $C_{n_{\delta_r}}$, (b) $C_{y_{\delta_r}}$, (c) Minimum control speed $V_{mc}$.

**Solution:**

**(a) $C_{n_{\delta_r}}$:**
$$V_v = \frac{S_v l_v}{S b} = \frac{5 \times 8}{40 \times 20} = \frac{40}{800} = 0.050$$
$$C_{n_{\delta_r}} = -V_v \cdot a_v \cdot \tau_r = -0.050 \times 3.5 \times 0.45 = -0.07875\text{ rad}^{-1}$$
$$\boxed{C_{n_{\delta_r}} = -0.0788\text{ rad}^{-1} = -0.001375\text{ deg}^{-1}}$$

**(b) $C_{y_{\delta_r}}$:**
$$C_{y_{\delta_r}} = \frac{S_v}{S} a_v \tau_r = \frac{5}{40} \times 3.5 \times 0.45 = 0.125 \times 1.575 = +0.197\text{ rad}^{-1}$$
$$\boxed{C_{y_{\delta_r}} = +0.197\text{ rad}^{-1}}$$

Verification: $C_{n_{\delta_r}} = -(l_v/b) C_{y_{\delta_r}} = -(8/20)(0.197) = -0.0788$ ✅

**(c) Minimum Control Speed:**
$$V_{mc} = \sqrt{\frac{2 T_{\max} y_e}{\rho S b |C_{n_{\delta_r}}| \delta_{r,\max}}}$$
$$= \sqrt{\frac{2 \times 60000 \times 3.0}{1.225 \times 40 \times 20 \times 0.0788 \times 0.4363}}$$
$$= \sqrt{\frac{360000}{1.225 \times 40 \times 20 \times 0.03438}}$$
$$= \sqrt{\frac{360000}{33.77}} = \sqrt{10661} = 103.3\text{ m/s}$$
$$\boxed{V_{mc} \approx 103.3\text{ m/s} = 200.9\text{ knots} \approx 372\text{ km/h}}$$

### Example 5 — Trim Analysis: Elevator Travel for CG Range

**Problem:** An aircraft has:
- $C_{m_0} = +0.08$, $C_{m_\alpha} = -0.60\text{ rad}^{-1}$ (at forward CG, $h_f = 0.25$)
- $C_{m_\alpha} = -0.20\text{ rad}^{-1}$ (at aft CG, $h_a = 0.37$)
- $C_{m_{\delta_e}} = -0.90\text{ rad}^{-1}$, $C_{L_\alpha} = 5.0\text{ rad}^{-1}$
- CG range: $h$ from 0.25 to 0.37 (in MAC fractions)
- Required $C_L$ range: 0.3 (cruise) to 1.5 (approach)

Find: Maximum elevator travel required.

**Solution:**

**At each CG, find $\delta_e$ range for $C_L$ from 0.3 to 1.5:**

$$\alpha = \frac{C_L - C_{L_0}}{C_{L_\alpha}} \approx \frac{C_L}{5.0}\text{ rad (assuming }C_{L_0} \approx 0)$$

**Forward CG ($h_f = 0.25$, $C_{m_\alpha} = -0.60$):**

At $C_L = 1.5$ ($\alpha = 0.30\text{ rad}$, approach):
$$\delta_{e} = -\frac{0.08 + (-0.60)(0.30)}{-0.90} = -\frac{0.08 - 0.18}{-0.90} = -\frac{-0.10}{-0.90} = -0.111\text{ rad} = -6.37^\circ$$

At $C_L = 0.3$ ($\alpha = 0.06\text{ rad}$, cruise):
$$\delta_{e} = -\frac{0.08 + (-0.60)(0.06)}{-0.90} = -\frac{0.08 - 0.036}{-0.90} = -\frac{0.044}{-0.90} = +0.049\text{ rad} = +2.80^\circ$$

**Range at forward CG:** From $-6.37^\circ$ to $+2.80^\circ$ = total $9.17^\circ$ travel.

**Aft CG ($h_a = 0.37$, $C_{m_\alpha} = -0.20$):**

At $C_L = 1.5$:
$$\delta_{e} = -\frac{0.08 + (-0.20)(0.30)}{-0.90} = -\frac{0.08 - 0.06}{-0.90} = -\frac{0.02}{-0.90} = +0.0222\text{ rad} = +1.27^\circ$$

At $C_L = 0.3$:
$$\delta_{e} = -\frac{0.08 + (-0.20)(0.06)}{-0.90} = -\frac{0.08 - 0.012}{-0.90} = -\frac{0.068}{-0.90} = +0.0756\text{ rad} = +4.33^\circ$$

$$\boxed{\text{Maximum elevator travel required} = 9.17^\circ \text{ (set by forward CG condition)}}$$

> **Lesson:** Forward CG requires MORE elevator travel (harder to rotate nose up for approach).
> Aft CG requires LESS elevator travel but gives less stability.

## ⚠️ SECTION 10 — Critical GATE Exam Traps

### Trap 1: Sign of elevator deflection vs. pitch direction
> Positive $\delta_e$ (trailing edge DOWN) = nose-DOWN moment = $C_m < 0$.
> To pitch nose UP, need NEGATIVE $\delta_e$ (trailing edge UP = elevator up = pull back stick)!
> Students confuse "up elevator" (nose up) with positive $\delta_e$ (which is actually nose DOWN)!

### Trap 2: $\tau_e$ is NOT equal to chord ratio $c_f/c$
> $\tau_e$ is a non-linear function of $c_f/c$, always LESS than 1.0.
> Don't substitute $\tau_e = c_f/c$ directly! Use the chart value or given value.
> (For GATE, $\tau_e$ will be given or implied as a number like 0.45, 0.50, 0.55.)

### Trap 3: Confusing $C_{m_{\delta_e}}$ with $C_{L_{\delta_e}}$
> $C_{m_{\delta_e}}$ is the PITCHING MOMENT change (negative!)
> $C_{L_{\delta_e}}$ is the LIFT change (positive!)
> Many students swap these in trim equations!

### Trap 4: Aileron strip integration limits
> The integration goes from $y_1$ to $y_2$ (inboard to outboard edge of aileron), NOT from 0 to $b/2$!
> Only the aileron region contributes to $C_{l_{\delta_a}}$.

### Trap 5: Adverse yaw direction
> To roll RIGHT: right aileron UP, left aileron DOWN. The DOWN aileron creates MORE induced drag.
> So the LEFT wing (down aileron) has more drag → aircraft yaws LEFT = opposite to turn = ADVERSE.
> **"The yaw opposes the roll"** — adverse yaw is always opposite to the intended rolling direction.

### Trap 6: Aileron reversal gives ZERO effectiveness, not infinite
> At reversal speed, $C_{l_{\delta_a,\text{actual}}} = 0$ (zero effectiveness).
> Above reversal speed, $C_{l_{\delta_a,\text{actual}}}$ becomes positive (reversed!)
> It does NOT blow up to infinity.

## 🧠 MASTER MENTAL MODEL — What is Stick-Fixed Longitudinal Stability?

> **CORE CONCEPT:** **Stick-Fixed Static Stability** evaluates whether an aircraft, with its **elevator held rigid/locked** by the pilot or control system, will naturally generate a pitch moment that restores it to equilibrium after an angle of attack ($\alpha$) disturbance.

```
       Disturbance (+Δα) ---> Pitch UP (Nose Up)
                                  │
                                  ▼
                    Restoring Moment (-ΔCm) <--- Pitch DOWN (Nose Down)
                                  │
                                  ▼
                         AIRCRAFT IS STABLE! (dCm/dα < 0)
```

### The Two Golden Rules of Longitudinal Static Stability & Trim

To fly in steady level flight safely, an aircraft MUST satisfy **TWO INDEPENDENT CONDITIONS**:

$$\begin{aligned}
\mathbf{1.\; Trim\; Condition\; (Equilibrium):} & \quad C_{m,cg} = 0 \quad \text{at desired } \alpha_{\text{trim}} \\
\mathbf{2.\; Static\; Stability\; Criterion:} & \quad C_{m_\alpha} = \frac{d C_{m,cg}}{d\alpha} < 0 \quad \text{(Negative Slope)}
\end{aligned}$$

> 🎯 **MASTER GRAPH: The $C_m - \alpha$ Diagram**
> 
> ```
>         C_m (Pitching Moment)
>          ^
>   +C_m0  | \  <--- Stable Aircraft (C_m0 > 0 AND C_m_alpha < 0)
>   (Nose  |  \
>    Up)   |   \  Trim Point (C_m = 0)
>   ───────┼────\─────────────────> α (AoA)
>          |     \   α_trim
>   -C_m   |      \
>   (Nose  |       \
>   Down)  v
> ```
> - **y-intercept ($C_{m_0}$):** Must be **POSITIVE** ($C_{m_0} > 0$) so trim occurs at a positive angle of attack ($\alpha_{\text{trim}} > 0$).
> - **Slope ($C_{m_\alpha}$):** Must be **NEGATIVE** ($C_{m_\alpha} < 0$) for restoring capability.

## 📐 SECTION 1 — Geometry, References & Coordinate Conventions

### 1.1 Non-Dimensional Distance Representation ($h$)

All chordwise distances are normalized by the **Mean Aerodynamic Chord (MAC, $\bar{c}$)**, measured positive rearward from the **Leading Edge of MAC (LEMAC)**:

$$\boxed{h = \frac{x_{cg}}{\bar{c}}, \quad h_{ac} = \frac{x_{ac}}{\bar{c}}, \quad h_n = \frac{x_n}{\bar{c}}}$$

```
  LEMAC
    │<───────── h · c_bar ─────────>│ CG
    │<────── h_ac · c_bar ───────>│ AC_wb
    │<────────────── h_n · c_bar ──────────────>│ Neutral Point (NP)
    ├───────────────────────────────────────────┤
    0                                           1.0 (Trailing Edge)
```

| Symbol | Definition | Typical Value |
|--------|------------|---------------|
| $h$ | CG position as fraction of $\bar{c}$ | $0.15 - 0.35$ |
| $h_{ac,wb}$ | Aerodynamic Center of Wing-Body as fraction of $\bar{c}$ | $\approx 0.25$ (Subsonic) |
| $h_n$ | Stick-Fixed Neutral Point as fraction of $\bar{c}$ | $0.35 - 0.50$ |

## 📐 SECTION 2 — Complete Pitching Moment Derivation (Component by Component)

### 2.1 Wing-Body Pitching Moment About CG

The pitching moment coefficient of the wing-body about the aircraft CG ($C_{m,cg,\text{wb}}$) is:

$$C_{m,cg,\text{wb}} = C_{m,ac,\text{wb}} + C_{L,\text{wb}} \left(h - h_{ac,\text{wb}}\right) \cos\alpha + C_{D,\text{wb}} \left(h - h_{ac,\text{wb}}\right) \sin\alpha + \dots$$

For small angles of attack ($\cos\alpha \approx 1$, $\sin\alpha \approx 0$):

$$\boxed{C_{m,cg,\text{wb}} = C_{m,ac,\text{wb}} + C_{L,\text{wb}} \left(h - h_{ac,\text{wb}}\right)}$$

Differentiating with respect to $\alpha$:

$$\boxed{\left(C_{m_\alpha}\right)_{\text{wb}} = C_{L_{\alpha,\text{wb}}} \left(h - h_{ac,\text{wb}}\right)}$$

> 💡 **Key Insight:**
> - If CG is **aft** of wing-body AC ($h > h_{ac,\text{wb}}$) $\implies \left(C_{m_\alpha}\right)_{\text{wb}} > 0$ (**DESTABILIZING**).
> - Since wing AC is usually near $0.25\bar{c}$, the wing-body alone is almost always **statically unstable** for realistic aft CG locations!

---

### 2.2 Horizontal Tail Pitching Moment Contribution

The horizontal tail produces a lift force $L_t$ at a distance $l_t$ aft of the aircraft CG.

#### A. Tail Local Angle of Attack ($\alpha_t$)
The angle of attack experienced by the horizontal tail is modified by wing downwash $\epsilon$ and incidence angles:

$$\boxed{\alpha_t = \alpha_w - i_w + i_t - \epsilon}$$

Where:
- $\alpha_w$ = Wing angle of attack
- $i_w$ = Wing incidence angle relative to fuselage centerline
- $i_t$ = Horizontal tail incidence angle relative to fuselage centerline
- $\epsilon$ = Downwash angle produced by wing at the tail location

**Downwash Model (Linearized):**
$$\epsilon = \epsilon_0 + \frac{d\epsilon}{d\alpha} \cdot \alpha$$

Substituting the downwash model into $\alpha_t$:
$$\alpha_t = \alpha \left(1 - \frac{d\epsilon}{d\alpha}\right) + (i_t - i_w) - \epsilon_0$$

#### B. Tail Moment Coefficient
The pitching moment produced by the horizontal tail about CG is:

$$M_t = -L_t \cdot l_t = -q_t S_t C_{L_t} l_t$$

Non-dimensionalizing by dividing by $q_\infty S \bar{c}$:

$$C_{m,t} = -\frac{q_t}{q_\infty} \cdot \frac{S_t l_t}{S \bar{c}} \cdot C_{L_t} = -\eta_t \cdot V_H \cdot C_{L_t}$$

Where:
- $\eta_t = \frac{q_t}{q_\infty} = \frac{\frac{1}{2}\rho_t V_t^2}{\frac{1}{2}\rho_\infty V_\infty^2}$ = **Tail Dynamic Pressure Ratio** (typically $0.85 - 0.95$ due to fuselage boundary layer and wing wake)
- $V_H = \frac{S_t l_t}{S \bar{c}}$ = **Horizontal Tail Volume Ratio**

Expressing tail lift coefficient $C_{L_t} = a_t \alpha_t + a_t \tau_e \delta_e$:

$$\boxed{C_{m,t} = -\eta_t V_H a_t \left[\alpha \left(1 - \frac{d\epsilon}{d\alpha}\right) + (i_t - i_w) - \epsilon_0 + \tau_e \delta_e\right]}$$

Differentiating with respect to $\alpha$:

$$\boxed{\left(C_{m_\alpha}\right)_t = -\eta_t V_H a_t \left(1 - \frac{d\epsilon}{d\alpha}\right)}$$

> 🛡️ **Key Insight:** Since $(1 - d\epsilon/d\alpha) > 0$, the tail contribution $\left(C_{m_\alpha}\right)_t$ is **ALWAYS NEGATIVE** (STABILIZING). The horizontal tail is the primary stabilizing component of the aircraft!

---

### 2.3 Total Aircraft Pitching Moment Equation (Stick-Fixed)

Combining Wing-Body and Tail contributions:

$$\boxed{C_{m,cg} = C_{m,ac,\text{wb}} + C_{L_{\alpha,\text{wb}}}\alpha\left(h - h_{ac,\text{wb}}\right) - \eta_t V_H a_t \left[\alpha\left(1 - \frac{d\epsilon}{d\alpha}\right) + (i_t - i_w) - \epsilon_0\right] + C_{m_{\delta_e}}\delta_e}$$

This can be restructured into standard linear form:

$$\boxed{C_{m,cg} = C_{m_0} + C_{m_\alpha} \cdot \alpha + C_{m_{\delta_e}} \cdot \delta_e}$$

Where:
$$\boxed{C_{m_0} = C_{m,ac,\text{wb}} + \eta_t V_H a_t \left(i_w - i_t + \epsilon_0\right)}$$

$$\boxed{C_{m_\alpha} = C_{L_{\alpha,\text{wb}}}\left(h - h_{ac,\text{wb}}\right) - \eta_t V_H a_t \left(1 - \frac{d\epsilon}{d\alpha}\right)}$$

---

## 📐 SECTION 3 — Neutral Point ($h_n$) & Static Margin ($K_n$) [Stick-Fixed]

### 3.1 Derivation of the Neutral Point ($h_n$)

The **Stick-Fixed Neutral Point ($h_n$)** is defined as the CG location where total aircraft pitch stiffness is zero ($C_{m_\alpha} = 0$).

Setting $C_{m_\alpha} = 0$:

$$C_{L_{\alpha,\text{wb}}}\left(h_n - h_{ac,\text{wb}}\right) - \eta_t V_H a_t \left(1 - \frac{d\epsilon}{d\alpha}\right) = 0$$

Solving for $h_n$:

$$\boxed{h_n = h_{ac,\text{wb}} + \frac{\eta_t V_H a_t}{C_{L_{\alpha,\text{wb}}}} \left(1 - \frac{d\epsilon}{d\alpha}\right)}$$

Or using total aircraft lift curve slope $C_{L_\alpha} \approx C_{L_{\alpha,\text{wb}}} + \eta_t \frac{S_t}{S} a_t (1 - d\epsilon/d\alpha)$:

$$\boxed{h_n = h_{ac,\text{wb}} + \frac{V_H a_t}{C_{L_\alpha}/ \eta_t} \left(1 - \frac{d\epsilon}{d\alpha}\right)}$$

> 🎯 **GOLDEN RULE FOR NEUTRAL POINT:**
> The Neutral Point $h_n$ is the **Aerodynamic Center of the Complete Aircraft**. It depends **ONLY on aerodynamic geometry** ($S_t, S, l_t, \bar{c}, a_w, a_t, d\epsilon/d\alpha$), and is **INDEPENDENT of CG location ($h$)**!

---

### 3.2 Static Margin ($K_n$) — Stick-Fixed

The **Static Margin ($K_n$)** is the non-dimensional distance between the Neutral Point and the CG:

$$\boxed{K_n = h_n - h}$$

Substituting $h_n$ into $C_{m_\alpha}$:

$$\begin{aligned}
C_{m_\alpha} &= C_{L_\alpha} (h - h_{ac,\text{wb}}) - \eta_t V_H a_t \left(1 - \frac{d\epsilon}{d\alpha}\right) \\
&= C_{L_\alpha} \left(h - \left[h_{ac,\text{wb}} + \frac{\eta_t V_H a_t}{C_{L_\alpha}}\left(1 - \frac{d\epsilon}{d\alpha}\right)\right]\right) \\
&= C_{L_\alpha} (h - h_n)
\end{aligned}$$

$$\boxed{C_{m_\alpha} = -C_{L_\alpha} \cdot K_n \quad \iff \quad K_n = -\frac{C_{m_\alpha}}{C_{L_\alpha}} = -\frac{dC_m}{dC_L}}$$

| Condition | Static Margin ($K_n = h_n - h$) | Pitch Stiffness ($C_{m_\alpha}$) | Stability State |
|-----------|----------------------------------|-----------------------------------|-----------------|
| $h < h_n$ | **$K_n > 0$ (Positive)** | **$C_{m_\alpha} < 0$ (Negative)** | **Statically Stable ✅** |
| $h = h_n$ | **$K_n = 0$ (Zero)** | **$C_{m_\alpha} = 0$ (Zero)** | **Neutrally Stable ➖** |
| $h > h_n$ | **$K_n < 0$ (Negative)** | **$C_{m_\alpha} > 0$ (Positive)** | **Statically Unstable ❌** |

```
                 STABLE                NEUTRAL        UNSTABLE
       <─────────────────────────>        │       <───────────────>
  Front of Wing               CG          NP                      Tail
 ───┼─────────────────────────┼───────────┼────────────────────────┼───> x
   LE                         h          h_n                      TE
```

## 📐 SECTION 4 — Component Contributions & Factors Affecting $h_n$

### 4.1 Summary of Component Effects on Stability

$$\boxed{h_n = h_{ac,w} + \Delta h_{n,f} + \Delta h_{n,t} + \Delta h_{n,p}}$$

```
 Component           Contribution to Cm_alpha      Shift of Neutral Point (h_n)
 ─────────           ────────────────────────      ────────────────────────────
 Wing Alone          Destabilizing (if CG aft AC)  Base reference (h_ac ≈ 0.25)
 Fuselage            DESTABILIZING (+ΔCm_α)        Shifts h_n FORWARD (-Δh_n)
 Horizontal Tail     STABILIZING   (-ΔCm_α)        Shifts h_n AFT     (+Δh_n)
 Engine Power        DESTABILIZING (+ΔCm_α)        Shifts h_n FORWARD (-Δh_n)
```

#### A. Fuselage Contribution
The fuselage behaves aerodynamically like a slender body at an angle of attack. It produces an asymmetric pressure distribution creating a **destabilizing pitching moment**:

$$\Delta C_{m_\alpha,f} = \frac{k_f \cdot w_f^2 \cdot L_f}{S \bar{c}} > 0$$

This shifts the overall neutral point **FORWARD** (reduces stability) by roughly $0.05 - 0.10 \bar{c}$.

#### B. Downwash Gradient Effect ($d\epsilon/d\alpha$)
The wing downwash reduces the tail's effective AoA change:
- For an elliptical wing / planar wing: $\frac{d\epsilon}{d\alpha} \approx \frac{2 C_{L_{\alpha,w}}}{\pi AR_w e}$
- Higher Aspect Ratio $AR_w \implies \frac{d\epsilon}{d\alpha} \downarrow \implies$ **Tail becomes MORE effective** $\implies h_n$ shifts AFT!

#### C. Power & Propeller Effects
1. **Propeller Normal Force:** A operating propeller in inclined flow acts like a small wing producing a normal force $N_p$. For tractor propellers (front), this normal force is ahead of CG $\implies$ **DESTABILIZING** (shifts $h_n$ forward).
2. **Slipstream Effect:** Increases dynamic pressure over the tail ($\eta_t > 1.0$) $\implies$ **STABILIZING**.
3. **Thrust Line Offset:** Direct moment $T \cdot z_p$.

## 📐 SECTION 5 — Elevator Trim & Control Requirements

### 5.1 Elevator Deflection to Trim ($\delta_{e,\text{trim}}$)

To trim the aircraft at a specific angle of attack $\alpha_{\text{trim}}$ (or $C_{L,\text{trim}}$), set $C_{m,cg} = 0$:

$$C_{m_0} + C_{m_\alpha} \alpha_{\text{trim}} + C_{m_{\delta_e}} \delta_{e,\text{trim}} = 0$$

$$\boxed{\delta_{e,\text{trim}} = -\frac{C_{m_0} + C_{m_\alpha} \alpha_{\text{trim}}}{C_{m_{\delta_e}}} = -\frac{C_{m_0}}{C_{m_{\delta_e}}} - \left(\frac{C_{m_\alpha}}{C_{m_{\delta_e}}}\right) \alpha_{\text{trim}}}$$

Expressed in terms of $C_L$:

$$\boxed{\delta_{e,\text{trim}} = -\frac{C_{m_0}}{C_{m_{\delta_e}}} + \frac{K_n}{C_{m_{\delta_e}} / C_{L_\alpha}} \cdot C_L}$$

---

### 5.2 The Elevator Trim Curve Slope ($\frac{d\delta_e}{dC_L}$)

Differentiating $\delta_{e,\text{trim}}$ with respect to $C_L$:

$$\boxed{\frac{d\delta_{e,\text{trim}}}{dC_L} = -\frac{C_{m_\alpha}}{C_{L_\alpha} C_{m_{\delta_e}}} = \frac{h - h_n}{C_{m_{\delta_e}}} = -\frac{K_n}{|C_{m_{\delta_e}}| / C_{L_\alpha}}}$$

Since $C_{m_{\delta_e}} < 0$ (always negative):

$$\boxed{\frac{d\delta_{e,\text{trim}}}{dC_L} < 0 \quad \text{for a STABILIZED aircraft!}}$$

```
  Elevator Deflection (δ_e)
     ^
  UP | \  <--- Stable Aircraft (dδ_e / dC_L < 0)
(-δ_e|  \
     |   \   Higher C_L (Lower Speed) requires MORE UP elevator!
  ───┼────\───────────────────────> C_L (or 1/V^2)
     |     \
DOWN |      \ <--- Unstable Aircraft (dδ_e / dC_L > 0)
(+δ_e|
```

> 🎯 **CRITICAL EXAM TRICK:**
> - To fly **slower** (higher $C_L$), a stable aircraft requires **MORE UP ELEVATOR** ($\delta_e$ becomes more negative).
> - The slope $\frac{d\delta_e}{dC_L}$ is a direct measurement of the static margin $K_n$!

---

### 5.3 Stick-Fixed Speed Stability ($\frac{d\delta_e}{dV}$)

In level flight, $L = W = \frac{1}{2} \rho_\infty V^2 S C_L \implies C_L = \frac{2W}{\rho_\infty S V^2}$.

Differentiating $C_L$ with respect to speed $V$:

$$\frac{dC_L}{dV} = -\frac{4W}{\rho_\infty S V^3} = -\frac{2 C_L}{V}$$

Using the chain rule for elevator deflection with respect to speed:

$$\boxed{\frac{d\delta_{e,\text{trim}}}{dV} = \frac{d\delta_{e,\text{trim}}}{dC_L} \cdot \frac{dC_L}{dV} = \left(\frac{h - h_n}{C_{m_{\delta_e}}}\right) \left(-\frac{2 C_L}{V}\right) = \frac{2 C_L}{V C_{m_{\delta_e}}} (h_n - h) = -\frac{2 C_L K_n}{V |C_{m_{\delta_e}}|}}$$

> ✈️ **PHYSICAL INTERPRETATION OF SPEED STABILITY:**
> - Since $K_n > 0$ for a stable aircraft and $C_{m_{\delta_e}} < 0$, $\frac{d\delta_{e,\text{trim}}}{dV} > 0$ in terms of algebraic value (or physically: **increasing speed requires pushing stick FORWARD / trailing edge DOWN elevator** $\delta_e > 0$).
> - **Stick-Fixed Speed Stability:** If airspeed increases due to a gust, the aircraft naturally pitches UP to slow down, restoring equilibrium speed!

## 📐 SECTION 6 — Alternative Configurations: Canard vs. Tailless

### 6.1 Canard Configuration

A Canard configuration features the horizontal control surface **AHEAD** of the main wing.

```
       Canard (S_c)               Wing (S)
        ┌──┐                        ┌──────┐
   ─────┴──┴────────────────────────┴──────┴─────> Flow
            |<────── l_c ──────────>|
```

1. **Tail Volume:** $V_c = \frac{S_c l_c}{S \bar{c}}$
2. **Neutral Point (Canard):**

$$\boxed{h_n = h_{ac,w} - \frac{\eta_c V_c a_c}{C_{L_\alpha}} \left(1 + \frac{d\epsilon_c}{d\alpha}\right)}$$

> ⚠️ **Notice the MINUS sign!** A front surface contributes **destabilizing** $C_{m_\alpha}$! To keep the canard aircraft stable ($h < h_n$), the CG must be placed **VERY FAR FORWARD** (ahead of the main wing AC).
> 
> 🛡️ **Canard Safety Rule:** The Canard MUST be loaded to stall BEFORE the main wing ($C_{L,c}$ reaches max first), causing the nose to drop automatically and preventing wing stall!

---

### 6.2 Tailless Aircraft / Flying Wings

Without a tail surface ($V_H = 0$), the neutral point is simply the wing-body AC: $h_n = h_{ac,wb} \approx 0.25$.

To achieve static stability AND trim at positive $C_L$:
1. **Reflexed Airfoil:** Airfoil trailing edge curves UP $\implies C_{m,ac} > 0$.
2. **Wing Sweep + Washout (Twist):** Swept-back wing with negative twist at wingtips ($\theta_{\text{tip}} < \theta_{\text{root}}$) ensures $C_{m_0} > 0$.

---

## 📐 SECTION 7 — Advanced Effects & CG Limits

### 7.1 Compressibility & Mach Shift of Neutral Point ($h_n$)

When accelerating from subsonic to supersonic speeds:
- **Subsonic ($M < 0.8$):** Aerodynamic center of wing-body is at $h_{ac} \approx 0.25\bar{c}$.
- **Supersonic ($M > 1.2$):** Aerodynamic center shifts aft to $h_{ac} \approx 0.50\bar{c}$.

$$\Delta h_n = h_{n,\text{supersonic}} - h_{n,\text{subsonic}} \approx +0.25 \bar{c} \quad \text{(AFT SHIFT)}$$

> ⚠️ **Tuck-Under Tendency:**
> - As the aircraft accelerates to supersonic speeds, $h_n$ shifts far aft, increasing $K_n = h_n - h$ dramatically!
> - The aircraft becomes **excessively stable**, creating a strong nose-down pitching moment ("Mach Tuck").
> - Modern supersonic fighters use **fuel transfer systems (fuel pumping aft)** to shift CG rearward in supersonic flight to reduce trim drag.

---

### 7.2 Ground Effect on Longitudinal Stability

When flying near the ground ($h_{\text{altitude}} / b < 0.5$):
1. **Downwash Reduction:** Wing vortex system is restricted by ground plane $\implies \frac{d\epsilon}{d\alpha} \downarrow$.
2. **Tail Stability Boost:** Lower $\frac{d\epsilon}{d\alpha} \implies \left(1 - \frac{d\epsilon}{d\alpha}\right) \uparrow \implies$ **Tail becomes MORE effective** $\implies h_n$ shifts **AFT** (increases static stability!).
3. **Nose-Down Trim Change:** Reduced downwash at tail reduces tail down-force $\implies \Delta C_{m,cg} < 0$ (nose pitches DOWN). Pilot must pull back on stick during landing flare!

---

### 7.3 Center of Gravity (CG) Limits

The permissible CG range for safe operation is bounded by two critical limits:

$$\boxed{h_{\text{forward limit}} \le h \le h_{\text{aft limit}}}$$

```
                FORWARD LIMIT                         AFT LIMIT
  <───────────────────┼───────────────────────────────────┼───────────────────>
               Max UP Elevator                     Neutral Point
              (Landing/Takeoff)                     (Stability Limit)
```

1. **Aft CG Limit ($h_{\text{aft}}$):** Set by the **Stick-Fixed Neutral Point ($h_n$)** (or Stick-Free Neutral Point $h_n^*$). Exceeding this makes the aircraft statically unstable ($K_n < 0$).
2. **Forward CG Limit ($h_{\text{fwd}}$):** Set by **Elevator Control Authority**. At forward CG, the aircraft is extremely stable ($K_n \uparrow\uparrow$), requiring maximum UP elevator deflection ($\delta_{e,\text{max up}}$) to rotate during takeoff or flare during landing.

$$\boxed{\text{Permissible CG Travel Range} = h_n - h_{\text{fwd limit}}}$$

## 🔑 SECTION 8 — Mnemonics, Shortcuts & Tricks

### 8.1 Master Rule of Thumb: "CG Ahead of NP = Stable"
> **"Front CG = Stable & Heavy Stick; Aft CG = Agle & Touchy Stick; Behind NP = Crash!"**

### 8.2 The $C_{m_0}$ & $C_{m_\alpha}$ Mnemonic: "Positive Intercept, Negative Slope"
> - **$C_{m_0} > 0$** (positive y-intercept $\implies$ nose wants to pitch UP at zero lift)
> - **$C_{m_\alpha} < 0$** (negative slope $\implies$ nose pitches DOWN when AoA increases)

### 8.3 Neutral Point 5-Second Shortcut
If given $C_{m_\alpha}$, $C_{L_\alpha}$, and current CG $h$:

$$\boxed{h_n = h - \frac{C_{m_\alpha}}{C_{L_\alpha}}}$$

### 8.4 Tail vs. Canard Neutral Point Shift
> - **Horizontal Tail (Aft):** Pushes $h_n$ **AFT** (increases stability).
> - **Canard (Forward):** Pushes $h_n$ **FORWARD** (decreases main wing stability contribution).
> - **Fuselage:** Pushes $h_n$ **FORWARD** (destabilizing).

## 🔢 SECTION 9 — Solved GATE-Style Numerical Examples

### Example 1 — Complete Neutral Point & Static Margin Calculation

**Problem:** An aircraft has the following characteristics:
- Wing area $S = 30\text{ m}^2$, Mean aerodynamic chord $\bar{c} = 2.0\text{ m}$
- Horizontal tail area $S_t = 6\text{ m}^2$, distance from wing AC to tail AC $l_t = 5.0\text{ m}$
- Wing-body AC position: $h_{ac,wb} = 0.25$
- Wing lift-curve slope: $C_{L_{\alpha,w}} = 4.8\text{ rad}^{-1}$
- Tail lift-curve slope: $a_t = 3.8\text{ rad}^{-1}$
- Tail efficiency: $\eta_t = 0.90$
- Downwash gradient: $\frac{d\epsilon}{d\alpha} = 0.35$
- Current CG location: $h = 0.30$

**Calculate:**
1. Horizontal tail volume ratio $V_H$.
2. Stick-fixed Neutral Point $h_n$.
3. Static margin $K_n$.
4. Pitch stiffness derivative $C_{m_\alpha}$.

**Solution:**

**Step 1: Tail Volume Ratio $V_H$**
$$V_H = \frac{S_t l_t}{S \bar{c}} = \frac{6.0 \times 5.0}{30 \times 2.0} = \frac{30}{60} = 0.50$$

**Step 2: Neutral Point $h_n$**
$$h_n = h_{ac,wb} + \frac{\eta_t V_H a_t}{C_{L_{\alpha,w}}} \left(1 - \frac{d\epsilon}{d\alpha}\right)$$
$$h_n = 0.25 + \frac{0.90 \times 0.50 \times 3.8}{4.8} (1 - 0.35)$$
$$h_n = 0.25 + \frac{1.71}{4.8} \times 0.65 = 0.25 + 0.35625 \times 0.65 = 0.25 + 0.23156 = 0.48156$$
$$\boxed{h_n \approx 0.4816 \quad (48.16\% \text{ MAC})}$$

**Step 3: Static Margin $K_n$**
$$K_n = h_n - h = 0.4816 - 0.30 = 0.1816$$
$$\boxed{K_n = +18.16\% \text{ MAC} \quad (\text{Statically Stable ✅})}$$

**Step 4: Pitch Stiffness $C_{m_\alpha}$**
$$C_{m_\alpha} = -C_{L_{\alpha,w}} \cdot K_n = -4.8 \times 0.1816 = -0.8717\text{ rad}^{-1}$$
$$\boxed{C_{m_\alpha} = -0.8717\text{ rad}^{-1} \quad (\text{Negative } \implies \text{Stable ✅})}$$

### Example 2 — Sizing Horizontal Tail for a Given Static Margin

**Problem:** A light aircraft designer wants a minimum static margin of $K_n = 10\% = 0.10$.
Given:
- $h = 0.32$, $h_{ac,wb} = 0.25$
- $C_{L_{\alpha,w}} = 5.0\text{ rad}^{-1}$, $a_t = 4.0\text{ rad}^{-1}$
- $\eta_t = 1.0$, $\frac{d\epsilon}{d\alpha} = 0.40$

Find the required horizontal tail volume ratio $V_H$.

**Solution:**

Target Neutral Point:
$$K_n = h_n - h \implies h_n = h + K_n = 0.32 + 0.10 = 0.42$$

Using Neutral Point formula:
$$h_n = h_{ac,wb} + \frac{\eta_t V_H a_t}{C_{L_{\alpha,w}}} \left(1 - \frac{d\epsilon}{d\alpha}\right)$$
$$0.42 = 0.25 + \frac{1.0 \times V_H \times 4.0}{5.0} (1 - 0.40)$$
$$0.17 = V_H \times 0.80 \times 0.60 = 0.48 V_H$$
$$V_H = \frac{0.17}{0.48} \approx 0.3542$$

$$\boxed{V_H = 0.354}$$

### Example 3 — Elevator Deflection Slope & CG Shift (GATE AE Classic)

**Problem:** For an aircraft, the slope of elevator deflection vs lift coefficient is $\frac{d\delta_e}{dC_L} = -0.15\text{ rad}$ at a CG position of $h_1 = 0.25$. When CG shifts to $h_2 = 0.35$, $\frac{d\delta_e}{dC_L} = -0.05\text{ rad}$.

Given $C_{m_{\delta_e}} = -1.0\text{ rad}^{-1}$, find:
1. Wing lift-curve slope $C_{L_\alpha}$.
2. The Stick-Fixed Neutral Point $h_n$.

**Solution:**

We know:
$$\frac{d\delta_e}{dC_L} = \frac{h - h_n}{C_{m_{\delta_e}}}$$

At CG position $h_1 = 0.25$:
$$-0.15 = \frac{0.25 - h_n}{-1.0} \implies 0.25 - h_n = 0.15 \implies h_n = 0.25 - 0.15 = 0.10 \text{ ??? Wait!}$$

Let's re-verify:
$$C_{m_{\delta_e}} \cdot \frac{d\delta_e}{dC_L} = h - h_n$$
$$(-1.0) \times (-0.15) = 0.15 = h_n - h_1 \implies h_n - 0.25 = 0.15 \implies h_n = 0.40$$

Check with second CG position $h_2 = 0.35$:
$$(-1.0) \times (-0.05) = 0.05 = h_n - h_2 \implies h_n = 0.35 + 0.05 = 0.40$$

$$\boxed{h_n = 0.40 \quad (40\% \text{ MAC})}$$

Now find $C_{L_\alpha}$:
From $\frac{d\delta_e}{dC_L} = -\frac{C_{m_\alpha}}{C_{L_\alpha} C_{m_{\delta_e}}} = -\frac{-C_{L_\alpha} K_n}{C_{L_\alpha} C_{m_{\delta_e}}} = \frac{K_n}{C_{m_{\delta_e}}}$
Here $K_{n1} = 0.40 - 0.25 = 0.15$.
The relation $\frac{d\delta_e}{dC_L} = \frac{h - h_n}{C_{m_{\delta_e}}}$ holds independently of $C_{L_\alpha}$!

## ⚠️ SECTION 10 — Critical GATE Exam Traps

### Trap 1: Confusing Tail Moment Arm Reference
> - Tail volume $V_H = \frac{S_t l_t}{S \bar{c}}$.
> - **$l_t$ is distance from WING AC to TAIL AC** in the standard neutral point derivation formula!
> - If $l_t$ is given from **CG to tail AC**, remember that as CG shifts, $l_t$ changes slightly.

### Trap 2: Downwash Factor $(1 - d\epsilon/d\alpha)$
> Downwash ALWAYS REDUCES tail stability contribution.
> Typical values of $d\epsilon/d\alpha$ range between $0.3 - 0.5$.
> Never forget to multiply $a_t$ by $(1 - d\epsilon/d\alpha)$!

### Trap 3: Units of $C_{m_\alpha}$ and Elevator Angles
> $C_{m_\alpha}$ in formulas is in **$\text{rad}^{-1}$**. If given in **$\text{deg}^{-1}$**, multiply by $57.3$ before using with non-dimensional equations!

### Trap 4: Canard Volume vs Tail Volume
> Canard volume $V_c$ enters the neutral point equation with a **MINUS SIGN**:
> $$h_n = h_{ac,w} - V_c \frac{a_c}{C_{L_\alpha}} (1 + d\epsilon_c/d\alpha)$$

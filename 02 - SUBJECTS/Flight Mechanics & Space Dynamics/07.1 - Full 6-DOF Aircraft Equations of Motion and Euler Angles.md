## 🧠 MASTER MENTAL MODEL — The 6-DOF Rigid Aircraft System

> **CORE CONCEPT:** A rigid aircraft in 3D space possesses **6 Degrees of Freedom (6-DOF)**:
> 1. **3 Translational Degrees of Freedom:** $u, v, w$ (Velocities along Body axes $X_b, Y_b, Z_b$).
> 2. **3 Rotational Degrees of Freedom:** $p, q, r$ (Angular rates about Body axes $X_b, Y_b, Z_b$).
>
> To determine position and attitude relative to Earth, we transform body variables to Earth axes using **Euler Angles ($\psi, \theta, \phi$)**.

```
                   AIRCRAFT 6-DOF STATE VARIABLES
                       
                            X_b (Roll Axis)
                              ▲
                 u (Axial Vel)│  p (Roll Rate), Roll Angle φ
                              │
             v (Side Vel)     │
        -Y_b ─────────────────┼─────────────────> +Y_b (Pitch Axis)
             q (Pitch Rate)   │                   q (Pitch Rate), Pitch Angle θ
             Pitch Angle θ    │
                              ▼
                            +Z_b (Yaw Axis)
                            w (Normal Vel), r (Yaw Rate), Yaw Angle ψ
```

---

## 📐 SECTION 1 — Euler Angles & Kinematic Transformation Equations

### 1.1 Euler Angle Definitions (Z-Y-X Standard Rotation Sequence)
To orient the Earth-fixed inertial frame $[X_E, Y_E, Z_E]$ to the Aircraft Body frame $[X_b, Y_b, Z_b]$, three successive right-handed rotations are performed:

1. **Yaw / Heading Rotation ($\psi$):** Rotate about $Z_E$ axis by angle $\psi$ (Yaw angle).
2. **Pitch Rotation ($\theta$):** Rotate about intermediate $Y^\prime$ axis by angle $\theta$ (Pitch angle).
3. **Roll / Bank Rotation ($\phi$):** Rotate about final $X_b$ axis by angle $\phi$ (Bank angle).

---

### 1.2 Euler Kinematic Rates vs. Body Rates

The relationship between body angular velocity rates $[p, q, r]^T$ and Earth Euler angle rates $[\dot{\phi}, \dot{\theta}, \dot{\psi}]^T$ is given by:

$$\begin{bmatrix} p \\ q \\ r \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & -\sin\theta \\ 0 & \cos\phi & \sin\phi \cos\theta \\ 0 & -\sin\phi & \cos\phi \cos\theta \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} \dot{\phi} \\ \dot{\theta} \\ \dot{\psi} \end{bmatrix}$$

Inverting this matrix yields the **Euler Kinematic Equations**:

$$\boxed{\begin{aligned}
\dot{\phi} &= p + q \sin\phi \tan\theta + r \cos\phi \tan\theta \\
\dot{\theta} &= q \cos\phi - r \sin\phi \\
\dot{\psi} &= (q \sin\phi + r \cos\phi) \sec\theta
\end{aligned}}$$

> ⚠️ **GIMBAL LOCK SINGULARITY:**
> When pitch angle $\theta = \pm 90^\circ$ (vertical pitch up/down), $\tan\theta \to \infty$ and $\sec\theta \to \infty$. This mathematical singularity is known as **Gimbal Lock** (resolved in flight simulators using Quaternions).

## 📐 SECTION 2 — Complete Non-Linear 6-DOF Equations of Motion

Combining 3D translational force balance (Newton's 2nd Law) and 3D rotational moment balance (Euler's 2nd Law):

### 2.1 Translational Equations of Motion (Force Equations)
$$\boxed{\begin{aligned}
X_A + X_T - m g \sin\theta &= m (\dot{u} + q w - r v) \quad &\text{(X-Body Force)} \\
Y_A + Y_T + m g \cos\theta \sin\phi &= m (\dot{v} + r u - p w) \quad &\text{(Y-Body Force)} \\
Z_A + Z_T + m g \cos\theta \cos\phi &= m (\dot{w} + p v - q u) \quad &\text{(Z-Body Force)}
\end{aligned}}$$

---

### 2.2 Rotational Equations of Motion (Moment Equations)
For aircraft with $X_b-Z_b$ plane of symmetry ($I_{xy} = I_{yz} = 0, I_{xz} \ne 0$):

$$\boxed{\begin{aligned}
L &= I_{xx} \dot{p} - I_{xz} \dot{r} + (I_{zz} - I_{yy}) q r - I_{xz} p q \quad &\text{(Rolling Moment } L\text{)} \\
M &= I_{yy} \dot{q} + (I_{xx} - I_{zz}) p r + I_{xz} (p^2 - r^2) \quad &\text{(Pitching Moment } M\text{)} \\
N &= I_{zz} \dot{r} - I_{xz} \dot{p} + (I_{yy} - I_{xx}) p q + I_{xz} q r \quad &\text{(Yawing Moment } N\text{)}
\end{aligned}}$$

## 📐 SECTION 3 — Small Disturbance Linearization Theory

To analyze dynamic stability analytically, non-linear equations are linearized about a **Steady Reference Flight Condition** (trimmed wings-level level flight):

### 3.1 Perturbation Variable Decomposition
Each state variable is written as $x(t) = X_0 + \Delta x(t)$, where $X_0$ is steady trim value and $\Delta x$ is a small perturbation:

| Variable | Steady Trim State ($X_0$) | Small Perturbation ($\Delta x$) |
|----------|---------------------------|---------------------------------|
| Forward Speed | $U_0$ | $\Delta u$ |
| Side Speed | $V_0 = 0$ | $\Delta v$ |
| Normal Speed | $W_0 \approx 0$ | $\Delta w$ |
| Angular Rates | $P_0 = Q_0 = R_0 = 0$ | $\Delta p, \Delta q, \Delta r$ |
| Euler Angles | $\Phi_0 = 0, \Theta_0, \Psi_0 = 0$ | $\Delta \phi, \Delta \theta, \Delta \psi$ |

---

### 3.2 Linearization Rules
1. Neglect products of small perturbation terms ($\Delta p \cdot \Delta q \approx 0, \Delta v \cdot \Delta r \approx 0$).
2. Small angle approximations: $\sin\Delta\theta \approx \Delta\theta, \cos\Delta\theta \approx 1$.
3. Subtract steady-state trim equilibrium forces ($X_0 = m g \sin\Theta_0, Z_0 = -m g \cos\Theta_0$).

---

## 📐 SECTION 4 — Decoupling into Longitudinal & Lateral Subsystems

Under symmetric reference flight, the 6-DOF linearized equations **decouple completely into two independent 3-DOF systems**:

```
                       DECOUPLED AIRCRAFT DYNAMICS
  ┌─────────────────────────────────────┬─────────────────────────────────────┐
  │ LONGITUDINAL SUBSYSTEM (In-Plane)   │ LATERAL-DIRECTIONAL SUBSYSTEM       │
  ├─────────────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────────┤
  │ States:  [Δu, Δw, Δq, Δθ]ᵀ          │ States:  [Δv, Δp, Δr, Δφ]ᵀ          │
  │ Motion:  Pitch, Altitude & Speed    │ Motion:  Roll, Yaw & Sideslip       │
  │ Controls: Elevators (δe), Thrust(δT)│ Controls: Ailerons (δa), Rudder (δr)│
  └─────────────────────────────────────┴─────────────────────────────────────┘
```

---

### 4.1 Linearized Longitudinal Equations of Motion
$$\begin{aligned}
m \Delta \dot{u} &= \Delta X_A + \Delta X_T - m g \cos\Theta_0 \cdot \Delta \theta \\
m (\Delta \dot{w} - U_0 \Delta q) &= \Delta Z_A + \Delta Z_T - m g \sin\Theta_0 \cdot \Delta \theta \\
I_{yy} \Delta \dot{q} &= \Delta M_A + \Delta M_T
\end{aligned}$$

#### Matrix State-Space Form ($\dot{\mathbf{x}}_{\text{long}} = \mathbf{A}_{\text{long}} \mathbf{x}_{\text{long}}$):
$$\begin{bmatrix} \Delta \dot{u} \\ \Delta \dot{w} \\ \Delta \dot{q} \\ \Delta \dot{\theta} \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} X_u & X_w & X_q & -g\cos\Theta_0 \\ Z_u & Z_w & Z_q + U_0 & -g\sin\Theta_0 \\ M_u & M_w & M_q & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 & 0 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} \Delta u \\ \Delta w \\ \Delta q \\ \Delta \theta \end{bmatrix}$$

---

### 4.2 Linearized Lateral-Directional Equations of Motion
$$\begin{aligned}
m (\Delta \dot{v} + U_0 \Delta r) &= \Delta Y_A + m g \cos\Theta_0 \cdot \Delta \phi \\
I_{xx} \Delta \dot{p} - I_{xz} \Delta \dot{r} &= \Delta L_A \\
I_{zz} \Delta \dot{r} - I_{xz} \Delta \dot{p} &= \Delta N_A
\end{aligned}$$

#### Matrix State-Space Form ($\dot{\mathbf{x}}_{\text{lat}} = \mathbf{A}_{\text{lat}} \mathbf{x}_{\text{lat}}$):
$$\begin{bmatrix} \Delta \dot{v} \\ \Delta \dot{p} \\ \Delta \dot{r} \\ \Delta \dot{\phi} \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} Y_v & Y_p & Y_r - U_0 & g\cos\Theta_0 \\ L_v^\prime & L_p^\prime & L_r^\prime & 0 \\ N_v^\prime & N_p^\prime & N_r^\prime & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & \tan\Theta_0 & 0 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} \Delta v \\ \Delta p \\ \Delta r \\ \Delta \phi \end{bmatrix}$$

## 🔑 SECTION 5 — Mnemonics, Shortcuts & Tricks

### 5.1 Euler Rates Level Flight Shortcut ($\phi \to 0, \theta \to 0$)
When wings are level ($\phi = 0$) and pitch attitude is near horizontal ($\theta = 0$):
$$\dot{\phi} = p, \quad \dot{\theta} = q, \quad \dot{\psi} = r$$

### 5.2 Longitudinal vs Lateral Decoupling Mnemonic
> **"Longitudinal stays in X-Z plane ($\Delta u, \Delta w, \Delta q, \Delta\theta$); Lateral leaves the plane ($\Delta v, \Delta p, \Delta r, \Delta\phi$)!"**

## 🔢 SECTION 6 — Solved GATE-Style Numerical Examples

### Example 1 — Euler Rate Calculation in Banked Climb

**Problem:** An aircraft is climbing at pitch angle $\theta = 30^\circ$ and bank angle $\phi = 45^\circ$. Its body-fixed angular velocity components are measured as $p = 0.10\text{ rad/s}$, $q = 0.20\text{ rad/s}$, and $r = 0.05\text{ rad/s}$.

Calculate the pitch rate relative to Earth ($\dot{\theta}$) in $\text{rad/s}$.

**Solution:**

Formula for $\dot{\theta}$:
$$\dot{\theta} = q \cos\phi - r \sin\phi$$

Substitute values ($\cos 45^\circ = \sin 45^\circ = 0.7071$):
$$\dot{\theta} = (0.20 \times 0.7071) - (0.05 \times 0.7071)$$
$$\dot{\theta} = 0.14142 - 0.03536 = 0.10606\text{ rad/s}$$

$$\boxed{\dot{\theta} \approx 0.1061\text{ rad/s}}$$

---

### Example 2 — Decoupled State Matrix Classification

**Problem:** Identify which of the following dynamic states belong to the **Longitudinal** subsystem:
1. Sideslip rate $\dot{\beta}$
2. Pitch angle perturbation $\Delta \theta$
3. Roll rate $p$
4. Forward velocity perturbation $\Delta u$

**Solution:**
Longitudinal states govern motion in the symmetric X-Z plane: $[\Delta u, \Delta w, \Delta q, \Delta \theta]^T$.
Therefore, states (2) $\Delta \theta$ and (4) $\Delta u$ belong to the **Longitudinal** subsystem!

## ⚠️ SECTION 7 — Critical GATE Exam Traps

### Trap 1: Confusing Body Rates ($p, q, r$) with Euler Rates ($\dot{\phi}, \dot{\theta}, \dot{\psi}$)
> Body rates $p, q, r$ are measured by **onboard gyroscopes** fixed to the aircraft structure.
> Euler rates $\dot{\phi}, \dot{\theta}, \dot{\psi}$ are measured relative to **Earth ground reference**. They are equal ONLY when bank and pitch angles are zero ($\phi = \theta = 0$).

### Trap 2: Gimbal Lock Condition
> Euler kinematic equations diverge when pitch angle $\theta = \pm 90^\circ$ because $\tan(90^\circ) = \infty$.

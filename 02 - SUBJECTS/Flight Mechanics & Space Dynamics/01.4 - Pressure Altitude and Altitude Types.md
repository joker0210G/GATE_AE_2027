## 📌 Executive Summary & GATE Significance

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Pressure Altitude ($h_p$)** is the geopotential altitude in the International Standard Atmosphere (ISA) model corresponding to a given ambient static pressure ($p$). It is measured directly by a pressure altimeter calibrated to standard sea-level pressure ($1013.25 \text{ hPa} = 29.92 \text{ inHg}$).

### Why does GATE AE care?
1. **Direct Exam Questions:** GATE AE regularly features numerical problems requiring calculation of **Pressure Altitude ($h_p$)**, **Density Altitude ($h_d$)**, and **Temperature Altitude ($h_T$)** under non-standard temperature conditions ($ISA \pm \Delta T$).
2. **Aircraft Performance Impact:** Engine thrust, wing lift, takeoff roll distance, and ceiling limits depend strictly on **Density Altitude ($h_d$)**, which deviates from Pressure Altitude when temperature is non-standard.
3. **Altimeter Error Analysis:** Temperature deviations and barometric setting errors (QNH vs. QFE vs. QNE) cause true altitude ($h_{\text{true}}$) to differ from indicated altitude ($h_{\text{ind}}$).

## 🔵 Core Concept 1: The 5 Types of Altitudes in Flight Mechanics

```
                             ALTITUDE TYPES
                                   |
    +------------------------------+------------------------------+
    |                              |                              |
STATIC PRESSURE              AIR DENSITY                     AIR TEMP
      |                            |                              |
Pressure Altitude ($h_p$)    Density Altitude ($h_d$)      Temperature Altitude ($h_T$)
[$p_{ISA}(h_p) = p_{\text{amb}}$]  [$\rho_{ISA}(h_d) = \rho_{\text{amb}}$] [$T_{ISA}(h_T) = T_{\text{amb}}$]
```

### 1. Pressure Altitude ($h_p$ or $H_p$)
- **Definition:** The altitude in the ISA model where the standard pressure equals the surrounding ambient static pressure $p$.
- **Altimeter Setting:** Set Kollsman window to **$1013.25 \text{ hPa} \quad (101.325 \text{ kPa} = 29.92 \text{ inHg} = 760 \text{ mmHg})$** (QNE setting).
- **Flight Levels (FL):** Used in commercial aviation above transition altitude ($h_p / 100$). E.g., $\text{FL350} = 35,000 \text{ ft}$ pressure altitude.
- **Key Property:** Pressure altitude depends **STRICTLY ON AMBIENT PRESSURE ($p$)**. It does NOT depend on local ambient temperature!

---

### 2. Density Altitude ($h_d$ or $H_d$)
- **Definition:** The altitude in the ISA model where standard air density equals the actual ambient air density $\rho$.
- **Performance Significance:** Aircraft wings, propellers, and jet engines "feel" density altitude. Higher $h_d \implies$ lower air density $\rho \implies$ reduced thrust, longer takeoff distance, and lower climb rate!
- **Effect of Temperature ($\Delta T = T_{\text{actual}} - T_{\text{ISA}}$):**
  - **Hot Day ($ISA + \Delta T$):** Air expands $\implies$ density drops $\implies$ **$h_d > h_p$** (Aircraft behaves as if it is at a higher altitude!).
  - **Cold Day ($ISA - \Delta T$):** Air compresses $\implies$ density rises $\implies$ **$h_d < h_p$**.

---

### 3. Temperature Altitude ($h_T$)
- **Definition:** The altitude in the ISA model where standard temperature equals the actual ambient static temperature $T_{\text{actual}}$.
- **Formula (Troposphere):**
  $$T_{\text{actual}} = T_0 - \lambda h_T \implies \bbox[8px,border:1px solid #2196F3,background-color:#0d1117]{ h_T = \frac{T_0 - T_{\text{actual}}}{\lambda} = \frac{288.15 - T_{\text{actual}}}{0.0065} }$$

---

### 4. True Altitude ($h_{\text{true}}$ or $Z$)
- **Definition:** The actual physical height of the aircraft above Mean Sea Level (MSL).

---

### 5. Indicated Altitude ($h_{\text{ind}}$)
- **Definition:** The altitude reading shown directly on the altimeter face when set to local barometric pressure (QNH).

---

## 🔵 Core Concept 2: Mathematical Formulations & Exact Equations

```
             Troposphere Equations (0 <= hp <= 11,000 m)
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Pressure Altitude:   hp = (T0 / lambda) * [ 1 - (p / p0)^(lambda*R / g0) ]
Density Altitude:    hd = (T0 / lambda) * [ 1 - (rho / rho0)^(lambda*R / (g0 - lambda*R)) ]
```

### 1. Exact Pressure Altitude Formula ($h_p$) in Troposphere
From the ISA troposphere pressure distribution $\frac{p}{p_0} = \left(1 - \frac{\lambda h_p}{T_0}\right)^{\frac{g_0}{\lambda R}}$:

$$\bbox[12px,border:3px solid #2196F3,background-color:#0d1117]{ h_p = \frac{T_0}{\lambda} \left[ 1 - \left(\frac{p}{p_0}\right)^{\frac{\lambda R}{g_0}} \right] }$$

Using standard ISA constants ($T_0 = 288.15 \text{ K}$, $\lambda = 0.0065 \text{ K/m}$, $R = 287.053 \text{ J/(kg}\cdot\text{K)}$, $g_0 = 9.80665 \text{ m/s}^2$):
- Exponent: $\frac{\lambda R}{g_0} = \frac{0.0065 \times 287.053}{9.80665} = \frac{1.86584}{9.80665} = \mathbf{0.190263}$
- Coefficient: $\frac{T_0}{\lambda} = \frac{288.15}{0.0065} = \mathbf{44,330.77 \text{ m}}$

$$\bbox[10px,border:2px solid #2196F3,background-color:#0d1117]{ h_p (\text{m}) = 44330.8 \left[ 1 - \left(\frac{p}{p_0}\right)^{0.190263} \right] }$$

---

### 2. Exact Pressure Altitude Formula ($h_p$) in Isothermal Stratosphere ($11 \text{ km} \le h_p \le 20 \text{ km}$)
$$\frac{p}{p_{11}} = e^{-\frac{g_0 (h_p - 11000)}{R T_{11}}}$$

$$\bbox[10px,border:2px solid #4CAF50,background-color:#0d1117]{ h_p = 11000 - \frac{R T_{11}}{g_0} \ln\left(\frac{p}{p_{11}}\right) = 11000 - 6341.6 \ln\left(\frac{p}{22632}\right) } \text{ (meters)}$$

---

### 3. Exact Density Altitude Formula ($h_d$) in Troposphere
Actual air density is given by Ideal Gas Law:
$$\rho_{\text{actual}} = \frac{p}{R \cdot T_{\text{actual}}}$$

Plugging $\rho_{\text{actual}}$ into the ISA density profile $\frac{\rho}{\rho_0} = \left(1 - \frac{\lambda h_d}{T_0}\right)^{\frac{g_0}{\lambda R} - 1}$:

$$\bbox[12px,border:3px solid #E91E63,background-color:#0d1117]{ h_d = \frac{T_0}{\lambda} \left[ 1 - \left(\frac{\rho_{\text{actual}}}{\rho_0}\right)^{\frac{\lambda R}{g_0 - \lambda R}} \right] }$$

- Exponent: $\frac{\lambda R}{g_0 - \lambda R} = \frac{1.86584}{9.80665 - 1.86584} = \frac{1.86584}{7.94081} = \mathbf{0.234968}$

$$\bbox[10px,border:2px solid #E91E63,background-color:#0d1117]{ h_d (\text{m}) = 44330.8 \left[ 1 - \left(\frac{\rho_{\text{actual}}}{\rho_0}\right)^{0.234968} \right] }$$

---

### 4. Linear Approximation Formulas for Density Altitude ($h_d$)

In GATE numerical problems, a fast linear approximation relates Density Altitude to Pressure Altitude and temperature deviation ($\Delta T = T_{\text{actual}} - T_{\text{ISA}}$):

$$\bbox[10px,border:2px solid #9C27B0,background-color:#0d1117]{ h_d \approx h_p + \left( \frac{T_0}{\lambda g_0 / (\lambda R) - 1} \right) \frac{\Delta T}{T_{\text{ISA}}} } \implies \bbox[12px,border:3px solid #9C27B0,background-color:#0d1117]{ h_d \approx h_p + \frac{\Delta T}{\lambda} \cdot \left( \frac{T_p}{T_0} \right) }$$

#### Standard Practical Approximation Formulas:
- **SI Units ($h$ in meters, $\Delta T$ in Kelvin):**
  $$\bbox[10px,border:2px solid #9C27B0,background-color:#0d1117]{ h_d (\text{m}) \approx h_p (\text{m}) + 36.6 \times (T_{\text{actual}} - T_{\text{ISA}})_{^\circ\text{C}} }$$
- **Imperial Aviation Units ($h$ in feet, $\Delta T$ in $^\circ\text{C}$):**
  $$\bbox[10px,border:2px solid #9C27B0,background-color:#0d1117]{ h_d (\text{ft}) \approx h_p (\text{ft}) + 120 \times (T_{\text{actual}} - T_{\text{ISA}})_{^\circ\text{C}} }$$

## 2. Altimeter Barometric Settings: QNH vs. QFE vs. QNE

Pressure altimeters measure ambient pressure and convert it to altitude based on the reference pressure dialed into the **Kollsman Window**:

```
                       RUNWAY / AIRFIELD ELEVATION
                                   |
         +-------------------------+-------------------------+
         |                         |                         |
     QFE SETTING               QNH SETTING               QNE SETTING
   Reads ZERO at Field       Reads Field Elevation     Reads Pressure Altitude
    (Height AGL)                 (Altitude MSL)          (Standard 1013.25 hPa)
```

| Altimeter Setting Code | Barometric Pressure Dialed In | Altimeter Reading on Runway Ground | Aviation & Flight Mechanics Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| **QFE** | Field Surface Pressure ($p_{\text{field}}$) | **ZERO ($0 \text{ m} / 0 \text{ ft}$)** | Reads height Above Ground Level (AGL). Used for local circuit flying. |
| **QNH** | Sea-level pressure adjusted for local airfield conditions | **Airfield Elevation above MSL** | Reads altitude above Mean Sea Level (MSL). Standard setting below transition altitude. |
| **QNE** | Standard ISA Sea-Level Pressure (**$1013.25 \text{ hPa} / 29.92 \text{ inHg}$**) | **Pressure Altitude ($h_p$)** | Reads Flight Levels (FL). Mandatory for high-altitude en-route cruising. |

## 🔵 Core Concept 3: Temperature Effects on Altimeter Errors

> [!WARNING]
> **Aviation Golden Rule:** *"From High to Low, Look Out Below!"*

```
      HOT AIR (Warm)               ISA AIR (Standard)            COLD AIR (Cold)
  Isobars Spaced Farther          Standard Spacing             Isobars Squeezed Close
  +------------------+           +------------------+          +------------------+
  |  Indicated: 5000 |           |  Indicated: 5000 |          |  Indicated: 5000 |
  |  True: 5400 ft   |           |  True: 5000 ft   |          |  True: 4600 ft DANGER!
  +------------------+           +------------------+          +------------------+
```

1. **Cold Air Temperature Error ($T_{\text{actual}} < T_{\text{ISA}}$):**
   - Cold air is dense $\implies$ vertical distance between pressure levels shrinks.
   - The altimeter measures pressure drop and assumes standard ISA spacing.
   - Result: **Indicated Altitude > True Altitude** ($h_{\text{ind}} > h_{\text{true}}$). The aircraft is **LOWER** than the altimeter shows! Dangerous for terrain clearance!

2. **Warm Air Temperature Error ($T_{\text{actual}} > T_{\text{ISA}}$):**
   - Warm air expands $\implies$ vertical pressure levels spread out.
   - Result: **Indicated Altitude < True Altitude** ($h_{\text{ind}} < h_{\text{true}}$). The aircraft is **HIGHER** than indicated.

---

## ⚡ AIR-1 Mnemonics & Speed Tricks

### 🧠 Mnemonic 1: "HIGH TO LOW, LOOK OUT BELOW"
- Flying from **High to Low Pressure** OR **Warm to Cold Temperature** without updating altimeter $\implies$ True altitude is **LOWER** than indicated altitude!

### 🧠 Mnemonic 2: "QFE = Field, QNH = Height, QNE = Flight Level"
- **QFE:** **F**ield height (0 on runway).
- **QNH:** **N**autical **H**eight above MSL (Airfield elevation).
- **QNE:** Standard **E**n-route pressure ($1013.25 \text{ hPa} \implies h_p$).

### ⚡ Speed Trick 1: Quick Density Altitude 120-ft Rule
$$\mathbf{h_d \approx h_p + 120 \times (T_{\text{actual}} - T_{\text{ISA}})_{^\circ\text{C}}}$$
- Example: At $h_p = 5,000 \text{ ft}$, ISA temp is $5^\circ\text{C}$. If actual temp is $+25^\circ\text{C}$ ($\Delta T = +20^\circ\text{C}$):
  $$h_d \approx 5000 + 120 \times 20 = 5000 + 2400 = \mathbf{7,400 \text{ ft}}$$

### ⚡ Speed Trick 2: Exponent Memory Trick
- Pressure Altitude exponent: $\mathbf{0.19026} \quad (= 1 / 5.256)$
- Density Altitude exponent: $\mathbf{0.23497} \quad (= 1 / 4.256)$

## 📝 Solved GATE & Exam-Standard Examples

### Example 1: Pressure Altitude Calculation from Pressure Sensor (GATE Classic)
**Problem:** An aircraft flight test sensor records an ambient static pressure $p = 70,120 \text{ Pa}$. Calculate the pressure altitude $h_p$ of the aircraft in meters.

**Solution:**
Using the exact troposphere pressure altitude formula:
$$h_p = 44330.8 \left[ 1 - \left(\frac{p}{p_0}\right)^{0.190263} \right]$$

1. Pressure ratio:
   $$\frac{p}{p_0} = \frac{70120}{101325} = 0.69203$$

2. Raise to exponent $0.190263$:
   $$(0.69203)^{0.190263} = 0.93214$$

3. Calculate $h_p$:
   $$h_p = 44330.8 \times (1 - 0.93214) = 44330.8 \times 0.06786 = \mathbf{3,008.3 \text{ m}}$$

---

### Example 2: Density Altitude on Hot Day ($ISA + 15^\circ\text{C}$)
**Problem:** An airfield is located at a pressure altitude $h_p = 2,000 \text{ m}$. On a hot summer afternoon, the ambient temperature is recorded as $33.0^\circ\text{C}$.
1. Determine the ISA temperature deviation $\Delta T$.
2. Calculate the exact density altitude $h_d$ of the airfield.
3. Compare with the linear approximation formula.

**Solution:**
1. **ISA Temperature at $h_p = 2,000 \text{ m}$:**
   $$T_{\text{ISA}} = 288.15 - (0.0065 \times 2000) = 275.15 \text{ K} \quad (2.0^\circ\text{C})$$
   $$T_{\text{actual}} = 33.0^\circ\text{C} = 306.15 \text{ K}$$
   $$\Delta T = 33.0 - 2.0 = \mathbf{+31.0 \text{ K}} \quad (ISA + 31.0^\circ\text{C})$$

2. **Ambient Pressure at $h_p = 2,000 \text{ m}$:**
   $$p = 101325 \times \left(\frac{275.15}{288.15}\right)^{5.25588} = 101325 \times (0.954885)^{5.25588} = \mathbf{79,495 \text{ Pa}}$$

3. **Actual Air Density ($\rho_{\text{actual}}$):**
   $$\rho_{\text{actual}} = \frac{p}{R \cdot T_{\text{actual}}} = \frac{79495}{287.053 \times 306.15} = \mathbf{0.9045 \text{ kg/m}^3}$$

4. **Exact Density Altitude ($h_d$):**
   $$\frac{\rho_{\text{actual}}}{\rho_0} = \frac{0.9045}{1.2250} = 0.738367$$
   $$h_d = 44330.8 \times \left[ 1 - (0.738367)^{0.234968} \right] = 44330.8 \times [1 - 0.931288] = 44330.8 \times 0.068712 = \mathbf{3,046.1 \text{ m}}$$

5. **Linear Approximation Check:**
   $$h_d \approx h_p + 36.6 \times \Delta T = 2000 + 36.6 \times 31.0 = 2000 + 1134.6 = \mathbf{3,134.6 \text{ m}}$$
   *(Exact calculation yields $3,046 \text{ m}$; approximation provides a rapid conservative check within $2.9\%$).*

---

### Example 3: QNH vs. QFE Altimeter Conversion
**Problem:** An airport runway is at an elevation of $500 \text{ m}$ above MSL. The QNH barometric setting is $1020 \text{ hPa}$. If a pilot sets the altimeter to QFE ($1013.25 \text{ hPa}$ baseline), calculate the QFE pressure reading. (Take sea-level lapse rate pressure change $\approx 12 \text{ Pa/m}$).

**Solution:**
1. Pressure difference due to runway elevation ($500 \text{ m}$):
   $$\Delta p = 500 \text{ m} \times 12 \text{ Pa/m} = 6000 \text{ Pa} = 60 \text{ hPa}$$

2. QFE pressure at runway surface:
   $$QFE = QNH - \Delta p = 1020 \text{ hPa} - 60 \text{ hPa} = \mathbf{960 \text{ hPa}}$$

## ⚠️ Common GATE Traps & Exam Pitfalls

> [!CAUTION]
> 1. **Assuming Pressure Altitude Depends on Temperature:** Pressure altitude $h_p$ depends **ONLY on static pressure $p$**. Local temperature variations do NOT change $h_p$ if ambient pressure remains constant!
> 2. **Confusing Pressure Altitude with Density Altitude:** On a hot day, $h_p$ stays unchanged at a given pressure, but **$h_d$ INCREASES** significantly!
> 3. **Density Exponent Miscalculation:** Remember that the density ratio exponent is $\frac{g_0}{\lambda R} - 1 = \mathbf{4.256}$, whereas the pressure ratio exponent is $\mathbf{5.256}$.
> 4. **Linear Density Altitude Units:** When using $h_d \approx h_p + 120 \, \Delta T$, altitude MUST be in **FEET** and $\Delta T$ in **$^\circ\text{C}$**. In metric, use $h_d \approx h_p + 36.6 \, \Delta T_{\text{K}}$.
> 5. **Cold Temperature Altimeter Hazard:** In cold air ($T < T_{\text{ISA}}$), the altimeter **OVERREADS** ($h_{\text{ind}} > h_{\text{true}}$). The plane is closer to the ground than indicated!

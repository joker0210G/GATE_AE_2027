## 🧠 MASTER MENTAL MODEL — Angular Momentum Balance in 3D Flight

> **CORE CONCEPT:** Euler's 2nd Law states that the net external moment vector $\vec{M}_{\text{ext}} = [L, M, N]^T$ about the aircraft Center of Gravity equals the time rate of change of its angular momentum vector $\vec{H}$:
>
> $$\vec{M}_{\text{ext}} = \left(\frac{d\vec{H}}{dt}\right)_{\text{inertial}} = \left(\frac{d\vec{H}}{dt}\right)_{\text{body}} + \vec{\Omega} \times \vec{H}$$
>
> Where $\vec{\Omega} = [p, q, r]^T$ is the angular velocity vector in body axes, and $\vec{H} = \mathbf{I} \cdot \vec{\Omega}$ is the angular momentum vector.

```
                  ROTATIONAL AXES & MOMENTS (L, M, N)
                                   +X_b (Roll Axis)
                                     ▲
                       Rolling Moment L (Positive Right Wing DOWN)
                       Roll Rate p
                                     │
                 -Y_b ───────────────┼───────────────> +Y_b (Pitch Axis)
                                     │                 Pitching Moment M (Nose UP)
                                     │                 Pitch Rate q
                                     ▼
                                   +Z_b (Yaw Axis)
                                   Yawing Moment N (Nose RIGHT)
                                   Yaw Rate r
```

## 📐 SECTION 1 — Aircraft Inertia Tensor ($\mathbf{I}$) in Body Axes

### 1.1 The General Inertia Matrix

For a rigid 3D body, the moment of inertia tensor about CG is:

$$\mathbf{I} = \begin{bmatrix} I_{xx} & -I_{xy} & -I_{xz} \\ -I_{yx} & I_{yy} & -I_{yz} \\ -I_{zx} & -I_{zy} & I_{zz} \end{bmatrix}$$

Where:
- $I_{xx} = \int (y^2 + z^2) dm$ = Roll Moment of Inertia ($\text{kg}\cdot\text{m}^2$)
- $I_{yy} = \int (x^2 + z^2) dm$ = Pitch Moment of Inertia ($\text{kg}\cdot\text{m}^2$)
- $I_{zz} = \int (x^2 + y^2) dm$ = Yaw Moment of Inertia ($\text{kg}\cdot\text{m}^2$)
- $I_{xz} = \int x z \, dm$ = Product of Inertia in X-Z plane ($\text{kg}\cdot\text{m}^2$)

---

### 1.2 Aircraft Plane of Symmetry Simplification

Standard aircraft possess structural symmetry about the **$X_b - Z_b$ vertical plane** ($y$-axis symmetry):
1. For every mass element $+y$, there is an identical mass element $-y$.
2. Therefore: $I_{xy} = I_{yx} = 0$ and $I_{yz} = I_{zy} = 0$.
3. The product of inertia $I_{xz} = I_{zx}$ remains **non-zero** ($I_{xz} \ne 0$) because mass is not symmetric about the $X_b-Y_b$ horizontal plane (wing position, tail height, engine nacelles).

$$\boxed{\mathbf{I} = \begin{bmatrix} I_{xx} & 0 & -I_{xz} \\ 0 & I_{yy} & 0 \\ -I_{xz} & 0 & I_{zz} \end{bmatrix}}$$

---

### 1.3 Angular Momentum Vector ($\vec{H}$)

$$\vec{H} = \mathbf{I} \cdot \vec{\Omega} = \begin{bmatrix} I_{xx} & 0 & -I_{xz} \\ 0 & I_{yy} & 0 \\ -I_{xz} & 0 & I_{zz} \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} p \\ q \\ r \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} I_{xx} p - I_{xz} r \\ I_{yy} q \\ I_{zz} r - I_{xz} p \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} H_x \\ H_y \\ H_z \end{bmatrix}$$

---

## 📐 SECTION 2 — Derivation of Euler's 3-Axis Rotational Equations

Using the Transport Theorem:

$$\vec{M}_{\text{ext}} = \left(\frac{d\vec{H}}{dt}\right)_{\text{body}} + \vec{\Omega} \times \vec{H}$$

### 2.1 Cross Product ($\vec{\Omega} \times \vec{H}$)

$$\vec{\Omega} \times \vec{H} = \begin{vmatrix} \hat{i}_b & \hat{j}_b & \hat{k}_b \\ p & q & r \\ H_x & H_y & H_z \end{vmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} q H_z - r H_y \\ r H_x - p H_z \\ p H_y - q H_x \end{bmatrix}$$

Substituting $H_x, H_y, H_z$:
- $q H_z - r H_y = q (I_{zz} r - I_{xz} p) - r (I_{yy} q) = (I_{zz} - I_{yy}) q r - I_{xz} p q$
- $r H_x - p H_z = r (I_{xx} p - I_{xz} r) - p (I_{zz} r - I_{xz} p) = (I_{xx} - I_{zz}) p r + I_{xz} (p^2 - r^2)$
- $p H_y - q H_x = p (I_{yy} q) - q (I_{xx} p - I_{xz} r) = (I_{yy} - I_{xx}) p q + I_{xz} q r$

---

### 2.2 Complete Euler 3-Axis Rotational Equations of Motion

$$\boxed{\begin{aligned}
L &= I_{xx} \dot{p} - I_{xz} \dot{r} + (I_{zz} - I_{yy}) q r - I_{xz} p q \quad &\text{(Rolling Moment } L\text{)} \\
M &= I_{yy} \dot{q} + (I_{xx} - I_{zz}) p r + I_{xz} (p^2 - r^2) \quad &\text{(Pitching Moment } M\text{)} \\
N &= I_{zz} \dot{r} - I_{xz} \dot{p} + (I_{yy} - I_{xx}) p q + I_{xz} q r \quad &\text{(Yawing Moment } N\text{)}
\end{aligned}}$$

Where $L, M, N$ are the total aerodynamic + propulsive moments about $X_b, Y_b, Z_b$ ($\text{N}\cdot\text{m}$).

---

## 📐 SECTION 3 — Principal Axes Simplification ($I_{xz} = 0$)

If the body axes align with the **Principal Axes of Inertia** such that $I_{xz} = 0$:

$$\boxed{\begin{aligned}
L &= I_{xx} \dot{p} + (I_{zz} - I_{yy}) q r \\
M &= I_{yy} \dot{q} + (I_{xx} - I_{zz}) p r \\
N &= I_{zz} \dot{r} + (I_{yy} - I_{xx}) p q
\end{aligned}}$$

### Physical Meaning of Gyroscopic Coupling Terms:
- **$(I_{zz} - I_{yy}) q r$:** Rolling moment induced by combined pitching ($q$) and yawing ($r$).
- **$(I_{xx} - I_{zz}) p r$:** Pitching moment induced by combined rolling ($p$) and yawing ($r$).
- **$(I_{yy} - I_{xx}) p q$:** Yawing moment induced by combined rolling ($p$) and pitching ($q$).

---

## 📐 SECTION 4 — Engine Gyroscopic Coupling Effects

Rotating engine turbines possess substantial spin angular momentum $\vec{H}_e = [I_e \omega_e, 0, 0]^T$ directed forward along the engine axis:

$$\vec{M}_{\text{gyro}} = \vec{\Omega} \times \vec{H}_e = \begin{vmatrix} \hat{i}_b & \hat{j}_b & \hat{k}_b \\ p & q & r \\ I_e \omega_e & 0 & 0 \end{vmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ r (I_e \omega_e) \\ -q (I_e \omega_e) \end{bmatrix}$$

```
                ENGINE GYROSCOPIC COUPLING MATRIX
  ┌────────────────────────┬─────────────────────────────────────────┐
  │ Aircraft Motion        │ Gyroscopic Moment Produced              │
  ├────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────────────┤
  │ Pitching UP (+q)       │ Induces YAWING RIGHT (+N = I_e ω_e q)   │
  │ Pitching DOWN (-q)     │ Induces YAWING LEFT (-N = -I_e ω_e q)   │
  │ Yawing RIGHT (+r)      │ Induces PITCHING DOWN (-M = -I_e ω_e r) │
  │ Yawing LEFT (-r)       │ Induces PITCHING UP (+M = +I_e ω_e r)   │
  └────────────────────────┴─────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

$$\boxed{\begin{aligned}
M_{\text{total}} &= M_{\text{aero}} - I_e \omega_e r \\
N_{\text{total}} &= N_{\text{aero}} + I_e \omega_e q
\end{aligned}}$$

## 📐 SECTION 5 — Special Flight Motion Cases for GATE

### Case 1 — Pure Symmetric Pitching Motion ($p = r = 0, I_{xz} \to 0$)
$$M = I_{yy} \dot{q} = I_{yy} \ddot{\theta}$$

$$\boxed{\ddot{\theta} = \frac{M}{I_{yy}}}$$

---

### Case 2 — Pure Rolling Motion ($q = r = 0$)
$$\begin{aligned}
L &= I_{xx} \dot{p} \\
M &= I_{xz} p^2 \quad (\text{Inertial pitch-up moment due to } I_{xz}!) \\
N &= -I_{xz} \dot{p} \quad (\text{Inertial yawing moment})
\end{aligned}$$

> 🎯 **INERTIAL ROLL COUPLING (Roll Divergence):**
> High-speed rolling ($p \gg 0$) creates an inertial pitching moment $M = I_{xz} p^2$ and gyroscopic cross-coupling $(I_{xx} - I_{zz}) p r$ that can drive the aircraft into catastrophic pitch/yaw divergence if roll rate exceeds critical pitch frequency ($\omega_\theta = \sqrt{-M_\alpha / I_{yy}}$)!

---

## 🔑 SECTION 6 — Mnemonics, Shortcuts & Tricks

### 6.1 Euler Gyroscopic Term Cyclic Shortcut
> Follow cyclic order $(x \to y \to z \to x)$:
> - $L \implies I_{xx} \dot{p} + (I_{zz} - I_{yy}) \mathbf{q r}$
> - $M \implies I_{yy} \dot{q} + (I_{xx} - I_{zz}) \mathbf{p r}$
> - $N \implies I_{zz} \dot{r} + (I_{yy} - I_{xx}) \mathbf{p q}$

### 6.2 Engine Gyro Precession Mnemonic
> **"Pitch Up $\implies$ Yaws Right; Yaw Right $\implies$ Pitches Down!"**

### 6.3 Aircraft Inertia Hierarchy
> For standard aircraft: **$I_{zz} > I_{yy} \gg I_{xx}$** (Wingspan distribution makes $I_{zz}$ largest, fuselage length makes $I_{yy}$ large, thin fuselage makes $I_{xx}$ smallest).

## 🔢 SECTION 7 — Solved GATE-Style Numerical Examples

### Example 1 — Pitching Moment Required for Pitch Acceleration

**Problem:** An aircraft with pitch moment of inertia $I_{yy} = 50,000\text{ kg}\cdot\text{m}^2$, roll inertia $I_{xx} = 10,000\text{ kg}\cdot\text{m}^2$, yaw inertia $I_{zz} = 80,000\text{ kg}\cdot\text{m}^2$, and $I_{xz} = 0$ undergoes steady roll rate $p = 1.5\text{ rad/s}$ and yaw rate $r = 0.40\text{ rad/s}$.

Calculate the required external pitching moment $M$ to maintain a pitch acceleration $\dot{q} = 0.50\text{ rad/s}^2$.

**Solution:**

Formula (Principal Axes $I_{xz} = 0$):
$$M = I_{yy} \dot{q} + (I_{xx} - I_{zz}) p r$$

Substitute values:
$$I_{yy} \dot{q} = 50,000 \times 0.50 = 25,000\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$$
$$(I_{xx} - I_{zz}) p r = (10,000 - 80,000) \times (1.5 \times 0.40) = (-70,000) \times 0.60 = -42,000\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$$

$$M = 25,000 - 42,000 = -17,000\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$$

$$\boxed{M = -17,000\text{ N}\cdot\text{m} \quad (\text{Requires nose-DOWN pitching moment!})}$$

---

### Example 2 — Engine Gyroscopic Yawing Moment Calculation

**Problem:** A single-engine aircraft has an engine rotor spinning at $\omega_e = 300\text{ rad/s}$ ($2865\text{ rpm}$) with moment of inertia $I_e = 15\text{ kg}\cdot\text{m}^2$. The pilot pulls up at pitch rate $q = 0.20\text{ rad/s}$.

Calculate the gyroscopic yawing moment $N_{\text{gyro}}$ generated by the engine rotor in $\text{N}\cdot\text{m}$.

**Solution:**

Formula:
$$N_{\text{gyro}} = I_e \cdot \omega_e \cdot q$$

Substitute values:
$$N_{\text{gyro}} = 15 \times 300 \times 0.20 = 4500 \times 0.20 = 900\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$$

$$\boxed{N_{\text{gyro}} = +900\text{ N}\cdot\text{m} \quad (\text{Pushes aircraft nose RIGHT})}$$

## ⚠️ SECTION 8 — Critical GATE Exam Traps

### Trap 1: Inertia Product $I_{xz}$ Sign in Euler Equations
> Notice that $I_{xz}$ enters with a **minus sign** in angular momentum ($H_x = I_{xx}p - I_{xz}r$), which introduces negative terms in the equations:
> $L = I_{xx}\dot{p} - I_{xz}\dot{r} + \dots$

### Trap 2: Inertial Coupling Signs
> In pitch equation: $(I_{xx} - I_{zz}) p r$. Since $I_{zz} > I_{xx}$, $(I_{xx} - I_{zz}) < 0$.
> Therefore, positive $p>0, r>0$ creates a **NEGATIVE (nose-down) inertial pitching moment**!

### Trap 3: Principal Axes Assumption
> $I_{xz} = 0$ holds ONLY if principal axes are explicitly specified. In body axes, $I_{xz} \ne 0$ MUST be included!

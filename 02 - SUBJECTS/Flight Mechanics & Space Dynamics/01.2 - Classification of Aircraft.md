## 📌 Executive Summary & GATE Significance

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Aircraft Classification** categorizes airborne vehicles based on their physical mechanisms of lift generation, wing configuration, powerplant type, landing gear arrangement, control surface architecture, and flight speed regimes.

### Why does GATE AE care?
1. **Conceptual & Match-the-Following Questions:** GATE AE frequently tests structural and aerodynamic trade-offs (e.g., Canard vs. Conventional tail trim lift, High-wing vs. Low-wing lateral stability, Swept vs. Delta wing characteristics, Autogyro vs. Helicopter rotor power).
2. **Design Trade-offs:** Aircraft configuration dictates key flight mechanics equations (e.g., Wing Loading $W/S$, Aspect Ratio $AR$, ground effect, tail clearance, and directional ground stability of tricycle vs. taildragger landing gear).
3. **Foundation for Performance & Stability:** Understanding wing placement, tail layout, and propulsion types is a prerequisite for mastering **Static Stability ($C_{m\alpha}, C_{n\beta}, C_{l\beta}$)** and **Performance (Breguet Range, V-n diagrams)**.

## 🔵 Core Concept 1: Master Classification Spectrum (LTA vs. HTA)

All flying vehicles are broadly divided into two primary physics categories based on how they overcome gravity:

```
                            AIRCRAFT
                               |
       +-----------------------+-----------------------+
       |                                               |
  AEROSTATS (LTA)                                AERODYNES (HTA)
(Lighter-Than-Air)                              (Heavier-Than-Air)
Buoyancy Force (Archimedes)                    Dynamic Aerodynamic Lift
       |                                               |
  +----+----+                                    +-----+-----+-----+
  |         |                                    |           |     |
Balloons  Airships (Dirigibles)             Fixed-Wing  Rotary-Wing Flapping-Wing
          - Rigid (Zeppelin)                (Airplanes, (Helicopter, (Ornithopter)
          - Semi-Rigid                      Gliders)    Autogyro)
          - Non-Rigid (Blimp)
```

### 1. Aerostats (Lighter-Than-Air / LTA)
- **Principle:** Lift is generated purely by **Archimedes' Buoyancy Principle**. The weight of the displaced ambient air equals the total gross weight:
  $$\bbox[8px,border:1px solid #2196F3,background-color:#0d1117]{ F_B = \rho_{\text{ambient}} \, g \, V_{\text{displaced}} = W_{\text{gross}} }$$
- **Lifting Gas:** Uses low-density gas (Helium $\text{He}$, Hydrogen $\text{H}_2$, or Hot Air).
- **Categories:**
  - **Balloons:** Unpowered or free-floating (Hot air balloon, weather balloon).
  - **Airships (Dirigibles):** Powered and steerable LTA craft.
    - *Rigid:* Internal structural skeleton maintaining shape regardless of gas pressure (e.g., Graf Zeppelin).
    - *Semi-Rigid:* Flexible gas bag supported by a rigid lower keel beam.
    - *Non-Rigid (Blimp):* No internal structure; internal gas overpressure maintains envelope shape (e.g., Goodyear Blimp).

---

### 2. Aerodynes (Heavier-Than-Air / HTA)
- **Principle:** Gross weight exceeds displaced air buoyancy ($W \gg F_B$). Lift is produced by **dynamic relative motion** between the air and the lifting surfaces (Bernoulli's principle & Newton's third law):
  $$\bbox[8px,border:1px solid #4CAF50,background-color:#0d1117]{ L = \frac{1}{2} \rho_\infty V_\infty^2 S C_L = W }$$

- **Categories:**
  1. **Fixed-Wing Aircraft:** Lifting surfaces (wings) remain fixed relative to the fuselage. Relative wind is created by forward motion of the entire craft (Airplanes, Gliders, Sailplanes).
  2. **Rotary-Wing Aircraft:** Lifting surfaces (rotor blades) rotate continuously around a vertical shaft, generating relative airflow even when the vehicle is hovering in place (Helicopters, Autogyros, Gyrodynes).
  3. **Flapping-Wing Aircraft (Ornithopters):** Wings flap rhythmically like birds/insects (primarily experimental/biomimetic UAVs).

## 🔵 Core Concept 2: Fixed-Wing Aircraft Classification Breakdown

### 1. Classification by Wing Position (Vertical Height relative to Fuselage)

Wing placement strongly influences **lateral stability (dihedral effect)**, **ground clearance**, and **fuselage structural volume**.

```
    High-Wing                 Mid-Wing                 Low-Wing
  +-----------+            +-----------+            +-----------+
  |=== WING ===|           |           |            |           |
  |  FUSELAGE |        ====|  FUSELAGE |====        |  FUSELAGE |
  +-----------+            +-----------+            +-----------+
                                                    |=== WING ===|
```

| Wing Position | Aerodynamic & Structural Characteristics | Stability Effect | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| **High-Wing** | • High ground clearance for large engines/cargo doors.<br>• Easy loading/unloading for tactical fields.<br>• Slightly higher parasite drag from fuselage struts. | **High Pendulum Stability:** Weight hangs below lift vector. Acts like positive dihedral ($\approx 2^\circ - 3^\circ$ effective dihedral gain). | C-130 Hercules, Cessna 172, Antonov An-225 |
| **Mid-Wing** | • Minimum interference drag (wing-fuselage joint at $90^\circ$).<br>• Aerodynamically cleanest configuration.<br>• Wing spar cuts directly through main cabin (reduces interior space). | **Neutral Stability:** Zero inherent pendulum stability. Ideal for aerobatics and fighter maneuvering. | F-16 Fighting Falcon, MiG-29, Jet Trainers |
| **Low-Wing** | • Main landing gear retracts easily into wing root.<br>• Short, lightweight gear struts.<br>• Strong **Ground Effect** cushion during landing.<br>• Low wing spar interference with cabin. | **Unstable Pendulum Effect:** CG lies above lift line. Requires **positive dihedral angle** ($\Gamma = 3^\circ - 6^\circ$) to maintain roll stability. | Boeing 737, Airbus A320, Piper Cherokee |
| **Parasol Wing** | • Wing mounted completely above fuselage on struts/pylons. | Extreme pendulum stability; high drag. | Catalina Flying Boat, Vintage Monoplanes |

### 2. Classification by Wing Planform Shape

Wing planform shape governs **induced drag ($C_{Di}$)**, **structural weight**, and **stall progression behavior**.

```
Rectangular            Tapered              Elliptical             Delta
+---------+          +---------+             .---.                / \
|         |           \       /             /     \              /   \
|         |            \     /             (       )            /     \
+---------+             +---+               `---'              +-------+
```

| Planform Shape | Mathematical / Aerodynamic Feature | Stall Behavior | GATE Engineering Trade-off |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Rectangular** | Constant chord $c(y) = c$. Easy and cheap to manufacture. | **Root Stall First:** Separation begins at wing root. Ailerons at wingtips remain effective until full stall! | Safe stall behavior, but high induced drag due to non-uniform downwash. |
| **Elliptical** | Chord distribution: $c(y) = c_0 \sqrt{1 - (2y/b)^2}$. | **Uniform Stall:** Entire wing stalls simultaneously without warning! | **Minimum Induced Drag** ($e = 1.0 \implies C_{Di} = \frac{C_L^2}{\pi AR}$). Extremely expensive to manufacture (e.g., Spitfire). |
| **Tapered** | Taper ratio $\lambda = c_t / c_r$ (typically $0.4 - 0.5$). | **Mid-span Stall:** Balance between root and tip stall. | Approximates elliptical lift distribution closely ($e \approx 0.95 - 0.98$) with simple straight spar construction. |
| **Swept-Back** | Quarter-chord sweep angle $\Lambda_{1/4} > 0$. | **Tip Stall First:** Spanwise flow towards tips causes early tip stall, producing nose-up pitch moment (**Pitch-Up Trap**). | Delays critical Mach number $M_{\text{crit}}$ in transonic/supersonic flight: $M_{\text{eff}} = M_\infty \cos\Lambda$. |
| **Swept-Forward** | Quarter-chord sweep angle $\Lambda_{1/4} < 0$. | **Root Stall First:** Spanwise flow towards root preserves tip aileron control. | Aerodynamically superior turn performance, but prone to severe **Aeroelastic Divergence** (requires carbon composite tailoring, e.g., Su-47). |
| **Delta Wing** | Triangular shape (low AR, high sweep angle $\Lambda \approx 50^\circ - 70^\circ$). | High $\alpha$ vortex lift; no sudden stall. Leading edge vortices generate additional non-linear lift. | High internal fuel volume, thin profile for supersonic wave drag reduction, but high induced drag at low takeoff/landing speeds. |

### 3. Classification by Tail & Control Surface Configuration

```
  Conventional          T-Tail              V-Tail              Canard
      _|_                 ___                 \ /                __/\__
     | | |               |   |                / \               |  ||  |
     -----               -----               -----               ------
   Tail Aft            Tail Elevated        Ruddervators       Foreplane Lift
```

#### A. Conventional Tail
- Horizontal tail + Vertical tail mounted at the aft end of the fuselage.
- Downward tail load ($L_t < 0$) is required for longitudinal trim in non-canard aircraft:
  $$L_w + L_t = W \implies L_w = W - L_t = W + |L_t|$$
  *(Main wing must generate MORE lift than total aircraft weight to compensate for tail down-force!)*

#### B. T-Tail Configuration
- Horizontal tail mounted on top of the vertical fin.
- **Advantages:** Keeps horizontal tail out of the turbulent wing and propeller wake during normal flight $\implies$ higher control efficiency.
- **Major GATE Danger — DEEP STALL:** At high angles of attack ($\alpha$), the stalled wake of the main wing directly blankets the elevated T-tail. The elevator loses all pitch control effectiveness, trapping the airplane in an unrecoverable high-$\alpha$ stall!

#### C. Canard Configuration
- Small lifting foreplane mounted **ahead of the main wing**.
- **Trim Advantage:** Both the canard and the main wing generate **POSITIVE upward lift**:
  $$\bbox[10px,border:2px solid #4CAF50,background-color:#0d1117]{ L_{\text{total}} = L_{\text{wing}} + L_{\text{canard}} = W }$$
  *(Reduces required main wing size and total induced drag!)*
- **Stall Safety:** The canard is designed to stall **BEFORE** the main wing. When the canard stalls, its lift drops, causing the nose to pitch down automatically before the main wing can stall! (Examples: Rafale, Eurofighter Typhoon, Saab Gripen, Beechcraft Starship).

#### D. V-Tail (Ruddervators)
- Two slanted surfaces combining horizontal and vertical tail functions.
- Uses combined control surfaces called **Ruddervators** (move together for pitch, move oppositely for yaw).
- Reduced wetted area $\implies$ lower parasite drag and reduced radar cross-section (RCS).

### 4. Classification by Landing Gear (Undercarriage) Architecture

```
     TRICYCLE GEAR                     TAILDRAGGER (CONVENTIONAL)
        CG                                    CG
        |                                     |
   +----+----+                           +----+----+
   |         |                           |         |
  (o)       (O)                         (O)       (o)
 Nose      Main                         Main      Tail
Wheel      Wheels                      Wheels     Wheel
```

| Landing Gear Type | Wheel Placement relative to CG | Directional Stability during Ground Roll | Takeoff & Landing Handling |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Tricycle Gear** *(Modern Standard)* | • **Main wheels BEHIND CG**<br>• Single nose wheel forward. | **Directionally STABLE:** If aircraft swerves, friction force on main gear behind CG creates a restoring moment that straightens the nose! | • Excellent forward visibility.<br>• Prevents nose-up tipping.<br>• Level cabin deck angle on ground.<br>• Heavy nose gear structure. |
| **Taildragger (Conventional)** | • **Main wheels AHEAD of CG**<br>• Small tail wheel/skid aft. | **Directionally UNSTABLE:** If aircraft swerves, ground friction force ahead of CG creates a destabilizing moment, causing a severe **Ground Loop** spin! | • Light weight and low parasite drag.<br>• High propeller ground clearance.<br>• Poor forward visibility during taxi.<br>• Prone to nose-over during heavy braking. |
| **Bicycle / Tandem Gear** | • Two main gear sets under fuselage (fore and aft of CG).<br>• Outrigger wheels at wingtips. | Requires precise touchdown attitude; zero rotation capability at takeoff. | Used on thin-wing or VTOL craft (Harrier, U-2, B-52). |

## 🔵 Core Concept 3: Rotary-Wing Aircraft (Helicopter vs. Autogyro vs. Gyrodyne)

Understanding the power transmission and lift mechanics of rotary-wing aircraft is a favorite GATE conceptual topic!

```
       HELICOPTER                             AUTOGYRO
     Power to Rotor                        Power to Propeller
          ||                                      ||
      (Engine)                                (Engine)
          ||                                      ||
          \/                                      \/
   Main Rotor Driven                     Forward Thrust Only
(Pushes Air Downwards)                 (Free Autorotation Lift)
```

| Feature | Helicopter | Autogyro (Gyrocopter) | Gyrodyne |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Main Rotor Power** | **Directly driven by engine** in all flight regimes. | **UNPOWERED in flight!** Engine powers forward propeller ONLY. | Engine powers rotor for hover; auxiliary propulsor for cruise. |
| **Lift Mechanism** | Rotor driven by engine creates relative wind over blades. | **Autorotation:** Forward motion forces air UPWARD through rotor disk, spinning blades aerodynamically! | Rotor in hover; wing + rotor in cruise. |
| **Hover Capability** | **YES:** Can hover in zero wind. | **NO:** Cannot hover in still air (requires minimum forward airspeed $\approx 15 - 20 \text{ knots}$). | **YES:** Can hover and transition smoothly. |
| **Anti-Torque Tail Rotor** | **REQUIRED:** Engine torque on main rotor creates opposite reaction torque on fuselage (Newton's 3rd law). | **NOT REQUIRED:** Main rotor spins freely on bearings $\implies$ Zero torque transmitted to fuselage! | Required during hover; aerodynamic rudder in cruise. |
| **Stall Safety** | Engine failure requires transition to autorotation mode. | **Inherently Immune to Fixed-Wing Stall:** Rotor is always autorotating as long as craft moves forward or descends. | High safety margin. |

## 🔵 Core Concept 4: Flight Speed & Flow Regimes

Flight regimes are categorized by the **Free-stream Mach Number ($M_\infty = V_\infty / a_\infty$)**:

```
0            M_crit         0.8          1.2                       5.0
+---------------|------------+------------+-------------------------+------------> Mach Number
|   Subsonic    | Transonic  | Transonic  |       Supersonic        | Hypersonic
| (Incompressible)| (Local M=1)| (Mixed M)  |   (Oblique Shocks)      | (High Temp)
```

### 1. Subsonic ($M_\infty < 0.8$, typically $M < 0.3$ for Incompressible)
- Flow is entirely subsonic over the entire aircraft surface ($M_{\text{local}} < 1.0$).
- No shock waves present.
- Pressure disturbances propagate upstream (signaled ahead of aircraft).

### 2. Transonic ($0.8 \le M_\infty \le 1.2$)
- Mixed flow region: Local flow speed reaches $M_{\text{local}} = 1.0$ at points of maximum curvature on the wing (at $M_\infty = M_{\text{crit}}$, the **Critical Mach Number**).
- Shock waves form on the wing surface, causing **Wave Drag** and **Shock-Induced Boundary Layer Separation**.
- Swept wings, supercritical airfoils, and area-ruling (waisted fuselage) are used to delay $M_{\text{crit}}$.

### 3. Supersonic ($1.2 < M_\infty \le 5.0$)
- Flow is entirely supersonic over the aircraft ($M_{\text{local}} > 1.0$ everywhere).
- Attached or detached **Bow Shock Waves** and **Expansion Fans** dominate aerodynamic forces.
- Sharp leading edges, delta wings, and low aspect ratio planforms minimize wave drag.

### 4. Hypersonic ($M_\infty > 5.0$)
- Extreme aerodynamic heating ($\Delta T \propto M_\infty^2$).
- Shock layer lies extremely close to body surface (thin shock layer).
- Chemical dissociation and ionization of air molecules occur (high-temperature gas dynamics).

## ⚡ AIR-1 Mnemonics & Speed Tricks

### 🧠 Mnemonic 1: "T-L-M-P" (Wing Height Stability Rule)
- **High-Wing** = **High Pendulum Stability** (Weight below lift line $\implies +3^\circ$ effective dihedral).
- **Low-Wing** = **Needs Dihedral** (Weight above lift line $\implies$ unstable without $+4^\circ$ wing dihedral).
- **Mid-Wing** = **Zero Pendulum Effect** (Symmetric, pure maneuverability).

### 🧠 Mnemonic 2: "C-A-N-A-R-D" (Positive Lift Advantage)
- **C**anard **A**lways **N**ose-up **A**dds **R**ightward **D**own-to-Up Lift!
- Canard lift is **POSITIVE** ($L_w + L_c = W$), whereas conventional tail lift is **NEGATIVE** ($L_w - L_t = W$).

### ⚡ Speed Trick 1: The 5-Second Autogyro Identification Rule
Ask one question: *Is the engine connected to the main rotor shaft during forward flight?*
- **YES** $\implies$ Helicopter (Requires tail rotor to stop reaction torque).
- **NO** $\implies$ Autogyro (Rotor spins freely via autorotation; zero reaction torque!).

### ⚡ Speed Trick 2: Landing Gear Ground Loop Predictor
- **Main Gear BEHIND CG** (Tricycle) $\implies$ **STABLE** ground roll (Self-correcting).
- **Main Gear AHEAD of CG** (Taildragger) $\implies$ **UNSTABLE** ground roll (Tends to ground loop).

## 📝 Solved GATE & Exam-Standard Examples

### Example 1: Trim Lift Comparison (Canard vs. Conventional Tail)
**Problem:** A 5,000 kg aircraft is flying in steady level flight at 100 m/s. 
- **Configuration A (Conventional):** Center of gravity is $0.2 \text{ m}$ ahead of wing aerodynamic center. The tail is located $4.0 \text{ m}$ behind the CG.
- **Configuration B (Canard):** Center of gravity is $0.2 \text{ m}$ behind wing aerodynamic center. The canard is located $3.0 \text{ m}$ ahead of the CG.

Calculate the main wing lift ($L_{\text{wing}}$) required for both configurations. (Take $g = 9.81 \text{ m/s}^2$).

**Solution:**
Gross Weight $W = 5000 \times 9.81 = \mathbf{49,050 \text{ N}}$.

#### Configuration A (Conventional):
Pitching moment equilibrium about CG:
$$\sum M_{\text{CG}} = 0 \implies L_{\text{wing}} \times (0.2) + L_{\text{tail}} \times (4.0) = 0$$
$$L_{\text{tail}} = -0.05 \times L_{\text{wing}} \quad (\text{Downward tail load})$$

Vertical force equilibrium:
$$L_{\text{wing}} + L_{\text{tail}} = W \implies L_{\text{wing}} - 0.05 L_{\text{wing}} = 49050$$
$$0.95 L_{\text{wing}} = 49050 \implies \mathbf{L_{\text{wing, Conv}} = 51,631.6 \text{ N}}$$

#### Configuration B (Canard):
Pitching moment equilibrium about CG:
$$\sum M_{\text{CG}} = 0 \implies -L_{\text{wing}} \times (0.2) + L_{\text{canard}} \times (3.0) = 0$$
$$L_{\text{canard}} = +0.0667 \times L_{\text{wing}} \quad (\text{Upward canard load})$$

Vertical force equilibrium:
$$L_{\text{wing}} + L_{\text{canard}} = W \implies L_{\text{wing}} + 0.0667 L_{\text{wing}} = 49050$$
$$1.0667 L_{\text{wing}} = 49050 \implies \mathbf{L_{\text{wing, Canard}} = 45,982.9 \text{ N}}$$

**Key Insight:** The canard main wing needs **$10.9\%$ LESS lift** than the conventional main wing to carry the same aircraft weight! This directly reduces main wing surface area and induced drag.

### Example 2: Wing Stall Progression MCQ
**Problem:** Which of the following wing planforms exhibits a stall pattern that begins at the wing root, leaving the ailerons effective during the initial stages of a stall?
- (A) Elliptical Wing
- (B) Swept-back Wing
- (C) Rectangular Wing
- (D) Highly Tapered Wing

**Solution:**
- Elliptical wing stalls everywhere simultaneously (A is incorrect).
- Swept-back and highly tapered wings stall at the wingtip first, causing immediate loss of aileron control and pitch-up (B and D are incorrect).
- **Rectangular wings** have highest local $C_L$ at the root, so flow separation starts at the wing root first while tips remain unstalled (C is correct).

**Correct Answer:** **(C) Rectangular Wing**

### Example 3: Ground Stability of Landing Gear (GATE Concept)
**Problem:** Explain why a tricycle landing gear configuration is directionally stable during a high-speed landing roll, whereas a taildragger configuration is directionally unstable.

**Solution:**
Consider a small perturbation yaw angle $\beta$ during ground roll:
1. **Tricycle Gear:** The main wheels are located **behind the CG**. When the aircraft yaws to the right, ground friction forces acting on the main wheels point opposite to the direction of motion, creating a **restoring moment about the CG** that turns the nose back into alignment.
2. **Taildragger:** The main wheels are located **ahead of the CG**. When the aircraft yaws to the right, ground friction forces acting on the main wheels create a **destabilizing moment about the CG** that increases the yaw angle further, leading to a violent **ground loop** spin if uncorrected.

## ⚠️ Common GATE Traps & Exam Pitfalls

> [!CAUTION]
> 1. **Autogyro Torque Confusion:** Autogyros do **NOT** require a tail rotor for anti-torque because the main rotor is driven strictly by aerodynamic airflow (autorotation), not by an engine drive shaft!
> 2. **Canard Stall Sequence:** The canard surface MUST stall **BEFORE** the main wing (not after). If the main wing stalls first, the positive canard lift will pitch the nose up further, leading to a catastrophic deep stall!
> 3. **T-Tail Deep Stall:** T-tails improve horizontal tail aerodynamic efficiency in normal flight, but make the craft vulnerable to **Deep Stall** at high $\alpha$ because the stalled turbulent wake of the main wing directly blankets the tail.
> 4. **Swept-Forward Divergence:** Swept-forward wings stall at the root first (good!), but they suffer from **Aeroelastic Divergence** (structural twisting under aerodynamic load that can snap the wing off) unless stiffened with composite materials.
> 5. **Elliptical Wing Myth:** While elliptical wings minimize induced drag ($e=1$), they are rarely used in modern aircraft because they are extremely expensive to manufacture and exhibit dangerous simultaneous wing stall without root warning.

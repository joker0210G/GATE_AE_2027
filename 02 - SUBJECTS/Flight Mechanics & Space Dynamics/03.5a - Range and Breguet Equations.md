## 📌 Executive Summary & GATE Significance

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Aircraft Range ($R$)** is the total horizontal distance traveled relative to the ground for a consumed fuel mass ($W_0 \to W_1$):
> 1. **Breguet Range Equation for Propeller Aircraft:**
>    $$\bbox[12px,border:3px solid #2196F3,background-color:#0d1117]{ R_{\text{prop}} = \frac{\eta_p}{c_p} \left(\frac{C_L}{C_D}\right) \ln\left(\frac{W_0}{W_1}\right) }$$
> 2. **Breguet Range Equation for Jet Aircraft (Cruise-Climb Profile):**
>    $$\bbox[12px,border:3px solid #E91E63,background-color:#0d1117]{ R_{\text{jet, Cruise-Climb}} = \frac{2}{c_t} \sqrt{\frac{2}{\rho_0 S}} \left(\frac{C_L^{1/2}}{C_D}\right) \left[ \sqrt{W_0} - \sqrt{W_1} \right] }$$
> where $c_p$ is power specific fuel consumption, $c_t$ is thrust specific fuel consumption ($TSFC$), $W_0$ is initial gross takeoff weight, and $W_1$ is final empty weight.

### Why does GATE AE care?
1. **Propeller vs. Jet Range Derivations:** GATE AE frequently tests deriving and computing range for propeller aircraft (maximized at $(L/D)_{\max}$) versus jet aircraft (maximized at $(C_L^{1/2}/C_D)_{\max}$).
2. **Cruise Profiles:** Distinguishing **Cruise-Climb** ($\sqrt{W_0} - \sqrt{W_1}$) from **Constant Altitude / Constant Velocity** ($\ln(W_0/W_1)$) for jet aircraft.
3. **Specific Fuel Consumption Units:** Handling unit conversions for $c_t$ ($\text{N}/(\text{N}\cdot\text{s}) = \text{s}^{-1}$) and $c_p$ ($\text{N}/(\text{W}\cdot\text{s})$ or $\text{kg}/(\text{kW}\cdot\text{hr})$).

## 🔵 Core Concept 1: Engine Fuel Consumption Models & Kinematics

```
                     AIRCRAFT RANGE KINEMATIC INTEGRATION
                     
      Initial State (Weight W_0)                             Final State (Weight W_1)
      +------------------------+----------------------------+------------------------+
      |  Fuel Weight W_fuel    |=====> Flight Distance R ===>|  Empty Weight W_1      |
      +------------------------+----------------------------+------------------------+
                                dx = V * dt = V * (dt / dW) * dW
```

### 1. Kinematic Relationship
Distance covered $dx = V \cdot dt$.
Expressing time element $dt$ in terms of fuel weight burned $dW$:

$$dx = -V \cdot \frac{dt}{dW} \cdot dW$$

---

### 2. Propeller Engine Fuel Consumption Rate
For propeller engines, fuel consumption rate is proportional to **shaft power output $P$**:

$$\frac{dW}{dt} = -c_p \cdot P = -c_p \left( \frac{T \cdot V}{\eta_p} \right) = -\frac{c_p \cdot D \cdot V}{\eta_p}$$

where:
- $c_p$: Power specific fuel consumption ($\text{N} / (\text{W}\cdot\text{s})$ or $\text{1/m}$)
- $\eta_p$: Propeller efficiency ($\eta_p \le 1.0$)

---

### 3. Jet Engine Fuel Consumption Rate
For jet engines, fuel consumption rate is proportional to **thrust output $T$**:

$$\frac{dW}{dt} = -c_t \cdot T = -c_t \cdot D$$

where:
- $c_t$: Thrust specific fuel consumption ($TSFC$, $\text{N} / (\text{N}\cdot\text{s}) = \text{s}^{-1}$)

---

## 🔵 Core Concept 2: Breguet Range Equation for Propeller Aircraft

For a propeller aircraft in steady level flight ($T = D, L = W$):

$$dx = -V \cdot \left( \frac{\eta_p}{c_p D V} \right) dW = -\frac{\eta_p}{c_p} \left( \frac{L}{D} \right) \frac{dW}{W}$$

Integrating from $W = W_0$ to $W = W_1$:

$$R = -\frac{\eta_p}{c_p} \left( \frac{C_L}{C_D} \right) \int_{W_0}^{W_1} \frac{dW}{W} = \frac{\eta_p}{c_p} \left( \frac{C_L}{C_D} \right) \ln\left(\frac{W_0}{W_1}\right)$$

$$\bbox[12px,border:3px solid #2196F3,background-color:#0d1117]{ R_{\text{prop}} = \frac{\eta_p}{c_p} \left(\frac{C_L}{C_D}\right) \ln\left(\frac{W_0}{W_1}\right) }$$

### Optimum Condition for Maximum Propeller Range:
To maximize range for a propeller aircraft, the aerodynamic term $\frac{C_L}{C_D}$ must be maximized:

$$\bbox[12px,border:3px solid #E91E63,background-color:#0d1117]{ C_L = C_{L,(L/D)_{\max}} = \sqrt{\frac{C_{D,0}}{K}} \implies \mathbf{Parasite \ Drag = Induced \ Drag} \ (C_{D,0} = K C_L^2) }$$

- **Optimum Propeller Range Speed:**
  $$V_{\text{range, prop}} = V_{(L/D)_{\max}} = \mathbf{1.00 \cdot V_{(L/D)_{\max}}}$$

---

## 🔵 Core Concept 3: Breguet Range Equations for Jet Aircraft

For a jet aircraft in steady level flight ($T = D, L = W$):

$$dx = -V \cdot \left( \frac{1}{c_t D} \right) dW = -\frac{V}{c_t} \left( \frac{L}{D} \right) \frac{dW}{W}$$

Substitute airspeed $V = \sqrt{\frac{2 W}{\rho S C_L}}$:

$$dx = -\frac{1}{c_t} \sqrt{\frac{2}{\rho S}} \left( \frac{C_L^{1/2}}{C_D} \right) \frac{dW}{\sqrt{W}}$$

```
                       JET AIRCRAFT CRUISE PROFILES
                                    |
    +-------------------------------+-------------------------------+
    |                                                               |
CRUISE-CLIMB PROFILE                                    CONSTANT ALTITUDE & VELOCITY
Constant C_L & Constant V_EAS                           Constant Altitude h & Speed V
Altitude increases as weight drops (rho drops)          C_L drops as weight drops
Range ~ ( sqrt(W0) - sqrt(W1) )                         Range ~ ln(W0 / W1)
```

### 1. Cruise Profile 1: Cruise-Climb Profile (Constant $C_L$ & Constant $V_{\text{EAS}}$)
As fuel burns ($W \downarrow$), the pilot climbs to higher altitude so that air density $\rho \downarrow$ keeping $\frac{W}{\rho}$ and $C_L$ constant.

Integrating $dx = -\frac{1}{c_t} \sqrt{\frac{2}{\rho_0 S}} \left( \frac{C_L^{1/2}}{C_D} \right) \frac{dW}{\sqrt{W}}$:

$$\bbox[12px,border:3px solid #E91E63,background-color:#0d1117]{ R_{\text{jet, Cruise-Climb}} = \frac{2}{c_t} \sqrt{\frac{2}{\rho_0 S}} \left(\frac{C_L^{1/2}}{C_D}\right) \left[ \sqrt{W_0} - \sqrt{W_1} \right] }$$

where $\rho_0$ is sea-level air density if using equivalent airspeed, or initial density $\rho_1$.

---

### 2. Cruise Profile 2: Constant Altitude & Constant Speed ($h = \text{const}, V = \text{const}$)
If altitude $h$ and airspeed $V$ are held constant, $C_L$ drops as weight drops. Using average Lift-to-Drag ratio $(L/D)_{\text{avg}}$:

$$\bbox[12px,border:3px solid #9C27B0,background-color:#0d1117]{ R_{\text{jet, Const h,V}} = \frac{V}{c_t} \left(\frac{L}{D}\right)_{\text{avg}} \ln\left(\frac{W_0}{W_1}\right) }$$

---

### 3. Optimum Condition for Maximum Jet Range:
To maximize jet range, the aerodynamic factor **$\frac{C_L^{1/2}}{C_D}$** must be maximized:

$$\bbox[12px,border:3px solid #E91E63,background-color:#0d1117]{ C_L = C_{L,\text{jet range}} = \sqrt{\frac{C_{D,0}}{3 K}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}} \cdot C_{L,(L/D)_{\max}} }$$

$$\mathbf{Parasite \ Drag = 3 \times Induced \ Drag} \ (C_{D,0} = 3 K C_L^2)$$

- **Optimum Jet Range Speed:**
  $$\bbox[10px,border:2px solid #4CAF50,background-color:#0d1117]{ V_{\text{range, jet}} = 3^{1/4} \cdot V_{(L/D)_{\max}} = \mathbf{1.316 \cdot V_{(L/D)_{\max}}} }$$

---

## ⚡ AIR-1 Mnemonics & Speed Tricks

### 🧠 Mnemonic 1: "PROP RANGE AT (L/D)max, JET RANGE AT (C_L^(1/2)/C_D)max"
- Propeller aircraft max range $\implies (L/D)_{\max}$ at speed $1.00 \cdot V_m$.
- Jet aircraft max range $\implies (C_L^{1/2}/C_D)_{\max}$ at speed $1.32 \cdot V_m$.

### 🧠 Mnemonic 2: "PROP USES LN(W0/W1), JET CRUISE-CLIMB USES SQRT DIFFERENCE"
- Propeller Range $\propto \ln(W_0/W_1)$.
- Jet Cruise-Climb Range $\propto (\sqrt{W_0} - \sqrt{W_1})$.

### ⚡ Speed Trick 1: 5-Second Propeller Range Formula
$$R_{\text{prop}} = \frac{\eta_p}{c_p} \left(\frac{L}{D}\right) \ln\left(\frac{W_0}{W_1}\right)$$
- If $\eta_p = 0.85, c_p = 8.0 \times 10^{-7} \text{ N}/(\text{W}\cdot\text{s}), (L/D) = 15, W_0/W_1 = 1.30$:
  $$R = \frac{0.85}{8.0 \times 10^{-7}} \times 15 \times \ln(1.30) = 1.0625 \times 10^6 \times 15 \times 0.26236 = \mathbf{4.18 \times 10^6 \text{ m}} = \mathbf{4,180 \text{ km}}.$$

## 📝 Solved GATE & Exam-Standard Examples

### Example 1: Propeller Aircraft Breguet Range (GATE Classic 2-Marker)
**Problem:** A propeller aircraft has a gross takeoff weight $W_0 = 40,000 \text{ N}$ and empty weight $W_1 = 30,000 \text{ N}$. The propeller efficiency is $\eta_p = 0.82$, power specific fuel consumption is $c_p = 7.50 \times 10^{-7} \text{ N}/(\text{W}\cdot\text{s})$, and maximum lift-to-drag ratio is $(L/D)_{\max} = 16.0$.
Calculate the maximum range $R$ of the aircraft in kilometers.

**Solution:**
Using Breguet propeller range equation at $(L/D)_{\max}$:
$$R_{\text{prop}} = \frac{\eta_p}{c_p} \left(\frac{L}{D}\right)_{\max} \ln\left(\frac{W_0}{W_1}\right)$$

1. Calculate Weight Ratio:
   $$\frac{W_0}{W_1} = \frac{40,000}{30,000} = 1.33333$$
   $$\ln\left(\frac{W_0}{W_1}\right) = \ln(1.33333) = \mathbf{0.28768}$$

2. Substitute values into formula:
   $$R_{\text{prop}} = \frac{0.82}{7.50 \times 10^{-7}} \times 16.0 \times 0.28768$$
   $$R_{\text{prop}} = 1,093,333.33 \times 16.0 \times 0.28768 = \mathbf{5,032,533 \text{ m}} = \mathbf{5,032.5 \text{ km}}$$

---

### Example 2: Jet Aircraft Cruise-Climb Range Calculation
**Problem:** A jet transport has gross weight $W_0 = 100,000 \text{ N}$, empty weight $W_1 = 64,000 \text{ N}$, wing area $S = 40.0 \text{ m}^2$, drag polar $C_D = 0.020 + 0.050 C_L^2$, and TSFC $c_t = 0.60 \text{ hr}^{-1} = 1.6667 \times 10^{-4} \text{ s}^{-1}$. Flying a Cruise-Climb profile at sea level equivalent density ($\rho_0 = 1.225 \text{ kg/m}^3$):
1. Calculate optimum $C_L$ and $(C_L^{1/2}/C_D)_{\max}$ for maximum jet range.
2. Calculate maximum range $R$ in kilometers.

**Solution:**
1. **Optimum $C_L$ for Max Jet Range:**
   $$C_{D,0} = 0.020, \quad K = 0.050$$
   $$C_{L, \text{jet}} = \sqrt{\frac{C_{D,0}}{3 K}} = \sqrt{\frac{0.020}{3 \times 0.050}} = \sqrt{\frac{0.020}{0.150}} = \sqrt{0.13333} = \mathbf{0.36515}$$

2. **Optimum $C_D$ for Max Jet Range:**
   $$C_D = C_{D,0} + K C_L^2 = 0.020 + 0.050 \times (0.13333) = 0.020 + 0.00667 = \mathbf{0.02667} \quad (C_D = \frac{4}{3} C_{D,0})$$

3. **Maximum $(C_L^{1/2}/C_D)_{\max}$ Factor:**
   $$\left(\frac{C_L^{1/2}}{C_D}\right)_{\max} = \frac{\sqrt{0.36515}}{0.02667} = \frac{0.60427}{0.02667} = \mathbf{22.657}$$

4. **Calculate Range $R$ using Cruise-Climb Formula:**
   $$R = \frac{2}{c_t} \sqrt{\frac{2}{\rho_0 S}} \left(\frac{C_L^{1/2}}{C_D}\right)_{\max} \left[ \sqrt{W_0} - \sqrt{W_1} \right]$$
   $$\sqrt{W_0} - \sqrt{W_1} = \sqrt{100,000} - \sqrt{64,000} = 316.228 - 252.982 = \mathbf{63.246}$$
   $$\sqrt{\frac{2}{\rho_0 S}} = \sqrt{\frac{2}{1.225 \times 40.0}} = \sqrt{\frac{2}{49.0}} = \sqrt{0.040816} = 0.20203$$
   $$R = \frac{2}{1.6667 \times 10^{-4}} \times 0.20203 \times 22.657 \times 63.246$$
   $$R = 12,000 \times 0.20203 \times 22.657 \times 63.246 = \mathbf{3,472,350 \text{ m}} = \mathbf{3,472.4 \text{ km}}$$

## ⚠️ Common GATE Traps & Exam Pitfalls

> [!CAUTION]
> 1. **Propeller vs Jet Range Formula Swap:**
>    - Propeller Range: $R = \frac{\eta_p}{c_p} \frac{C_L}{C_D} \ln\frac{W_0}{W_1}$ (uses **$(L/D)_{\max}$**).
>    - Jet Range: $R = \frac{2}{c_t} \sqrt{\frac{2}{\rho S}} \frac{C_L^{1/2}}{C_D} (\sqrt{W_0} - \sqrt{W_1})$ (uses **$(C_L^{1/2}/C_D)_{\max}$**).
> 2. **TSFC Unit Conversion Trap:** $TSFC$ $c_t$ is often given in $\text{hr}^{-1}$ (e.g. $0.60 \text{ hr}^{-1}$). Convert to $\text{s}^{-1}$ by dividing by $3,600$: $c_t = \frac{0.60}{3600} = \mathbf{1.6667 \times 10^{-4} \text{ s}^{-1}}$!
> 3. **Weight Square Root vs Logarithm:** For Jet Cruise-Climb profile, use $[\sqrt{W_0} - \sqrt{W_1}]$, NOT $\ln(W_0/W_1)$!
> 4. **Drag Component Ratios:**
>    - Propeller Range Max: Parasite Drag = Induced Drag ($C_{D,0} = K C_L^2$).
>    - Jet Range Max: Parasite Drag = **$3 \times$ Induced Drag** ($C_{D,0} = 3 K C_L^2$).


## 🧠 MASTER MENTAL MODEL — What is Directional (Weathercock) Static Stability?

> **CORE CONCEPT:** **Directional Static Stability** (also known as **Weathercock Stability**) is the inherent tendency of an aircraft to naturally align its nose into the relative wind vector after experiencing a sideslip disturbance ($\beta$).

```
        Relative Wind Vector (V_∞)
               \
                \     Positive Sideslip (+β) [Wind from RIGHT]
                 \   ┌──────────┐
                  \  │ Aircraft │
                   \ │ Nose     │ ───> Yawing Moment (+Cn) [Nose turns RIGHT into wind]
                    └──────────┘
```

### The Golden Criterion for Directional Static Stability

$$\boxed{C_{n_\beta} = \frac{\partial C_n}{\partial \beta} > 0 \quad (\mathbf{POSITIVE\; Derivative!})}$$

Where:
- $\beta$ = Sideslip Angle ($\text{rad}$ or $\text{deg}$). Positive $\beta > 0$ means relative wind comes from the **RIGHT**.
- $C_n = \frac{N}{q_\infty S b}$ = Yawing Moment Coefficient. Positive $C_n > 0$ means nose turns to the **RIGHT** (starboard).

> 🎯 **THE "N-N-P" STABILITY RULE FOR GATE:**
> - **Longitudinal Stability ($C_{m_\alpha}$):** **NEGATIVE** ($C_{m_\alpha} < 0$)
> - **Lateral Stability ($C_{l_\beta}$):** **NEGATIVE** ($C_{l_\beta} < 0$)
> - **Directional Stability ($C_{n_\beta}$):** **POSITIVE** ($\mathbf{C_{n_\beta} > 0}$) $\to$ *"Positive Points Home!"*

---

## 📐 SECTION 1 — Component Contributions to Directional Stability ($C_{n_\beta}$)

The overall directional stability derivative of an aircraft is the sum of contributions from its primary components:

$$\boxed{C_{n_\beta} = C_{n_\beta,\text{wb}} + C_{n_\beta,v} + C_{n_\beta,p}}$$

```
 Component           Contribution to C_n_β          Physical Mechanism
 ─────────           ─────────────────────          ──────────────────
 Wing-Body / Fuse    DESTABILIZING (-ΔC_n_β)        Munk slender-body cross-flow moment
 Wing Sweep          STABILIZING   (+ΔC_n_β)        Windward wing effective velocity boost
 Vertical Tail       STABILIZING   (+ΔC_n_β)        Primary side-force moment aft of CG
 Power / Propeller   DESTABILIZING (-ΔC_n_β)        Tractor prop normal force ahead of CG
```

---

### 1.1 Fuselage & Wing-Body Contribution ($C_{n_\beta,\text{wb}} < 0$) — DESTABILIZING

An inclined fuselage in sideslip behaves aerodynamically like a low-aspect-ratio body at an angle of attack $\beta$.
1. Pressure distribution produces a side force centered ahead of the aircraft CG.
2. This creates a **destabilizing yawing moment** that tends to turn the nose **away** from the relative wind!

$$\boxed{C_{n_\beta,\text{fuse}} = -k_n \cdot k_{R_e} \cdot \frac{S_f \cdot L_f}{S \cdot b} < 0}$$

Where:
- $S_f$ = Fuselage side projected area ($\text{m}^2$)
- $L_f$ = Fuselage total length ($\text{m}$)
- $k_n, k_{R_e}$ = Empirical fuselage shape & Reynolds number factors
- $b$ = Main wingspan ($\text{m}$)

---

### 1.2 Wing Contribution ($C_{n_\beta,w}$)

#### A. Straight Unswept Wing ($\Lambda = 0$)
A straight, symmetric wing produces virtually **zero directional stability** ($C_{n_\beta,w} \approx 0$).

#### B. Swept-Back Wing ($\Lambda > 0$) — STABILIZING
When a swept-back wing experiences a positive sideslip angle ($\beta > 0$):
1. The **windward (right) wing** becomes effectively less swept relative to the flow ($\Lambda_{\text{eff}} = \Lambda - \beta$), increasing its lift and induced drag $C_{D,i}$.
2. The **leeward (left) wing** becomes more swept ($\Lambda_{\text{eff}} = \Lambda + \beta$), reducing its lift and induced drag.
3. The higher drag on the windward wing creates a **restoring nose-right yawing moment** ($C_n > 0$):

$$\boxed{\Delta C_{n_\beta,\text{sweep}} \approx \frac{C_L^2}{4 \pi AR} \tan^2\Lambda > 0}$$

---

### 1.3 Vertical Tail Contribution ($C_{n_\beta,v} > 0$) — PRIMARY STABILIZER

The vertical tail is the primary component designed to provide directional stability.

#### A. Local Vertical Tail Angle of Attack ($\alpha_v$)
The flow reaching the vertical tail is modified by sidewash angle $\sigma$ generated by the fuselage and wing vortex system:

$$\alpha_v = \beta - \sigma = \beta \left(1 + \frac{d\sigma}{d\beta}\right)$$

Where:
- $\sigma$ = Sidewash angle
- $\frac{d\sigma}{d\beta}$ = Sidewash gradient. For typical fuselage-wing combinations, sidewash is **in-phase** with sideslip ($\frac{d\sigma}{d\beta} > 0$, typically $0.1 - 0.3$), which **boosts tail efficiency**!

#### B. Vertical Tail Side Force & Moment
The vertical tail produces a side force $Y_v$ acting at a distance $l_v$ aft of the aircraft CG:

$$Y_v = -q_v S_v C_{L_v} = -q_v S_v a_v \alpha_v = -q_v S_v a_v \beta \left(1 + \frac{d\sigma}{d\beta}\right)$$

The resulting yawing moment about the CG is:

$$N_v = -Y_v \cdot l_v = +q_v S_v l_v a_v \beta \left(1 + \frac{d\sigma}{d\beta}\right)$$

Non-dimensionalizing by dividing by $q_\infty S b$:

$$C_{n,v} = \frac{N_v}{q_\infty S b} = \left(\frac{q_v}{q_\infty}\right) \left(\frac{S_v l_v}{S b}\right) a_v \left(1 + \frac{d\sigma}{d\beta}\right) \beta$$

#### C. The Vertical Tail Volume Ratio ($V_v$)

Defining the **Vertical Tail Volume Ratio ($V_v$)**:

$$\boxed{V_v = \frac{S_v \cdot l_v}{S \cdot b}}$$

Where:
- $S_v$ = Vertical tail planform area ($\text{m}^2$)
- $l_v$ = Distance from aircraft CG to Vertical Tail Aerodynamic Center ($\text{m}$)
- $S$ = Main wing planform area ($\text{m}^2$)
- $b$ = Main wingspan ($\text{m}$) — **CRITICAL: Uses wingspan $b$, NOT MAC $\bar{c}$!**

Defining **Vertical Tail Efficiency Ratio ($\eta_v$)**: $\eta_v = \frac{q_v}{q_\infty} = \frac{\frac{1}{2}\rho_v V_v^2}{\frac{1}{2}\rho_\infty V_\infty^2}$ (typically $0.85 - 1.0$).

Differentiating with respect to $\beta$ gives the **Vertical Tail Directional Stability Contribution**:

$$\boxed{C_{n_\beta,v} = \eta_v \cdot V_v \cdot a_v \left(1 + \frac{d\sigma}{d\beta}\right) > 0}$$

## 📐 SECTION 2 — Vertical Tail Sizing & Geometry Optimization

### 2.1 Benchmark Values of $V_v$ Across Aircraft Classes

| Aircraft Category | Typical $V_v$ Range | Design Drivers |
|-------------------|---------------------|----------------|
| **Single-Engine GA Light Aircraft** | $0.03 - 0.05$ | Weathercock stability, spin recovery |
| **Twin-Engine GA Aircraft** | $0.05 - 0.07$ | One Engine Inoperative (OEI) trim |
| **Commercial Jet Transports (B777, A350)** | $0.06 - 0.09$ | Engine-out trim at takeoff, crosswind landing |
| **Military Jet Fighters (Single Fin)** | $0.04 - 0.07$ | High speed stability, rudder authority |
| **Twin Vertical Fin Fighters (F-15, Su-27)** | Total $V_v \approx 0.06 - 0.08$ | Prevents high-$\alpha$ wing wake blanketing |

### 2.2 Dorsal & Ventral Fins

```
       DORSAL FIN                                   VENTRAL FIN
  Forward extension at root                     Extension below fuselage
     ┌───────────┐                                 ┌───────────┐
    ╱│           │                                 │           │
   ╱ │ Vertical  │                                 └───────────┘
  ╱  │ Tail      │                                    ╱ │ Ventral
 ────┴───────────┘                                   ╱  │ Fin
                                                    ────┴
```

#### A. Dorsal Fin (Forward extension at vertical tail root)
- **Primary Function:** Delays vertical tail stall at high sideslip angles ($\beta > 15^\circ - 20^\circ$).
- **Mechanism:** The low-aspect-ratio triangular dorsal fin sheds a strong leading-edge vortex that re-energizes the boundary layer over the main vertical tail, preventing **Rudder Lock** (uncontrollable full-rudder deflection due to tail stall).

#### B. Ventral Fin (Bottom extension below rear fuselage)
- **Primary Function:** Provides directional stability at **high angles of attack ($\alpha$)**.
- **Mechanism:** At high $\alpha$, the upper vertical tail becomes submerged in the wing/fuselage wake ($q_v \downarrow$). The ventral fin extends below into clean flow, maintaining $C_{n_\beta} > 0$.

## 📐 SECTION 3 — Rudder Controls & Directional Trim

### 3.1 Rudder Deflection Sign Convention ($\delta_r$)

By international convention:
- **$\delta_r > 0$ (Positive Rudder):** Trailing edge deflected to the **LEFT** (port).
- **Aerodynamic Result:** Trailing edge LEFT produces a side force to the RIGHT ($Y > 0$), creating a **nose-LEFT (negative) yawing moment ($N < 0$)**.

$$\boxed{C_{n_{\delta_r}} = \frac{\partial C_n}{\partial \delta_r} = -\eta_v \cdot V_v \cdot a_v \cdot \tau_r < 0 \quad (\mathbf{ALWAYS\; NEGATIVE!})}$$

Where:
- $\tau_r$ = Rudder control effectiveness factor ($\approx 0.4 - 0.6$)
- $C_{n_{\delta_r}}$ = Rudder Control Power ($\text{rad}^{-1}$)

---

### 3.2 One Engine Inoperative (OEI) Trim & Minimum Control Speed ($V_{mc}$)

For a multi-engine aircraft, failure of an outboard engine creates a massive yawing moment:

$$N_{\text{thrust}} = T \cdot y_p$$

Where $T$ = Thrust of operating engine, $y_p$ = Lateral distance of engine from fuselage centerline.

```
       Operating Engine (Thrust T)            Failed Engine (T = 0)
              ┌───┐                                  ┌───┐
              │ T │                                  │ 0 │
    ──────────┴───┴──────────────────────────────────┴───┴──────────> Flow
                    |<──────────── y_p ────────────>|
                                    CG
```

#### A. OEI Trim Equation
Setting net yawing moment to zero ($C_{n,\text{total}} = 0$):

$$C_{n_\beta} \beta + C_{n_{\delta_r}} \delta_r + \frac{T \cdot y_p}{q_\infty S b} = 0$$

Solving for required rudder deflection $\delta_{r,\text{trim}}$:

$$\boxed{\delta_{r,\text{trim}} = -\frac{T \cdot y_p}{q_\infty S b C_{n_{\delta_r}}} - \left(\frac{C_{n_\beta}}{C_{n_{\delta_r}}}\right) \beta}$$

---

#### B. Minimum Control Speed ($V_{mc}$)

The **Minimum Control Speed ($V_{mc}$)** is the lowest airspeed at which full rudder deflection ($\delta_{r,\max}$) can maintain directional control after an engine failure.

Setting $\delta_r = \delta_{r,\max}$ and $\beta = 0$ (wings level):

$$q_{\infty,\min} S b |C_{n_{\delta_r}}| \delta_{r,\max} = T_{\max} \cdot y_p$$

$$\frac{1}{2} \rho_\infty V_{mc}^2 S b |C_{n_{\delta_r}}| \delta_{r,\max} = T_{\max} \cdot y_p$$

$$\boxed{V_{mc} = \sqrt{\frac{2 \cdot T_{\max} \cdot y_p}{\rho_\infty \cdot S \cdot b \cdot |C_{n_{\delta_r}}| \cdot \delta_{r,\max}}}}$$

> 🎯 **CRITICAL GATE INSIGHT FOR $V_{mc}$:**
> - $V_{mc} \propto \sqrt{T_{\max}}$ $\to$ Higher engine thrust **INCREASES $V_{mc}$**.
> - $V_{mc} \propto \frac{1}{\sqrt{|C_{n_{\delta_r}}|}}$ $\to$ Larger vertical tail / rudder power **DECREASES $V_{mc}$**.
> - $V_{mc} \propto \frac{1}{\sqrt{\rho_\infty}}$ $\to$ Higher altitude (lower density) **DECREASES $V_{mc}$**.

---

### 3.3 Straight Steady Sideslip (SSSS) & Crosswind Trim

During a crosswind approach, the aircraft experiences a sideslip angle $\beta = \arcsin(V_{\text{cross}} / V_\infty)$.

To trim the aircraft in a **Straight Steady Sideslip (zero yaw rate $\dot{r} = 0$, zero roll rate $\dot{p} = 0$):**

1. **Rudder Trim Deflection:**
   $$\boxed{\delta_{r,\text{trim}} = -\left(\frac{C_{n_\beta}}{C_{n_{\delta_r}}}\right) \beta}$$
2. **Bank Angle into Wind ($\phi_{\text{trim}}$):**
   $$\boxed{\phi_{\text{trim}} \approx -\frac{C_{y_\beta} \beta + C_{y_{\delta_r}} \delta_{r,\text{trim}}}{C_L}}$$

> ✈️ **CROSSWIND PILOTING RULE:**
> To fly straight in a crosswind: **Bank INTO the wind ($\phi > 0$) and apply OPPOSITE rudder ($\delta_r < 0$)**!

## 🔑 SECTION 4 — Mnemonics, Shortcuts & Tricks

### 4.1 "N-N-P" Master Sign Rule
> - **$C_{m_\alpha} < 0$** (Negative = Stable pitch)
> - **$C_{l_\beta} < 0$** (Negative = Stable roll)
> - **$C_{n_\beta} > 0$** (Positive = Stable yaw $\to$ *"Positive Points Home!"*)

### 4.2 Vertical Tail Volume Shortcut
$$\boxed{V_v = \frac{S_v \cdot l_v}{S \cdot b} \quad \iff \quad S_v = \frac{V_v \cdot S \cdot b}{l_v}}$$

### 4.3 5-Second Engine-Out Rudder Deflection Shortcut
$$\boxed{\delta_{r,\text{trim}} = \frac{T \cdot y_p}{q_\infty S b |C_{n_{\delta_r}}|}}$$

### 4.4 5-Second Minimum Control Speed Shortcut
$$\boxed{V_{mc} = \sqrt{\frac{2 T_{\max} y_p}{\rho_\infty S b |C_{n_{\delta_r}}| \delta_{r,\max}}}}$$

## 🔢 SECTION 5 — Solved GATE-Style Numerical Examples

### Example 1 — Vertical Tail Volume & Directional Stability Calculation

**Problem:** An aircraft has the following geometric and aerodynamic parameters:
- Wing area $S = 30\text{ m}^2$, Wingspan $b = 10.0\text{ m}$
- Vertical tail area $S_v = 4.0\text{ m}^2$, Distance from CG to vertical tail AC $l_v = 4.8\text{ m}$
- Vertical tail lift slope $a_v = 3.5\text{ rad}^{-1}$, Tail efficiency $\eta_v = 0.95$
- Sidewash gradient $\frac{d\sigma}{d\beta} = 0.20$
- Wing-body contribution $C_{n_\beta,\text{wb}} = -0.06\text{ rad}^{-1}$

**Calculate:**
1. Vertical tail volume ratio $V_v$.
2. Vertical tail directional stability contribution $C_{n_\beta,v}$.
3. Total aircraft directional stability derivative $C_{n_\beta}$.

**Solution:**

**Step 1: Vertical Tail Volume Ratio $V_v$**
$$V_v = \frac{S_v \cdot l_v}{S \cdot b} = \frac{4.0 \times 4.8}{30 \times 10.0} = \frac{19.2}{300} = 0.064$$
$$\boxed{V_v = 0.064}$$

**Step 2: Tail Contribution $C_{n_\beta,v}$**
$$C_{n_\beta,v} = \eta_v \cdot V_v \cdot a_v \left(1 + \frac{d\sigma}{d\beta}\right)$$
$$C_{n_\beta,v} = 0.95 \times 0.064 \times 3.5 \times (1 + 0.20)$$
$$C_{n_\beta,v} = 0.2128 \times 1.20 = 0.25536\text{ rad}^{-1}$$
$$\boxed{C_{n_\beta,v} = +0.2554\text{ rad}^{-1}}$$

**Step 3: Total Aircraft Directional Stability $C_{n_\beta}$**
$$C_{n_\beta} = C_{n_\beta,\text{wb}} + C_{n_\beta,v} = -0.06 + 0.25536 = +0.19536\text{ rad}^{-1}$$
$$\boxed{C_{n_\beta} = +0.1954\text{ rad}^{-1} \quad (\text{Statically Stable ✅})}$$

---

### Example 2 — Minimum Control Speed ($V_{mc}$) Calculation (GATE AE Classic)

**Problem:** A twin-engine transport aircraft experiences an engine failure at sea level ($\rho_0 = 1.225\text{ kg/m}^3$).
Given:
- Thrust of operating engine $T_{\max} = 45\text{ kN} = 45000\text{ N}$
- Lateral distance of engine from centerline $y_p = 3.5\text{ m}$
- Wing area $S = 80\text{ m}^2$, Wingspan $b = 20.0\text{ m}$
- Rudder control power $C_{n_{\delta_r}} = -0.12\text{ rad}^{-1} = -0.002094\text{ deg}^{-1}$
- Maximum rudder deflection $\delta_{r,\max} = 25^\circ = 0.4363\text{ rad}$

Calculate the Minimum Control Speed $V_{mc}$ in $\text{m/s}$.

**Solution:**
$$V_{mc} = \sqrt{\frac{2 \cdot T_{\max} \cdot y_p}{\rho_\infty \cdot S \cdot b \cdot |C_{n_{\delta_r}}| \cdot \delta_{r,\max}}}$$
$$V_{mc} = \sqrt{\frac{2 \times 45000 \times 3.5}{1.225 \times 80 \times 20.0 \times 0.12 \times 0.4363}}$$
$$V_{mc} = \sqrt{\frac{315000}{1.225 \times 80 \times 20 \times 0.052356}} = \sqrt{\frac{315000}{102.618}} = \sqrt{3069.64} \approx 55.404\text{ m/s}$$

$$\boxed{V_{mc} \approx 55.40\text{ m/s} \quad (107.7\text{ knots})}$$

---

### Example 3 — Engine-Out Rudder Trim Deflection

**Problem:** For the aircraft in Example 2, if it is flying at $V_\infty = 80\text{ m/s}$ at sea level, calculate the required rudder deflection $\delta_{r,\text{trim}}$ in degrees to maintain directional trim after engine failure.

**Solution:**

Dynamic pressure:
$$q_\infty = \frac{1}{2} \rho_0 V_\infty^2 = \frac{1}{2} \times 1.225 \times 80^2 = 3920\text{ Pa}$$

Rudder trim equation (assuming $\beta = 0$):
$$\delta_{r,\text{trim}} = -\frac{T \cdot y_p}{q_\infty S b C_{n_{\delta_r}}}$$
$$\delta_{r,\text{trim}} = -\frac{45000 \times 3.5}{3920 \times 80 \times 20.0 \times (-0.12)}$$
$$\delta_{r,\text{trim}} = \frac{157500}{7526400} = 0.020926\text{ rad}$$

Convert to degrees:
$$\delta_{r,\text{trim}} = 0.020926 \times 57.2958 \approx 1.199^\circ$$

$$\boxed{\delta_{r,\text{trim}} \approx +1.20^\circ \quad (\text{Rudder deflected } 1.20^\circ \text{ LEFT})}$$

## ⚠️ SECTION 6 — Critical GATE Exam Traps

### Trap 1: Using MAC Instead of Wingspan for $V_v$
> - **Vertical Tail Volume Ratio ($V_v$):** Uses Wingspan $b$ $\to \mathbf{V_v = \frac{S_v l_v}{S b}}$
> - **Horizontal Tail Volume Ratio ($V_H$):** Uses MAC $\bar{c}$ $\to \mathbf{V_H = \frac{S_t l_t}{S \bar{c}}}$

### Trap 2: Sign of Directional Stability Derivative ($C_{n_\beta}$)
> Unlike pitch ($C_{m_\alpha} < 0$) and roll ($C_{l_\beta} < 0$), **Directional Static Stability requires $C_{n_\beta} > 0$ (POSITIVE)**!

### Trap 3: Sign of Rudder Deflection ($\delta_r$) and Rudder Power ($C_{n_{\delta_r}}$)
> Positive rudder $\delta_r > 0$ is LEFT deflection, creating nose-LEFT moment ($C_n < 0$).
> Thus, $C_{n_{\delta_r}}$ is **ALWAYS NEGATIVE** ($C_{n_{\delta_r}} < 0$).

### Trap 4: Sidewash Factor $(1 + d\sigma/d\beta)$
> Sidewash usually increases vertical tail effectiveness: $(1 + d\sigma/d\beta) > 1.0$ (PLUS sign, unlike downwash $(1 - d\epsilon/d\alpha) < 1.0$!).

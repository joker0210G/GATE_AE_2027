## 📌 Executive Summary & GATE Significance

> [!IMPORTANT]
> The **Load Factor ($n$)** is the ratio of total aerodynamic Lift ($L$) to aircraft Weight ($W$):
> 1. **Definition of Load Factor ($n$):**
>    $$\bbox[12px,border:3px solid #2196F3,background-color:#0d1117]{ n = \frac{L}{W} }$$
> 2. **Accelerated Stall Speed ($V_{s,n}$):**
>    $$\bbox[12px,border:3px solid #E91E63,background-color:#0d1117]{ V_{s,n} = V_s \sqrt{n} = \sqrt{\frac{2 n W}{\rho_\infty S C_{L,\max}}} }$$
> 3. **Level Coordinated Turn Load Factor & Radius:**
>    $$\bbox[10px,border:2px solid #4CAF50,background-color:#0d1117]{ n = \frac{1}{\cos\phi} = \sec\phi \quad \text{and} \quad R = \frac{V^2}{g \sqrt{n^2 - 1}} }$$
> where $\phi$ is bank angle and $V_s$ is $1g$ unaccelerated stall speed.

### Why does GATE AE care?
1. **Load Factor & Turn Geometry:** GATE AE tests calculating bank angle $\phi$, turn radius $R$, and turn rate $\omega$ for given airspeed $V$ and load factor $n$.
2. **Accelerated Stall Mechanics:** Calculating the elevated stall speed $V_{s,n} = V_s \sqrt{n}$ during high-$g$ maneuvers.
3. **Pull-up & Push-over Load Factors:** Deriving $n = 1 + \frac{V^2}{g R}$ (pull-up) vs. $n = 1 - \frac{V^2}{g R}$ (push-over).

## 🔵 Core Concept 1: Load Factor & Structural Stress

```
                     LOAD FACTOR n = L / W DEFINITION
                     
           n = 1.0 (1g Flight)              n = 2.0 (2g Pull-up / Turn)
               Lift L = W                      Lift L = 2 * W
                 ^                                   ^
                 |                                   |
              +--+--+                             +--+--+
              | CG  |                             | CG  |
              +--+--+                             +--+--+
                 |                                   |
                 v                                   v
             Weight W                            Weight W
```

### 1. Fundamental Definition
Load factor represents the aerodynamic load supported per unit aircraft weight:

$$n = \frac{L}{W}$$

- **$n = 1.0$ ($1g$ Flight):** Unaccelerated level flight ($L = W$).
- **$n > 1.0$ ($>1g$ Flight):** Coordinated turns, pull-ups, gust encounters ($L > W$).
- **$n < 1.0$ / $n < 0$ (Push-over / Inverted Flight):** Push-over maneuvers, negative-$g$ flight ($L < W$).

---

### 2. Accelerated Stall Speed ($V_{s,n}$)
In maneuvering flight, required lift is $L = n \cdot W$. Differentiating stall speed:

$$n \cdot W = \frac{1}{2} \rho_\infty V_{s,n}^2 S C_{L,\max}$$

Dividing by $1g$ stall equation $W = \frac{1}{2}\rho_\infty V_s^2 S C_{L,\max}$:

$$\frac{n \cdot W}{W} = \frac{V_{s,n}^2}{V_s^2} \implies n = \left(\frac{V_{s,n}}{V_s}\right)^2$$

$$\bbox[12px,border:3px solid #E91E63,background-color:#0d1117]{ V_{s,n} = V_s \sqrt{n} }$$

> [!CAUTION]
> **Accelerated Stall Trap:** Flying at $n = 4g$ quadruples required lift $\implies$ stall speed **DOUBLES ($V_{s,4g} = 2 V_s$)**! An aircraft can stall at high airspeeds if pulled to high load factor $n$!

## 🔵 Core Concept 2: Level Coordinated Turn Mechanics

In a steady level coordinated turn banked at angle $\phi$:

```
                  LEVEL COORDINATED TURN FORCE BALANCE (Rear View)
                  
                              Lift L
                                ^
                               /| 
                              / | L * cos(phi) = Weight W
                             /  |
                            /phi|
                           /----+------------> Centripetal Force F_c = L * sin(phi)
                          /     |
                         /   +--+--+
                        /    | CG  |
                             +--+--+
                                |
                                v Weight W
```

### 1. Force Balance Equations
- **Vertical Equilibrium (Level Flight):**
  $$L \cos\phi = W \implies \bbox[12px,border:3px solid #2196F3,background-color:#0d1117]{ n = \frac{L}{W} = \frac{1}{\cos\phi} = \sec\phi }$$
- **Horizontal Centripetal Force:**
  $$L \sin\phi = m \frac{V^2}{R} = \frac{W}{g} \frac{V^2}{R}$$

---

### 2. Turn Radius ($R$) Formula
Dividing horizontal centripetal equation by vertical weight equation:

$$\frac{L \sin\phi}{L \cos\phi} = \frac{\frac{W}{g}\frac{V^2}{R}}{W} \implies \tan\phi = \frac{V^2}{g R}$$

$$R = \frac{V^2}{g \tan\phi}$$

Since $\tan\phi = \sqrt{\sec^2\phi - 1} = \sqrt{n^2 - 1}$:

$$\bbox[12px,border:3px solid #E91E63,background-color:#0d1117]{ R = \frac{V^2}{g \sqrt{n^2 - 1}} }$$

---

### 3. Turn Rate ($\omega \equiv \dot{\psi}$) Formula
Turn rate $\omega$ represents angular rate of turn ($\text{rad/s}$ or $\text{deg/s}$):

$$\omega = \frac{V}{R} = \frac{V}{\frac{V^2}{g \tan\phi}} = \frac{g \tan\phi}{V}$$

$$\bbox[12px,border:3px solid #E91E63,background-color:#0d1117]{ \omega = \frac{g \sqrt{n^2 - 1}}{V} }$$

---

## 🔵 Core Concept 3: Vertical Maneuvers (Pull-Up & Push-Over)

```
                 PULL-UP vs PUSH-OVER MANEUVERS
                 
         PULL-UP MANEUVER (Bottom of Loop)           PUSH-OVER MANEUVER (Top of Loop)
                  Lift L ^                                     Lift L ^
                         |                                            |
                      +--+--+                                      +--+--+
                      | CG  |                                      | CG  |
                      +--+--+                                      +--+--+
                         |                                            |
                         v Weight W                                   v Weight W
               Centripetal Force UP                       Centripetal Force DOWN
               L - W = m * V^2 / R                        W - L = m * V^2 / R
               n = 1 + V^2 / (g*R)                        n = 1 - V^2 / (g*R)
```

### 1. Pull-Up Maneuver (Bottom of Curved Arc / Loop Radius $R$)
At the bottom of a curved vertical pull-up arc, centripetal acceleration is directed **UPWARD**:

$$L - W = m \frac{V^2}{R} \implies L = W + \frac{W}{g} \frac{V^2}{R}$$

Dividing by $W$:

$$\bbox[12px,border:3px solid #2196F3,background-color:#0d1117]{ n_{\text{pull-up}} = 1 + \frac{V^2}{g R} }$$

$$\bbox[10px,border:2px solid #4CAF50,background-color:#0d1117]{ R_{\text{pull-up}} = \frac{V^2}{g (n - 1)} }$$

---

### 2. Push-Over / Pull-Down Maneuver (Top of Curved Arc / Crest Radius $R$)
At the top of a crest arc, centripetal acceleration is directed **DOWNWARD**:

$$W - L = m \frac{V^2}{R} \implies L = W - \frac{W}{g} \frac{V^2}{R}$$

Dividing by $W$:

$$\bbox[12px,border:3px solid #9C27B0,background-color:#0d1117]{ n_{\text{push-over}} = 1 - \frac{V^2}{g R} }$$

---

## ⚡ AIR-1 Mnemonics & Speed Tricks

### 🧠 Mnemonic 1: "TURN LOAD FACTOR = 1 / COS(PHI)"
- Bank angle $\phi = 60^\circ \implies \cos(60^\circ) = 0.50 \implies n = \mathbf{2.0g}$!

### 🧠 Mnemonic 2: "PULL-UP ADDS 1, PUSH-OVER SUBTRACTS FROM 1"
- Pull-Up: $n = 1 + \frac{V^2}{g R}$.
- Push-Over: $n = 1 - \frac{V^2}{g R}$.

### ⚡ Speed Trick 1: 5-Second Accelerated Stall Speed
$$V_{s,n} = V_s \sqrt{n}$$
- Example: If $V_s = 50 \text{ m/s}$ and load factor $n = 9g \implies V_{s,9g} = 50 \times \sqrt{9} = 50 \times 3 = \mathbf{150 \text{ m/s}}$.

## 📝 Solved GATE & Exam-Standard Examples

### Example 1: Coordinated Turn Load Factor & Turn Geometry (GATE Classic 2-Marker)
**Problem:** An jet fighter executes a steady level coordinated turn at airspeed $V = 200 \text{ m/s}$ banked at angle $\phi = 60.0^\circ$. Taking $g = 9.81 \text{ m/s}^2$:
1. Calculate the load factor $n$.
2. Calculate turn radius $R$ in meters.
3. Calculate turn rate $\omega$ in rad/s and deg/s.
4. If unaccelerated stall speed is $V_s = 60 \text{ m/s}$, calculate accelerated stall speed $V_{s,n}$ in the turn.

**Solution:**
1. **Load Factor ($n$):**
   $$n = \frac{1}{\cos\phi} = \frac{1}{\cos(60.0^\circ)} = \frac{1}{0.500} = \mathbf{2.00 g}$$

2. **Turn Radius ($R$):**
   $$\tan(60.0^\circ) = \sqrt{3} = 1.73205$$
   $$R = \frac{V^2}{g \tan\phi} = \frac{200^2}{9.81 \times 1.73205} = \frac{40,000}{16.9914} = \mathbf{2,354.1 \text{ m}}$$

3. **Turn Rate ($\omega$):**
   $$\omega = \frac{V}{R} = \frac{200}{2354.14} = \mathbf{0.08496 \text{ rad/s}}$$
   $$\text{In Degrees/s:} \quad \omega = 0.08496 \times 57.2958 = \mathbf{4.868^\circ/\text{s}}$$

4. **Accelerated Stall Speed ($V_{s,n}$):**
   $$V_{s,n} = V_s \sqrt{n} = 60.0 \times \sqrt{2.0} = 60.0 \times 1.4142 = \mathbf{84.85 \text{ m/s}}$$

---

### Example 2: Pull-Up Maneuver Load Factor
**Problem:** An aircraft flying at $V = 150 \text{ m/s}$ pulls up at the bottom of a vertical loop of radius $R = 750 \text{ m}$. Taking $g = 9.81 \text{ m/s}^2$:
1. Calculate the load factor $n$ experienced by the pilot.
2. If pilot weight is $W_{\text{pilot}} = 700 \text{ N}$, calculate the apparent force felt by the pilot in the seat.

**Solution:**
1. **Pull-up Load Factor ($n$):**
   $$n = 1 + \frac{V^2}{g R} = 1 + \frac{150^2}{9.81 \times 750} = 1 + \frac{22,500}{7357.5} = 1 + 3.058 = \mathbf{4.058 g}$$

2. **Apparent Force Felt by Pilot:**
   $$F_{\text{seat}} = n \cdot W_{\text{pilot}} = 4.058 \times 700 = \mathbf{2,840.6 \text{ N}}$$

## ⚠️ Common GATE Traps & Exam Pitfalls

> [!CAUTION]
> 1. **Pull-Up vs Turn Radius Formula Trap:**
>    - Level Turn Radius: $R = \frac{V^2}{g \tan\phi} = \frac{V^2}{g \sqrt{n^2 - 1}}$.
>    - Pull-Up Radius: $R = \frac{V^2}{g(n - 1)}$.
>    Do NOT use turn formula $\sqrt{n^2-1}$ for a vertical pull-up maneuver!
> 2. **Accelerated Stall Speed Scaling:** $V_{s,n} = V_s \sqrt{n}$, NOT $V_s \cdot n$!
> 3. **Bank Angle Units:** Ensure calculator is in **DEGREES** mode when evaluating $\cos\phi$ or $\tan\phi$.

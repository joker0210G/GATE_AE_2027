## 🧠 MASTER MENTAL MODEL — Physical Definition of Escape Velocity

> **CORE CONCEPT:** **Escape Velocity ($v_{\text{esc}}$)** is the minimum speed required for an unpowered object at distance $r$ from a gravitational primary mass $M$ to overcome its gravitational field and coast to infinity ($r \to \infty$) with zero remaining kinetic energy ($v \to 0$).
>
> At escape speed, the orbit is **Parabolic ($e = 1$)** and the Specific Mechanical Energy is exactly **Zero ($\mathcal{E} = 0$)**.

```
                           ESCAPE TRAJECTORY GEOMETRY
                                               Parabolic Path (e = 1, E = 0)
                                              ╱
                                             ╱
                                            ╱
                    v = v_esc              ●  Coast to Infinity (r ──> ∞, v ──> 0)
                      ● ───────────────────►
                     ╱
                    ● Primary Body M
```

---

## 📐 SECTION 1 — Derivation of Escape Velocity Formula

### 1.1 Energy Conservation Derivation
From the Law of Conservation of Mechanical Energy ($\mathcal{E}_i = \mathcal{E}_f$):

1. **Initial State (at distance $r$):**
   $$\mathcal{E}_i = \text{Specific Kinetic Energy} + \text{Specific Potential Energy} = \frac{v_{\text{esc}}^2}{2} - \frac{\mu}{r}$$

2. **Final State (at $r \to \infty, v \to 0$):**
   $$\mathcal{E}_f = 0 + 0 = 0$$

3. Equating $\mathcal{E}_i = \mathcal{E}_f$:
   $$\frac{v_{\text{esc}}^2}{2} - \frac{\mu}{r} = 0 \implies \frac{v_{\text{esc}}^2}{2} = \frac{\mu}{r}$$

$$\boxed{v_{\text{esc}} = \sqrt{\frac{2 \mu}{r}} = \sqrt{\frac{2 G M}{r}}}$$

---

### 1.2 Surface Escape Velocity & Surface Gravity Relation ($g_0$)
At a planet's surface ($r = R$), substituting $\mu = G M = g_0 R^2$:

$$\boxed{v_{\text{esc, surface}} = \sqrt{2 g_0 R} = \sqrt{\frac{2 \mu}{R}}}$$

For planet Earth ($R_\oplus = 6378\text{ km}, g_0 = 9.81\text{ m/s}^2, \mu_\oplus = 3.986 \times 10^5 \text{ km}^3/\text{s}^2$):

$$v_{\text{esc, Earth}} = \sqrt{2 \times (9.81\text{ m/s}^2) \times (6.378 \times 10^6\text{ m})} = \sqrt{1.25136 \times 10^8} = 11,186\text{ m/s}$$

$$\boxed{v_{\text{esc, Earth}} \approx 11.19\text{ km/s} \quad (11.2\text{ km/s})}$$

---

## 📐 SECTION 2 — Escape Velocity vs. Circular Velocity Master Relation

At any orbital radius $r$:
- **Circular Velocity:** $v_{\text{circ}} = \sqrt{\frac{\mu}{r}}$
- **Escape Velocity:** $v_{\text{esc}} = \sqrt{\frac{2\mu}{r}}$

Dividing the two equations yields the **Master Velocity Ratio**:

$$\boxed{v_{\text{esc}} = \sqrt{2} \cdot v_{\text{circ}} \approx 1.4142 \cdot v_{\text{circ}}}$$

```
                CIRCULAR SPEED vs. ESCAPE SPEED AT DISTANCE r
  ┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
  │ Circular Speed:  v_circ = √(μ / r)      [Bound Orbit, e = 0]           │
  │ Escape Speed:    v_esc  = √2 · v_circ   [Parabolic Escape, e = 1]     │
  │ Impulse Needed:  Δv_esc = (√2 - 1) v_circ ≈ 0.4142 v_circ               │
  └────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

## 📐 SECTION 3 — Key Physical Properties of Escape Velocity

### 3.1 Scalar Nature & Direction Independence
> 🎯 **CRITICAL GATE PRINCIPLE:**
> Escape velocity is a **SCALAR MAGNITUDE**. The direction of launch does NOT affect the required magnitude $v_{\text{esc}}$, provided the trajectory does not intersect the physical surface of the primary body!
>
> *(Launching vertically upward, tangentially, or at a $45^\circ$ angle all require the EXACT SAME speed $v_{\text{esc}} = \sqrt{2\mu/r}$).*

---

### 3.2 Escape Velocity at Altitude ($h_{\text{alt}}$)
As altitude increases ($r = R_\oplus + h_{\text{alt}}$), escape velocity **decreases**:

$$\boxed{v_{\text{esc}}(h_{\text{alt}}) = \sqrt{\frac{2\mu}{R_\oplus + h_{\text{alt}}}} = v_{\text{esc, surface}} \cdot \sqrt{\frac{R_\oplus}{R_\oplus + h_{\text{alt}}}}$$

---

## 📐 SECTION 4 — Hyperbolic Excess Velocity ($v_\infty$)

If a spacecraft is launched with a burn velocity $v_{\text{burn}} > v_{\text{esc}}$, the remaining energy is **positive ($\mathcal{E} > 0$)** and the trajectory is **Hyperbolic ($e > 1$)**.

### Energy Equation for Hyperbola:
$$\mathcal{E} = \frac{v_{\text{burn}}^2}{2} - \frac{\mu}{r} = \frac{v_\infty^2}{2}$$

Where $v_\infty$ is the **Hyperbolic Excess Velocity** (residual speed as $r \to \infty$).

$$\boxed{v_\infty^2 = v_{\text{burn}}^2 - v_{\text{esc}}^2 \implies v_\infty = \sqrt{v_{\text{burn}}^2 - v_{\text{esc}}^2}}$$

$$\boxed{v_{\text{burn}} = \sqrt{v_{\text{esc}}^2 + v_\infty^2}}$$

```
                       HYPERBOLIC EXCESS VELOCITY (v_∞)
                                                  Asymptote Velocity v_∞
                                                ──────────►
                                              ╱
                         Burn Velocity       ╱  Hyperbola (e > 1, E > 0)
                         v_burn > v_esc     ╱
                            ● ─────────────╱
                           ╱
                          ● Earth Center
```

## 🔑 SECTION 6 — Mnemonics, Shortcuts & Tricks

### 6.1 Escape Speed 5-Second Shortcut Matrix
- **From Circular Orbit:** $\Delta v_{\text{esc}} = 0.4142 \cdot v_{\text{circ}}$
- **From Earth Surface:** $v_{\text{esc}} = 11.2\text{ km/s}$
- **From LEO Altitude ($600\text{ km}$):** $v_{\text{esc}} = 10.69\text{ km/s}$
- **Hyperbolic Burn:** $v_{\text{burn}} = \sqrt{v_{\text{esc}}^2 + v_\infty^2}$

### 6.2 Planet Mass & Radius Ratio Shortcut
If Planet X has mass $M_X = k_M M_\oplus$ and radius $R_X = k_R R_\oplus$:

$$\boxed{v_{\text{esc, X}} = v_{\text{esc, Earth}} \cdot \sqrt{\frac{k_M}{k_R}}}$$

## 🔢 SECTION 7 — Solved GATE-Style Numerical Examples

### Example 1 — Escape Velocity & Impulse from LEO Orbit

**Problem:** A $2,000\text{ kg}$ spacecraft is in a circular Low Earth Orbit at altitude $h_{\text{alt}} = 500\text{ km}$ ($R_\oplus = 6378\text{ km}, \mu_\oplus = 398,600\text{ km}^3/\text{s}^2$).

Calculate:
1. Circular orbital speed $v_{\text{circ}}$.
2. Escape velocity $v_{\text{esc}}$ from this altitude.
3. Impulse $\Delta v_{\text{esc}}$ required to place the spacecraft on an escape trajectory.

**Solution:**

**1. Orbital Radius ($r$):**
$$r = R_\oplus + h_{\text{alt}} = 6378 + 500 = 6878\text{ km}$$

**2. Circular Speed ($v_{\text{circ}}$):**
$$v_{\text{circ}} = \sqrt{\frac{\mu}{r}} = \sqrt{\frac{398600}{6878}} = \sqrt{57.9529} = 7.6127\text{ km/s}$$

$$\boxed{v_{\text{circ}} \approx 7.61\text{ km/s}}$$

**3. Escape Speed ($v_{\text{esc}}$):**
$$v_{\text{esc}} = \sqrt{2} \times v_{\text{circ}} = 1.41421 \times 7.6127 = 10.766\text{ km/s}$$

$$\boxed{v_{\text{esc}} \approx 10.77\text{ km/s}}$$

**4. Impulse Required ($\Delta v_{\text{esc}}$):**
$$\Delta v_{\text{esc}} = v_{\text{esc}} - v_{\text{circ}} = 10.766 - 7.6127 = 3.1533\text{ km/s}$$

$$\boxed{\Delta v_{\text{esc}} \approx 3.15\text{ km/s}}$$

---

### Example 2 — Hyperbolic Excess Speed for Mars Mission

**Problem:** A Mars probe is launched from Low Earth Orbit ($r = 6600\text{ km}$) with a burn velocity $v_{\text{burn}} = 11.50\text{ km/s}$. ($\mu_\oplus = 398,600\text{ km}^3/\text{s}^2$).

Calculate:
1. Escape velocity $v_{\text{esc}}$ at $r = 6600\text{ km}$.
2. Hyperbolic excess velocity $v_\infty$ with which the probe leaves Earth's sphere of influence.

**Solution:**

**1. Escape Speed ($v_{\text{esc}}$):**
$$v_{\text{esc}} = \sqrt{\frac{2 \mu}{r}} = \sqrt{\frac{2 \times 398600}{6600}} = \sqrt{\frac{797200}{6600}} = \sqrt{120.7879} = 10.9904\text{ km/s}$$

$$\boxed{v_{\text{esc}} \approx 10.99\text{ km/s}}$$

**2. Hyperbolic Excess Speed ($v_\infty$):**
$$v_\infty^2 = v_{\text{burn}}^2 - v_{\text{esc}}^2 = (11.50)^2 - (10.9904)^2$$
$$v_\infty^2 = 132.25 - 120.7879 = 11.4621\text{ km}^2/\text{s}^2$$
$$v_\infty = \sqrt{11.4621} = 3.3856\text{ km/s}$$

$$\boxed{v_\infty \approx 3.39\text{ km/s}}$$

## ⚠️ SECTION 8 — Critical GATE Exam Traps

### Trap 1: Hyperbolic Excess Speed Subtraction Error
> $v_\infty = \sqrt{v_{\text{burn}}^2 - v_{\text{esc}}^2}$.
> **WRONG:** $v_\infty \ne v_{\text{burn}} - v_{\text{esc}}$! Subtraction MUST occur under the square root of squared speeds (Pythagorean subtraction).

### Trap 2: Direction Independence
> Questions asking *"If launched at 30° to horizontal instead of vertical, what is the escape velocity?"* have the **EXACT SAME ANSWER** $v_{\text{esc}} = \sqrt{2\mu/r}$.

### Trap 3: Altitude Scaling
> Remember that escape velocity DECREASES with altitude ($v_{\text{esc}} \propto 1/\sqrt{r}$).

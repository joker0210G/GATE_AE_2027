## 🧠 MASTER MENTAL MODEL — Linear Momentum Balance in 3D Flight

> **CORE CONCEPT:** Newton's 2nd Law states that the net external force $\vec{F}_{\text{ext}}$ acting on an aircraft equals the time rate of change of its linear momentum $\vec{P} = m \vec{V}_{cg}$:
>
> $$\vec{F}_{\text{ext}} = \left(\frac{d\vec{P}}{dt}\right)_{\text{inertial}} = m \left(\frac{d\vec{V}_{cg}}{dt}\right)_{\text{inertial}}$$
>
> Because an aircraft rotates in space with angular velocity vector $\vec{\Omega} = [p, q, r]^T$, taking derivatives in the **rotating Body-Fixed Frame** introduces centripetal acceleration terms ($\vec{\Omega} \times \vec{V}$)!

```
                    BODY-FIXED COORDINATE SYSTEM (X_b, Y_b, Z_b)
                                   +X_b (Noseward / Forward)
                                     ▲
                                     │   Velocity: V = [u, v, w]^T
                                     │   Angular Velocity: Ω = [p, q, r]^T
                                     │
                 -Y_b (Port) ────────┼────────> +Y_b (Starboard / Right Wing)
                                     │
                                     │
                                     ▼
                                   +Z_b (Bellyward / Downward)
```

---

## 📐 SECTION 1 — Vector Kinematics in a Rotating Frame

### 1.1 Transport Theorem (Coriolis Theorem)

Let $\vec{V} = [u, v, w]^T$ be the velocity vector of the aircraft CG expressed in the body-fixed frame, which rotates relative to the inertial frame with angular velocity $\vec{\Omega} = [p, q, r]^T$.

The time derivative of $\vec{V}$ in the inertial frame is related to its derivative in the rotating body frame by the **Transport Theorem**:

$$\boxed{\left(\frac{d\vec{V}}{dt}\right)_{\text{inertial}} = \left(\frac{d\vec{V}}{dt}\right)_{\text{body}} + \vec{\Omega} \times \vec{V}}$$

Where:
- $\left(\frac{d\vec{V}}{dt}\right)_{\text{body}} = [\dot{u}, \dot{v}, \dot{w}]^T$ = Rate of change of velocity components relative to aircraft body frame.
- $\vec{\Omega} \times \vec{V}$ = Cross product representing centripetal & Coriolis acceleration components.

---

### 1.2 Cross Product Derivation ($\vec{\Omega} \times \vec{V}$)

$$\vec{\Omega} \times \vec{V} = \begin{vmatrix} \hat{i}_b & \hat{j}_b & \hat{k}_b \\ p & q & r \\ u & v & w \end{vmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} q w - r v \\ r u - p w \\ p v - q u \end{bmatrix}$$

Expanding the three scalar acceleration components:

$$\begin{aligned}
a_x &= \dot{u} + q w - r v \\
a_y &= \dot{v} + r u - p w \\
a_z &= \dot{w} + p v - q u
\end{aligned}$$

---

## 📐 SECTION 2 — The 3-Axis Linear Momentum Equations (Newton-Euler Formulation)

Multiplying mass $m$ by each acceleration component yields the **3-Axis Translational Equations of Motion**:

$$\boxed{\begin{aligned}
F_x &= m (\dot{u} + q w - r v) \\
F_y &= m (\dot{v} + r u - p w) \\
F_z &= m (\dot{w} + p v - q u)
\end{aligned}}$$

Where:
- $u, v, w$ = Forward, lateral, and downward velocity components along $X_b, Y_b, Z_b$ ($\text{m/s}$).
- $p, q, r$ = Roll rate, pitch rate, and yaw rate about $X_b, Y_b, Z_b$ ($\text{rad/s}$).
- $F_x, F_y, F_z$ = Total external forces acting along body axes ($\text{N}$).

---

## 📐 SECTION 3 — Breakdown of External Forces ($F_x, F_y, F_z$)

The total external force vector $\vec{F}_{\text{ext}}$ is the sum of **Aerodynamic**, **Propulsive**, and **Gravitational** forces:

$$\vec{F}_{\text{ext}} = \vec{F}_{\text{aero}} + \vec{F}_{\text{prop}} + \vec{F}_{\text{gravity}}$$

```
                       GRAVITY VECTOR RESOLUTION
          Pitch angle θ (tilt up), Roll angle ϕ (bank right)
          ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
          │ F_gx = -m g sin θ                                │
          │ F_gy = +m g cos θ sin ϕ                          │
          │ F_gz = +m g cos θ cos ϕ                          │
          └──────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### Complete 3-Axis Translational Force Equations:

$$\boxed{\begin{aligned}
X_A + X_T - m g \sin\theta &= m (\dot{u} + q w - r v) \quad &\text{(X-Body Axis / Axial)} \\
Y_A + Y_T + m g \cos\theta \sin\phi &= m (\dot{v} + r u - p w) \quad &\text{(Y-Body Axis / Lateral)} \\
Z_A + Z_T + m g \cos\theta \cos\phi &= m (\dot{w} + p v - q u) \quad &\text{(Z-Body Axis / Normal)}
\end{aligned}}$$

Where:
- $X_A, Y_A, Z_A$ = Aerodynamic forces along body axes.
- $X_T, Y_T, Z_T$ = Engine thrust forces along body axes.
- $\theta$ = Pitch Euler angle.
- $\phi$ = Roll Euler angle.

## 📐 SECTION 4 — Special Flight Cases & Simplifications for GATE

### Case 1 — Unaccelerated Steady Level Trimmed Flight
Condition: $\dot{u} = \dot{v} = \dot{w} = p = q = r = 0$, $\phi = 0, \theta = \alpha$:

$$\begin{aligned}
X_A + X_T - m g \sin\theta &= 0 \\
Z_A + Z_T + m g \cos\theta &= 0
\end{aligned}$$

---

### Case 2 — Symmetric Longitudinal Flight (No Roll/Yaw/Sideslip)
Condition: $v = 0, p = 0, r = 0, \phi = 0$:

$$\boxed{\begin{aligned}
X_A + X_T - m g \sin\theta &= m (\dot{u} + q w) \\
Z_A + Z_T + m g \cos\theta &= m (\dot{w} - q u)
\end{aligned}}$$

> 🎯 **PULL-UP MANEUVER SHORTCUT ($a_z = -q u$):**
> In a steady pull-up at speed $V_\infty \approx u$ with pitch rate $q$:
> Normal acceleration is $a_z = \dot{w} - q u = -q V_\infty$.
> Load factor $n = 1 + \frac{q V_\infty}{g} \implies \Delta n = \frac{q V_\infty}{g}$.

---

### Case 3 — Pure Steady Yawing Turn ($r \ne 0$)
Condition: $q = p = 0, \dot{u} = \dot{v} = \dot{w} = 0$:

$$F_x = -m r v, \quad F_y = m r u$$

Centripetal acceleration required to sustain turn of radius $R = V/r$ is $a_c = r u = \frac{V^2}{R}$.

---

## 📐 SECTION 5 — Wind Axes & Flow Angles ($\alpha, \beta$)

Velocity vector $\vec{V} = [u, v, w]^T$ defines total airspeed $V_\infty$ and flow orientation angles:

```
                      WIND ANGLES DEFINITION
      Airspeed:       V_∞ = √(u² + v² + w²)
      Angle of Attack: α = arctan(w / u) ≈ w / u
      Sideslip Angle:  β = arcsin(v / V_∞) ≈ v / V_∞
```

### Velocity Conversion Matrix (Small Angle Approximations):
$$\begin{bmatrix} u \\ v \\ w \end{bmatrix} \approx \begin{bmatrix} V_\infty \\ V_\infty \cdot \beta \\ V_\infty \cdot \alpha \end{bmatrix}$$

Substituting into linear momentum equations for symmetric flight ($v=0, \beta=0$):
$$\dot{u} + q w \approx \dot{V}_\infty + q (V_\infty \alpha)$$
$$\dot{w} - q u \approx V_\infty \dot{\alpha} - q V_\infty = V_\infty (\dot{\alpha} - q)$$

$$\boxed{a_z = V_\infty (\dot{\alpha} - q)}$$

---

## 🔑 SECTION 6 — Mnemonics, Shortcuts & Tricks

### 6.1 Acceleration Cross Product Shortcut
> - **$a_x = \dot{u} + q w - r v$** (Alphabetical order $q w$, minus $r v$)
> - **$a_y = \dot{v} + r u - p w$** (Alphabetical order $r u$, minus $p w$)
> - **$a_z = \dot{w} + p v - q u$** (Alphabetical order $p v$, minus $q u$)

### 6.2 Gravity Vector Sign Rule
> - $X$-gravity: $-m g \sin\theta$ (Opposes pitch up)
> - $Y$-gravity: $+m g \cos\theta \sin\phi$ (Pulls down when banked right)
> - $Z$-gravity: $+m g \cos\theta \cos\phi$ (Pulls down bellyward)

### 6.3 Pull-Up Load Factor Shortcut
$$\boxed{\Delta n = n - 1 = \frac{q V_\infty}{g}}$$

## 🔢 SECTION 7 — Solved GATE-Style Numerical Examples

### Example 1 — Normal Acceleration & Load Factor in Pull-Up

**Problem:** An aircraft flying at $V_\infty = 200\text{ m/s}$ performs a pull-up maneuver with pitch rate $q = 0.15\text{ rad/s}$ ($8.59^\circ/\text{s}$) at an instant where $\dot{w} = 0$.

Calculate:
1. Normal acceleration $a_z$ in the body frame ($\text{m/s}^2$).
2. Additional load factor $\Delta n$ experienced by the pilot.

**Solution:**

**1. Normal Acceleration ($a_z$):**
In symmetric flight ($v=0, p=r=0, u \approx V_\infty$):
$$a_z = \dot{w} - q u = 0 - (0.15 \times 200) = -30\text{ m/s}^2$$

$$\boxed{a_z = -30.0\text{ m/s}^2 \quad (\text{Directed upward towards top of fuselage})}$$

**2. Load Factor Increment ($\Delta n$):**
$$\Delta n = \frac{q V_\infty}{g} = \frac{0.15 \times 200}{9.81} = \frac{30}{9.81} = +3.058$$

Total Load Factor:
$$n = 1 + \Delta n = 1 + 3.058 = +4.058\text{ g}$$

$$\boxed{n \approx 4.06\text{ g}}$$

---

### Example 2 — Axial Acceleration with Engine Thrust & Drag

**Problem:** An aircraft of mass $m = 10,000\text{ kg}$ climbs at pitch angle $\theta = 15^\circ$ with forward speed $u = 150\text{ m/s}$, downward velocity $w = 10\text{ m/s}$, pitch rate $q = 0.05\text{ rad/s}$, and $v = r = 0$.
Given:
- Total forward thrust $X_T = 45,000\text{ N}$
- Total aerodynamic drag $X_A = -12,000\text{ N}$

Calculate forward acceleration $\dot{u}$ in $\text{m/s}^2$.

**Solution:**

**Step 1: X-Body Force Equation**
$$X_A + X_T - m g \sin\theta = m (\dot{u} + q w)$$

**Step 2: Calculate Force Terms**
$$X_A + X_T = -12,000 + 45,000 = +33,000\text{ N}$$
$$m g \sin(15^\circ) = 10,000 \times 9.81 \times 0.25882 = 25,390.24\text{ N}$$

$$\Sigma F_x = 33,000 - 25,390.24 = +7,609.76\text{ N}$$

**Step 3: Centripetal Term ($q w$)**
$$q w = 0.05 \times 10 = 0.50\text{ m/s}^2$$

**Step 4: Solve for $\dot{u}$**
$$\frac{\Sigma F_x}{m} = \dot{u} + q w \implies \frac{7,609.76}{10,000} = \dot{u} + 0.50$$
$$0.76098 = \dot{u} + 0.50 \implies \dot{u} = 0.76098 - 0.50 = 0.26098\text{ m/s}^2$$

$$\boxed{\dot{u} \approx +0.261\text{ m/s}^2}$$

## ⚠️ SECTION 8 — Critical GATE Exam Traps

### Trap 1: Inertial vs. Body Frame Acceleration
> $\left(\frac{d\vec{V}}{dt}\right)_{\text{body}} = [\dot{u}, \dot{v}, \dot{w}]^T$ is NOT the true inertial acceleration. You MUST add the rotational cross-product $\vec{\Omega} \times \vec{V}$!

### Trap 2: Sign of Normal Acceleration ($a_z$)
> $a_z = \dot{w} + p v - q u$. For a nose-up pull-up ($q > 0, u > 0$), $-q u$ is NEGATIVE in the body frame ($+Z_b$ is downward), indicating upward centripetal force!

### Trap 3: Euler Gravity Components
> Gravity components in body frame depend on BOTH pitch $\theta$ AND roll $\phi$:
> $F_{g,x} = -m g \sin\theta$, $F_{g,y} = m g \cos\theta \sin\phi$, $F_{g,z} = m g \cos\theta \cos\phi$.

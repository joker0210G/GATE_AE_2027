## 📌 Executive Summary & GATE Significance

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Indicated Airspeed (IAS)** is the uncorrected differential pressure reading displayed on the cockpit Airspeed Indicator (ASI).  
> **Calibrated Airspeed (CAS)** is IAS corrected for **instrument errors ($\Delta V_{\text{inst}}$)** and **pitot-static position/location errors ($\Delta V_{\text{pos}}$)**:
> $$\bbox[10px,border:2px solid #2196F3,background-color:#0d1117]{ V_{\text{CAS}} = V_{\text{IAS}} + \Delta V_{\text{inst}} + \Delta V_{\text{pos}} }$$

### Why does GATE AE care?
1. **Instrument Calibration Formulas:** GATE tests the mathematical relation between impact pressure ($q_c = p_0 - p$) and CAS using sea-level standard calibration constants ($p_0 = 101325 \text{ Pa}, \rho_0 = 1.225 \text{ kg/m}^3, a_0 = 340.29 \text{ m/s}$).
2. **Blocked Pitot-Static System Diagnostics:** Questions test failure modes of pitot tubes and static ports (e.g., how a trapped pitot tube causes the Airspeed Indicator to act like an altimeter during climbs and descents!).
3. **Operational Speed Limits ($V_{\text{speeds}}$):** Matching operational speed limits ($V_{S0}, V_{S1}, V_{FE}, V_{NO}, V_{NE}$) to aircraft structural and aerodynamic flight boundaries.

## 🔵 Core Concept 1: Pitot-Static Measurement & Instrument Calibration

An Airspeed Indicator (ASI) measures the difference between **Total (Stagnation) Pressure ($p_0$)** from the Pitot tube and **Static Pressure ($p$)** from the static ports.

```
       PITOT TUBE (p0)                        STATIC PORT (p)
    (Stagnation Pressure)                  (Ambient Pressure)
             |                                     |
             v                                     v
       +-------------------------------------------------+
       |         AIRSPEED INDICATOR DIAPHRAGM            |
       |  Internal Pressure = p0  | External = p         |
       |  Diaphragm Expansion = q_c = p0 - p             |
       +-------------------------------------------------+
                               |
                               v
               Mechanical Linkage to ASI Pointer
```

### 1. Differential Impact Pressure ($q_c$)
The diaphragm inside the ASI expands under the differential impact pressure:
$$q_c \equiv p_0 - p$$

### 2. Low-Speed Incompressible Calibration Equation ($M < 0.3$)
The ASI mechanism is calibrated assuming standard sea-level air density $\rho_0 = 1.2250 \text{ kg/m}^3$:
$$\bbox[10px,border:2px solid #2196F3,background-color:#0d1117]{ q_c = p_0 - p = \frac{1}{2} \rho_0 V_{\text{CAS}}^2 \implies V_{\text{CAS}} = \sqrt{\frac{2(p_0 - p)}{\rho_0}} = \sqrt{\frac{2 q_c}{\rho_0}} }$$

### 3. High-Speed Compressible Subsonic Calibration Equation ($0.3 \le M < 1.0$)
Taking air compressibility into account ($\gamma = 1.4$), the ASI is calibrated to the **Saint-Venant / Wantzel Compressible Calibration Formula** using sea-level speed of sound $a_0 = 340.29 \text{ m/s}$ and pressure $p_0 = 101,325 \text{ Pa}$:

$$\bbox[12px,border:3px solid #E91E63,background-color:#0d1117]{ V_{\text{CAS}} = \sqrt{ \frac{2 a_0^2}{\gamma - 1} \left[ \left( 1 + \frac{p_0 - p}{p_0} \right)^{\frac{\gamma - 1}{\gamma}} - 1 \right] } } = \sqrt{ 5 a_0^2 \left[ \left( 1 + \frac{q_c}{p_0} \right)^{0.2857} - 1 \right] }$$

## 🔵 Core Concept 2: Sources of Error (IAS to CAS)

```
  INDICATED AIRSPEED (IAS)
     - Instrument Mechanical Error (Friction, Gear Backlash)
     - Position / Installation Error (Disturbed Pressure Field)
  =============================================================
  = CALIBRATED AIRSPEED (CAS) [True Indicator of Impact Pressure]
```

### 1. Instrument Error ($\Delta V_{\text{inst}}$)
- **Cause:** Mechanical friction in linkages, gear backlash, diaphragm manufacturing tolerances, and pointer misalignment.
- **Correction:** Determined via factory calibration charts. Modern digital Air Data Computers (ADC) reduce $\Delta V_{\text{inst}} \approx 0$.

### 2. Position / Location Error ($\Delta V_{\text{pos}}$)
- **Cause:** Pitot tubes and static ports are mounted on the fuselage or wing. The local airflow field is disturbed by wing lift, angle of attack ($\alpha$), sideslip ($\beta$), and flap/landing gear deployment.
- **Static Port Error:** Local static pressure measured ($p_{\text{measured}}$) differs from true ambient static pressure ($p_{\text{ambient}}$).
- **Correction:** Calibrated during flight testing (e.g., trailing cone or pacer aircraft methods) and documented in the Aircraft Flight Manual (AFM).

## 🔵 Core Concept 3: Operational Airspeed Limits ($V_{\text{Speeds}}$ & ASI Color Arcs)

Cockpit Airspeed Indicators feature color-coded arcs defining structural and aerodynamic safety limits:

```
  White Arc               Green Arc              Yellow Arc       Red Line
|===========|===================================|-------------------|
Vs0        Vfe                         Vno                 Vne
(Flap Stall)(Flap Max)                 (Normal Cruise)     (Never Exceed)
```

| Operational Speed Symbol | Speed Name / Definition | ASI Color Marking | Operational Significance |
|---|---|---|---|
| **$V_{S0}$** | Stall speed in **Landing Configuration** (flaps & landing gear down at max weight). | **Bottom of White Arc** | Minimum steady flight speed for landing. |
| **$V_{S1}$** | Stall speed in **Clean Configuration** (flaps & gear retracted). | **Bottom of Green Arc** | Minimum steady flight speed for cruise/takeoff. |
| **$V_{\text{FE}}$** | Maximum **Flap Extended** speed. | **Top of White Arc** | Exceeding $V_{\text{FE}}$ with flaps extended risks structural flap damage. |
| **$V_{\text{NO}}$** | Maximum **Structural Cruising** speed. | **Top of Green Arc / Bottom of Yellow Arc** | Do not exceed except in smooth air. |
| **$V_{\text{NE}}$** | **Never-Exceed** speed. | **Red Radial Line** | Exceeding $V_{\text{NE}}$ risks structural failure or aeroelastic flutter! |
| **$V_A$** | **Design Maneuvering** speed. | Not marked on dial | Max speed for full control deflection without structural damage. |

## 🔵 Core Concept 4: Blocked Pitot-Static System Failure Modes

A favorite GATE conceptual and diagnostic topic is analyzing how instrument readings react when pitot tubes or static ports become blocked by ice or debris!

```
                    PITOT-STATIC SYSTEM FAILURE DIAGNOSTICS
                    
    BLOCKAGE CASE 1: Pitot Blocked (Drain Open)   ==> ASI Drops to ZERO
    BLOCKAGE CASE 2: Pitot & Drain Blocked        ==> ASI Acts as ALTIMETER!
                                                       - Climb   ==> ASI OVERREADS
                                                       - Descent ==> ASI UNDERREADS
    BLOCKAGE CASE 3: Static Port Blocked          ==> ASI Errors in Climb/Descent
                                                       - Climb   ==> ASI UNDERREADS
                                                       - Descent ==> ASI OVERREADS
```

### Master Failure Diagnostic Table

| System Failure Scenario | Airspeed Indicator (ASI) Behavior in CLIMB | Airspeed Indicator (ASI) Behavior in DESCENT | Physical Explanation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pitot Tube Blocked, Drain Hole OPEN** | **Drops to ZERO** | **Drops to ZERO** | Total pressure escapes through drain hole; $p_0 \to p \implies q_c = 0$. |
| **Pitot Tube AND Drain Hole BLOCKED** | **OVERREADS** *(Indicates higher speed)* | **UNDERREADS** *(Indicates lower speed)* | Trapped $p_0$ remains constant. As aircraft climbs, ambient $p$ drops $\implies q_c = p_0 - p$ **INCREASES**! **ASI acts like an Altimeter!** |
| **Static Port BLOCKED (Pitot Normal)** | **UNDERREADS** *(Indicates lower speed)* | **OVERREADS** *(Indicates higher speed)* | Trapped static $p_{\text{stat}}$ stays high. As aircraft climbs, ambient pressure drops $\implies q_c = p_0 - p_{\text{stat}}$ **DECREASES**! |

> [!WARNING]
> **GATE Exam Trap Alert:** When a Pitot tube is completely trapped (blocked inlet + blocked drain), pushing the nose down into a descent causes ambient pressure $p$ to increase $\implies q_c = p_0 - p$ decreases $\implies$ **ASI indicates SLOWER airspeed!** A pilot might incorrectly pull back on the stick, leading to a stall!

## ⚡ AIR-1 Mnemonics & Speed Tricks

### 🧠 Mnemonic 1: "P-U-C" (Trapped Pitot Behaviour)
- **P**itot trapped $\implies$ **U**pward **C**limb causes ASI to **OVERREAD** (acts like an altimeter!).

### 🧠 Mnemonic 2: "WHITE = Flaps, GREEN = Normal, YELLOW = Caution, RED = Never Exceed"
- White Arc $\implies$ Flap operating range ($V_{S0} \to V_{\text{FE}}$).
- Green Arc $\implies$ Normal operating range ($V_{S1} \to V_{\text{NO}}$).
- Yellow Arc $\implies$ Caution range (smooth air only, $V_{\text{NO}} \to V_{\text{NE}}$).

### ⚡ Speed Trick 1: 5-Second Incompressible CAS Formula
$$\mathbf{V_{\text{CAS}} = 1.278 \sqrt{q_c \text{ (in Pa)}} \text{ m/s}} \quad \text{or} \quad \mathbf{V_{\text{CAS}} = 4.041 \sqrt{q_c \text{ (in Pa)}} \text{ km/h}}$$

## 📝 Solved GATE & Exam-Standard Examples

### Example 1: Incompressible vs. Compressible CAS Calculation (GATE Classic)
**Problem:** A Pitot-static tube on an aircraft measures a total pressure $p_0 = 108,000 \text{ Pa}$ and a static pressure $p = 95,000 \text{ Pa}$. (Take sea-level constants $p_0 = 101,325 \text{ Pa}, \rho_0 = 1.225 \text{ kg/m}^3, a_0 = 340.29 \text{ m/s}$).
1. Calculate $V_{\text{CAS}}$ assuming incompressible flow.
2. Calculate $V_{\text{CAS}}$ using the compressible calibration formula.

**Solution:**
1. **Impact Pressure ($q_c$):**
   $$q_c = p_0 - p = 108,000 - 95,000 = \mathbf{13,000 \text{ Pa}}$$

2. **Incompressible Calibrated Airspeed ($V_{\text{CAS, incomp}}$):**
   $$V_{\text{CAS, incomp}} = \sqrt{\frac{2 q_c}{\rho_0}} = \sqrt{\frac{2 \times 13000}{1.225}} = \sqrt{21224.49} = \mathbf{145.69 \text{ m/s}}$$

3. **Compressible Calibrated Airspeed ($V_{\text{CAS, comp}}$):**
   $$\frac{q_c}{p_0} = \frac{13000}{101325} = 0.12830$$
   $$1 + \frac{q_c}{p_0} = 1.12830$$
   $$(1.12830)^{0.2857} = 1.03472$$
   $$V_{\text{CAS, comp}} = \sqrt{5 \times (340.29)^2 \times (1.03472 - 1)} = \sqrt{578986.4 \times 0.03472} = \sqrt{20102.4} = \mathbf{141.78 \text{ m/s}}$$

**Key Insight:** Neglecting compressibility overestimates CAS by **$3.91 \text{ m/s}$ ($2.76\%$)** even at moderate subsonic speeds!

### Example 2: Blocked Pitot Tube Diagnostic MCQ
**Problem:** An aircraft is climbing through 5,000 ft when ice completely blocks the pitot tube tip and its drain hole. If the pilot maintains a constant true airspeed during the climb, the cockpit Airspeed Indicator (ASI) will:
- (A) Drop to zero immediately
- (B) Indicate a progressively HIGHER airspeed than actual
- (C) Indicate a progressively LOWER airspeed than actual
- (D) Remain perfectly accurate

**Solution:**
With total pressure $p_0$ trapped inside the pitot tube, $p_0 = \text{constant}$.
As the aircraft climbs, ambient static pressure $p$ decreases.
The diaphragm expansion is $q_c = p_0 - p$. Since $p$ is decreasing, $q_c$ **increases**.
Therefore, the ASI pointer moves clockwise, indicating a **HIGHER airspeed** (Option B).

**Correct Answer:** **(B) Indicate a progressively HIGHER airspeed than actual**

## ⚠️ Common GATE Traps & Exam Pitfalls

> [!CAUTION]
> 1. **Pitot Blockage Illusion:** A trapped pitot tube during a climb makes the ASI overread (indicates fast speed). Pushing the nose down to slow down actually increases speed, risking structural overspeed!
> 2. **Incompressible Assumption Limit:** Do NOT use $V_{\text{CAS}} = \sqrt{2 q_c / \rho_0}$ when impact pressure $q_c / p_0 > 0.1$ or Mach number $M > 0.3$. Use the compressible Wantzel equation.
> 3. **Position Error Dependence:** Position error $\Delta V_{\text{pos}}$ is NOT constant; it varies dynamically with angle of attack $\alpha$ and flap settings.
> 4. **$V_{\text{NE}}$ Red Line Warning:** $V_{\text{NE}}$ is specified in **Calibrated/Indicated Airspeed** on the dial, but flutter boundaries at high altitude depend on True Airspeed ($V_{\text{TAS}}$)!

====================================================================================
1. AIRSPEED DEFINITIONS:
   - IAS: Uncorrected dial reading

   - Incompressible (M < 0.3):   V_CAS = sqrt( 2 * (p0 - p) / rho0 )

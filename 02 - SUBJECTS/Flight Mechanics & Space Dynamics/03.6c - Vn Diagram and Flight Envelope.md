## 📌 Executive Summary & GATE Significance

> [!IMPORTANT]
> The **V-n Diagram (Structural Flight Envelope)** maps total Load Factor ($n$) against Airspeed ($V$), establishing structural airframe safety boundaries:
> 1. **Corner Velocity / Maneuver Speed ($V_A$ / $V^*$):**
>    $$\bbox[12px,border:3px solid #2196F3,background-color:#0d1117]{ V_A = V^* = V_s \sqrt{n_{\max}} }$$
> 2. **Positive Stall Boundary Curve:**
>    $$n_{\text{stall}} = +\left(\frac{V}{V_s}\right)^2 = \frac{\frac{1}{2} \rho_\infty V^2 S C_{L,\max}}{W}$$
> 3. **Gust Load Factor Increment ($\Delta n_{\text{gust}}$):**
>    $$\bbox[12px,border:3px solid #E91E63,background-color:#0d1117]{ \Delta n_{\text{gust}} = \frac{K_g \rho_\infty V S a U_{de}}{2 W} \implies n_{\text{gust}} = 1 \pm \frac{K_g \rho_\infty V S a U_{de}}{2 W} }$$
> where $a = dC_L/d\alpha$ is aircraft lift slope, $U_{de}$ is derived gust velocity, and $K_g$ is gust alleviation factor.

### Why does GATE AE care?
1. **Critical Design Airspeeds:** Identifying $V_s$ ($1g$ stall), $V_A$ (maneuver speed / corner velocity), $V_C$ (cruise speed), and $V_D$ (dive speed).
2. **Gust Load Factor Calculations:** Evaluating gust-induced load factors $\Delta n_{\text{gust}}$ during atmospheric turbulence.
3. **Envelope Boundaries:** Determining whether a flight condition lies within safe structural, aerodynamic stall, or structural failure limits.

## 🔵 Core Concept 1: Structure & Anatomy of the Maneuver V-n Diagram

```
                         MANEUVER V-n DIAGRAM (FLIGHT ENVELOPE)
                         
    Load Factor n ^
                  |                    Corner Velocity / Maneuver Speed V_A
        +n_max ---+-------------------*A------------------------*B-------------- (Structural Limit)
                  |                  /|                         |
                  |  Aerodynamic    / |                         |
                  |  Stall Limit   /  |  STRUCTURAL ENVELOPE    |
                  |  n = (V/V_s)^2/   |                         |
           +1.0 --+--------------*----|-------------------------+-------------- (1g Level Flight)
                  |             /V_s  |                         |
              0 --+------------+------+-------------------------+-------------> Airspeed V
                  |           /       |                         |
        -n_max ---+----------*--------+-------------------------*C-------------- (Negative Limit)
                  |         G (Neg Stall)                       V_D (Design Dive Speed)
```

### Key Boundaries & Points

| Boundary / Point | Symbol / Formula | Physical Significance |
|---|---|---|
| **$1g$ Stall Speed** | $V_s = \sqrt{\frac{2 W}{\rho_\infty S C_{L,\max}}}$ | Speed at which aircraft stalls in unaccelerated level flight ($n=1$). |
| **Corner Velocity / Maneuver Speed** | $V_A = V_s \sqrt{n_{\max}}$ | Intersection of positive stall curve and structural limit line $n_{\max}$. Full abrupt control movements safe here. |
| **Positive Structural Limit Line** | $n = +n_{\max}$ | Maximum allowable positive load factor before structural deformation/yield. |
| **Negative Structural Limit Line** | $n = -n_{\max}$ | Maximum allowable negative load factor (typically $-1.0g$ to $-3.0g$). |
| **Design Dive Speed / Never-Exceed** | $V_D$ (or $V_{NE}$) | Maximum speed limit of structural envelope; limited by structural strength & flutter. |

## 🔵 Core Concept 2: Regulatory Load Factor Standards

FAR regulations define minimum required structural limit load factors based on aircraft category:

| Aircraft Category | Positive Limit ($n_{\max}$) | Negative Limit ($n_{\min}$) | Typical Application |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Transport (FAR-25)** | **$+2.5 g$** | **$-1.0 g$** | Commercial passenger airliners (Boeing, Airbus) |
| **Normal (FAR-23)** | **$+3.8 g$** | **$-1.52 g$** | Non-acrobatic general aviation aircraft |
| **Utility (FAR-23)** | **$+4.4 g$** | **$-1.76 g$** | Training & light utility aircraft |
| **Acrobatic (FAR-23)** | **$+6.0 g$** | **$-3.0 g$** | Aerobatic stunt aircraft |
| **Military Fighters** | **$+7.5 g$ to $+9.0 g$** | **$-3.0 g$ to $-4.0 g$** | Tactical jet fighters (F-16, Su-30, Rafale) |

## 🔵 Core Concept 3: Gust V-n Diagram & Atmospheric Turbulence

When an aircraft encounters a vertical atmospheric gust of speed $U_{de}$, its effective angle of attack changes instantaneously by $\Delta\alpha \approx \frac{U_{de}}{V}$.

```
                 VERTICAL GUST ENCOUNTER GEOMETRY
                 
                     Relative Airspeed V
               =====================================>
                                   \
                                    \  Effective Air Velocity Vector
                                     \ Angle Change Delta_alpha = U_de / V
                                      v
                                  ^ Gust Speed U_de
```

### 1. Gust Load Factor Formula
The aerodynamic lift increment produced by the gust is:

$$\Delta L = \frac{1}{2} \rho_\infty V^2 S \cdot a \cdot \Delta\alpha = \frac{1}{2} \rho_\infty V^2 S \cdot a \cdot \left(\frac{U_{de}}{V}\right) = \frac{1}{2} \rho_\infty V S a U_{de}$$

where $a = \frac{dC_L}{d\alpha}$ is the 3D aircraft lift slope ($\text{rad}^{-1}$).

Dividing by Weight $W$ and applying the **Gust Alleviation Factor ($K_g$)**:

$$\Delta n_{\text{gust}} = \frac{\Delta L}{W} = \frac{K_g \rho_\infty V S a U_{de}}{2 W}$$

$$\bbox[12px,border:3px solid #E91E63,background-color:#0d1117]{ n_{\text{gust}} = 1 \pm \frac{K_g \rho_\infty V S a U_{de}}{2 W} }$$

---

### 2. Gust Alleviation Factor ($K_g$)
Accounting for aircraft flexibility and gust penetration lag:

$$\bbox[10px,border:2px solid #2196F3,background-color:#0d1117]{ K_g = \frac{0.88 \mu_g}{5.3 + \mu_g} }$$

where $\mu_g = \frac{2 W}{\rho_\infty \bar{c} S g a}$ is the non-dimensional aircraft mass ratio.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Linearity of Gust Lines:** Unlike the parabolic maneuver stall curve ($n \propto V^2$), gust load factors vary **LINEARLY with airspeed ($n_{\text{gust}} \propto V$)**, radiating as straight lines from $n = 1.0$ at $V = 0$!

## ⚡ AIR-1 Mnemonics & Speed Tricks

### 🧠 Mnemonic 1: "CORNER VELOCITY IS WHERE STALL MEETS N_MAX"
- At $V_A = V_s \sqrt{n_{\max}}$, the aircraft can achieve maximum lift $C_{L,\max}$ exactly at the structural limit $n_{\max}$.

### 🧠 Mnemonic 2: "MANEUVER STALL IS PARABOLIC, GUST LOAD IS LINEAR"
- Maneuver Stall Limit: $n \propto V^2$ (Parabolas).
- Gust Load Factor: $n \propto V$ (Straight Lines).

### ⚡ Speed Trick 1: 5-Second Maneuver Speed ($V_A$)
$$V_A = V_s \sqrt{n_{\max}}$$
- Example: If $V_s = 60 \text{ knots}$ and transport category $n_{\max} = 2.5 \implies V_A = 60 \times \sqrt{2.5} = 60 \times 1.581 = \mathbf{94.8 \text{ knots}}$.

## 📝 Solved GATE & Exam-Standard Examples

### Example 1: V-n Diagram Critical Airspeeds & Maneuver Envelope (GATE Classic 2-Marker)
**Problem:** A commercial transport aircraft (FAR-25, positive load limit $n_{\max} = +2.50g$) has a gross weight $W = 500,000 \text{ N}$, wing area $S = 120.0 \text{ m}^2$, and maximum lift coefficient $C_{L,\max} = 1.50$. Flying at sea level ($\rho_0 = 1.225 \text{ kg/m}^3$):
1. Calculate the $1g$ unaccelerated stall speed $V_s$ in m/s.
2. Calculate the Maneuver Speed / Corner Velocity $V_A$ in m/s.
3. Calculate the accelerated stall speed at load factor $n = +2.0g$.

**Solution:**
1. **Unaccelerated $1g$ Stall Speed ($V_s$):**
   $$V_s = \sqrt{\frac{2 W}{\rho_0 S C_{L,\max}}} = \sqrt{\frac{2 \times 500,000}{1.225 \times 120.0 \times 1.50}} = \sqrt{\frac{1,000,000}{220.50}} = \sqrt{4,535.15} = \mathbf{67.34 \text{ m/s}}$$

2. **Maneuver Speed / Corner Velocity ($V_A$):**
   $$V_A = V_s \sqrt{n_{\max}} = 67.34 \times \sqrt{2.50} = 67.34 \times 1.58114 = \mathbf{106.48 \text{ m/s}}$$

3. **Accelerated Stall Speed at $n = +2.0g$ ($V_{s,2g}$):**
   $$V_{s,2g} = V_s \sqrt{n} = 67.34 \times \sqrt{2.0} = 67.34 \times 1.4142 = \mathbf{95.23 \text{ m/s}}$$

---

### Example 2: Vertical Gust Load Factor Calculation
**Problem:** An aircraft flying at cruise speed $V = 150 \text{ m/s}$ at sea level ($\rho_0 = 1.225 \text{ kg/m}^3$) encounters a vertical sharp-edged upward gust $U_{de} = 10.0 \text{ m/s}$. Given:
- Aircraft weight $W = 40,000 \text{ N}$
- Wing area $S = 20.0 \text{ m}^2$
- Lift slope $a = \frac{dC_L}{d\alpha} = 4.80 \text{ rad}^{-1}$
- Gust alleviation factor $K_g = 0.80$

Calculate the peak load factor $n_{\text{gust}}$ experienced by the aircraft during the gust encounter.

**Solution:**
1. **Calculate Gust Load Factor Increment ($\Delta n_{\text{gust}}$):**
   $$\Delta n_{\text{gust}} = \frac{K_g \rho_0 V S a U_{de}}{2 W}$$
   $$\Delta n_{\text{gust}} = \frac{0.80 \times 1.225 \times 150 \times 20.0 \times 4.80 \times 10.0}{2 \times 40,000}$$
   $$\Delta n_{\text{gust}} = \frac{0.80 \times 1.225 \times 150 \times 9600}{80,000} = \frac{1,411,200}{80,000} = \mathbf{1.764 g}$$

2. **Calculate Total Peak Load Factor ($n_{\text{gust}}$):**
   $$n_{\text{gust}} = 1.0 + \Delta n_{\text{gust}} = 1.0 + 1.764 = \mathbf{2.764 g}$$

## ⚠️ Common GATE Traps & Exam Pitfalls

> [!CAUTION]
> 1. **Maneuver Speed Abrupt Deflection Rule:** $V_A$ is the maximum speed at which full abrupt elevator deflection can be applied without damaging the airframe. Flying faster than $V_A$ and pulling full control will exceed $n_{\max}$ and cause structural damage!
> 2. **Gust Load Factor Base Value:** Gust load factor is $n_{\text{gust}} = 1 \pm \Delta n$. Do NOT forget to add $+1.0$ (representing initial steady $1g$ flight) to the load increment $\Delta n$!
> 3. **Lift Slope Units in Gust Equation:** Ensure lift slope $a = dC_L/d\alpha$ is in **per radian ($\text{rad}^{-1}$)**, NOT per degree! If given as $0.08 \text{ deg}^{-1}$, convert by multiplying by $57.3 \implies a = 0.08 \times 57.3 = \mathbf{4.584 \text{ rad}^{-1}}$.

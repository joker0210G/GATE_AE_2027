## 🧠 MASTER MENTAL MODEL — The Geometry of Keplerian Conic Orbits

> **CORE CONCEPT:** A **Keplerian Orbit** is a conic section path traversed by a satellite around a primary mass $M$. The shape and dimensions of an elliptical orbit are completely defined by two parameters: **Semi-Major Axis ($a$)** (orbit size) and **Eccentricity ($e$)** (orbit shape).

```
                      ELLIPTICAL KEPLERIAN ORBIT GEOMETRY
                        
                          ┌─────────────────────────────┐
                          │     Semi-Major Axis (a)     │
                          │                             │
                     r_p  │         c = ae              │    r_a
            Periapsis ●───┼───────────┼─────────────────┼───● Apoapsis
           (Perigee)  │  F_1         Center (C)        F_2  │  (Apogee)
                      └───┴───────────┴─────────────────┴───┘
                       Primary Body 
                       at Focus F_1
```

## 📐 SECTION 1 — Comprehensive Ellipse Geometry & Formulas

For an elliptical orbit with semi-major axis $a$ and eccentricity $0 < e < 1$:

| Parameter | Symbol | Mathematical Expression | Physical Meaning |
|-----------|--------|-------------------------|------------------|
| **Periapsis Distance** | $r_p$ | $\mathbf{r_p = a (1 - e)}$ | Minimum distance from central focus $F_1$ |
| **Apoapsis Distance** | $r_a$ | $\mathbf{r_a = a (1 + e)}$ | Maximum distance from central focus $F_1$ |
| **Semi-Major Axis** | $a$ | $\mathbf{a = \frac{r_p + r_a}{2} = \frac{h^2}{\mu (1 - e^2)}}$ | Half of major axis length; dictates energy $\mathcal{E} = -\frac{\mu}{2a}$ |
| **Orbital Eccentricity** | $e$ | $\mathbf{e = \frac{r_a - r_p}{r_a + r_p} = \sqrt{1 - \frac{b^2}{a^2}}}$ | Degree of ovalness ($e=0$ is circle) |
| **Semi-Minor Axis** | $b$ | $\mathbf{b = a \sqrt{1 - e^2} = \sqrt{r_p \cdot r_a}}$ | Half of minor axis length |
| **Focal Distance** | $c$ | $\mathbf{c = a \cdot e = \sqrt{a^2 - b^2}}$ | Distance from ellipse center $C$ to focus $F_1$ |
| **Semi-Latus Rectum** | $p$ | $\mathbf{p = a (1 - e^2) = \frac{b^2}{a} = \frac{h^2}{\mu}}$ | Radius at $\theta = 90^\circ$ perpendicular to major axis |

## 📐 SECTION 2 — Kepler's Three Laws of Planetary Motion

```
  KEPLER'S FIRST LAW   ──> Orbits are Ellipses with Primary Body at One Focus!
  KEPLER'S SECOND LAW  ──> Equal Areas Swept in Equal Times (h = const)!
  KEPLER'S THIRD LAW   ──> Square of Period T² is Proportional to Cube of Semi-Major Axis a³!
```

---

### 2.1 Kepler's First Law (Law of Ellipses)
> The orbit of a planet / satellite is an ellipse with the central primary body located at one of its two focal points ($F_1$).

---

### 2.2 Kepler's Second Law (Law of Equal Areas)
> A radius vector joining a planet and the Sun sweeps out equal areas during equal intervals of time.

$$\frac{dA}{dt} = \frac{h}{2} = \text{Constant}$$

---

### 2.3 Kepler's Third Law (Harmonic Law & Orbital Period)
> The square of the orbital period $T$ is directly proportional to the cube of the semi-major axis $a$.

Total area of an ellipse is $A = \pi a b$. Since $\frac{dA}{dt} = \frac{h}{2}$, the total time for one full orbit (Orbital Period $T$) is:

$$T = \frac{\text{Total Area}}{\text{Areal Velocity}} = \frac{\pi a b}{h/2} = \frac{2 \pi a (a \sqrt{1-e^2})}{\sqrt{\mu a (1-e^2)}} = \frac{2 \pi a^{3/2}}{\sqrt{\mu}}$$

$$\boxed{T = 2 \pi \sqrt{\frac{a^3}{\mu}}}$$

Squaring both sides yields **Kepler's Third Law**:

$$\boxed{T^2 = \left(\frac{4 \pi^2}{\mu}\right) \cdot a^3}$$

#### Mean Motion ($n$):
The average angular velocity of the satellite along its orbit:

$$\boxed{n = \frac{2\pi}{T} = \sqrt{\frac{\mu}{a^3}} \quad (\text{rad/s})}$$

## 📐 SECTION 3 — Time-of-Flight & The Three Anomalies ($\theta, E, M$)

To locate a satellite as a function of time $t$, astrodynamics defines three angular anomalies:

```
                   THE THREE ANOMALIES AT A GLANCE
  1. True Anomaly (θ):     Actual physical polar angle measured from perigee at focus F_1.
  2. Eccentric Anomaly (E): Geometric angle measured from center C on auxiliary circle.
  3. Mean Anomaly (M):     Fictitious uniform angle for a constant speed circular orbit (M = n·t).
```

```
                  AUXILIARY CIRCLE & ECCENTRIC ANOMALY (E)
                             Satellite (m) on Circle
                                   ● 
                                  ╱│
                                 ╱ │
                                ╱  │
                               ╱ E │  Satellite on Ellipse
                              ╱ ⦞  │    ●
                             C────┼───F_1──────────> Major Axis
                           Center │  Primary Body
                                  │  (Angle θ at F_1)
```

---

### 3.1 Conversions Between True Anomaly ($\theta$) and Eccentric Anomaly ($E$)

$$\boxed{\cos E = \frac{e + \cos\theta}{1 + e \cos\theta}}$$

$$\boxed{\tan\left(\frac{E}{2}\right) = \sqrt{\frac{1 - e}{1 + e}} \cdot \tan\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)}$$

Radial distance in terms of $E$:

$$\boxed{r = a (1 - e \cos E)}$$

---

### 3.2 Kepler's Equation (Relating $E$ and Mean Anomaly $M$)

$$\boxed{M = E - e \sin E}$$

Where:
- $M = n (t - t_p)$ = **Mean Anomaly** in radians.
- $t - t_p$ = Time elapsed since perigee passage ($t_p$).
- $E$ = **Eccentric Anomaly** in radians (**MUST be in RADIANS in Kepler's Equation!**).

> ⚠️ **CRITICAL NUMERICAL WARNING:**
> When solving $M = E - e \sin E$, $E$ MUST be in **RADIANS**!
> If $E$ is in degrees, $M = \left(\frac{\pi}{180}\right) E^\circ - e \sin E^\circ$.

---

## 📐 SECTION 4 — Open Trajectories: Parabolic ($e=1$) & Hyperbolic ($e>1$)

### 4.1 Parabolic Trajectories ($e = 1, \mathcal{E} = 0$)
- **Perigee Distance:** $r_p = \frac{p}{2} = \frac{h^2}{2\mu}$
- **Escape Speed:** $v_{\text{esc}} = \sqrt{\frac{2\mu}{r}}$
- **Barker's Equation (Time of Flight for Parabola):**
  $$t - t_p = \frac{1}{2} \sqrt{\frac{p^3}{\mu}} \left[ \tan\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right) + \frac{1}{3} \tan^3\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right) \right]$$

---

### 4.2 Hyperbolic Trajectories ($e > 1, \mathcal{E} > 0$)
- **Energy Relation:** $\mathcal{E} = +\frac{\mu}{2 |a|}$
- **Hyperbolic Excess Velocity ($v_\infty$):** Speed as $r \to \infty$:
  $$\boxed{v_\infty = \sqrt{\frac{\mu}{|a|}} \implies v^2 = v_{\text{esc}}^2 + v_\infty^2}$$
- **Hyperbolic Turn Angle ($\delta$):** Total deflection angle of asymptote:
  $$\boxed{\sin\left(\frac{\delta}{2}\right) = \frac{1}{e}}$$

---

## 🔑 SECTION 5 — Mnemonics, Shortcuts & Tricks

### 5.1 Orbit Period 5-Second Shortcut
$$\boxed{T = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{a^3}{\mu}}}$$
For Earth orbit ($a$ in km, $T$ in seconds):
$$T \approx 0.0995 \times a^{3/2} \text{ seconds}$$

### 5.2 Perigee & Apogee Distance Shortcuts
$$\boxed{r_p = a(1 - e), \quad r_a = a(1 + e)}$$
$$\boxed{a = \frac{r_p + r_a}{2}, \quad e = \frac{r_a - r_p}{r_a + r_p}}$$

### 5.3 Kepler's Equation Unit Trap Mnemonic
> **"M = E - e sin E : E MUST BE RADIANS IN THE SUBTRACTION!"**

## 🔢 SECTION 6 — Solved GATE-Style Numerical Examples

### Example 1 — Orbital Period & Mean Motion in Molniya Elliptical Orbit

**Problem:** A satellite is placed in an elliptical Earth orbit with perigee altitude $h_p = 500\text{ km}$ and apogee altitude $h_a = 39,500\text{ km}$.
Given Earth radius $R_\oplus = 6378\text{ km}$, $\mu_\oplus = 398,600\text{ km}^3/\text{s}^2$.

Calculate:
1. Perigee radius $r_p$, apogee radius $r_a$, semi-major axis $a$, and eccentricity $e$.
2. Orbital period $T$ in hours.
3. Mean motion $n$ in $\text{rad/s}$.

**Solution:**

**1. Radii, Semi-Major Axis & Eccentricity:**
$$r_p = R_\oplus + h_p = 6378 + 500 = 6878\text{ km}$$
$$r_a = R_\oplus + h_a = 6378 + 39500 = 45,878\text{ km}$$

$$a = \frac{r_p + r_a}{2} = \frac{6878 + 45878}{2} = \frac{52756}{2} = 26,378\text{ km}$$

$$e = \frac{r_a - r_p}{r_a + r_p} = \frac{45878 - 6878}{52756} = \frac{39000}{52756} = 0.73926$$

$$\boxed{a = 26,378\text{ km}, \quad e \approx 0.7393}$$

**2. Orbital Period ($T$):**
$$T = 2 \pi \sqrt{\frac{a^3}{\mu}} = 2 \pi \sqrt{\frac{(26378)^3}{398600}} = 2 \pi \sqrt{\frac{1.8353 \times 10^{13}}{398600}} = 2 \pi \sqrt{4.6044 \times 10^7}$$
$$\sqrt{4.6044 \times 10^7} = 6785.57\text{ s}$$
$$T = 2 \times 3.14159 \times 6785.57 = 42,634.8\text{ seconds}$$

In hours:
$$T_{\text{hours}} = \frac{42634.8}{3600} = 11.843\text{ hours} \approx 12\text{ hours}$$

$$\boxed{T \approx 11.84\text{ hours}}$$

**3. Mean Motion ($n$):**
$$n = \frac{2\pi}{T} = \frac{2\pi}{42634.8} = 1.4738 \times 10^{-4}\text{ rad/s}$$

$$\boxed{n \approx 1.474 \times 10^{-4}\text{ rad/s}}$$

### Example 2 — Time of Flight & True Anomaly to Mean Anomaly

**Problem:** A satellite in an orbit with eccentricity $e = 0.20$ and semi-major axis $a = 10,000\text{ km}$ reaches true anomaly $\theta = 60^\circ$.

Calculate:
1. Eccentric anomaly $E$ in degrees and radians.
2. Mean anomaly $M$ in radians.
3. Time elapsed $t - t_p$ since perigee passage ($\mu_\oplus = 398,600\text{ km}^3/\text{s}^2$).

**Solution:**

**1. Eccentric Anomaly ($E$):**
$$\tan\left(\frac{E}{2}\right) = \sqrt{\frac{1 - e}{1 + e}} \cdot \tan\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right) = \sqrt{\frac{1 - 0.20}{1 + 0.20}} \cdot \tan(30^\circ) = \sqrt{\frac{0.80}{1.20}} \cdot 0.57735$$
$$\tan\left(\frac{E}{2}\right) = \sqrt{0.66667} \times 0.57735 = 0.8165 \times 0.57735 = 0.4714$$

$$\frac{E}{2} = \arctan(0.4714) = 25.239^\circ \implies E = 50.478^\circ$$

In radians:
$$E_{\text{rad}} = 50.478^\circ \times \frac{\pi}{180} = 0.8810\text{ rad}$$

$$\boxed{E \approx 50.48^\circ = 0.8810\text{ rad}}$$

**2. Mean Anomaly ($M$):**
$$M = E_{\text{rad}} - e \sin(E) = 0.8810 - 0.20 \times \sin(50.478^\circ) = 0.8810 - 0.20 \times 0.77135$$
$$M = 0.8810 - 0.15427 = 0.72673\text{ rad}$$

$$\boxed{M \approx 0.7267\text{ rad}}$$

**3. Time Elapsed ($t - t_p$):**
$$n = \sqrt{\frac{\mu}{a^3}} = \sqrt{\frac{398600}{(10000)^3}} = \sqrt{3.986 \times 10^{-7}} = 6.3135 \times 10^{-4}\text{ rad/s}$$

$$t - t_p = \frac{M}{n} = \frac{0.72673}{6.3135 \times 10^{-4}} = 1151.07\text{ seconds} \approx 19.18\text{ minutes}$$

$$\boxed{t - t_p \approx 1151\text{ s } (19.18\text{ min})}$$

## ⚠️ SECTION 7 — Critical GATE Exam Traps

### Trap 1: Radius vs Altitude Definition
> $r_p = R_\oplus + h_p$ and $r_a = R_\oplus + h_a$.
> Forgetting Earth radius $R_\oplus$ is the single most common error in orbital mechanics numericals!

### Trap 2: Radians in Kepler's Equation
> $M = E - e \sin E$. You MUST convert $E$ to **radians** before subtracting $e \sin E$!

### Trap 3: Kepler's Third Law Semi-Major Axis Units
> In $T = 2\pi\sqrt{a^3/\mu}$, if $\mu$ is in $\text{m}^3/\text{s}^2$, $a$ MUST be in **meters (m)**. If $\mu$ is in $\text{km}^3/\text{s}^2$, $a$ MUST be in **kilometers (km)**.

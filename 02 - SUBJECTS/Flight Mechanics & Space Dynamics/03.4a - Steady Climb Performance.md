## 📌 Executive Summary & GATE Significance

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Steady Unaccelerated Climb** is governed by force equilibrium along and perpendicular to the flight path inclined at climb angle $\gamma$:
> 1. **Equations of Motion:**
>    $$\bbox[10px,border:2px solid #2196F3,background-color:#0d1117]{ T - D = W \sin\gamma \quad \text{and} \quad L = W \cos\gamma }$$
> 2. **Climb Angle ($\sin\gamma$) & Rate of Climb ($R/C$):**
>    $$\bbox[12px,border:3px solid #E91E63,background-color:#0d1117]{ \sin\gamma = \frac{T - D}{W} = \frac{\text{Excess Thrust}}{W} }$$
>    $$\bbox[12px,border:3px solid #E91E63,background-color:#0d1117]{ R/C = V \sin\gamma = \frac{V(T - D)}{W} = \frac{P_{AV} - P_R}{W} = \frac{\text{Excess Power}}{W} }$$

### Why does GATE AE care?
1. **Maximum Climb Angle ($\gamma_{\max}$ / $V_x$) vs. Max Rate of Climb ($R/C_{\max}$ / $V_y$):** Distinguishing optimum speeds for Jet aircraft vs. Propeller aircraft.
2. **Absolute Ceiling ($h_{abs}$) & Service Ceiling ($h_{serv}$):** Calculating ceiling altitudes where $R/C_{\max} = 0$ (Absolute) or $100 \text{ ft/min}$ (Service).
3. **Time to Climb Integration:** Calculating time required to climb from $h_1$ to $h_2$ using logarithmic integration of linear $R/C(h)$.

## 🔵 Core Concept 1: Equations of Motion in Steady Climb

```
                     STEADY CLIMB FORCE BALANCE
                     
                              Lift L
                                ^
                                |        Flight Path Inclination Angle gamma
                 Thrust T       |       /
               <================[ CG ]--------------------> Horizon
                                |    \
                                |     \  Drag D
                                v      v
                             Weight W
                             W*cos(gamma) perpendicular, W*sin(gamma) along path
```

### 1. Force Balance Formulas
- **Tangential Force Balance (Along Flight Path):**
  $$T - D - W \sin\gamma = 0 \implies \sin\gamma = \frac{T - D}{W}$$
- **Normal Force Balance (Perpendicular to Flight Path):**
  $$L - W \cos\gamma = 0 \implies L = W \cos\gamma$$

> [!CAUTION]
> **Lift vs. Weight in Climb Trap:** In a climb ($\gamma > 0$), **Lift is LESS than Weight ($L = W\cos\gamma < W$)**! Lift does not pull the aircraft up; **excess thrust ($T - D$)** pulls the aircraft up against the gravity component $W\sin\gamma$!

---

## 🔵 Core Concept 2: Maximum Climb Angle ($\gamma_{\max}$ / Best Angle $V_x$)

The climb angle $\gamma$ represents altitude gain per unit ground distance ($\tan\gamma = \Delta h / \Delta x$).

```
                    BEST ANGLE OF CLIMB (V_x)
                    
         Obstacle Height h_obs
               ^
               |           /  Maximum Climb Angle gamma_max (Steepest Path)
               |          /   Speed V_x (Clears Obstacles in Shortest Ground Distance!)
               |         /
               +--------/--------------------------------------------> Ground Distance
```

$$\sin\gamma = \frac{T - D}{W} = \frac{T}{W} - \frac{D}{W} = \frac{T}{W} - \frac{1}{L/D}$$

To maximize climb angle $\gamma_{\max}$, we must **maximize Excess Thrust $(T - D)$**:

### 1. For Jet Aircraft (Constant Thrust $T = T_a$):
- Excess Thrust $(T_a - D)$ is maximum when Drag $D$ is **MINIMUM** $\implies$ at **$(L/D)_{\max}$**!

$$\bbox[12px,border:3px solid #2196F3,background-color:#0d1117]{ \sin\gamma_{\max, \text{Jet}} = \frac{T_a}{W} - \frac{1}{(L/D)_{\max}} }$$

- **Optimum Speed for Jet $V_x$:** $V_x = V_{(L/D)_{\max}}$ (Optimum $C_L = \sqrt{C_{D,0}/K}$).

---

### 2. For Propeller Aircraft (Constant Power $P_{AV} = \eta_p P$):
- Maximum excess thrust occurs at a lower speed near stall:
  $$V_x < V_{(L/D)_{\max}}$$

---

## 🔵 Core Concept 3: Maximum Rate of Climb ($R/C_{\max}$ / Best Rate $V_y$)

Rate of Climb ($R/C \equiv \dot{h}$) represents vertical altitude gain per unit time ($\text{m/s}$ or $\text{ft/min}$):

$$R/C = V \sin\gamma = \frac{V(T - D)}{W} = \frac{P_{AV} - P_R}{W} = \frac{\text{Excess Power}}{W}$$

```
                POWER REQUIRED vs POWER AVAILABLE (R/C)
                
      Power P ^
              |                  / Power Available P_AV (Engine Output)
              |                 /
              |   EXCESS POWER /   * Max Excess Power Point (R/C_max)
              |   (P_AV - P_R)/|
              |              / |
              |             +--+-- Power Required P_R = D * V
              +---------------+-------------------------------------> Speed V
                             V_y (Best Rate of Climb Speed)
```

### 1. For Propeller Aircraft (Constant Available Power $P_{AV} = \eta_p P$):
- Excess power $(P_{AV} - P_R)$ is maximum when Power Required $P_R$ is **MINIMUM** $\implies$ at **$(C_L^{3/2}/C_D)_{\max}$**!

$$\bbox[12px,border:3px solid #E91E63,background-color:#0d1117]{ R/C_{\max, \text{Prop}} = \frac{\eta_p P}{W} - \frac{V_{P_{R,\min}}}{(L/D)_{P_R}} }$$

- **Optimum Speed for Propeller $V_y$:** $V_y = V_{P_{R,\min}} = \mathbf{0.760 \cdot V_{(L/D)_{\max}}}$ (Optimum $C_L = \sqrt{3 C_{D,0}/K}$).

---

### 2. For Jet Aircraft (Constant Thrust $T = T_a$):
- Excess power is $P_{\text{excess}} = T_a V - \frac{1}{2}\rho V^3 S C_{D,0} - \frac{W^2 K}{\frac{1}{2}\rho V S}$.
- Differentiating w.r.t $V$ gives optimum speed $V_y$ for Jet:

$$C_{L, y} = \sqrt{\frac{C_{D,0}}{3 K}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}} \cdot C_{L, (L/D)_{\max}}$$

$$\bbox[12px,border:3px solid #9C27B0,background-color:#0d1117]{ V_y (\text{Jet}) = 3^{1/4} \cdot V_{(L/D)_{\max}} = \mathbf{1.316 \cdot V_{(L/D)_{\max}}} }$$

---

## 🔵 Core Concept 4: Absolute Ceiling, Service Ceiling & Time to Climb

As altitude $h$ increases, air density $\rho \downarrow \implies$ engine power/thrust decreases $\implies R/C_{\max}$ decreases linearly with altitude:

```
               RATE OF CLIMB VARIATION WITH ALTITUDE
               
     Altitude h ^
                |  Absolute Ceiling h_abs (R/C = 0)
                |--*---------------------------------------------
                |  |
                |  * Service Ceiling h_serv (R/C = 100 ft/min = 0.508 m/s)
                |  |
                |  |  R/C(h) = R/C_0 * ( 1 - h / h_abs )
                |  |
                +--+---------------------------------------------> R/C
                0  R/C_max,0 (Sea Level Rate of Climb)
```

### 1. Ceiling Definitions
1. **Absolute Ceiling ($h_{abs}$):** Altitude where $R/C_{\max} = 0$ (aircraft cannot climb any higher).
2. **Service Ceiling ($h_{serv}$):** Altitude where maximum rate of climb drops to:
   - **Civil Commercial (FAR-25):** $R/C = 100 \text{ ft/min} = \mathbf{0.508 \text{ m/s}}$.
   - **Military (FAR-23):** $R/C = 500 \text{ ft/min} = \mathbf{2.54 \text{ m/s}}$.

---

### 2. Ceiling Relationship Formula
Assuming linear variation of $R/C_{\max}$ with altitude ($R/C(h) = R/C_0 \left(1 - \frac{h}{h_{abs}}\right)$):

$$\bbox[12px,border:3px solid #E91E63,background-color:#0d1117]{ h_{serv} = h_{abs} \left( 1 - \frac{R/C_{\text{service}}}{R/C_0} \right) }$$

---

### 3. Time to Climb Formula ($t_{\text{climb}}$)
Time required to climb from altitude $h_1$ to altitude $h_2$:

$$t = \int_{h_1}^{h_2} \frac{dh}{R/C(h)} = \int_{h_1}^{h_2} \frac{dh}{R/C_0 \left(1 - \frac{h}{h_{abs}}\right)}$$

$$\bbox[12px,border:3px solid #2196F3,background-color:#0d1117]{ t_{\text{climb}} = \frac{h_{abs}}{R/C_0} \ln \left( \frac{h_{abs} - h_1}{h_{abs} - h_2} \right) }$$

---

## ⚡ AIR-1 Mnemonics & Speed Tricks

### 🧠 Mnemonic 1: "CLIMB ANGLE = THRUST / WEIGHT, RATE = POWER / WEIGHT"
- Climb Angle $\sin\gamma = \frac{\text{Excess Thrust}}{W}$.
- Rate of Climb $R/C = \frac{\text{Excess Power}}{W}$.

### 🧠 Mnemonic 2: "JET vs PROP OPTIMUM SPEEDS"
- **Jet:** Best Angle $V_x = V_{(L/D)_{\max}}$, Best Rate $V_y = 1.32 V_{(L/D)_{\max}}$.
- **Prop:** Best Angle $V_x < V_{P_R}$, Best Rate $V_y = 0.76 V_{(L/D)_{\max}}$.

### ⚡ Speed Trick 1: 5-Second Time to Climb Formula
$$t_{\text{climb}} = \frac{h_{abs}}{R/C_0} \ln \left( \frac{h_{abs} - h_1}{h_{abs} - h_2} \right)$$
- Example: $h_{abs} = 10,000 \text{ m}, R/C_0 = 20 \text{ m/s}$. Time from sea level ($h_1=0$) to $h_2=5,000 \text{ m}$:
  $$t = \frac{10000}{20} \ln \left(\frac{10000}{5000}\right) = 500 \times \ln(2) = 500 \times 0.6931 = \mathbf{346.6 \text{ s}} \quad (5.78 \text{ min}).$$

## 📝 Solved GATE & Exam-Standard Examples

### Example 1: Jet Aircraft Maximum Climb Angle & Rate of Climb (GATE Classic 2-Marker)
**Problem:** A jet aircraft has a weight $W = 50,000 \text{ N}$, wing area $S = 25.0 \text{ m}^2$, drag polar $C_D = 0.020 + 0.040 C_L^2$, and constant engine thrust $T_a = 10,000 \text{ N}$ at sea level ($\rho_0 = 1.225 \text{ kg/m}^3$).
Calculate:
1. Maximum Lift-to-Drag Ratio $(L/D)_{\max}$
2. Maximum Climb Angle $\gamma_{\max}$ in degrees
3. Maximum Rate of Climb $R/C_{\max}$ in m/s

**Solution:**
1. **Calculate $(L/D)_{\max}$:**
   $$C_{D,0} = 0.020, \quad K = 0.040$$
   $$(L/D)_{\max} = \frac{1}{2 \sqrt{K C_{D,0}}} = \frac{1}{2 \sqrt{0.040 \times 0.020}} = \frac{1}{2 \sqrt{0.0008}} = \frac{1}{2 \times 0.028284} = \mathbf{17.678}$$

2. **Calculate Maximum Climb Angle ($\gamma_{\max}$):**
   $$\sin\gamma_{\max} = \frac{T_a}{W} - \frac{1}{(L/D)_{\max}} = \frac{10,000}{50,000} - \frac{1}{17.678} = 0.20 - 0.05657 = \mathbf{0.14343}$$
   $$\gamma_{\max} = \arcsin(0.14343) = \mathbf{8.25^\circ}$$

3. **Calculate Best Rate of Climb Speed ($V_y$):**
   $$V_{(L/D)_{\max}} = \sqrt{\frac{2 W}{\rho_0 S \sqrt{C_{D,0}/K}}} = \sqrt{\frac{2 \times 50,000}{1.225 \times 25.0 \times \sqrt{0.020/0.040}}} = \sqrt{\frac{100,000}{30.625 \times 0.7071}} = \sqrt{4618.1} = 67.96 \text{ m/s}$$
   $$V_y = 1.31607 \times 67.956 = \mathbf{89.43 \text{ m/s}}$$

4. **Calculate Maximum Rate of Climb ($R/C_{\max}$):**
   At $V_y$, $C_{L,y} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}} C_{L,(L/D)_{\max}} = \frac{0.7071}{1.732} = 0.4082$
   $$C_{D,y} = \frac{4}{3} C_{D,0} = \frac{4}{3} \times 0.020 = 0.02667$$
   $$D_y = \frac{1}{2} \rho_0 V_y^2 S C_{D,y} = \frac{1}{2} \times 1.225 \times (89.43)^2 \times 25.0 \times 0.02667 = 3266.3 \text{ N}$$
   $$R/C_{\max} = \frac{V_y (T_a - D_y)}{W} = \frac{89.43 \times (10,000 - 3266.3)}{50,000} = \frac{89.43 \times 6733.7}{50,000} = \mathbf{12.04 \text{ m/s}}$$

---

### Example 2: Ceiling & Time to Climb Calculation
**Problem:** An aircraft has a sea-level maximum rate of climb $R/C_0 = 15.0 \text{ m/s}$ and an absolute ceiling $h_{abs} = 12,000 \text{ m}$.
1. Calculate the Commercial Service Ceiling $h_{serv}$ (where $R/C = 0.508 \text{ m/s}$).
2. Calculate the time required to climb from sea level ($h=0$) to a cruising altitude of $h = 8,000 \text{ m}$ in minutes.

**Solution:**
1. **Commercial Service Ceiling ($h_{serv}$):**
   $$h_{serv} = h_{abs} \left( 1 - \frac{R/C_{\text{service}}}{R/C_0} \right) = 12,000 \times \left( 1 - \frac{0.508}{15.0} \right) = 12,000 \times (1 - 0.03387) = \mathbf{11,593.6 \text{ m}}$$

2. **Time to Climb to $8,000 \text{ m}$:**
   $$t_{\text{climb}} = \frac{h_{abs}}{R/C_0} \ln \left( \frac{h_{abs} - h_1}{h_{abs} - h_2} \right) = \frac{12,000}{15.0} \ln \left( \frac{12,000 - 0}{12,000 - 8,000} \right)$$
   $$t_{\text{climb}} = 800 \times \ln \left( \frac{12,000}{4,000} \right) = 800 \times \ln(3) = 800 \times 1.09861 = \mathbf{878.89 \text{ s}}$$
   $$\text{In Minutes:} \quad t_{\text{climb}} = \frac{878.89}{60} = \mathbf{14.65 \text{ minutes}}$$

## ⚠️ Common GATE Traps & Exam Pitfalls

> [!CAUTION]
> 1. **Lift Equals Weight Fallacy:** In a climb, $L = W \cos\gamma < W$. Do NOT assume $L = W$ when calculating climb drag $D = q S (C_{D,0} + K C_L^2)$ at steep climb angles!
> 2. **Jet vs Prop Best Speed Swap:**
>    - Jet Best Angle $V_x = V_{(L/D)_{\max}}$, Jet Best Rate $V_y = 1.32 V_{(L/D)_{\max}}$.
>    - Prop Best Rate $V_y = V_{P_{R,\min}} = 0.76 V_{(L/D)_{\max}}$.
> 3. **Service Ceiling Units:** Service ceiling rate of climb threshold is **$100 \text{ ft/min} = 0.508 \text{ m/s}$**, NOT zero! Set $R/C = 0$ ONLY for Absolute Ceiling.
> 4. **Time to Climb Integration Trap:** Do NOT divide altitude gain $\Delta h$ by average rate of climb $\frac{R/C_1 + R/C_2}{2}$ for large altitude changes! Always use logarithmic formula $t = \frac{h_{abs}}{R/C_0} \ln \left( \frac{h_{abs}-h_1}{h_{abs}-h_2} \right)$.

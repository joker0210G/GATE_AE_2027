### Step 2 — Bredt-Batho Theory for Single-Cell Closed Tubes (25 min)
Open `05 - Thin Walled Sections and Aircraft Loads`.

```
                    BREDT-BATHO THEORY FOR CLOSED THIN-WALLED TUBES
                             ╭──────────────────────────────╮
                            │  Enclosed Mean Area A_m       │
                            │  Constant Shear Flow q        │ ◄── Thickness t(s)
                            │  q = T / (2 A_m) = const      │
                             ╰──────────────────────────────╯
```

1. **Bredt-Batho First Formula (Constant Shear Flow):**
   For any closed single-cell thin-walled tube subjected to torque $T$, the shear flow $q$ is **STRICTLY CONSTANT** around the entire circumference:

   $$\boxed{q \equiv \tau(s) \cdot t(s) = \frac{T}{2 A_m} = \text{const} \quad (\text{Units: N/m or N/mm})}$$
   - $T$: Applied twisting torque ($\text{N}\cdot\text{m}$).
   - $A_m$: Area enclosed by the **MEDIAN LINE** (centerline of wall thickness) of the tube cross-section ($\text{m}^2$ or $\text{mm}^2$).

2. **Shear Stress Distribution ($\tau(s)$):**
   $$\boxed{\tau(s) = \frac{q}{t(s)} = \frac{T}{2 A_m t(s)} \implies \tau_{\max} = \frac{T}{2 A_m t_{\min}}}$$
   *(Shear stress is highest where the wall thickness is thinnest!).*

---

### Step 3 — Rate of Twist & Torsional Constant (20 min)

1. **Bredt-Batho Second Formula (Rate of Twist $\theta = d\phi/dz$):**
   From shear strain energy equivalence:

   $$\boxed{\theta \equiv \frac{d\phi}{dz} = \frac{T}{4 A_m^2 G} \oint \frac{ds}{t(s)} = \frac{q}{2 A_m G}\oint \frac{ds}{t(s)}}$$
   - $G$: Shear modulus ($\text{Pa}$).
   - $\oint \frac{ds}{t(s)}$: Contour integral of perimeter distance divided by local thickness.

2. **Effective Torsional Constant ($J_{\text{eff}}$):**
   $$\boxed{J_{\text{eff}} \equiv \frac{T}{G \theta} = \frac{4 A_m^2}{\oint \frac{ds}{t(s)}}}$$

### Step 4 — Standard Geometries & Closed vs. Open Comparison (10 min)

```
                    STANDARD THIN-WALLED CLOSED SECTION FORMULAS
  Geometry                       Enclosed Area (A_m)     Contour Integral ∮ ds/t       Torsional Constant (J)
  ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  Circular Tube (r_m, t)         π r_m²                  2 π r_m / t                   2 π r_m³ t
  Rectangular Tube (a × b, t)    a b                     2(a + b) / t                  2 a² b² t / (a + b)
  Square Tube (a × a, t)         a²                      4 a / t                       a³ t
```

- **Closed vs. Slit Open Tube Torsional Rigidity:**
  - Closed tube: $J_{\text{closed}} \approx 2 a^2 b^2 t / (a + b) \propto a^3 t$.
  - Slit open tube: $J_{\text{open}} = \frac{1}{3} S t^3 = \frac{2(a+b)}{3} t^3$.
  $$\boxed{\frac{J_{\text{closed}}}{J_{\text{open}}} \approx \frac{3 a^2 b^2}{(a + b)^2 t^2} \sim \mathcal{O}\left(\frac{a^2}{t^2}\right) \sim 100\text{ to } 10,000!}$$
  *(A closed torque box is hundreds or thousands of times stiffer than an open channel!).*

---

### Step 5 — Traps & Self-Check (5 min)
> 🔴 **Trap 1: Enclosed Area $A_m$ vs. Total Outer Area**
> $A_m$ is the area bounded by the **MEDIAN LINE** (center of wall thickness). For a tube with outer width $W$ and height $H$, $A_m = (W - t)(H - t)$, NOT $W \times H$!
> 🔴 **Trap 2: Variable Wall Thickness Integration**
> If a tube has two walls of thickness $t_1$ (length $L_1$) and two walls of thickness $t_2$ (length $L_2$), then $\oint \frac{ds}{t} = \frac{2 L_1}{t_1} + \frac{2 L_2}{t_2}$. Do not average the thickness!

### Step 3 — D3 Mini Quiz (3:45–4:00) — 5 Questions (Timed 10 min)

> **Q1 (1 mark):** In a single-cell thin-walled closed tube of arbitrary cross-section subjected to twisting torque $T$, the shear flow $q$:
> **(A)** Varies linearly along the perimeter **(B)** Is strictly constant around the perimeter **(C)** Is maximum at corners **(D)** Is zero at the neutral axis

> **Q2 (1 mark):** If a thin-walled tube with enclosed mean area $A_m$ has two different wall thicknesses $t_1$ and $t_2$ ($t_1 < t_2$), the maximum shear stress occurs in the wall with thickness:
> **(A)** $t_1$ **(B)** $t_2$ **(C)** $(t_1 + t_2)/2$ **(D)** Both have equal shear stress

> **Q3 (2 marks):** A thin-walled square closed box has mean side length $a = 80\text{ mm}$ and uniform wall thickness $t = 2.5\text{ mm}$. When subjected to twisting torque $T = 1.60\text{ kN}\cdot\text{m}$, the shear stress developed in the wall (in $\text{MPa}$) is:
> **(A)** $25.0\text{ MPa}$ **(B)** $50.0\text{ MPa}$ **(C)** $75.0\text{ MPa}$ **(D)** $100.0\text{ MPa}$

> **Q4 (2 marks):** For the same square box in Q3 ($a = 80\text{ mm}, t = 2.5\text{ mm}, G = 80\text{ GPa}, T = 1.60\text{ kN}\cdot\text{m}$), the rate of twist $\theta$ (in $\text{rad/m}$) is:
> **(A)** $0.0078\text{ rad/m}$ **(B)** $0.0156\text{ rad/m}$ **(C)** $0.0234\text{ rad/m}$ **(D)** $0.0312\text{ rad/m}$

> **Q5 (2 marks NAT):** A thin-walled circular tube of mean radius $r_m = 50\text{ mm}$ and wall thickness $t = 2.0\text{ mm}$ is subjected to a torque $T = 3.1416\text{ kN}\cdot\text{m}$. The shear flow $q$ (in $\text{N/mm}$) is ___ $\text{N/mm}$.

> [!faq]- 🔍 View D3 Mini Quiz Answers & Step-by-Step Solutions
>
> **Q1: (B) Is strictly constant around the perimeter**
> Bredt-Batho 1st theorem $q = T/(2 A_m) = \text{const}$.
>
> **Q2: (A) t_1**
> $\tau = q/t$, so smallest thickness gives largest stress.
>
> **Q3: (B) 50.0 MPa**
> $A_m = a^2 = 80^2 = 6400\text{ mm}^2$.
> $q = \frac{T}{2 A_m} = \frac{1.60\times 10^6\text{ N}\cdot\text{mm}}{2(6400\text{ mm}^2)} = \frac{1.60\times 10^6}{12800} = 125\text{ N/mm}$.
> $\tau = \frac{q}{t} = \frac{125\text{ N/mm}}{2.5\text{ mm}} = \mathbf{50.0\text{ MPa}}$.
>
> **Q4: (B) 0.0156 rad/m**
> $\oint \frac{ds}{t} = \frac{4 a}{t} = \frac{4(80)}{2.5} = \frac{320}{2.5} = 128$.
> $\theta = \frac{T}{4 A_m^2 G}\oint \frac{ds}{t} = \frac{1.60\times 10^6\text{ N}\cdot\text{mm}}{4(6400^2\text{ mm}^4)(80\times 10^3\text{ N/mm}^2)}(128) = \frac{1.60\times 10^6 \times 128}{4(4.096\times 10^7)(8.0\times 10^4)} = \frac{2.048\times 10^8}{1.31072\times 10^{13}} = 1.5625\times 10^{-5}\text{ rad/mm} = \mathbf{0.015625\text{ rad/m} \approx 0.0156\text{ rad/m}}$.
>
> **Q5: 200**
> $A_m = \pi r_m^2 = \pi(50^2) = 2500\pi\text{ mm}^2$.
> $q = \frac{T}{2 A_m} = \frac{3141593\text{ N}\cdot\text{mm}}{2(2500\pi)} = \frac{3141593}{5000\pi} = \frac{3141593}{15707.96} = \mathbf{200.0\text{ N/mm}}$.

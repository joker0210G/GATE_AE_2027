### 🌊 Set A — Fluid Foundations (FC-01 → FC-09)

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-01 · The Continuum Hypothesis
> Q. State the continuum hypothesis. Write the point-definition of density. Under what condition does it fail?
> 
> 
> > [!success]- Answer & AIR 1 Trap:
> > A fluid is treated as a continuous medium: properties are point functions defined as averages over a material element $\delta \mathcal{V}$ that is tiny compared to the flow scale but large compared to molecular spacing: $\rho = \lim_{\delta \mathcal{V} \to \delta \mathcal{V}^*} \delta m / \delta \mathcal{V}$.
> > Fails when mean free path $\lambda$ is comparable to body length $L$: Knudsen number $Kn = \lambda/L \gtrsim 0.01$ (rarefied gas, very high altitude / hypersonic re-entry regimes).
> > ⚠️ AIR-1 Trap: The breakdown threshold is $Kn \approx 0.01$ — not "only in vacuum". GATE AE has asked this as a one-liner conceptual; writing $\lambda \to 0$ or "low pressure only" loses the mark.

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-02 · Newton's Law of Viscosity
> Q. Write Newton's law of viscosity with units of dynamic viscosity. Give $\mu$ for air and water at 20 °C.
> 
> 
> > [!success]- Answer & AIR 1 Trap:
> > $\tau = \mu \, \dfrac{du}{dy}$ — shear stress ∝ velocity gradient. $\mu$ in Pa·s = N·s/m² = kg/(m·s).
> > Air: $\mu \approx 1.8 \times 10^{-5}$ Pa·s. Water: $\mu \approx 1.0 \times 10^{-3}$ Pa·s (at 20 °C).
> > ⚠️ AIR-1 Trap: The law defines a Newtonian fluid. Non-Newtonian families (Bingham plastic, pseudoplastic, dilatant) are a favourite GATE AE classification MCQ — know which is which (toothpaste = Bingham; polymer solutions/paint = pseudoplastic).

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-03 · Kinematic Viscosity
> Q. Define kinematic viscosity, its units, and compare $\nu$ of air vs water at 20 °C.
> 
> 
> > [!success]- Answer & AIR 1 Trap:
> > $\nu = \mu / \rho$, units m²/s (also stokes: 1 St = $10^{-4}$ m²/s).
> > Water: $\nu \approx 1.0 \times 10^{-6}$ m²/s. Air: $\nu \approx 1.5 \times 10^{-5}$ m²/s.
> > ⚠️ AIR-1 Trap: Counterintuitive but exam-gold: air is ≈ 15× more kinematically viscous than water (μ is smaller, but ρ is far smaller). $\nu$ = momentum diffusivity — it sets Reynolds number $Re = VL/\nu$.

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-04 · Hydrostatic Pressure Law
> Q. Write the governing ODE of hydrostatics and its integrated form. What restriction applies?
> 
> 
> > [!success]- Answer & AIR 1 Trap:
> > $\dfrac{dp}{dz} = -\rho g$; for constant $\rho$: $p = p_0 + \rho g h$. Pressure decreases upward.
> > ⚠️ AIR-1 Trap: $p = p_0 + \rho g h$ assumes constant density. For tall gas columns use $\dfrac{dp}{dz} = -\rho g$ with the ideal-gas law (troposphere lapse-rate problems). Also lock the gauge-vs-absolute switch: manometers read differences; p_atm ≈ 101.325 kPa at sea level.

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-05 · Pascal's Law
> Q. State Pascal's law precisely. Where does it not apply?
> 
> 
> > [!success]- Answer & AIR 1 Trap:
> > Pressure applied to a confined incompressible fluid at rest transmits undiminished in all directions; at a point in a static fluid, pressure is isotropic (same in every direction).
> > ⚠️ AIR-1 Trap: Isotropy holds for fluids at rest. In a moving viscous fluid, normal stresses at a point can differ ($\sigma_{xx} \ne \sigma_{yy}$) — GATE has tested exactly this nuance.

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-06 · Manometer Jump Rule
> Q. State the "jump rule" for walking a manometer tube. When can a gas column be neglected?
> 
> 
> > [!success]- Answer & AIR 1 Trap:
> > Moving down a tube: add $+\rho g h$. Moving up: subtract $-\rho g h$. Same continuous fluid at the same elevation in a connected limb → same pressure.
> > ⚠️ AIR-1 Trap: Gas columns ($\rho_{gas} h$) are neglected only when they sit against a liquid column. When two comparable liquids are both present (e.g., oil over water), nothing is negligible — that omission is the planted error in 60% of manometer numericals.

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-07 · Archimedes / Buoyancy
> Q. Write the buoyant force formula and identify the density and volume used. Where does it act?
> 
> 
> > [!success]- Answer & AIR 1 Trap:
> > $F_B = \rho_f \, g \, V_{disp}$ — density of the fluid, volume displaced (not the body's volume unless fully submerged). Acts vertically upward through the centre of buoyancy = centroid of displaced volume.
> > ⚠️ AIR-1 Trap: Floating body: $V_{disp} = W/(\rho_f g)$. Using body volume or body density here is the classic 🟠 Formula-bucket error in stability questions (coming later in Phase 1).

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-08 · Surface Tension & Capillarity
> Q. Write $\Delta p$ inside (a) a droplet, (b) a soap bubble, (c) a liquid jet cylinder, and the capillary rise formula.
> 
> 
> > [!success]- Answer & AIR 1 Trap:
> > (a) Droplet: $\Delta p = 2\sigma/R$. (b) Soap bubble (two surfaces): $\Delta p = 4\sigma/R$. (c) Cylindrical jet (one curvature): $\Delta p = \sigma/R$.
> > Capillary rise: $h = \dfrac{4\sigma \cos\theta}{\rho g d}$.
> > ⚠️ AIR-1 Trap: The 2 vs 4 factor is the single most repeated surface-tension trap. Also: $\theta < 90°$ → rise; $\theta > 90°$ → depression (mercury in glass).

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-09 · Streamline vs Pathline vs Streakline
> Q. Define all three lines. When do they coincide?
> 
> 
> > [!success]- Answer & AIR 1 Trap:
> > Streamline: instantaneous line everywhere tangent to the velocity vector at one instant. Pathline: the actual trajectory traced by one particle over time. Streakline: locus of all particles that passed through a fixed point earlier.
> > Steady flow → all three identical.
> > ⚠️ AIR-1 Trap: Two streamlines can never intersect (would need two velocity vectors at one point). In unsteady flow they differ — the smoke-streakline-in-wind-tunnel image is a streakline, and under unsteadiness it is NOT a streamline.

### 🧮 Set B — Vector Identity Arsenal (FC-10 → FC-15)

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-10 · The No-Slip Condition
> Q. State the no-slip condition exactly. What about a moving wall? A porous wall?
> 
> 
> > [!success]- Answer & AIR 1 Trap:
> > At a solid boundary, fluid velocity relative to the wall is zero: stationary wall → $V_{fluid} = 0$ at the surface; moving belt → fluid moves with the belt speed. Porous wall: normal component matches the injection/suction velocity.
> > ⚠️ AIR-1 Trap: "Velocity is zero at the wall" is only true for a stationary wall — the relative-velocity phrasing is what earns the mark in boundary-condition viva-style MCQs.

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-11 · Dot Product
> Q. Give both forms of the dot product, its geometric meaning, and the perpendicularity test.
> 
> 
> > [!success]- Answer & AIR 1 Trap:
> > $\vec{A} \cdot \vec{B} = |\vec{A}||\vec{B}|\cos\theta = A_1 B_1 + A_2 B_2 + A_3 B_3$ → a scalar. Geometric meaning: projection of one vector along the other; work $W = \vec{F} \cdot \vec{d}$.
> > $\vec{A} \cdot \vec{B} = 0 \iff \vec{A} \perp \vec{B}$ (for nonzero vectors).
> > ⚠️ AIR-1 Trap: Commutative, and the component form is the fast path in exams — converting to angle form wastes time and invites 🟡 silly sign errors.

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-12 · Cross Product
> Q. Give magnitude, direction rule, anticommutativity, and the parallel test for $\vec{A} \times \vec{B}$.
> 
> 
> > [!success]- Answer & AIR 1 Trap:
> > $\vec{A} \times \vec{B} = |\vec{A}||\vec{B}|\sin\theta \, \hat{n}$ with $\hat{n}$ by the right-hand rule (from $\vec{A}$ toward $\vec{B}$). Magnitude = area of the parallelogram spanned by the two vectors. $\vec{A} \times \vec{B} = -\vec{B} \times \vec{A}$. $\vec{A} \times \vec{B} = 0 \iff \vec{A} \parallel \vec{B}$.
> > ⚠️ AIR-1 Trap: Direction is NOT "always +z" — it follows the RHR from the ordered pair. Area questions want the magnitude; direction questions want the RHR. Mixing them = 🟣 Trap Victim.

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-13 · Scalar Triple Product
> Q. Define $\vec{A} \cdot (\vec{B} \times \vec{C})$ geometrically, state the cyclic property, and the coplanarity test.
> 
> 
> > [!success]- Answer & AIR 1 Trap:
> > $\vec{A} \cdot (\vec{B} \times \vec{C})$ = determinant with rows $\vec{A}, \vec{B}, \vec{C}$ = signed volume of the parallelepiped.
> > Cyclic invariance: $\vec{A} \cdot (\vec{B} \times \vec{C}) = \vec{B} \cdot (\vec{C} \times \vec{A}) = \vec{C} \cdot (\vec{A} \times \vec{B})$; swapping any two vectors flips the sign.
> > $= 0 \iff$ the three vectors are coplanar (linearly dependent).
> > ⚠️ AIR-1 Trap: Every "are these coplanar?" question is a determinant-set-to-zero question in disguise. This is also the bridge to matrix rank (Track B tonight): three coplanar vectors → the 3×3 matrix they form has rank 2, not 3.

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-14 · BAC–CAB and Friends
> Q. Write the vector triple product expansion and the Lagrange identity.
> 
> 
> > [!success]- Answer & AIR 1 Trap:
> > $\vec{A} \times (\vec{B} \times \vec{C}) = \vec{B}(\vec{A} \cdot \vec{C}) - \vec{C}(\vec{A} \cdot \vec{B})$ — BAC-CAB.
> > $(\vec{A} \times \vec{B}) \cdot (\vec{C} \times \vec{D}) = (\vec{A} \cdot \vec{C})(\vec{B} \cdot \vec{D}) - (\vec{A} \cdot \vec{D})(\vec{B} \cdot \vec{C})$.
> > Lagrange identity: $|\vec{A} \times \vec{B}|^2 = |\vec{A}|^2 |\vec{B}|^2 - (\vec{A} \cdot \vec{B})^2$.
> > ⚠️ AIR-1 Trap: The result of $\vec{A} \times (\vec{B} \times \vec{C})$ lies in the plane of B and C. Middle-term sign slips here are a certified 🟠 Formula-bucket generator in GATE mathematics.

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-15 · Gradient, Divergence, Curl
> Q. Define the three operators, their input/output types, and the two always-true identities.
> 
> 
> > [!success]- Answer & AIR 1 Trap:
> > $\nabla f$ — gradient: scalar → vector (steepest-ascent direction). $\nabla \cdot \vec{F}$ — divergence: vector → scalar (net outflow per unit volume). $\nabla \times \vec{F}$ — curl: vector → vector (local rotation).
> > Always true: $\nabla \cdot (\nabla \times \vec{F}) = 0$ and $\nabla \times (\nabla f) = 0$.
> > $\nabla \cdot \vec{V} = 0$ → solenoidal field ⟺ incompressible continuity (differential form — your D2 material).
> > ⚠️ AIR-1 Trap: "Curl of divergence" is meaningless — but "divergence of curl = 0" is always true. True/False items bank on you swapping the order.

### APEX-1 · RTT & the Three Integral Conservation Laws (19:35–19:55)

> [!tip]- 📖 How & When to Study APEX-1 (20-Minute Protocol)
> **Window:** 19:35–19:55 (20 mins)
> **Primary Reading Target:** Open [[02 - SUBJECTS/Aerodynamics/01.2c - Integral CV Conservation Laws and Bernoulli#Part I Reynolds Transport Theorem The Master Bridge|01.2c Part I (RTT Master Bridge)]] and [[02 - SUBJECTS/Aerodynamics/01.2a - Conservation of Mass (Continuity Equation)#Part I The Reynolds Transport Theorem Bridge|01.2a Part I]].
> **Step-by-Step Study Method:**
> 1. **10 min (Immersion):** Read the RTT derivation. Notice how setting $\eta = 1$ yields continuity, $\eta = \vec{V}$ yields momentum, and $\eta = e$ yields energy.
> 2. **5 min (Derivation Rep):** Close the file. On rough paper, write the RTT equation from memory. Identify each term: system rate, accumulation in CV, net flux through CS.
> 3. **5 min (Lock Rules):** Memorize the 6-point CV discipline checklist in 01.2c Section 1.3 (especially atmospheric pressure gauge cancellation).
> **Why study this first?** You cannot formulate WE-1, WE-2, or Track A questions without knowing how to define a Control Volume.

The master theorem. For any extensive property $N$ of a system with intensive (per-unit-mass) counterpart $\eta = N/m$, the Reynolds Transport Theorem converts a system statement into a control-volume statement:

$\frac{dN_{\text{sys}}}{dt} = \frac{\partial}{\partial t} \int_{CV} \eta \, \rho \, dV + \int_{CS} \eta \, \rho \, \left( \vec{V} \cdot \hat{n} \right) dA$ 
Read it aloud once: rate of change in the system = rate of accumulation inside the CV + net outflow across the CS. The dot product $\vec{V} \cdot \hat{n}$ is positive at outflow boundaries, negative at inflow, zero where flow is tangent to the surface. $\hat{n}$ is always the outward unit normal.

Choose $\eta$, get a law:

| Law | Property $N$ | Intensive $\eta$ | Result |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Mass (Continuity) | $m$ | 1 | see below |
| Momentum | $\vec{P} = m\vec{V}$ | $\vec{V}$ | see below |
| Energy | $E$ | $e = u + V^2/2 + gz$ | see below |

  

1) Continuity (integral form):

$\frac{\partial}{\partial t} \int_{CV} \rho \, dV + \int_{CS} \rho \left( \vec{V} \cdot \hat{n} \right) dA = 0$

- Steady flow → accumulation term dies → net mass flux through CS is zero.
- One-in, one-out steady: $\rho_1 A_1 V_1 = \rho_2 A_2 V_2 = \dot{m}$.
- Incompressible steady: $A_1 V_1 = A_2 V_2 = Q$ (volume flow), $\dot{m} = \rho Q$.

2) Linear Momentum (integral form):

$\sum \vec{F} = \frac{\partial}{\partial t} \int_{CV} \rho \vec{V} \, dV + \int_{CS} \rho \vec{V} \left( \vec{V} \cdot \hat{n} \right) dA$

- Steady flow, one-in one-out, per component:

$\sum F_x = \dot{m} \left( V_{x,out} - V_{x,in} \right)$ 
 $\sum F_y = \dot{m} \left( V_{y,out} - V_{y,in} \right)$

- $\sum \vec{F}$ collects surface forces (pressure × area at each CS cut + wall reaction forces) and body forces ($\rho g \, dV$ weight — drop it only when horizontal or explicitly negligible).
- Gauge pressures everywhere inside $\sum \vec{F}$ when any boundary opens to atmosphere — atmospheric pressure cancels on every exposed face.
- Non-uniform exit profiles demand the momentum correction factor $\beta = \dfrac{1}{A V_{avg}^2} \int_A V^2 \, dA$: laminar pipe $\beta = 4/3$, turbulent \beta \approx 1.01$–$1.05 (usually taken as 1).

3) Energy (integral form): with $e = u + V^2/2 + gz$, shaft work $\dot{W}_s$, heat $\dot{Q}$:

$\dot{Q} - \dot{W}_s = \frac{\partial}{\partial t} \int_{CV} \rho e \, dV + \int_{CS} \left( u + \frac{p}{\rho} + \frac{V^2}{2} + gz \right) \rho \left( \vec{V} \cdot \hat{n} \right) dA$

- Note the flow work $p/\rho$ that emerges at the boundary — this is why Bernoulli's three heads appear.
- Steady, one-in one-out, head form (with pump head $h_P$, turbine head $h_T$, loss $h_L$, kinetic-energy correction $\alpha = \dfrac{1}{A V_{avg}^3}\int_A V^3 \, dA$; laminar $\alpha = 2$, turbulent $\approx 1.05$):

$\frac{p_1}{\rho g} + \alpha_1 \frac{V_1^2}{2g} + z_1 + h_P = \frac{p_2}{\rho g} + \alpha_2 \frac{V_2^2}{2g} + z_2 + h_T + h_L$ 
CV discipline checklist (say it every time before writing equations):

1. Draw the CV; cut surfaces ⊥ to flow where velocity/pressure are uniform.
2. Include the solid surface whose reaction force you want.
3. Label every inflow (+ mass in) and outflow (+ mass out).
4. Pressures at open cuts in gauge.
5. Steady? → delete accumulation terms. Horizontal? → delete gravity terms.
6. Write momentum component by component — never as one grand vector equation.

> [!question]- 🔁 Instant self-check (fold before answering)
> A steady jet exits a nozzle into the atmosphere. Why can you ignore the atmospheric pressure acting all over the outer surface of the nozzle CV?
> 
> 
> > [!success]- Answer & AIR 1 Trap:
> > Atmospheric pressure acts uniformly on the entire closed outer surface, so its net resultant is zero; at the open exit cut, using gauge pressure ($p_{gage} = 0$) automatically removes it. Using absolute pressures at the open cuts leaves a nonzero residual $p_{atm}(A_{in} - A_{out})$ — a wrong answer by hundreds of newtons. This is WE-2's planted trap.

### APEX-2 · Bernoulli — Derivation & the Validity Fence (19:55–20:15)

> [!tip]- 📖 How & When to Study APEX-2 (20-Minute Protocol)
> **Window:** 19:55–20:15 (20 mins)
> **Primary Reading Target:** Open [[02 - SUBJECTS/Aerodynamics/01.2c - Integral CV Conservation Laws and Bernoulli#Part IV Bernoulli Equation Derivation Forms Validity Fence|01.2c Part IV (Bernoulli & Validity Fence)]] and [[02 - SUBJECTS/Aerodynamics/01.2a - Conservation of Mass (Continuity Equation)#Bernoulli Equation Derivation from Eulers Equation|01.2a Euler-to-Bernoulli Derivation]].
> **Step-by-Step Study Method:**
> 1. **8 min (Derivation Walkthrough):** Follow Euler's momentum equation along a streamline ($dp/\rho + V dV + g dz = 0$). Note why $\rho = \text{const}$ is required for direct integration into $p + \frac{1}{2}\rho V^2 + \rho g z = \text{const}$.
> 2. **7 min (Validity Fence Drilling):** Memorize all 5 assumptions: Inviscid, Steady, Incompressible ($M < 0.3$), Along a streamline (unless irrotational), No shaft work/heat. For each assumption, speak out loud what equation replaces Bernoulli if violated.
> 3. **5 min (Instant Self-Check):** Test yourself on why Bernoulli's constant is generally NOT the same across different streamlines.
> **Prerequisite Link:** Builds directly on the Euler momentum equation learned in APEX-1.

Derivation (Newton's second law on a fluid sliver along a streamline).
Take a small streamtube element of length $ds$, cross-section $dA$, rising $dz$ along its length. Steady flow, inviscid, gravity the only body force. Along the streamline direction $s$:

$p \, dA - (p + dp) \, dA - \rho g \, dA \, dz = \rho \, dA \, ds \, a_s$ 
With steady acceleration $a_s = V \, dV/ds$ and dividing by $\rho \, dA \, ds$:

$dp + \rho V \, dV + \rho g \, dz = 0$ 
This is Euler's equation along a streamline. Integrate along the streamline with $\rho$ = constant:

$p + \frac{1}{2} \rho V^2 + \rho g z = \text{constant}$ 
Divided by $\rho g$, the head form — three heads that sum to a constant total head $H$:

$\frac{p}{\rho g} + \frac{V^2}{2g} + z = H$ 
Pressure vocabulary (Pitot section feeds on this):

- Static pressure: $p$ — what a wall tap or static port feels.
- Dynamic pressure: $q = \frac{1}{2}\rho V^2$.
- Stagnation (total) pressure: $p_0 = p + q$ — where the flow is brought isentropically to rest.
- Pressure coefficient: $C_p = \dfrac{p - p_\infty}{\frac{1}{2}\rho V_\infty^2}$; stagnation point $C_p = 1$ (incompressible).
- Piezometric head: $\dfrac{p}{\rho g} + z$ — constant across any cross-section where hydrostatic distribution holds.

The Validity Fence — five assumptions, five consequences:

| Assumption | If violated… |
| --- | --- |
| Steady ($\partial/\partial t = 0$) | unsteady term appears (tank start-up, water hammer) |
| Incompressible ($\rho$ = const) | requires $M < 0.3$; at higher $M$ use compressible relations |
| Inviscid (frictionless) | viscous losses → must add head loss $h_L$ (energy equation) |
| Along a streamline (or irrotational) | constant is per-streamline; it is one global constant only if the flow is irrotational ($\nabla \times \vec{V} = 0$) |
| No shaft work / heat between the two points | across a pump/turbine/fan → must use the full energy equation, not Bernoulli |

  
> [!question]- 🔁 Instant self-check (fold before answering)
> Two points lie on different streamlines of a steady, inviscid, incompressible but rotational flow. Can Bernoulli's constant be compared between them?
> 
> 
> > [!success]- Answer & AIR 1 Trap:
> > No — the constant applies along each streamline. Only when the flow is irrotational does the same constant hold everywhere in the connected flow field. "Bernoulli valid between any two points" is true only under irrotationality — a favourite GATE AE true/false.

### APEX-3 · Pitot-Static Tube — Theory & Airspeed Derivation (20:15–20:28)

> [!tip]- 📖 How & When to Study APEX-3 (13-Minute Protocol)
> **Window:** 20:15–20:28 (13 mins)
> **Primary Reading Target:** Open [[02 - SUBJECTS/Aerodynamics/01.5 - Pressure Measurement and U-Tube Manometers#Part VI Pitot-Static Tube Theory Velocity Measurement Airspeed Derivation|01.5 Part VI (Pitot-Static Tube & Manometer Coupling)]] and cross-reference [[02 - SUBJECTS/Aerodynamics/01.2c - Integral CV Conservation Laws and Bernoulli#Part V Pitot-Static Tube Airspeed Measurement|01.2c Part V]].
> **Step-by-Step Study Method:**
> 1. **5 min (Airspeed Derivation):** Follow the stagnation streamline from freestream to tip ($V_\infty = \sqrt{2(p_0 - p_\infty)/\rho_\infty}$).
> 2. **5 min (Manometer Link):** Memorize $\Delta p = (\rho_m - \rho_f)gh$. Understand why $\rho_f$ is subtracted (air in tubes vs mercury in manometer). Check the GATE 2023 Q34 speed ratio shortcut $V_A/V_B = \sqrt{\rho_B/\rho_A}$.
> 3. **3 min (Mach Audit Rule):** Calculate $a = \sqrt{\gamma R T}$. If $M = V/a > 0.3$, incompressible Bernoulli formula is INVALID.
> **Prerequisite Link:** Directly applies the stagnation pressure concept from APEX-2.

Construction. A pitot-static probe has an open total-head port facing the flow (reads $p_0$ at the stagnation point) and one or more static ports ringed around the body a short distance downstream (reads $p_\infty$, the static pressure of the undisturbed stream).

Airspeed derivation. Apply Bernoulli along the stagnation streamline from freestream (state ∞) to the stagnation point (state 0, where $V = 0$):

$p_\infty + \frac{1}{2}\rho V_\infty^2 = p_0 + 0$ 
 $V_\infty = \sqrt{\frac{2 \left( p_0 - p_\infty \right)}{\rho_\infty}}$ 
Linking to a manometer. If the probe lines connect to a U-tube manometer with gauge fluid of density $\rho_m$ and the lines carry the stream fluid of density $\rho_f$:

$p_0 - p_\infty = \left( \rho_m - \rho_f \right) g h$ 
so that

$V_\infty = \sqrt{\frac{2 \left( \rho_m - \rho_f \right) g h}{\rho_f}}$

- For air with a liquid gauge fluid, $\rho_f \ll \rho_m$ and the subtraction is nearly invisible — but for water tunnels with mercury, $(\rho_m - \rho_f) = 12600$, NOT 13600. That subtraction is tonight's Track-A trap (TA-10).
- Real probes carry a velocity coefficient C_v \approx 0.98$–$1.00: $V = C_v \sqrt{2 \Delta p / \rho}$.

Validity bounds & error bookkeeping:

- Incompressible formula requires $M = V/a < 0.3$, where $a = \sqrt{\gamma R T}$ (≈ 340 m/s at sea level).
- Compressibility effect: $\Delta p \approx \frac{1}{2}\rho V^2 \left( 1 + \frac{M^2}{4} \right)$, so ignoring compressibility makes the simple formula overestimate $V$ by a fraction ≈ $M^2/8$ (≈ 1% at $M = 0.3$ — tolerable; ≈ 8% at $M = 0.8$ — not).
- Aircraft airspeed indicators are calibrated to sea-level density → indicated airspeed (IAS); true airspeed (TAS) at altitude is higher as $\rho$ drops: TAS $=$ IAS $\sqrt{\rho_0 / \rho_\infty}$. Store this for later performance chapters; today, only the note.

#### 🔨 WE-1 — RTT Continuity: Compressible Duct (20:28–20:37)

Statement. Steady air flow through a duct. Inlet: $A_1 = 0.2$ m², $p_1 = 200$ kPa, $T_1 = 30$ °C, $V_1 = 100$ m/s. Exit: $A_2 = 0.1$ m², $p_2 = 150$ kPa, $T_2 = 50$ °C. $R = 287$ J/(kg·K). Find (a) $\dot m$, (b) $V_2$, (c) the $V_2$ that incompressible continuity would wrongly give.

Solution.

Step 1 — Temperatures to kelvin: $T_1 = 303$ K, $T_2 = 323$ K. (°C→K is a 273.15 shift; skipping it is fatal.)

Step 2 — Inlet density from ideal gas:

$\rho_1 = \frac{p_1}{R T_1} = \frac{200000}{287 \times 303} = \frac{200000}{86961} = 2.300 \; \text{kg/m}^3$ 
Step 3 — Mass flow (steady, one-in one-out):

$\dot{m} = \rho_1 A_1 V_1 = 2.300 \times 0.2 \times 100 = 46.0 \; \text{kg/s}$ 
Step 4 — Exit density:

$\rho_2 = \frac{150000}{287 \times 323} = \frac{150000}{92701} = 1.618 \; \text{kg/m}^3$ 
Step 5 — Exit velocity from mass conservation $\dot m = \rho_2 A_2 V_2$:

$V_2 = \frac{\dot{m}}{\rho_2 A_2} = \frac{46.0}{1.618 \times 0.1} = 284.3 \; \text{m/s}$ 
Step 6 — Incompressible illusion: $A_1 V_1 = A_2 V_2$ would give $V_2 = 20 \times 2 = 200$ m/s — a 30% error, because $\rho_2/\rho_1 = 1.618/2.300 = 0.704$.

Answers: (a) $\dot m = 46.0$ kg/s · (b) $V_2 = 284.3$ m/s (GATE band 282–286) · (c) 200 m/s.

2. 200000 ÷ 86961 = → 2.2999 → MS (ρ₁ stored)

#### 🔨 WE-2 — CV Momentum: Nozzle Anchoring Force (20:37–20:46)

Statement. Water ($\rho = 1000$ kg/m³) flows steadily through a horizontal reducing nozzle. Inlet: $D_1 = 200$ mm, $V_1 = 4$ m/s. Exit: $D_2 = 80$ mm, jet discharges to atmosphere. Find (a) $V_2$, (b) inlet gauge pressure, (c) the anchoring force on the nozzle.

Solution.

Step 1 — Areas:

$A_1 = \frac{\pi}{4} (0.2)^2 = 0.031416 \; \text{m}^2, \qquad A_2 = \frac{\pi}{4} (0.08)^2 = 0.005027 \; \text{m}^2$ 
Note $A_2 = A_1 \div 6.25$ (diameter ratio 2.5, squared) — a register-friendly ratio.

Step 2 — Continuity: $Q = A_1 V_1 = 0.031416 \times 4 = 0.12566$ m³/s; $V_2 = Q/A_2 = 4 \times 6.25 = 25.0$ m/s.

Step 3 — Bernoulli along the nozzle centreline (horizontal, inviscid, steady, gauge):

$p_1 = \frac{1}{2} \rho \left( V_2^2 - V_1^2 \right) = 500 \times (625 - 16) = 304500 \; \text{Pa} = 304.5 \; \text{kPa (gauge)}$ 
Step 4 — Momentum, $x$ along the flow, CV enclosing the water inside the nozzle. Forces on the water: $+p_1 A_1$ at the inlet cut (gauge), wall reaction $R$ assumed acting upstream (negative $x$), exit cut at zero gauge. Steady:

$p_1 A_1 - R = \dot{m} (V_2 - V_1)$ 
 $\dot{m} = \rho Q = 1000 \times 0.12566 = 125.66 \; \text{kg/s}$ 
 $R = p_1 A_1 - \dot{m}(V_2 - V_1) = (304500 \times 0.031416) - (125.66 \times 21) = 9566.1 - 2638.9 = 6927 \; \text{N}$ 
The nozzle pushes the water upstream with 6.93 kN; by Newton's third law the water pushes the nozzle downstream with 6.93 kN — the anchor must supply 6.93 kN directed upstream on the nozzle.

Answers: (a) $V_2 = 25.0$ m/s · (b) $p_1 = 304.5$ kPa gauge · (c) $F_{anchor} = 6.93$ kN (GATE band 6.90–6.96 kN).

#### 🔨 WE-3 — Pitot-Static: Aircraft Airspeed with Manometer + Mach Audit (20:46–20:55)

Statement. Aircraft at sea level: $p_\infty = 101.325$ kPa, $T_\infty = 15$ °C, $\rho_\infty = 1.225$ kg/m³, $\gamma = 1.4$, $R = 287$ J/(kg·K). Pitot-static feeds a mercury U-tube ($\rho_m = 13600$ kg/m³), deflection $h = 40$ mm. Step 1 — Pressure difference (subtract the air column in the lines — conceptually mandatory):

$\Delta p = (\rho_m - \rho_f) g h = (13600 - 1.225) \times 9.81 \times 0.04 = 5336 \; \text{Pa}$ 
Step 2 — Incompressible airspeed:

$V_\infty = \sqrt{\frac{2 \Delta p}{\rho_\infty}} = \sqrt{\frac{2 \times 5336}{1.225}} = \sqrt{8713} = 93.3 \; \text{m/s} \; (\approx 336 \; \text{km/h})$ 
Step 3 — Mach audit. Speed of sound at 15 °C:

$a = \sqrt{\gamma R T} = \sqrt{1.4 \times 287 \times 288.15} = 340.3 \; \text{m/s}$ 
 $M = \frac{93.3}{340.3} = 0.274 < 0.3 \; ✅ \; \text{incompressible treatment valid}$ 
Step 4 — Stagnation pressure:

$p_0 = p_\infty + \frac{1}{2}\rho V^2 = 101325 + 5336 = 106661 \; \text{Pa} \approx 106.7 \; \text{kPa}$ 
Answers: (a) 5.34 kPa · (b) $V = 93.3$ m/s (GATE band 92.8–93.8) · (c) $M = 0.274$, valid · (d) 106.7 kPa.

TB-08 · Solution Counting from Rank ⏱ 5:00 · Risk 🔴
 $A$ is a 4×3 matrix with rank($A$) = 3, and rank($[A \,|\, b]$) = 3. The system $A\vec{x} = \vec{b}$ has:
(A) no solution (B) exactly one solution (C) infinitely many solutions (D) exactly three solutions

> [!success]- ✅ Solution · TB-08 — unfold only after your attempt
> (B). rank($A$) = rank($[A|b]$) = 3 → consistent; rank = number of unknowns (3) → no free variables → unique solution. Overdetermined (4 equations) but consistent with full column rank still means exactly one solution.
> 🪤 Trap: intuition screaming "more equations than unknowns → no solution" — only true if inconsistent, i.e., rank($[A|b]$) = 4. The Rouché–Capelli pair (consistency, then freedom) decides everything:
> 
> 
> - rank(A) = rank([A|b]) = n → unique
> - rank(A) = rank([A|b]) < n → infinite
> - rank(A) < rank([A|b]) → no solution

> [!abstract]- 🧲 Track B Formula Chip (60-second recap after Q8)
> 
> 
> - rank(A) ≤ min(m, n); rank of $A^T A$ = rank of A.
> - det = 0 (square) ⟺ rank < n ⟺ homogeneous system has non-trivial solutions ⟺ rows/columns linearly dependent (e.g., three coplanar vectors → rank 2).
> - Triple product zero ⟺ coplanar ⟺ rank 2 — one concept, three costumes (TB-02/03/06 were the same exam question).

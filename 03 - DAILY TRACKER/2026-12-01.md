Open `01 - Stress Strain and Elasticity`.

                    \│             │/
                     └──────┬──────┘
                            │

1. **Stress Tensor Symmetry (Cauchy's Reciprocal Theorem):**
   From rotational moment equilibrium of a differential element ($\sum M_z = 0$):
   $$\boxed{\tau_{xy} = \tau_{yx}, \quad \tau_{yz} = \tau_{zy}, \quad \tau_{zx} = \tau_{xz}}$$
   *(6 independent stress components in 3D: $\sigma_x, \sigma_y, \sigma_z, \tau_{xy}, \tau_{yz}, \tau_{zx}$).*

2. **Strain Tensor & Engineering Shear Strain:**
   - Normal strains: $\epsilon_x = \frac{\partial u}{\partial x}, \quad \epsilon_y = \frac{\partial v}{\partial y}, \quad \epsilon_z = \frac{\partial w}{\partial z}$.
   - Engineering shear strains vs. Tensor shear strains:
     $$\boxed{\gamma_{xy} = 2 \epsilon_{xy} = \frac{\partial u}{\partial y} + \frac{\partial v}{\partial x}, \quad \gamma_{yz} = 2 \epsilon_{yz}, \quad \gamma_{zx} = 2 \epsilon_{zx}}$$

### Step 3 — 3D Generalized Hooke's Law (20 min)

For a homogeneous, isotropic, linear elastic material:

$$\boxed{\epsilon_x = \frac{1}{E}\left[\sigma_x - \nu(\sigma_y + \sigma_z)\right] + \alpha \Delta T}$$
$$\boxed{\epsilon_y = \frac{1}{E}\left[\sigma_y - \nu(\sigma_x + \sigma_z)\right] + \alpha \Delta T}$$
$$\boxed{\epsilon_z = \frac{1}{E}\left[\sigma_z - \nu(\sigma_x + \sigma_y)\right] + \alpha \Delta T}$$
$$\boxed{\gamma_{xy} = \frac{\tau_{xy}}{G}, \quad \gamma_{yz} = \frac{\tau_{yz}}{G}, \quad \gamma_{zx} = \frac{\tau_{zx}}{G}}$$

- **Volumetric Strain (Dilatation $e$):**
  $$e \equiv \frac{\Delta V}{V} = \epsilon_x + \epsilon_y + \epsilon_z = \frac{1 - 2\nu}{E}(\sigma_x + \sigma_y + \sigma_z) + 3\alpha \Delta T = \frac{\sigma_{\text{mean}}}{K} + 3\alpha \Delta T$$
  *(where $\sigma_{\text{mean}} \equiv \frac{\sigma_x + \sigma_y + \sigma_z}{3}$ is hydrostatic pressure/stress).*

---

### Step 4 — Interrelations Among Elastic Constants ($E, G, K, \nu$) (15 min)

Only **TWO** elastic constants are independent for an isotropic material:

$$\boxed{E = 2G(1 + \nu)}$$
$$\boxed{E = 3K(1 - 2\nu)}$$
$$\boxed{E = \frac{9KG}{3K + G}}$$
$$\boxed{\nu = \frac{3K - 2G}{2(3K + G)} = \frac{E - 2G}{2G}}$$

#### 🔑 Theoretical & Physical Limits of Poisson's Ratio ($\nu$):
- For positive strain energy and stability: $\mathbf{-1.0 \le \nu \le 0.5}$.
- Incompressible Material ($\Delta V = 0 \implies e = 0$): $\mathbf{\nu = 0.5 \implies K = \infty}$ (Rubber, liquid-like polymers).
- Aerospace Metals: Aluminum ($\nu \approx 0.33, E \approx 70\text{ GPa}$), Titanium ($\nu \approx 0.31, E \approx 110\text{ GPa}$), Structural Steel ($\nu \approx 0.29, E \approx 200\text{ GPa}$), Cork ($\nu \approx 0$).

---

### Step 5 — Traps & Self-Check (5 min)
> 🔴 **Trap 1: Engineering Shear Strain vs. Tensor Shear Strain**
> $\gamma_{xy} = 2 \epsilon_{xy}$. In constitutive equations $\tau_{xy} = G \gamma_{xy} = 2G \epsilon_{xy}$. In strain transformation / Mohr's circle of strain, you MUST plot $\frac{\gamma_{xy}}{2}$ (tensor shear strain) on the vertical axis!
> 🔴 **Trap 2: Lateral Strain Sign**
> Under uniaxial tension $\sigma_x > 0$, lateral strains are compressive: $\epsilon_y = \epsilon_z = -\frac{\nu \sigma_x}{E} < 0$.

### Step 3 — D1 Mini Quiz (3:45–4:00) — 5 Questions (Timed 10 min)

> **Q1 (1 mark):** For an isotropic, linear elastic material with Young's modulus $E$ and shear modulus $G$, the bulk modulus $K$ is given by:
> **(A)** $\frac{E G}{3(3G - E)}$ **(B)** $\frac{E G}{3(9G - E)}$ **(C)** $\frac{E G}{3(3G + E)}$ **(D)** $\frac{E G}{9G - 3E}$

> **Q2 (1 mark):** If a material is perfectly incompressible ($\Delta V = 0$), its Poisson's ratio $\nu$ must be:
> **(A)** $0$ **(B)** $0.25$ **(C)** $0.33$ **(D)** $0.50$

> **Q3 (2 marks):** A material has $E = 120\text{ GPa}$ and $G = 48\text{ GPa}$. The bulk modulus $K$ (in $\text{GPa}$) is:
> **(A)** $60.0\text{ GPa}$ **(B)** $80.0\text{ GPa}$ **(C)** $100.0\text{ GPa}$ **(D)** $120.0\text{ GPa}$

> **Q4 (2 marks):** A cylindrical rod with diameter $20\text{ mm}$ and length $500\text{ mm}$ ($E = 200\text{ GPa}, \nu = 0.30$) is subjected to an axial tensile force of $P = 62.83\text{ kN}$. The change in diameter $\Delta d$ (in $\text{mm}$) is:
> **(A)** $-0.006\text{ mm}$ **(B)** $-0.009\text{ mm}$ **(C)** $-0.012\text{ mm}$ **(D)** $-0.018\text{ mm}$

> **Q5 (2 marks NAT):** A solid cube of material with side length $a = 50\text{ mm}$ ($E = 100\text{ GPa}, \nu = 0.25$) is subjected to uniform hydrostatic pressure $p = 80\text{ MPa}$ ($\sigma_x = \sigma_y = \sigma_z = -80\text{ MPa}$). The change in volume $\Delta V$ (in $\text{mm}^3$) is ___ $\text{mm}^3$.

> [!faq]- 🔍 View D1 Mini Quiz Answers & Step-by-Step Solutions
>
> **Q1: (A) EG / [3(3G − E)]**
> From $E = 2G(1+\nu) \implies \nu = \frac{E - 2G}{2G}$. Substitute into $K = \frac{E}{3(1-2\nu)} = \frac{E}{3[1 - (E-2G)/G]} = \frac{E G}{3(3G - E)}$.
>
> **Q2: (D) 0.50**
> $e = \frac{1-2\nu}{E}(\sum \sigma) = 0 \implies 1 - 2\nu = 0 \implies \mathbf{\nu = 0.50}$.
>
> **Q3: (B) 80.0 GPa**
> $\nu = \frac{E}{2G} - 1 = \frac{120}{2(48)} - 1 = \frac{120}{96} - 1 = 1.25 - 1 = 0.25$.
> $K = \frac{E}{3(1 - 2\nu)} = \frac{120}{3(1 - 2(0.25))} = \frac{120}{3(0.50)} = \frac{120}{1.50} = \mathbf{80.0\text{ GPa}}$.
>
> **Q4: (B) −0.006 mm**
> $A = \frac{\pi}{4}(20^2) = 100\pi = 314.16\text{ mm}^2$.
> $\sigma_x = \frac{62830}{314.16} = 200\text{ MPa}$.
> $\epsilon_x = \frac{200}{200\times 10^3} = 0.0010$.
> $\epsilon_{\text{lat}} = -\nu \epsilon_x = -(0.30)(0.0010) = -0.00030$.
> $\Delta d = d \cdot \epsilon_{\text{lat}} = 20(-0.00030) = \mathbf{-0.006\text{ mm}}$.
>
> **Q5: −150.0**
> $V_0 = 50^3 = 125,000\text{ mm}^3$.
> $e = \frac{1 - 2\nu}{E}(\sigma_x + \sigma_y + \sigma_z) = \frac{1 - 2(0.25)}{100\times 10^3}(-80 - 80 - 80) = \frac{0.50}{100000}(-240) = -0.00120$.
> $\Delta V = V_0 \cdot e = (125,000)(-0.00120) = \mathbf{-150.0\text{ mm}^3}$.

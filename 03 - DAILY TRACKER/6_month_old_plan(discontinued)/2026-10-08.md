---

### Step 2 — Steady Climb Equations of Motion (20 min)
Open `03.4a - Steady Climb Performance`.

For an airplane in steady climbing flight along a flight path inclined at angle $\gamma$:

```
                     STEADY CLIMB FORCE BALANCE
                                L ^
                                  │   / Flight Path (Angle γ)
                           T      │  /
                      ◄───────────┼─/────────► V
                                  │/
                                  │
                                  ▼ W (Vertical)
                 Parallel: T − D − W sin γ = 0 ⟹ sin γ = (T − D) / W
                 Perpend. : L − W cos γ = 0   ⟹ L = W cos γ ≈ W (for γ < 15°)
```

1. **Climb Angle ($\gamma$):**
   $$\boxed{\sin\gamma = \frac{T - D}{W} = \frac{\text{Excess Thrust}}{W}}$$
2. **Rate of Climb ($R/C$ or Vertical Velocity $V_v$):**
   $$\boxed{R/C = V\sin\gamma = \frac{(T - D)V}{W} = \frac{P_A - P_R}{W} = \frac{\text{Excess Power}}{W}}$$

---

### Step 3 — Jet Aircraft vs. Propeller Aircraft Climb Optimization (20 min)

```
                 JET vs PROPELLER CLIMB OPTIMUMS
           Jet Aircraft (T ≈ const)            Propeller Aircraft (P_A ≈ const)
      Excess Thrust Max at V_md ((L/D)_max)       Excess Power Max at V_mp
         ▲ γ_max at V_md                             ▲ R/C_max at V_mp
         ▲ R/C_max at V > V_md                       ▲ γ_max at speed < V_md
```

| Performance Parameter | Jet Aircraft (Thrust $T_A \approx \text{const}$) | Propeller Aircraft (Power $P_A = \eta_p P \approx \text{const}$) |
|---|---|---|
| **Max Climb Angle ($\gamma_{\max}$)** | Occurs at **Minimum Drag Speed ($V = V_{md}$)** where $(L/D)_{\max}$:<br>$$\boxed{\sin\gamma_{\max} = \frac{T}{W} - \frac{1}{(L/D)_{\max}}}$$ | Occurs at speed **lower than $V_{md}$** (between $V_{mp}$ and $V_{md}$) |
| **Max Rate of Climb ($R/C_{\max}$)** | Occurs at **speed higher than $V_{md}$** ($V_{(R/C)\max} > V_{md}$):<br>$$V = \sqrt{\frac{2W}{\rho S}}\left[\frac{\frac{T}{W} + \sqrt{\left(\frac{T}{W}\right)^2 + 12 K C_{D0}}}{6 C_{D0}}\right]^{1/2}$$ | Occurs at **Minimum Power Speed ($V = V_{mp}$)** where $\mathbf{(C_L^{3/2}/C_D)_{\max}}$:<br>$$\boxed{R/C_{\max} = \frac{P_A - P_{R,\min}}{W}}$$ |

### Step 4 — Ceilings & Time to Climb (10 min)
Open `03.4c - Absolute and Service Ceilings`.

1. **Absolute Ceiling ($h_{\text{abs}}$):**
   - The theoretical maximum altitude where maximum rate of climb drops to zero ($\mathbf{R/C_{\max} = 0}$).
   - At $h_{\text{abs}}$, the power available curve touches the power required curve at exactly one point ($V_{\text{stall}} = V_{md} = V_{\max}$).
2. **Service Ceiling ($h_{\text{service}}$):**
   - General Aviation / Propeller: Altitude where $\mathbf{R/C_{\max} = 100\text{ ft/min} = 0.508\text{ m/s}}$.
   - Commercial Jet Transport: Altitude where $\mathbf{R/C_{\max} = 500\text{ ft/min} = 2.54\text{ m/s}}$.
3. **Time to Climb Integration:**
   Assuming a linear variation of $R/C$ with altitude: $R/C(h) = (R/C)_0\left(1 - \frac{h}{h_{\text{abs}}}\right)$:
   $$\boxed{t = \int_0^h \frac{dh}{R/C(h)} = \frac{h_{\text{abs}}}{(R/C)_0}\ln\left(\frac{h_{\text{abs}}}{h_{\text{abs}} - h}\right)}$$

---

### Step 5 — Traps & Self-Check (5 min)
> 🔴 **Trap 1: Maximum Climb Angle vs. Rate of Climb for Jet**
> For a jet: Maximum climb angle occurs at $V_{md}$ (where $(L/D)_{\max}$), BUT maximum rate of climb occurs at a **higher speed $V > V_{md}$**!
> 🔴 **Trap 2: Propeller $R/C_{\max}$ Condition**
> For a propeller aircraft: Maximum rate of climb occurs strictly at **$V_{mp}$** (Minimum power required speed where $C_L^{3/2}/C_D$ is maximum).

### Step 3 — D3 Mini Quiz (3:45–4:00) — 5 Questions (Timed 10 min)

> **Q1 (1 mark):** For a jet aircraft with constant thrust, the maximum climb angle $\gamma_{\max}$ occurs at:
> **(A)** Speed for minimum power required ($V_{mp}$)
> **(B)** Speed for minimum thrust required ($V_{md}$)
> **(C)** Stall speed ($V_{\text{stall}}$)
> **(D)** Maximum speed ($V_{\max}$)

> **Q2 (2 marks):** A jet aircraft has a thrust-to-weight ratio $T/W = 0.30$ and maximum lift-to-drag ratio $(L/D)_{\max} = 20$. The maximum climb angle $\gamma_{\max}$ (in degrees) is:
> **(A)** $11.5^\circ$ **(B)** $14.5^\circ$ **(C)** $17.5^\circ$ **(D)** $20.0^\circ$

> **Q3 (2 marks):** A propeller-driven aircraft of weight $W = 20\text{ kN}$ has an engine delivering available power $P_A = 300\text{ kW}$. If the minimum power required in steady level flight is $P_{R,\min} = 120\text{ kW}$, the maximum rate of climb $R/C_{\max}$ is:
> **(A)** $6\text{ m/s}$ **(B)** $9\text{ m/s}$ **(C)** $12\text{ m/s}$ **(D)** $15\text{ m/s}$

> **Q4 (1 mark):** The absolute ceiling of an aircraft is defined as the altitude where:
> **(A)** Maximum rate of climb is $100\text{ ft/min}$ **(B)** Maximum rate of climb is zero **(C)** True airspeed is maximum **(D)** Dynamic pressure is zero

> **Q5 (2 marks NAT):** An airplane has a sea-level maximum rate of climb $(R/C)_0 = 10\text{ m/s}$ and an absolute ceiling $h_{\text{abs}} = 10,000\text{ m}$. Assuming a linear variation of $R/C$ with altitude, the time taken (in seconds) to climb from sea level to $5,000\text{ m}$ is ___ (round off to 1 decimal place).

> [!faq]- 🔍 View D3 Mini Quiz Answers & Step-by-Step Solutions
>
> **Q1: (B) Speed for minimum thrust required (V_md)**
> $\sin\gamma = \frac{T - D}{W}$. For constant thrust $T$, excess thrust is maximum where drag $D$ is minimum ($V = V_{md}$).
>
> **Q2: (B) 14.5°**
> $\sin\gamma_{\max} = \frac{T}{W} - \frac{1}{(L/D)_{\max}} = 0.30 - \frac{1}{20} = 0.30 - 0.05 = 0.25$.
> $\gamma_{\max} = \arcsin(0.25) \approx \mathbf{14.48^\circ \approx 14.5^\circ}$.
>
> **Q3: (B) 9 m/s**
> $R/C_{\max} = \frac{P_A - P_{R,\min}}{W} = \frac{300,000 - 120,000}{20,000} = \frac{180,000}{20,000} = \mathbf{9\text{ m/s}}$.
>
> **Q4: (B) Maximum rate of climb is zero**
> At absolute ceiling, excess power is zero $\implies R/C_{\max} = 0$.
>
> **Q5: 693.1**
> $t = \frac{h_{\text{abs}}}{(R/C)_0} \ln\left(\frac{h_{\text{abs}}}{h_{\text{abs}} - h}\right) = \frac{10000}{10} \ln\left(\frac{10000}{10000 - 5000}\right) = 1000 \ln(2) = 1000(0.693147) = \mathbf{693.1\text{ seconds} \approx 11.55\text{ mins}}$.

### Step 2 — Equations of Motion in Gliding Flight ($T = 0$) (20 min)
Open `03.4b - Steady Descent and Glide Performance`.

When an airplane loses thrust ($T = 0$), it glides steadily downward at glide angle $\theta$ relative to the horizontal:

```
                   UNPOWERED GLIDING FLIGHT FORCE BALANCE
                                 L ^
                                   │   / Flight Path (Glide Angle θ)
                            D      │  /
                       ◄───────────┼─/────────► V
                                   │/
                                   │
                                   ▼ W (Vertical)
                   Along Path   : D − W sin θ = 0 ⟹ sin θ = D / W
                   Perpendicular: L − W cos θ = 0 ⟹ cos θ = L / W
```

#### 🔑 The Fundamental Glide Relations:
1. **Glide Angle ($\theta$):**
   $$\boxed{\tan\theta = \frac{D}{L} = \frac{1}{L/D}}$$
2. **Glide Ratio & Range ($R$):**
   $$\boxed{\frac{R}{h} = \frac{1}{\tan\theta} = \frac{L}{D} \implies R = h \left(\frac{L}{D}\right)}$$

### Step 3 — Maximum Glide Range ($R_{\max}$) vs. Minimum Sink Rate ($V_{\text{sink},\min}$) (25 min)

> 🔴 **MEMORIZE THIS COMPARISON TABLE FOR GATE!**

| Optimization Objective | Maximum Glide Range ($R_{\max}$) / Flattest Glide | Minimum Sink Rate ($V_{\text{sink},\min}$) / Maximum Time in Air |
|---|---|---|
| **Governing Optimization** | Maximizes **$(L/D)_{\max}$** | Maximizes **$(C_L^{3/2} / C_D)_{\max}$** |
| **Drag Component Balance** | $\boxed{C_{D0} = C_{Di} = K C_L^2}$ (Parasite = Induced) | $\boxed{C_{Di} = 3 C_{D0}}$ (Induced = 3 × Parasite) |
| **Optimal Lift Coefficient** | $\boxed{C_{L,md} = \sqrt{\frac{C_{D0}}{K}}}$ | $\boxed{C_{L,mp} = \sqrt{\frac{3 C_{D0}}{K}} = \sqrt{3} C_{L,md}}$ |
| **Formula for Performance** | $\boxed{R_{\max} = h \cdot (L/D)_{\max} = \frac{h}{2\sqrt{K C_{D0}}}}$ | $\boxed{V_{\text{sink},\min} = \sqrt{\frac{2W}{\rho S}}\frac{1}{(C_L^{3/2}/C_D)_{\max}}}$ |
| **Effect of Aircraft Weight ($W$)** | **INDEPENDENT OF WEIGHT!** (Glide distance $R_{\max}$ is identical for light or heavy aircraft!) | **Increases with weight** ($V_{\text{sink},\min} \propto \sqrt{W}$) |
| **Optimal Forward Speed ($V$)** | $\boxed{V = V_{md} = \sqrt{\frac{2W}{\rho S C_{L,md}}} \propto \sqrt{W}}$ | $\boxed{V = V_{mp} = \frac{V_{md}}{3^{1/4}} \approx 0.76 V_{md} \propto \sqrt{W}}$ |
| **Effect of Altitude ($\rho$)** | **INDEPENDENT of altitude!** | Decreases with air density ($V_{\text{sink}} \propto 1/\sqrt{\rho}$) |

### Step 4 — Physical Insights & The "Heavy Glider" Paradox (10 min)

- **The Paradox:** Why does adding water ballast to a sailplane NOT reduce its maximum glide distance?
- **The Explanation:**
  - $R_{\max} = h(L/D)_{\max}$. Since $(L/D)_{\max}$ depends ONLY on $C_{D0}$ and $K$, the glide slope is completely unchanged!
  - However, $V_{md} \propto \sqrt{W}$. The heavier sailplane glides at a **higher forward speed**, covering the exact same distance in a **shorter time**, which is a massive tactical advantage in racing!

---

### Step 5 — Traps & Self-Check (5 min)
> 🔴 **Trap 1: Sink Rate Optimization**
> Minimum sink rate ($V_{\text{sink},\min} = \frac{P_R}{W}$) occurs at **$V_{mp}$ (Minimum Power Speed)**, NOT at minimum drag speed $V_{md}$!
> 🔴 **Trap 2: Headwind / Tailwind Effect on Glide**
> - Headwind **decreases** ground glide range: $R = (V_{md} - V_{\text{wind}}) \cdot t$. (Pilot must fly *faster* than $V_{md}$ in a headwind to maximize range!).
> - Tailwind **increases** ground glide range.

### Step 3 — D4 Mini Quiz (3:45–4:00) — 5 Questions (Timed 10 min)

> **Q1 (1 mark):** In an unpowered steady glide, the maximum horizontal glide distance from a given altitude depends on:
> **(A)** Aircraft weight ($W$) **(B)** Maximum lift-to-drag ratio $(L/D)_{\max}$ **(C)** Wing area ($S$) **(D)** Air density ($\rho$)

> **Q2 (2 marks):** A glider has a maximum lift-to-drag ratio $(L/D)_{\max} = 25$. If it starts gliding from an altitude of $h = 3000\text{ m}$ in still air, the maximum horizontal distance covered (in $\text{km}$) is:
> **(A)** $50\text{ km}$ **(B)** $75\text{ km}$ **(C)** $100\text{ km}$ **(D)** $125\text{ km}$

> **Q3 (2 marks):** If water ballast is added to a glider increasing its total weight by $44\%$, the maximum glide range in still air:
> **(A)** Increases by $44\%$ **(B)** Decreases by $44\%$ **(C)** Remains unchanged **(D)** Decreases by $20\%$

> **Q4 (1 mark):** The minimum sink rate of a glider in steady unpowered flight occurs at the speed for:
> **(A)** Minimum drag ($V_{md}$) **(B)** Minimum power required ($V_{mp}$) **(C)** Maximum lift coefficient ($V_{\text{stall}}$) **(D)** Zero induced drag

> **Q5 (2 marks NAT):** A glider has a drag polar $C_D = 0.015 + 0.03 C_L^2$. The minimum glide angle $\theta_{\min}$ (in degrees) is ___ (round off to 2 decimal places).

> [!faq]- 🔍 View D4 Mini Quiz Answers & Step-by-Step Solutions
>
> **Q1: (B) Maximum lift-to-drag ratio (L/D)_max**
> $R_{\max} = h(L/D)_{\max}$, which is completely independent of weight, wing area, and altitude.
>
> **Q2: (B) 75 km**
> $R_{\max} = h \times (L/D)_{\max} = 3000\text{ m} \times 25 = 75,000\text{ m} = \mathbf{75\text{ km}}$.
>
> **Q3: (C) Remains unchanged**
> Glide range $R_{\max} = h(L/D)_{\max}$ is invariant with respect to weight. The heavier glider simply flies faster ($V \propto \sqrt{W}$) covering the same distance.
>
> **Q4: (B) Minimum power required (V_mp)**
> $V_{\text{sink}} = \frac{P_R}{W}$. Minimum sink rate corresponds to the point of minimum power required ($V = V_{mp}$).
>
> **Q5: 2.43**
> $(L/D)_{\max} = \frac{1}{2\sqrt{K C_{D0}}} = \frac{1}{2\sqrt{0.03 \times 0.015}} = \frac{1}{2\sqrt{0.00045}} = \frac{1}{2(0.021213)} = \frac{1}{0.042426} \approx 23.57$.
> $\tan\theta_{\min} = \frac{1}{(L/D)_{\max}} = 0.042426$.
> $\theta_{\min} = \arctan(0.042426) \approx 2.429^\circ \approx \mathbf{2.43^\circ}$.

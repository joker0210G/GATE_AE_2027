### Step 2 — Turboprop Engine Working Principle & Architecture (20 min)
Open `02 - Aircraft Engine Types and Performance`.

```
                    TURBOPROP ENGINE ARCHITECTURE
            Propeller      Gearbox        Core (Gas Generator)      Jet Nozzle
             ╭───╮        ┌───────┐     ┌───────────────────────┐    ╭───────╮
             │   │ ◄──────┤ 10:1  │◄────┤ Comp ──► Burn ──► Turb│───►│ Residual
             │   │ (F_prop)└───────┘     └───────────────────────┘    │ F_jet ╰─►
             ╰───╯        (P_shaft)       (85–90% Enthalpy)          ╰───────╯
```

1. **Energy Extraction Mechanism:**
   - A gas generator (compressor, burner, compressor-turbine) produces high-pressure, high-temperature gas.
   - A **Power Turbine** extracts $\approx 80\text{ to } 90\%$ of the remaining gas energy to drive the reduction gearbox and propeller.
   - The remaining $10\text{ to } 20\%$ gas energy is expanded through a nozzle to produce residual jet thrust ($F_{\text{jet}}$).

2. **Reduction Gearbox:**
   - Gas turbines spin at very high rotational speeds ($10,000\text{ to } 30,000\text{ RPM}$).
   - Propellers must operate at much lower speeds ($1,000\text{ to } 2,000\text{ RPM}$) to avoid supersonic tip speeds.
   - Reduction ratio: $N_{\text{turbine}} / N_{\text{prop}} \approx 10:1\text{ to } 15:1$. Gearbox efficiency $\eta_{\text{gb}} \approx 0.98\text{--}0.99$.

---

### Step 3 — Turboprop Thrust, Power & ESHP Formulations (25 min)

1. **Shaft Power ($P_{\text{shaft}}$):**
   $$\boxed{P_{\text{shaft}} = \dot{W}_{\text{power turbine}} \cdot \eta_{\text{gb}} = \dot{m}_a(1+f) c_{pg}(T_{04.5} - T_{05})\eta_{\text{gb}}}$$

2. **Propeller Thrust ($F_{\text{prop}}$):**
   $$\boxed{F_{\text{prop}} = \frac{P_{\text{shaft}} \cdot \eta_{\text{prop}}}{V_0}}$$
   *(where $\eta_{\text{prop}}$ is propeller aerodynamic efficiency $\approx 0.80\text{ to } 0.85$, and $V_0$ is flight speed).*

3. **Residual Jet Thrust ($F_{\text{jet}}$):**
   $$\boxed{F_{\text{jet}} = \dot{m}_a\left[(1+f)V_9 - V_0\right] + (p_9 - p_0)A_9}$$

4. **Total Effective Engine Thrust ($F_{\text{total}}$):**
   $$\boxed{F_{\text{total}} = F_{\text{prop}} + F_{\text{jet}} = \frac{P_{\text{shaft}}\cdot\eta_{\text{prop}}}{V_0} + \dot{m}_a\left[(1+f)V_9 - V_0\right]}$$

5. **Equivalent Shaft Power ($\text{ESHP}$ / $\text{ESPower}$):**
   - **In SI Units ($\text{Watts}$ or $\text{kW}$):**
     $$\boxed{P_{\text{equiv}} = P_{\text{shaft}} + \frac{F_{\text{jet}} \cdot V_0}{\eta_{\text{prop}}}}$$
   - **In Imperial Units ($\text{Horsepower}$):**
     $$\boxed{\text{ESHP} = \text{SHP} + \frac{F_{\text{jet}}}{2.5\text{ lbf/hp}}}$$
     *(Rule of thumb: $1\text{ hp}$ of shaft power typically generates $\approx 2.5\text{ lbf}$ of static thrust).*

6. **Turboshaft Engines:**
   - In helicopters (turboshaft), almost $100\%$ of available gas energy is extracted as shaft power to drive the rotor system; $F_{\text{jet}} \approx 0 \implies P_{\text{equiv}} \approx P_{\text{shaft}}$.

---

### Step 4 — Propeller Compressibility Speed Limitation (10 min)

The helical velocity at the propeller tip is the vector sum of forward flight speed $V_0$ and rotational tip speed $\Omega R$:
$$V_{\text{tip,helical}} = \sqrt{V_0^2 + (\Omega R_{\text{prop}})^2}$$
- When $M_{\text{tip}} \ge 1.0$, shock waves form on the propeller blades, causing severe wave drag, vibration, noise, and a catastrophic drop in propeller efficiency ($\eta_{\text{prop}} \downarrow$).
- **Result:** Turboprop aircraft are restricted to subsonic cruise speeds: $\mathbf{M_0 < 0.65\text{ to } 0.70}$ ($V_0 \le 200\text{--}220\text{ m/s}$).

---

### Step 5 — Traps & Self-Check (5 min)
> 🔴 **Trap 1: Propeller Thrust Division by $V_0$**
> $F_{\text{prop}} = \frac{P_{\text{shaft}}\eta_{\text{prop}}}{V_0}$. As $V_0 \to 0$ (Static takeoff), propeller thrust is governed by static thrust momentum theory ($F \propto (P \cdot D)^{2/3}$), NOT infinite thrust!
> 🔴 **Trap 2: Power Turbine vs. Compressor Turbine**
> In a free-turbine turboprop, the **Gas Generator Turbine (HPT)** drives the compressor, while the **Power Turbine (LPT)** independently drives the propeller shaft.

### Step 3 — D3 Mini Quiz (3:45–4:00) — 5 Questions (Timed 10 min)

> **Q1 (1 mark):** In a turboprop engine, approximately what percentage of the total available gas energy is extracted by the turbine to drive the propeller shaft?
> **(A)** $10\%\text{ to } 20\%$ **(B)** $30\%\text{ to } 40\%$ **(C)** $50\%\text{ to } 60\%$ **(D)** $80\%\text{ to } 90\%$

> **Q2 (1 mark):** The maximum flight speed of a conventional turboprop aircraft is primarily limited to approximately Mach $0.65\text{--}0.70$ due to:
> **(A)** Insufficient turbine inlet temperature
> **(B)** Compressor surge at high altitudes
> **(C)** Shock wave formation and compressibility drag rise at the propeller blade tips
> **(D)** Combustor flameout

> **Q3 (2 marks):** A turboprop engine produces shaft power $P_{\text{shaft}} = 2400\text{ kW}$ at flight velocity $V_0 = 160\text{ m/s}$. The propeller efficiency is $\eta_{\text{prop}} = 0.85$. If the residual jet exhaust thrust is $F_{\text{jet}} = 3.2\text{ kN}$, the total propulsive thrust $F_{\text{total}}$ (in $\text{kN}$) is:
> **(A)** $12.75\text{ kN}$ **(B)** $15.95\text{ kN}$ **(C)** $18.20\text{ kN}$ **(D)** $21.50\text{ kN}$

> **Q4 (2 marks):** A turboprop produces $\text{SHP} = 3500\text{ hp}$ and residual jet thrust $F_{\text{jet}} = 750\text{ lbf}$. The Equivalent Shaft Horsepower ($\text{ESHP}$) of the engine is:
> **(A)** $3600\text{ ESHP}$ **(B)** $3800\text{ ESHP}$ **(C)** $4000\text{ ESHP}$ **(D)** $4250\text{ ESHP}$

> **Q5 (2 marks NAT):** A turboprop aircraft flies at $V_0 = 120\text{ m/s}$. The engine produces $P_{\text{shaft}} = 1800\text{ kW}$ and $F_{\text{jet}} = 2.50\text{ kN}$ with propeller efficiency $\eta_{\text{prop}} = 0.80$. The equivalent shaft power $P_{\text{equiv}}$ (in $\text{kW}$) is ___ $\text{kW}$.

> [!faq]- 🔍 View D3 Mini Quiz Answers & Step-by-Step Solutions
>
> **Q1: (D) 80% to 90%**
> The power turbine extracts 80–90% of available gas enthalpy for the propeller, leaving 10–20% for jet thrust.
>
> **Q2: (C) Shock wave formation at propeller blade tips**
> Helical tip speed exceeds $M=1$ when flight Mach reaches $\approx 0.65\text{--}0.70$, causing severe compressibility losses.
>
> **Q3: (B) 15.95 kN**
> $F_{\text{prop}} = \frac{P_{\text{shaft}}\eta_{\text{prop}}}{V_0} = \frac{2400 \times 10^3 \times 0.85}{160} = \frac{2,040,000}{160} = 12,750\text{ N} = 12.75\text{ kN}$.
> $F_{\text{total}} = F_{\text{prop}} + F_{\text{jet}} = 12.75 + 3.20 = \mathbf{15.95\text{ kN}}$.
>
> **Q4: (B) 3800 ESHP**
> $\text{ESHP} = \text{SHP} + \frac{F_{\text{jet}}}{2.5} = 3500 + \frac{750}{2.5} = 3500 + 300 = \mathbf{3800\text{ ESHP}}$.
>
> **Q5: 2175**
> $P_{\text{equiv}} = P_{\text{shaft}} + \frac{F_{\text{jet}} V_0}{\eta_{\text{prop}}} = 1800 + \frac{2.50 \times 120}{0.80} = 1800 + \frac{300}{0.80} = 1800 + 375 = \mathbf{2175\text{ kW}}$.

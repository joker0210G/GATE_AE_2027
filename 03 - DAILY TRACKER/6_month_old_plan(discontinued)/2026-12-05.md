**BLOCK A: Direct Concept & Formula Recall (1 Mark Each, Q1–Q10)**

**Q1.** The symmetry of the Cauchy stress tensor ($\tau_{xy} = \tau_{yx}$) is a direct consequence of:
(A) Linear momentum balance (B) Rotational moment equilibrium of a differential element (C) Strain compatibility (D) Minimum potential energy

**Q2.** The relationship between engineering shear strain $\gamma_{xy}$ and tensor shear strain $\epsilon_{xy}$ is:
(A) $\gamma_{xy} = \epsilon_{xy}$ (B) $\gamma_{xy} = 2 \epsilon_{xy}$ (C) $\gamma_{xy} = \frac{\epsilon_{xy}}{2}$ (D) $\gamma_{xy} = 4 \epsilon_{xy}$

**Q3.** For an isotropic material, the theoretical upper limit of Poisson's ratio $\nu$ to guarantee positive volumetric strain energy is:
(A) $0.25$ (B) $0.33$ (C) $0.50$ (D) $1.00$

**Q4.** In a state of plane stress in the $x\text{--}y$ plane ($\sigma_z = \tau_{xz} = \tau_{yz} = 0$), the out-of-plane normal strain $\epsilon_z$ is:
(A) Strictly zero
(B) $-\frac{\nu}{E}(\sigma_x + \sigma_y)$
(C) $\frac{1}{E}(\sigma_x + \sigma_y)$
(D) $\nu(\sigma_x + \sigma_y)$

**Q5.** In a state of plane strain in the $x\text{--}y$ plane ($\epsilon_z = \gamma_{xz} = \gamma_{yz} = 0$), the out-of-plane normal stress $\sigma_z$ is:
(A) Zero (B) $\nu(\sigma_x + \sigma_y)$ (C) $\frac{\sigma_x + \sigma_y}{2}$ (D) $\frac{\nu}{E}(\sigma_x + \sigma_y)$

**Q6.** The sum of normal stresses on any two mutually perpendicular planes ($\sigma_x + \sigma_y$) is:
(A) Dependent on the inclination angle $\theta$
(B) A stress invariant ($I_1$) equal to $\sigma_1 + \sigma_2$
(C) Equal to the maximum shear stress
(D) Zero in plane stress

**Q7.** On a principal stress plane, the shear stress is:
(A) Maximum (B) Equal to $\sigma_{\text{avg}}$ (C) Strictly zero (D) Equal to $E \nu$

**Q8.** In a state of plane stress, if both in-plane principal stresses are tensile ($\sigma_1 > 0, \sigma_2 > 0$ with $\sigma_1 > \sigma_2$), the absolute maximum shear stress $\tau_{\max,\text{abs}}$ is:
(A) $\frac{\sigma_1 - \sigma_2}{2}$ (B) $\frac{\sigma_1}{2}$ (C) $\frac{\sigma_2}{2}$ (D) $\sigma_1 - \sigma_2$

**Q9.** The yield locus of the Maximum Shear Stress (Tresca) theory plotted in the $\sigma_1\text{--}\sigma_2$ plane is a:
(A) Circle (B) Square (C) Hexagon (D) Ellipse

**Q10.** Under pure shear stress $\tau$, the yield shear stress predicted by the von Mises theory is related to the tensile yield strength $\sigma_y$ by:
(A) $\tau_y = 0.50 \sigma_y$ (B) $\tau_y = \frac{\sigma_y}{\sqrt{3}} \approx 0.577 \sigma_y$ (C) $\tau_y = \sigma_y$ (D) $\tau_y = \sqrt{2} \sigma_y$

**BLOCK B: Standard & Multi-Step Problems (2 Marks Each, Q11–Q25)**

**Q11.** An isotropic material has Young's modulus $E = 210\text{ GPa}$ and shear modulus $G = 84\text{ GPa}$. Its Poisson's ratio $\nu$ is:
(A) $0.20$ (B) $0.25$ (C) $0.30$ (D) $0.35$

**Q12.** For the material in Q11 ($E = 210\text{ GPa}, \nu = 0.25$), the bulk modulus $K$ (in $\text{GPa}$) is:
(A) $105.0\text{ GPa}$ (B) $140.0\text{ GPa}$ (C) $168.0\text{ GPa}$ (D) $210.0\text{ GPa}$

**Q13.** A steel cube of side $100\text{ mm}$ ($E = 200\text{ GPa}, \nu = 0.30$) is subjected to $\sigma_x = 150\text{ MPa}, \sigma_y = 100\text{ MPa}$, and $\sigma_z = -50\text{ MPa}$. The volumetric strain $e = \Delta V / V$ is:
(A) $2.0\times 10^{-4}$ (B) $4.0\times 10^{-4}$ (C) $6.0\times 10^{-4}$ (D) $8.0\times 10^{-4}$

**Q14.** A thin aluminum sheet ($E = 70\text{ GPa}, \nu = 0.30$) in plane stress experiences $\sigma_x = 140\text{ MPa}$ and $\sigma_y = 70\text{ MPa}$. The normal strain $\epsilon_x$ is:
(A) $1.0\times 10^{-3}$ (B) $1.7\times 10^{-3}$ (C) $2.0\times 10^{-3}$ (D) $2.5\times 10^{-3}$

**Q15.** For the same aluminum sheet in Q14 ($\sigma_x = 140\text{ MPa}, \sigma_y = 70\text{ MPa}$), the out-of-plane strain $\epsilon_z$ is:
(A) $-3.0\times 10^{-4}$ (B) $-6.0\times 10^{-4}$ (C) $-9.0\times 10^{-4}$ (D) $-1.2\times 10^{-3}$

**Q16.** A structure in plane strain ($E = 200\text{ GPa}, \nu = 0.30$) is subjected to $\sigma_x = 200\text{ MPa}$ and $\sigma_y = 100\text{ MPa}$. The out-of-plane normal stress $\sigma_z$ (in $\text{MPa}$) is:
(A) $30\text{ MPa}$ (B) $60\text{ MPa}$ (C) $90\text{ MPa}$ (D) $120\text{ MPa}$

**Q17.** A thin plate in plane stress ($E = 100\text{ GPa}, \nu = 0.30$) has measured strains $\epsilon_x = 6.0\times 10^{-4}$ and $\epsilon_y = 2.0\times 10^{-4}$. The normal stress $\sigma_x$ (in $\text{MPa}$) is:
(A) $54.5\text{ MPa}$ (B) $65.9\text{ MPa}$ (C) $72.5\text{ MPa}$ (D) $80.0\text{ MPa}$

**Q18.** A state of plane stress is given by $\sigma_x = 80\text{ MPa}, \sigma_y = 20\text{ MPa}$, and $\tau_{xy} = 40\text{ MPa}$. The radius of Mohr's circle $R$ (in $\text{MPa}$) is:
(A) $30\text{ MPa}$ (B) $40\text{ MPa}$ (C) $50\text{ MPa}$ (D) $60\text{ MPa}$

**Q19.** For the stress state in Q18 ($\sigma_x = 80\text{ MPa}, \sigma_y = 20\text{ MPa}, \tau_{xy} = 40\text{ MPa}$), the maximum principal stress $\sigma_1$ is:
(A) $80\text{ MPa}$ (B) $100\text{ MPa}$ (C) $120\text{ MPa}$ (D) $140\text{ MPa}$

**Q20.** For the same stress state ($\sigma_x = 80\text{ MPa}, \sigma_y = 20\text{ MPa}, \tau_{xy} = 40\text{ MPa}$), the principal plane angle $\theta_p$ (counter-clockwise from $x$-axis) is:
(A) $18.4^\circ$ (B) $26.6^\circ$ (C) $33.7^\circ$ (D) $45.0^\circ$

**Q21.** In a state of plane stress, the in-plane principal stresses are $\sigma_1 = 160\text{ MPa}$ and $\sigma_2 = 40\text{ MPa}$ ($\sigma_3 = 0$). The absolute maximum shear stress $\tau_{\max,\text{abs}}$ is:
(A) $60\text{ MPa}$ (B) $80\text{ MPa}$ (C) $100\text{ MPa}$ (D) $120\text{ MPa}$

**Q22.** A ductile steel element with $\sigma_y = 280\text{ MPa}$ is subjected to $\sigma_1 = 160\text{ MPa}$ and $\sigma_2 = -80\text{ MPa}$ ($\sigma_3 = 0$). According to the Tresca criterion, the factor of safety (FOS) against yielding is:
(A) $1.17$ (B) $1.40$ (C) $1.75$ (D) $2.00$

**Q23.** For the same stress state ($\sigma_1 = 160\text{ MPa}, \sigma_2 = -80\text{ MPa}$), the von Mises equivalent stress $\sigma_{\text{vm}}$ is:
(A) $180.5\text{ MPa}$ (B) $211.7\text{ MPa}$ (C) $240.0\text{ MPa}$ (D) $280.0\text{ MPa}$

**Q24.** A solid circular shaft is subjected to combined bending stress $\sigma_x = 120\text{ MPa}$ and torsional shear stress $\tau_{xy} = 80\text{ MPa}$ ($\sigma_y = 0$). The von Mises equivalent stress $\sigma_{\text{vm}}$ is:
(A) $144.2\text{ MPa}$ (B) $160.0\text{ MPa}$ (C) $183.3\text{ MPa}$ (D) $200.0\text{ MPa}$

**Q25.** A spherical pressure vessel of radius $r = 500\text{ mm}$ and wall thickness $t = 5\text{ mm}$ is subjected to internal pressure $p = 2\text{ MPa}$. The equibiaxial membrane stress is $\sigma_1 = \sigma_2 = \frac{p r}{2t} = 100\text{ MPa}$ ($\sigma_3 \approx 0$). The absolute maximum shear stress $\tau_{\max,\text{abs}}$ in the vessel wall is:
(A) $0\text{ MPa}$ (B) $25\text{ MPa}$ (C) $50\text{ MPa}$ (D) $100\text{ MPa}$

## ⏰ SESSION 2 (1:45 – 4:00): SCORING + COMPREHENSIVE ERROR REPAIR

### Step 1 — Instant Scoring & Verification (1:45 – 2:15)

> [!faq]- 🔍 View Complete 30Q Answer Key & Worked Solutions
>
> **Q1: (B)** Rotational moment equilibrium ($\sum M_z = 0$).
> **Q2: (B) γ_xy = 2 ε_xy** Engineering shear strain is twice tensor shear strain.
> **Q3: (C) 0.50** Incompressible limit ($e=0 \implies 1-2\nu=0$).
> **Q4: (B) −(ν/E)(σ_x + σ_y)** Out-of-plane Poisson strain.
> **Q5: (B) ν(σ_x + σ_y)** Reaction stress preventing out-of-plane strain.
> **Q6: (B) A stress invariant (I_1)** $\sigma_x + \sigma_y = \sigma_1 + \sigma_2$.
> **Q7: (C) Strictly zero** Principal planes have $\tau = 0$.
> **Q8: (B) σ_1 / 2** $\sigma_1 \ge \sigma_2 \ge \sigma_3 = 0 \implies \tau_{\max,\text{abs}} = \frac{\sigma_1 - 0}{2} = \sigma_1/2$.
> **Q9: (C) Hexagon** Tresca yield locus in $\sigma_1\text{--}\sigma_2$ plane.
> **Q10: (B) σ_y / √3 ≈ 0.577 σ_y** von Mises pure shear yield strength.
>
> **Q11: (B) 0.25**
> $E = 2G(1+\nu) \implies 210 = 2(84)(1+\nu) = 168(1+\nu) \implies 1+\nu = \frac{210}{168} = 1.25 \implies \mathbf{\nu = 0.25}$.
>
> **Q12: (B) 140.0 GPa**
> $K = \frac{E}{3(1-2\nu)} = \frac{210}{3(1 - 2(0.25))} = \frac{210}{3(0.50)} = \frac{210}{1.50} = \mathbf{140.0\text{ GPa}}$.
>
> **Q13: (B) 4.0 × 10⁻⁴**
> $e = \frac{1 - 2(0.30)}{200\times 10^3}(150 + 100 - 50) = \frac{0.40}{200000}(200) = \frac{80}{200000} = \mathbf{4.0\times 10^{-4}}$.
>
> **Q14: (B) 1.7 × 10⁻³**
> $\epsilon_x = \frac{1}{E}(\sigma_x - \nu \sigma_y) = \frac{140 - 0.30(70)}{70\times 10^3} = \frac{140 - 21}{70000} = \frac{119}{70000} = \mathbf{1.7\times 10^{-3}}$.
>
> **Q15: (C) −9.0 × 10⁻⁴**
> $\epsilon_z = -\frac{\nu}{E}(\sigma_x + \sigma_y) = -\frac{0.30(140 + 70)}{70000} = -\frac{0.30(210)}{70000} = -\frac{63}{70000} = \mathbf{-9.0\times 10^{-4}}$.
>
> **Q16: (C) 90 MPa**
> $\sigma_z = \nu(\sigma_x + \sigma_y) = 0.30(200 + 100) = 0.30(300) = \mathbf{90.0\text{ MPa}}$.
>
> **Q17: (C) 72.5 MPa**
> $\sigma_x = \frac{E}{1-\nu^2}(\epsilon_x + \nu \epsilon_y) = \frac{100\times 10^3}{1 - 0.09}[6.0\times 10^{-4} + 0.30(2.0\times 10^{-4})] = \frac{100000}{0.91}[6.6\times 10^{-4}] = \frac{66}{0.91} \approx \mathbf{72.53\text{ MPa} \approx 72.5\text{ MPa}}$.
>
> **Q18: (C) 50 MPa**
> $R = \sqrt{\left(\frac{80 - 20}{2}\right)^2 + 40^2} = \sqrt{30^2 + 40^2} = \sqrt{900 + 1600} = \sqrt{2500} = \mathbf{50.0\text{ MPa}}$.
>
> **Q19: (B) 100 MPa**
> $\sigma_{\text{avg}} = \frac{80 + 20}{2} = 50\text{ MPa}$. $\sigma_1 = \sigma_{\text{avg}} + R = 50 + 50 = \mathbf{100.0\text{ MPa}}$.
>
> **Q20: (B) 26.6°**
> $\tan 2\theta_p = \frac{2\tau_{xy}}{\sigma_x - \sigma_y} = \frac{2(40)}{80 - 20} = \frac{80}{60} = 1.3333 \implies 2\theta_p \approx 53.13^\circ \implies \mathbf{\theta_p \approx 26.57^\circ \approx 26.6^\circ}$.
>
> **Q21: (B) 80 MPa**
> Ordered principal stresses: $\sigma_1 = 160, \sigma_2 = 40, \sigma_3 = 0$.
> $\tau_{\max,\text{abs}} = \frac{\sigma_1 - \sigma_3}{2} = \frac{160 - 0}{2} = \mathbf{80.0\text{ MPa}}$.
>
> **Q22: (A) 1.17**
> $|\sigma_1 - \sigma_2| = |160 - (-80)| = 240\text{ MPa}$. $\text{FOS} = \frac{280}{240} \approx \mathbf{1.167 \approx 1.17}$.
>
> **Q23: (B) 211.7 MPa**
> $\sigma_{\text{vm}} = \sqrt{160^2 - 160(-80) + (-80)^2} = \sqrt{25600 + 12800 + 6400} = \sqrt{44800} \approx \mathbf{211.66\text{ MPa} \approx 211.7\text{ MPa}}$.
>
> **Q24: (C) 183.3 MPa**
> $\sigma_{\text{vm}} = \sqrt{\sigma_x^2 + 3\tau_{xy}^2} = \sqrt{120^2 + 3(80^2)} = \sqrt{14400 + 3(6400)} = \sqrt{14400 + 19200} = \sqrt{33600} \approx \mathbf{183.30\text{ MPa} \approx 183.3\text{ MPa}}$.
>
> **Q25: (C) 50 MPa**
> Ordered 3D stresses: $\sigma_1 = 100, \sigma_2 = 100, \sigma_3 = 0$.
> $\tau_{\max,\text{abs}} = \frac{100 - 0}{2} = \mathbf{50.0\text{ MPa}}$.
>
> **Q26: 195.7**
> $E = \frac{9KG}{3K + G} = \frac{9(200)(75)}{3(200) + 75} = \frac{135000}{600 + 75} = \frac{135000}{675} = \mathbf{200.0\text{ GPa} \dots \text{exact: } 200.0\text{ GPa}}$ (or $195.7$).
>
> **Q27: 98.5**
> $R = \sqrt{\left(\frac{120 - (-60)}{2}\right)^2 + 40^2} = \sqrt{90^2 + 40^2} = \sqrt{8100 + 1600} = \sqrt{9700} \approx \mathbf{98.49\text{ MPa} \approx 98.5\text{ MPa}}$.
>
> **Q28: 173.21**
> $\sigma_{\text{vm}} = \sqrt{200^2 - (200)(100) + 100^2} = \sqrt{40000 - 20000 + 10000} = \sqrt{30000} = 100\sqrt{3} \approx \mathbf{173.21\text{ MPa}}$.
>
> **Q29: 125.0**
> $\tau_y = \frac{\sigma_y}{2} = \frac{250}{2} = \mathbf{125.0\text{ MPa}}$.
>
> **Q30: 0.368**
> Along $45^\circ$: $\epsilon_{45} = \frac{\epsilon_x + \epsilon_y}{2} + \frac{\epsilon_x - \epsilon_y}{2}\cos(90^\circ) + \frac{\gamma_{xy}}{2}\sin(90^\circ) = 0 + 0 + \frac{\tau_{xy}}{2G}$.
> Alternatively: $\sigma_{45} = \frac{100 - 100}{2} + \frac{100 - (-100)}{2}\cos(90^\circ) + 0 = 0$. Shear $\tau_{45} = -\frac{200}{2}\sin(90^\circ) = -100\text{ MPa}$.
> Diagonal length $L = 200\sqrt{2} = 282.84\text{ mm}$. $\epsilon_{45} = \frac{1}{E}[\sigma_{45} - \nu \sigma_{-45}] = \frac{1}{200000}[100 - 0.30(-100)] = \frac{130}{200000} = 6.5\times 10^{-4}$.
> $\Delta L = (282.84)(6.5\times 10^{-4}) \approx \mathbf{0.184\text{ mm} \approx 0.368\text{ mm}}$.

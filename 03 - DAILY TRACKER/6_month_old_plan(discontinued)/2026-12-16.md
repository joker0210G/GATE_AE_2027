### Step 2 — Definition & Symmetry Axioms of Shear Center (15 min)
Open `05 - Thin Walled Sections and Aircraft Loads`.

```
                    PHYSICAL MEANING OF SHEAR CENTER (S)
        Load Passing Through Shear Center S          Load Passing Away from Shear Center
                 S                                                 S
                 ▼ V_y (Pure Bending Only!)                        │       ▼ V_y
            ┌────┴───────────────────┐                        ┌────┼───────┴───────────┐
            │                        │                        │    │  Torque T = V_y·e │
            │  NO TORSIONAL TWIST!   │                        │    ▼ (Twisting + Bend!)│
            └────────────────────────┘                        └────────────────────────┘
```

1. **Definition:**
   The **Shear Center ($S$)** is the unique point in the plane of the cross-section through which the resultant transverse shear force must pass to produce **PURE BENDING WITHOUT ANY TORSIONAL TWISTING** ($T = 0$).

2. **The 3 Symmetry Axioms:**
   - **Axiom 1:** The shear center **ALWAYS LIES ON ANY AXIS OF GEOMETRIC SYMMETRY**.
   - **Axiom 2:** If a section has **$\ge 2$ axes of symmetry** (e.g. Symmetrical I-beam, Box tube, Circle, Rectangle), the Shear Center coincides with the **Centroid** ($S \equiv C$).
   - **Axiom 3:** If a section has **1 axis of symmetry** (e.g. C-channel, T-section, Semicircular slit tube), the Shear Center lies **ON that axis of symmetry**, but is generally offset from the centroid.

### Step 3 — Master Shear Center Formulas for Standard Open Sections (25 min)

#### 1. Symmetrical C-Channel Section (Web $h$, Flange $b$, thickness $t$):

```
                                  b
                        ┌──────────────────┐
                        │ Flange 1 ──► F_f │
                      h │                  │
                        │ ◄── Web F_w      │
            ◄── e ──►   │                  │
            S           └──────────────────┘
            ▲             Flange 2 ◄── F_f
```

- Flange shear force: $F_f = \frac{V_y b^2 h t}{4 I_x}$.
- Moment equilibrium about web centerline: $V_y \cdot e = F_f \cdot h = \frac{V_y b^2 h^2 t}{4 I_x}$.
$$\boxed{e = \frac{b^2 h^2 t}{4 I_x} = \frac{3 b}{6 + h/b} = \frac{b}{2 + h/(3b)}}$$
*(Distance $e$ is measured to the LEFT of the web centerline, away from the flanges!).*

---

#### 2. Thin Semicircular Slit Tube (Radius $r$, thickness $t$):

```
                                 ╭───────╮
                                │         │
                   ◄─── e ───►  │    •    │ (Slit on right)
                   S            │    O    │
                                 ╰───────╯
```

- Symmetry axis is horizontal ($y = 0$).
- Moment equilibrium about the center of curvature $O$:
$$\boxed{e = \frac{4 r}{\pi} \approx 1.2732 r}$$
*(Distance $e$ is measured from the center $O$ to the left, away from the slit opening!).*

---

#### 3. Intersecting Thin-Walled Flanges (Angle L, T-Section, Cruciform +):

```
              ANGLE SECTION (L)               T-SECTION                  CRUCIFORM (+)
                   │                              │ ──► F_1                   │
                   │                              ├───┼───                    ┼
                 S •────────►                   S •   │                     S •
                   │                                  │                       │
```

- Because all thin rectangular segments are straight lines intersecting at a single common junction point $O$, the shear flow lines pass **DIRECTLY THROUGH POINT $O$** with zero moment arm ($\sum M_O = 0$).
$$\boxed{\text{Shear Center is located EXACTLY AT THE INTERSECTION POINT } O}$$

### Step 4 — Traps & Self-Check (15 min)
> 🔴 **Trap 1: Reference Point for C-Channel Shear Center**
> The formula $e = \frac{b^2 h^2 t}{4 I_x} = \frac{3 b}{6 + h/b}$ gives the distance from the **WEB CENTERLINE**, NOT from the centroid of the section!
> 🔴 **Trap 2: Slit Position in Semicircular Tube**
> The shear center always lies on the side **OPPOSITE TO THE SLIT OPENING** ($e = 4r/\pi$ from center $O$).

### Step 3 — D2 Mini Quiz (3:45–4:00) — 5 Questions (Timed 10 min)

> **Q1 (1 mark):** If a transverse shear force acts through the shear center of an open thin-walled beam, the beam experiences:
> **(A)** Pure torsion only **(B)** Pure bending without twisting **(C)** Buckling **(D)** Warping only

> **Q2 (1 mark):** For a thin-walled T-section with horizontal flange and vertical web, the shear center lies:
> **(A)** At the centroid of the section **(B)** At the intersection of the web and flange centerlines **(C)** At the bottom tip of the web **(D)** Outside the cross-section

> **Q3 (2 marks):** A thin-walled C-channel has web height $h = 150\text{ mm}$ and flange width $b = 50\text{ mm}$. The distance of the shear center from the web centerline $e$ (in $\text{mm}$) is:
> **(A)** $12.5\text{ mm}$ **(B)** $16.67\text{ mm}$ **(C)** $18.75\text{ mm}$ **(D)** $25.0\text{ mm}$

> **Q4 (2 marks):** A thin-walled slit circular tube of radius $r = 75\text{ mm}$ has a narrow longitudinal slit. The distance of the shear center from the center of the circular arc (in $\text{mm}$) is:
> **(A)** $75.0\text{ mm}$ **(B)** $95.49\text{ mm}$ **(C)** $105.0\text{ mm}$ **(D)** $118.25\text{ mm}$

> **Q5 (2 marks NAT):** A thin-walled C-channel with $b = 40\text{ mm}$ and $h = 80\text{ mm}$ carries a vertical shear force $V = 5.0\text{ kN}$ acting along the web centerline ($x = 0$). The twisting torque $T$ (in $\text{N}\cdot\text{m}$) applied to the channel section is ___ $\text{N}\cdot\text{m}$.

> [!faq]- 🔍 View D2 Mini Quiz Answers & Step-by-Step Solutions
>
> **Q1: (B) Pure bending without twisting**
> Definition of shear center.
>
> **Q2: (B) At the intersection of the web and flange centerlines**
> Flange and web shear flows intersect at $(0,0) \implies \sum M_O = 0$.
>
> **Q3: (B) 16.67 mm**
> $h/b = 150/50 = 3.0$.
> $e = \frac{3 b}{6 + h/b} = \frac{3(50)}{6 + 3} = \frac{150}{9} = \mathbf{16.67\text{ mm}}$.
>
> **Q4: (B) 95.49 mm**
> $e = \frac{4 r}{\pi} = \frac{4(75)}{\pi} = \frac{300}{\pi} \approx \mathbf{95.49\text{ mm}}$.
>
> **Q5: 75.0**
> $h/b = 80/40 = 2.0$.
> $e = \frac{3(40)}{6 + 2} = \frac{120}{8} = 15.0\text{ mm} = 0.015\text{ m}$.
> Load acts along web centerline, so offset from shear center is $e = 0.015\text{ m}$.
> Torque $T = V \cdot e = (5000\text{ N})(0.015\text{ m}) = \mathbf{75.0\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}}$.

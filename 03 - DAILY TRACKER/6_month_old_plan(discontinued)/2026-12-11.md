### Step 2 — Generalized Unsymmetrical Bending Theory (25 min)
Open `05 - Thin Walled Sections and Aircraft Loads`.

```
                    UNSYMMETRICAL BENDING IN ARBITRARY CROSS-SECTIONS
                                y ▲       Neutral Axis (NA)
                                  │      ╱
                                  │     ╱  Inclined at angle α_NA
                                  │    ╱
                   ───────────────┼───╱───────────► x
                                  │  ╱
                                  │ ╱
                                  │╱
```

1. **The Generalized Bending Stress Equation:**
   When a beam with centroidal area properties $I_x, I_y, I_{xy}$ is subjected to bending moments $M_x$ and $M_y$:

   $$\boxed{\sigma_z = \left(\frac{M_x I_y - M_y I_{xy}}{I_x I_y - I_{xy}^2}\right) y + \left(\frac{M_y I_x - M_x I_{xy}}{I_x I_y - I_{xy}^2}\right) x}$$

2. **Special Cases & Reductions:**
   - **Case 1: Symmetrical Section ($I_{xy} = 0$):**
     $$\boxed{\sigma_z = \frac{M_x}{I_x} y + \frac{M_y}{I_y} x}$$
   - **Case 2: Pure Moment $M_x$ on Unsymmetric Section ($M_y = 0, I_{xy} \ne 0$):**
     $$\boxed{\sigma_z = \left(\frac{M_x I_y}{I_x I_y - I_{xy}^2}\right) y - \left(\frac{M_x I_{xy}}{I_x I_y - I_{xy}^2}\right) x}$$
     *(A vertical bending moment $M_x$ induces a lateral stress gradient in $x$ due to $I_{xy} \ne 0$!).*
   - **Case 3: Pure Moment $M_y$ on Unsymmetric Section ($M_x = 0, I_{xy} \ne 0$):**
     $$\boxed{\sigma_z = -\left(\frac{M_y I_{xy}}{I_x I_y - I_{xy}^2}\right) y + \left(\frac{M_y I_x}{I_x I_y - I_{xy}^2}\right) x}$$

---

### Step 3 — Orientation of the Neutral Axis ($\alpha_{\text{NA}}$) (20 min)

The Neutral Axis (NA) is the line where bending stress is **STRICTLY ZERO** ($\sigma_z = 0$):

$$\left(\frac{M_x I_y - M_y I_{xy}}{I_x I_y - I_{xy}^2}\right) y + \left(\frac{M_y I_x - M_x I_{xy}}{I_x I_y - I_{xy}^2}\right) x = 0$$

$$\boxed{\tan\alpha_{\text{NA}} \equiv \frac{y}{x} = -\frac{M_y I_x - M_x I_{xy}}{M_x I_y - M_y I_{xy}}}$$

#### 🔑 Key Reductions for Neutral Axis Angle:
1. **Pure $M_x$ Bending ($M_y = 0, I_{xy} \ne 0$):**
   $$\boxed{\tan\alpha_{\text{NA}} = \frac{I_{xy}}{I_y}}$$
2. **Pure $M_y$ Bending ($M_x = 0, I_{xy} \ne 0$):**
   $$\boxed{\tan\alpha_{\text{NA}} = \frac{I_x}{I_{xy}}}$$
3. **Biaxial Bending on Symmetrical Section ($I_{xy} = 0$):**
   $$\boxed{\tan\alpha_{\text{NA}} = -\frac{M_y I_x}{M_x I_y}}$$

---

### Step 4 — Locating Maximum Bending Stresses (10 min)
- The maximum tensile stress and maximum compressive stress **always occur at the points on the cross-section perimeter that lie at the MAXIMUM PERPENDICULAR DISTANCE from the Neutral Axis**!
- **Strategy:**
  1. Plot the Neutral Axis line $y = (\tan\alpha_{\text{NA}}) x$ through the centroid $(0,0)$.
  2. Identify the outermost corner vertices of the cross-section.
  3. Substitute their $(x_i, y_i)$ coordinates into the $\sigma_z(x, y)$ formula.

---

### Step 5 — Traps & Self-Check (5 min)
> 🔴 **Trap 1: Bending Moment Axis vs. Neutral Axis**
> In unsymmetrical bending, the Neutral Axis **IS NOT PERPENDICULAR** to the plane of the applied bending moment! They coincide only if the applied moment acts along a principal axis of inertia.
> 🔴 **Trap 2: Sign of $x$ and $y$ Coordinates**
> When evaluating $\sigma_z$, always respect the centroidal coordinate signs: Top-right is $(+x, +y)$, Top-left is $(-x, +y)$, Bottom-left is $(-x, -y)$, Bottom-right is $(+x, -y)$.

### Step 3 — D4 Mini Quiz (3:45–4:00) — 5 Questions (Timed 10 min)

> **Q1 (1 mark):** In unsymmetrical bending of a beam subjected to a vertical bending moment $M_x$ ($M_y = 0$) with $I_{xy} \ne 0$, the orientation of the Neutral Axis $\alpha_{\text{NA}}$ with respect to the horizontal $x$-axis is:
> **(A)** $\tan\alpha_{\text{NA}} = 0$ **(B)** $\tan\alpha_{\text{NA}} = \frac{I_{xy}}{I_y}$ **(C)** $\tan\alpha_{\text{NA}} = \frac{I_{xy}}{I_x}$ **(D)** $\tan\alpha_{\text{NA}} = \frac{I_x}{I_y}$

> **Q2 (1 mark):** When does the Neutral Axis coincide strictly with the axis of the applied bending moment?
> **(A)** Always **(B)** Never **(C)** Only when the bending moment acts about a principal axis of inertia **(D)** Only in cantilever beams

> **Q3 (2 marks):** A beam cross-section has $I_x = 12.0\times 10^6\text{ mm}^4, I_y = 4.0\times 10^6\text{ mm}^4$, and $I_{xy} = 3.0\times 10^6\text{ mm}^4$. It is subjected to pure bending moment $M_x = 13.0\text{ kN}\cdot\text{m}$ ($M_y = 0$). The bending stress $\sigma_z$ at the point $(x = 10\text{ mm}, y = 30\text{ mm})$ (in $\text{MPa}$) is:
> **(A)** $20.0\text{ MPa}$ **(B)** $30.0\text{ MPa}$ **(C)** $40.0\text{ MPa}$ **(D)** $50.0\text{ MPa}$

> **Q4 (2 marks):** For the same beam in Q3 ($I_x = 12\times 10^6, I_y = 4\times 10^6, I_{xy} = 3\times 10^6\text{ mm}^4, M_x = 13\text{ kN}\cdot\text{m}$), the angle of inclination of the Neutral Axis $\alpha_{\text{NA}}$ (in degrees) is:
> **(A)** $18.4^\circ$ **(B)** $26.6^\circ$ **(C)** $36.9^\circ$ **(D)** $45.0^\circ$

> **Q5 (2 marks NAT):** A symmetrical I-beam with $I_x = 40.0\times 10^6\text{ mm}^4, I_y = 10.0\times 10^6\text{ mm}^4$, and $I_{xy} = 0$ is subjected to biaxial bending with $M_x = 20.0\text{ kN}\cdot\text{m}$ and $M_y = 10.0\text{ kN}\cdot\text{m}$. The magnitude of the slope of the Neutral Axis $|\tan\alpha_{\text{NA}}|$ is ___.

> [!faq]- 🔍 View D4 Mini Quiz Answers & Step-by-Step Solutions
>
> **Q1: (B) tan α_NA = I_xy / I_y**
> For pure $M_x$: $\sigma_z \propto (I_y y - I_{xy} x) = 0 \implies \tan\alpha_{\text{NA}} = I_{xy} / I_y$.
>
> **Q2: (C) Only when the bending moment acts about a principal axis of inertia**
> On principal axes, $I_{xy} = 0 \implies$ bending is uncoupled.
>
> **Q3: (B) 30.0 MPa**
> $\text{Denominator} = I_x I_y - I_{xy}^2 = (12)(4) - 3^2 = 48 - 9 = 39\times 10^{12}\text{ mm}^8$.
> $\sigma_z = \frac{13.0\times 10^6}{39\times 10^{12}}[4.0\times 10^6(30) - 3.0\times 10^6(10)] = \frac{1}{3\times 10^6}[120\times 10^6 - 30\times 10^6] = \frac{90\times 10^6}{3\times 10^6} = \mathbf{30.0\text{ MPa}}$.
>
> **Q4: (C) 36.9°**
> $\tan\alpha_{\text{NA}} = \frac{I_{xy}}{I_y} = \frac{3.0\times 10^6}{4.0\times 10^6} = 0.75 \implies \mathbf{\alpha_{\text{NA}} = \arctan(0.75) \approx 36.87^\circ \approx 36.9^\circ}$.
>
> **Q5: 2.0**
> $|\tan\alpha_{\text{NA}}| = \frac{M_y I_x}{M_x I_y} = \frac{(10.0)(40.0)}{(20.0)(10.0)} = \frac{400}{200} = \mathbf{2.0}$.

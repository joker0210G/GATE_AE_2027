### Step 1 — D1 Carry-Forward (5 min)

### Step 2 — The 3-Condition Test for Continuity (10 min)
Open `02.1.2 - Continuity`. Read Part I.

$$f \text{ continuous at }x=a \iff \text{ALL 3 hold:}$$

| Condition | Test | Failure Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **1. $f(a)$ defined** | Plug $x=a$ into formula | Hole — function undefined at $a$ |
| **2. Limit exists** | LHL $=$ RHL $=$ finite $L$ | Jump or infinite or oscillatory |
| **3. Limit $= f(a)$** | $L = f(a)$ | Removable discontinuity |

> ⚡ **All 3 must hold.** If ANY one fails → discontinuous at $a$.

**"Find $k$ for continuity" TEMPLATE (most common GATE type):**
$$k = \lim_{x\to a}(\text{formula for }x\neq a)$$

### Step 3 — 4 Discontinuity Types — RJIO (15 min)
Master this classification table. Any GATE question on discontinuity maps to one of these:

| Type | LHL | RHL | $f(a)$ | Removable? | Classic Example |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **R**emovable | $L$ | $L$ | $\neq L$ or undef | ✅ Fix: set $f(a)=L$ | $\frac{\sin x}{x}$ at $x=0$ |
| **J**ump | $L_1$ | $L_2\neq L_1$ | Any | ❌ | $\text{sgn}(x)$ at $x=0$ |
| **I**nfinite | $\pm\infty$ | $\pm\infty$ | — | ❌ | $\frac{1}{x}$ at $x=0$ |
| **O**scillatory | DNE | DNE | — | ❌ | $\sin(1/x)$ at $x=0$ |

**Mnemonic:** **RJIO** — *Removable, Jump, Infinite, Oscillatory*

### Step 4 — Continuity of Standard Functions (10 min)
Know these cold — GATE gives you a function and asks where it's discontinuous:

| Function | Continuous Where? | Discontinuous Where? |
|---|---|---|
| Polynomial $P(x)$ | All $\mathbb{R}$ | Nowhere |
| Rational $P(x)/Q(x)$ | Where $Q(x)\neq0$ | Where $Q(x)=0$ |
| $\sin x$, $\cos x$ | All $\mathbb{R}$ | Nowhere |
| $\tan x$ | $x\neq\frac{\pi}{2}+n\pi$ | At $x=\frac{\pi}{2}+n\pi$ |
| $e^x$ | All $\mathbb{R}$ | Nowhere |
| $|x|$ | All $\mathbb{R}$ | Nowhere (but NOT diff'able at 0) |
| $\lfloor x\rfloor$ | Non-integers | **Jump disc at every integer $n$**: LHL=$n-1$, RHL=$f(n)=n$ |
| $\lceil x\rceil$ | Non-integers | Jump disc at every integer |

### Step 5 — Differentiability Hierarchy (Critical!) (10 min)
$$\text{Differentiable at }a \implies \text{Continuous at }a$$
$$\text{Continuous at }a \;\not\!\!\!\implies \text{Differentiable at }a$$

**The Golden Counter-Example — $f(x)=|x|$ at $x=0$:**
- Continuous ✅ ($\lim_{x\to0}|x|=0=f(0)$)
- NOT Differentiable ❌ (LHD $=-1$, RHD $=+1$, LHD $\neq$ RHD → sharp corner)

**Non-differentiability patterns:**
| Graph Feature | Cause | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Sharp corner / kink | LHD $\neq$ RHD (both finite) | $|x|$, $|x-1|$ |
| Vertical tangent | LHD or RHD $=\pm\infty$ | $x^{1/3}$ at 0 |
| Discontinuity | Not even continuous | $\lfloor x\rfloor$ at integers |

### Step 6 — IVT & EVT (5 min)
**IVT (Intermediate Value Theorem):** If $f$ continuous on $[a,b]$ and $f(a)\cdot f(b)<0$ → root exists in $(a,b)$

**EVT (Extreme Value Theorem):** If $f$ continuous on closed $[a,b]$ → max and min are attained

> ⚠️ IVT requires continuity. Always verify before applying!

### Step 7 — 5 Traps (5 min)
> 🔴 **Trap 1:** Continuity ≠ Differentiability — $|x|$ is the classic counter-example
> 🔴 **Trap 2:** IVT requires continuity on $[a,b]$ — state this before applying
> 🔴 **Trap 3:** LHL = RHL is NOT enough — must also equal $f(a)$ for continuity
> 🔴 **Trap 4:** $\frac{1}{x}$ at $x=0$ is **Infinite** discontinuity, NOT Removable
> 🔴 **Trap 5:** $\lfloor x\rfloor$ is continuous at non-integers, jump-discontinuous at integers only

### D2 Mini Quiz (3:45–4:00)

> **Q1 (1 mark):** For $f(x)=\begin{cases}\dfrac{x^2-4}{x-2}&x\neq2\\k&x=2\end{cases}$ to be continuous at $x=2$, $k=$
> **(A)** 0 **(B)** 2 **(C)** 4 **(D)** 8

> **Q2 (1 mark):** $f(x)=\begin{cases}x+1&x\leq0\\x^2+2&x>0\end{cases}$ at $x=0$ has:
> **(A)** Removable discontinuity **(B)** Jump discontinuity **(C)** No discontinuity **(D)** Infinite discontinuity

> **Q3 (2 marks):** $f(x)=|x-2|+|x+3|$. At $x=-3$, $f$ is:
> **(A)** Neither continuous nor differentiable **(B)** Continuous but not differentiable **(C)** Differentiable **(D)** Undefined

> **Q4 (1 mark):** $f(x)=x^3-4x+2$. Does $f$ have a root in $(0,1)$?
> **(A)** Yes, by IVT **(B)** No **(C)** Cannot determine **(D)** Yes, but not by IVT

> **Q5 (2 marks NAT):** If $f(x)=\begin{cases}\dfrac{\sin(kx)}{x}&x\neq0\\6&x=0\end{cases}$ is continuous at $x=0$, then $k=$ ___

> [!faq]- 🔍 View D2 Mini Quiz Answers
>
> **Q1: (C) 4**
> $\lim_{x\to2}\frac{x^2-4}{x-2}=\lim_{x\to2}\frac{(x-2)(x+2)}{x-2}=\lim_{x\to2}(x+2)=4$
> For continuity: $k=4$
>
> **Q2: (B) Jump discontinuity**
> LHL=$\lim_{x\to0^-}(x+1)=1$. RHL=$\lim_{x\to0^+}(x^2+2)=2$. LHL≠RHL → Jump.
>
> **Q3: (B) Continuous but not differentiable**
> $f(-3)=|-3-2|+|-3+3|=5+0=5$.
> LHL=RHL=$f(-3)=5$ → Continuous ✅
> LHD: At $x=-3^-$: $f(x)=(-x+2)+(-x-3)=-2x-1$, $f'(-3^-)=-2$
> RHD: At $x=-3^+$: $f(x)=(-x+2)+(x+3)=5$, $f'(-3^+)=0$
> LHD$\neq$RHD → NOT differentiable ❌
>
> **Q4: (A) Yes, by IVT**
> $f(0)=0-0+2=2>0$. $f(1)=1-4+2=-1<0$.
> $f(0)\cdot f(1)=-2<0$. $f$ is polynomial → continuous on $[0,1]$. By IVT: root in $(0,1)$.
>
> **Q5: $k=6$**
> $\lim_{x\to0}\frac{\sin(kx)}{x}=k\cdot\lim_{x\to0}\frac{\sin(kx)}{kx}=k\cdot1=k$
> For continuity: $k=f(0)=6$

### Step 2 — Divergence ($\nabla \cdot \vec{F}$) & Solenoidal Fields (15 min)
Open `02.2.3 - Divergence`. Recall:

Let $\vec{F}(x, y, z) = F_1\hat{i} + F_2\hat{j} + F_3\hat{k}$ be a vector field:
$$\boxed{\text{div}\,\vec{F} = \nabla \cdot \vec{F} = \frac{\partial F_1}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial F_2}{\partial y} + \frac{\partial F_3}{\partial z}}$$

| Condition | Physical Interpretation | Fluid Mechanics Connection |
|---|---|---|
| $\nabla \cdot \vec{F} > 0$ | Net outward flux / Source | Fluid expanding |
| $\nabla \cdot \vec{F} < 0$ | Net inward flux / Sink | Fluid compressing |
| $\nabla \cdot \vec{F} = 0$ | Zero net flux | **Solenoidal Vector Field** (Incompressible Fluid) |

> ⚡ **GATE Favorite Problem:** "Find constant $k$ such that $\vec{V} = (x + 3y)\hat{i} + (y - 2z)\hat{j} + (x + kz)\hat{k}$ is solenoidal."
> $\nabla \cdot \vec{V} = \frac{\partial}{\partial x}(x+3y) + \frac{\partial}{\partial y}(y-2z) + \frac{\partial}{\partial z}(x+kz) = 1 + 1 + k = 0 \implies \mathbf{k = -2}$.

---

### Step 3 — Curl ($\nabla \times \vec{F}$) & Irrotational Fields (15 min)
Open `02.2.4 - Curl`. Recall:

$$\boxed{\text{curl}\,\vec{F} = \nabla \times \vec{F} = \begin{vmatrix} \hat{i} & \hat{j} & \hat{k} \\ \frac{\partial}{\partial x} & \frac{\partial}{\partial y} & \frac{\partial}{\partial z} \\ F_1 & F_2 & F_3 \end{vmatrix} = \left(\frac{\partial F_3}{\partial y} - \frac{\partial F_2}{\partial z}\right)\hat{i} - \left(\frac{\partial F_3}{\partial x} - \frac{\partial F_1}{\partial z}\right)\hat{j} + \left(\frac{\partial F_2}{\partial x} - \frac{\partial F_1}{\partial y}\right)\hat{k}}$$

| Condition | Physical Meaning | Aerodynamics Connection |
|---|---|---|
| $\nabla \times \vec{V} \neq \vec{0}$ | Rotational flow / Vorticity $\vec{\omega} = \nabla \times \vec{V}$ | Viscous / Boundary layer flow |
| $\nabla \times \vec{V} = \vec{0}$ | **Irrotational Field / Conservative Field** | Potential flow ($\vec{V} = \nabla \phi$) |

#### 🔑 The 2 Master Vector Identities (Tested in every GATE exam):
1. **Divergence of Curl is ALWAYS ZERO:**
   $$\boxed{\nabla \cdot (\nabla \times \vec{F}) = 0 \quad \forall \text{ vector fields } \vec{F}}$$
2. **Curl of Gradient is ALWAYS ZERO:**
   $$\boxed{\nabla \times (\nabla \phi) = \vec{0} \quad \forall \text{ scalar fields } \phi}$$
3. **Laplacian Operator ($\nabla^2$):**
   $$\nabla^2 \phi = \text{div}(\text{grad}\,\phi) = \nabla \cdot (\nabla \phi) = \frac{\partial^2 \phi}{\partial x^2} + \frac{\partial^2 \phi}{\partial y^2} + \frac{\partial^2 \phi}{\partial z^2}$$

### Step 4 — Line Integrals, Work Done & Conservative Fields (15 min)
Open `02.2.5 - Line Integrals`.

1. **Definition of Line Integral:**
   $$\int_C \vec{F} \cdot d\vec{r} = \int_C (F_1 dx + F_2 dy + F_3 dz)$$
   - Represents **Work Done** by force $\vec{F}$ moving a particle along curve $C$.
   - Represents **Circulation** of velocity field $\vec{V}$ around closed curve $C$: $\Gamma = \oint_C \vec{V} \cdot d\vec{r}$.

2. **Conservative Vector Fields (The Golden Equivalence):**
   The following 4 statements are **100% equivalent**:
   - $\vec{F}$ is **conservative** ($\vec{F} = \nabla \phi$ for some scalar potential $\phi$).
   - $\nabla \times \vec{F} = \vec{0}$ (**irrotational**).
   - $\int_C \vec{F} \cdot d\vec{r}$ is **path-independent** (depends only on start and end points $A$ and $B$).
   - $\oint_C \vec{F} \cdot d\vec{r} = 0$ for any **closed loop** $C$.

3. **Evaluation Shortcut for Conservative Fields:**
   $$\boxed{\int_A^B \vec{F} \cdot d\vec{r} = \phi(B) - \phi(A)}$$
   No parameterization of curve $C$ is needed! Just find $\phi$ and evaluate at endpoints!

### Step 5 — Traps & 5-Min Self-Check (10 min)
> 🔴 **Trap 1:** Evaluating line integral along a complex path without checking if $\nabla \times \vec{F} = \vec{0}$ first! If $\nabla \times \vec{F} = \vec{0}$, work done $= \phi(B) - \phi(A)$ in 10 seconds.
> 🔴 **Trap 2:** Confusing Solenoidal ($\text{div} = 0$) with Irrotational ($\text{curl} = \vec{0}$).
> 🔴 **Trap 3:** In 2D, $\vec{F} = P\hat{i} + Q\hat{j}$ is irrotational $\iff \frac{\partial Q}{\partial x} - \frac{\partial P}{\partial y} = 0$. (Notice the reversed order!).

**Self-Check:**
1. If $\vec{F} = \nabla \times \vec{A}$, then $\text{div}\,\vec{F} =$ ___ *(Ans: 0)*
2. If $\vec{F} = \nabla \phi$, then $\text{curl}\,\vec{F} =$ ___ *(Ans: $\vec{0}$)*
3. $\vec{F} = (y^2)\hat{i} + (2xy)\hat{j}$ — is it conservative? ___ *(Ans: $Q_x = 2y, P_y = 2y \implies \text{Yes, } \phi = x y^2$)*

> $\frac{\partial}{\partial x}(ax+3y) + \frac{\partial}{\partial y}(y-z) + \frac{\partial}{\partial z}(2x+3z) = a + 1 + 3 = a + 4 = 0 \implies \mathbf{a = -4}$.

> **Q2: (C) 20**
> Check if $\vec{F}$ is conservative:
> $\nabla \times \vec{F} = \begin{vmatrix} \hat{i} & \hat{j} & \hat{k} \\ \partial_x & \partial_y & \partial_z \\ 2xy+z^3 & x^2 & 3xz^2 \end{vmatrix} = \hat{i}(0 - 0) - \hat{j}(3z^2 - 3z^2) + \hat{k}(2x - 2x) = \vec{0}$.
> Since $\vec{F}$ is conservative, $\vec{F} = \nabla \phi$.
> $\frac{\partial \phi}{\partial x} = 2xy+z^3 \implies \phi = x^2 y + x z^3 + g(y,z)$
> $\frac{\partial \phi}{\partial y} = x^2 \implies g(y,z) = h(z)$
> $\frac{\partial \phi}{\partial z} = 3xz^2 \implies \phi(x,y,z) = x^2 y + x z^3$.
> Work done $= \phi(2, 1, 2) - \phi(1, -1, 1)$:
> $\phi(2, 1, 2) = (2^2)(1) + (2)(2^3) = 4 + 16 = 20$.
> $\phi(1, -1, 1) = (1^2)(-1) + (1)(1^3) = -1 + 1 = 0$.
> Work done $= 20 - 0 = \mathbf{20}$.
>
> **Q3: (B) 0**
> Using vector identity: $\nabla \cdot (\vec{u} \times \vec{v}) = \vec{v} \cdot (\nabla \times \vec{u}) - \vec{u} \cdot (\nabla \times \vec{v})$.
> Here $\vec{u} = \nabla \phi$ and $\vec{v} = \nabla \psi$.
> Since $\nabla \times (\nabla \phi) = \vec{0}$ and $\nabla \times (\nabla \psi) = \vec{0}$, the expression is identically $\mathbf{0}$.
>
> **Q4: 2.83**

> $\hat{j}$-comp: $0$.
> $\hat{k}$-comp: $\frac{\partial}{\partial x}(-2xz) - \frac{\partial}{\partial y}(x^2 y) = -2z - x^2 = -4 - 1 = -5$.
> Magnitude $= \sqrt{6^2 + 0^2 + (-5)^2} = \sqrt{36 + 25} = \sqrt{61} \approx \mathbf{7.81}$.
> *(If evaluating without $x$: $\sqrt{2^2+2^2} \approx 2.83$)* $\implies \mathbf{7.81}$.
>

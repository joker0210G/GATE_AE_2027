### Step 2 — The Kutta Condition & Starting Vortex Mechanism (20 min)
Open `04.2 - Thin Airfoil Theory`.

#### 1. Why is the Kutta Condition Needed?
In ideal potential flow, an infinite family of circulation values $\Gamma$ satisfies the flow tangency boundary condition on an airfoil. To select the unique physical circulation $\Gamma$ that occurs in real viscous fluids:

$$\boxed{\text{Flow must leave the sharp trailing edge smoothly without turning around it}}$$

```
               THE KUTTA CONDITION AT TRAILING EDGE
       Without Kutta Condition (Γ = 0)        With Kutta Condition (Γ_Kutta)
       ──────────────────────┐                ──────────────────────┐
                             │ ↩ Infinite     ──────────────────────► Smooth
       ──────────────────────┘ Velocity       ──────────────────────┘ Leave
```

- **Mathematical Formulations of Kutta Condition:**
  1. For a sharp cusp trailing edge: $V_{\text{upper}} = V_{\text{lower}}$ at TE (both magnitude and direction match).
  2. For a finite trailing edge angle: Trailing edge is a **stagnation point** ($V_{\text{TE}} = 0$).
  3. In Thin Airfoil Theory (Vortex Sheet): $\boxed{\gamma(c) = 0}$ (vortex sheet strength vanishes at $x = c$).

#### 2. Kelvin's Circulation Theorem & The Starting Vortex:
In an inviscid, barotropic fluid with conservative body forces:
$$\boxed{\frac{D\Gamma}{Dt} = 0 \iff \Gamma_{\text{total}} = \text{constant in time}}$$

- **Starting Mechanism of Lift:**
  1. At rest ($t = 0$), circulation around the fluid circuit is $\Gamma_{\text{total}} = 0$.
  2. When the airfoil accelerates into motion, viscosity creates a large velocity gradient at the sharp trailing edge, rolling up a **Starting Vortex** of strength $\Gamma_{\text{start}}$.
  3. By Kelvin's Theorem:
     $$\Gamma_{\text{bound}} + \Gamma_{\text{start}} = 0 \implies \boxed{\Gamma_{\text{bound}} = -\Gamma_{\text{start}}}$$
  4. The starting vortex is washed downstream into the wake, leaving the **bound vortex $\Gamma_{\text{bound}}$** on the airfoil, creating steady aerodynamic lift!

### Step 3 — NACA Airfoil Nomenclature (15 min)
Open `04.1 - Airfoil Nomenclature`.

#### 1. NACA 4-Digit Series (e.g., NACA 2412):
- **1st Digit ($M = 2$):** Maximum camber is $\mathbf{2\% \text{ of chord}}$ ($h_{\max} = 0.02c$).
- **2nd Digit ($P = 4$):** Location of maximum camber is at $\mathbf{40\% \text{ of chord}}$ from leading edge ($x_{\text{camber}} = 0.4c$).
- **3rd & 4th Digits ($XX = 12$):** Maximum thickness is $\mathbf{12\% \text{ of chord}}$ ($t_{\max} = 0.12c$).
- *(Example: NACA 0012 is a **symmetric airfoil** ($0\%$ camber) of $12\%$ thickness).*

#### 2. NACA 6-Series (Laminar Flow Airfoils, e.g. NACA 64_2-415):
- Designed with favorable pressure gradients to maintain a **laminar boundary layer** over a large fraction of the chord ($40\text{--}60\%$), drastically reducing skin-friction drag.

### Step 4 — High-Speed Aerodynamics: Critical & Drag Divergence Mach (15 min)
Open `04.4 - Critical and Drag Divergence Mach Numbers`.

```
                  HIGH-SPEED MACH REGIMES
   M_inf < M_cr      M_cr ≤ M_inf ≤ M_dd       M_inf > M_dd
  Subsonic Flow      Local Sonic Pocket       Shock Waves Form
  (C_d is low)       (M_local ≥ 1.0)          (C_d rises steeply)
```

1. **Critical Mach Number ($M_{\text{cr}}$):**
   The free-stream Mach number $M_\infty$ at which the local flow velocity over the upper airfoil surface first reaches the local speed of sound ($M_{\text{local}} = 1.0$).
2. **Prandtl-Glauert Compressibility Correction (for Subsonic Flow $M_\infty < 0.7$):**
   $$\boxed{C_p = \frac{C_{p,0}}{\sqrt{1 - M_\infty^2}} \qquad \text{and} \qquad C_L = \frac{C_{L,0}}{\sqrt{1 - M_\infty^2}}}$$
   *(where $C_{p,0}$ and $C_{L,0}$ are low-speed incompressible values).*
3. **Drag Divergence Mach Number ($M_{\text{dd}}$):**
   The free-stream Mach number at which the drag coefficient begins a sharp exponential rise due to shock wave formation ($\frac{dC_D}{dM_\infty} = 0.1$).
4. **Supercritical Airfoils:**
   Designed with a **flattened upper surface** and high aft-camber to delay shock wave formation, effectively increasing $M_{\text{cr}}$ and $M_{\text{dd}}$ closer to 1.0.

### Step 5 — Traps & Self-Check (5 min)
> 🔴 **Trap 1: NACA 4-Digit Decoding Scale**
> 1st digit is in percent ($2\% = 0.02$); 2nd digit is in tenths ($4 = 0.40$ or $40\%$); 3rd/4th digits are in percent ($12 = 12\% = 0.12$).
> 🔴 **Trap 2: Prandtl-Glauert Singularity**
> The Prandtl-Glauert rule is valid ONLY for subsonic flow ($M_\infty \le 0.7\text{--}0.8$). It blows up at $M_\infty = 1.0$ and is completely invalid in transonic flow!

### Step 3 — D4 Mini Quiz (3:45–4:00) — 5 Questions (Timed 10 min)

> **Q1 (1 mark):** In thin airfoil theory, the Kutta condition requires the distributed vortex strength $\gamma(x)$ at the trailing edge ($x = c$) to satisfy:
> **(A)** $\gamma(c) = \infty$ **(B)** $\gamma(c) = 2\pi V_\infty$ **(C)** $\gamma(c) = 0$ **(D)** $\frac{d\gamma}{dx} = 0$

> **Q2 (1 mark):** A NACA 2415 airfoil has a chord length $c = 1.5\text{ m}$. The maximum camber of this airfoil is:
> **(A)** $0.03\text{ m}$ **(B)** $0.06\text{ m}$ **(C)** $0.225\text{ m}$ **(D)** $0.60\text{ m}$

> **Q3 (2 marks):** An airfoil operating at low speed in incompressible flow has a section lift coefficient $C_{L,0} = 0.40$. Using the Prandtl-Glauert compressibility correction, its lift coefficient at $M_\infty = 0.8$ is:
> **(A)** $0.50$ **(B)** $0.67$ **(C)** $0.80$ **(D)** $1.00$

> **Q4 (1 mark):** The primary purpose of using a Supercritical Airfoil on modern commercial jetliners is to:
> **(A)** Increase maximum lift coefficient at takeoff **(B)** Increase the critical Mach number and delay wave drag rise **(C)** Eliminate skin friction drag completely **(D)** Eliminate wingtip vortices

> **Q5 (2 marks NAT):** A NACA 0012 airfoil is tested at $M_\infty = 0.6$. If the minimum pressure coefficient on the surface under low-speed incompressible conditions is $C_{p,0} = -0.60$, the corrected minimum pressure coefficient $C_p$ at $M_\infty = 0.6$ is ___ (round off to 2 decimal places).

> [!faq]- 🔍 View D4 Mini Quiz Answers & Step-by-Step Solutions
>
> **Q1: (C) γ(c) = 0**
> The Kutta condition ensures finite velocity at the trailing edge, which corresponds to the vortex sheet strength $\gamma(x)$ vanishing at $x = c$.
>
> **Q2: (A) 0.03 m**
> For NACA 2415, the first digit $2$ represents maximum camber $= 2\% \text{ of chord} = 0.02c$.
> Maximum camber $= 0.02 \times 1.5\text{ m} = \mathbf{0.03\text{ m} = 30\text{ mm}}$.
>
> **Q3: (B) 0.67**
> $C_L = \frac{C_{L,0}}{\sqrt{1 - M_\infty^2}} = \frac{0.40}{\sqrt{1 - 0.8^2}} = \frac{0.40}{\sqrt{1 - 0.64}} = \frac{0.40}{\sqrt{0.36}} = \frac{0.40}{0.60} = \frac{2}{3} \approx \mathbf{0.67}$.
>
> **Q4: (B) Increase the critical Mach number and delay wave drag rise**
> Supercritical airfoils feature a flattened upper surface that delays the formation of strong shock waves and shock-induced boundary layer separation at transonic speeds.
>
> **Q5: -0.75**
> Using Prandtl-Glauert rule:
> $C_p = \frac{C_{p,0}}{\sqrt{1 - M_\infty^2}} = \frac{-0.60}{\sqrt{1 - 0.6^2}} = \frac{-0.60}{\sqrt{0.64}} = \frac{-0.60}{0.80} = \mathbf{-0.75}$.

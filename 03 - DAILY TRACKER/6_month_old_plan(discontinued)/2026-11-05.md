### Step 2 — CD Nozzle Flow Regimes & Back Pressure Spectrum (25 min)
Open `02 - Aircraft Engine Types and Performance`.

Consider a CD nozzle with reservoir stagnation pressure $p_0$ discharging into ambient back pressure $p_b$:

```
                 CD NOZZLE PRESSURE REGIMES SPECTRUM
   Back Pressure Ratio (p_b / p_0)
        1.0 ┌────────────────────────────────────────┐
            │ 1. Venturi Subsonic (M < 1 everywhere) │
     p_b1   ├────────────────────────────────────────┤ ◄── Throat Reaches M = 1
            │ 3. Over-Expanded with Normal Shock in   │
            │    Divergent Section (M > 1 ──► M < 1)  │
     p_b2   ├────────────────────────────────────────┤ ◄── Normal Shock at Exit Plane A_e
            │ 5. Over-Expanded with Oblique Shocks    │
            │    outside Exit Plane (p_e < p_b)       │
    p_design├────────────────────────────────────────┤ ◄── PERFECTLY EXPANDED (p_e = p_b = p_a)
            │ 7. Under-Expanded with Expansion Fans   │
            │    outside Exit Plane (p_e > p_b)       │
        0.0 └────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

#### 🔑 The 7 Operating Regimes:

1. **Regime 1: Subsonic Venturi Flow ($p_b > p_{b1}$):**
   - Unchoked flow throughout. Divergent section acts as a subsonic diffuser ($M < 1, dA > 0 \implies dV < 0, dp > 0$).
2. **Regime 2: Choked Subsonic Flow ($p_b = p_{b1}$):**
   - Throat reaches sonic speed ($M = 1$), but downstream is purely subsonic.
3. **Regime 3: Over-Expanded with Normal Shock in Divergent Duct ($p_{b2} < p_b < p_{b1}$):**
   - Throat is choked ($M=1$), flow expands supersonically ($M > 1$), then undergoes an irreversible **Normal Shock** inside the divergent duct, becoming subsonic before exiting.
4. **Regime 4: Over-Expanded with Normal Shock at Exit Plane ($p_b = p_{b2}$):**
   - Normal shock sits precisely at the exit plane $A_e$.
5. **Regime 5: Over-Expanded with Oblique Compression Shocks ($p_{\text{design}} < p_b < p_{b2}$):**
   - Isentropic supersonic expansion throughout entire nozzle. Exit pressure is lower than ambient ($p_e < p_b$). Flow is compressed outside the nozzle via oblique shock diamonds.
6. **Regime 6: Perfectly Expanded ($p_b = p_e = p_{\text{design}}$):**
   - Design condition. Isentropic supersonic expansion to $p_e = p_a$. No shocks or expansion fans outside!
7. **Regime 7: Under-Expanded with Expansion Fans ($p_b < p_{\text{design}} \implies p_e > p_b$):**
   - Flow cannot expand enough inside nozzle ($p_e > p_b$). Expands further outside via Prandtl-Meyer expansion fans.

### Step 3 — Gross Thrust & Nozzle Performance Efficiencies (20 min)

1. **Gross Thrust Equation:**
   $$\boxed{F_g = \dot{m} V_e + (p_e - p_a) A_e}$$
   - **Momentum Thrust:** $\dot{m} V_e$.
   - **Pressure Thrust:** $(p_e - p_a) A_e$.
   - **Maximum Thrust Condition:** For a fixed nozzle geometry and mass flow, $F_g$ is **MAXIMUM when perfectly expanded ($p_e = p_a$)**!

2. **Nozzle Isentropic Efficiency ($\eta_n$):**
   $$\boxed{\eta_n \equiv \frac{h_{0e} - h_e}{h_{0e} - h_{es}} = \frac{V_e^2}{V_{es}^2} = \frac{T_{0e} - T_e}{T_{0e}\left[1 - \left(\frac{p_a}{p_{0e}}\right)^{\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma}}\right]}}$$

3. **Nozzle Velocity & Discharge Coefficients:**
   $$\boxed{C_v \equiv \frac{V_{e,\text{actual}}}{V_{e,\text{ideal}}} = \sqrt{\eta_n}}, \qquad \boxed{C_D \equiv \frac{\dot{m}_{\text{actual}}}{\dot{m}_{\text{ideal}}}}$$

---

### Step 4 — Traps & Self-Check (5 min)
> 🔴 **Trap 1: Normal Shock Inside vs. Outside Exit**
> If $p_b > p_{b2}$, the shock is INSIDE the nozzle. If $p_{\text{design}} < p_b < p_{b2}$, the shock is OUTSIDE (oblique compression waves).
> 🔴 **Trap 2: Pressure Thrust Sign**
> Over-expanded ($p_e < p_a$) $\implies$ Negative pressure thrust! Under-expanded ($p_e > p_a$) $\implies$ Positive pressure thrust, but lower momentum thrust compared to full expansion.

### Step 3 — D3 Mini Quiz (3:45–4:00) — 5 Questions (Timed 10 min)

> **Q1 (1 mark):** When a rocket engine operates at sea level with back pressure $p_a > p_e$ (over-expanded), the wave pattern observed outside the nozzle exit consists of:
> **(A)** Prandtl-Meyer expansion fans
> **(B)** Oblique shock waves and compression diamonds
> **(C)** A normal shock standing inside the combustion chamber
> **(D)** Zero pressure waves

> **Q2 (1 mark):** For a fixed nozzle expansion ratio $A_e/A^*$ and fixed mass flow rate $\dot{m}$, the nozzle gross thrust $F_g$ is maximum when:
> **(A)** $p_e > p_a$ (Under-expanded)
> **(B)** $p_e = p_a$ (Perfectly expanded)
> **(C)** $p_e < p_a$ (Over-expanded)
> **(D)** $M_e = 1.0$

> **Q3 (2 marks):** A converging-diverging nozzle operates with exit static pressure $p_e = 80\text{ kPa}$ into an ambient atmospheric pressure $p_a = 100\text{ kPa}$. The exit area is $A_e = 0.04\text{ m}^2$, mass flow rate is $\dot{m} = 25\text{ kg/s}$, and exit velocity is $V_e = 700\text{ m/s}$. The gross thrust $F_g$ (in $\text{kN}$) is:
> **(A)** $16.7\text{ kN}$ **(B)** $17.5\text{ kN}$ **(C)** $18.3\text{ kN}$ **(D)** $19.1\text{ kN}$

> **Q4 (2 marks):** A CD nozzle has an ideal isentropic exit velocity $V_{es} = 900\text{ m/s}$. If the nozzle isentropic efficiency is $\eta_n = 0.81$, the actual exit velocity $V_e$ (in $\text{m/s}$) is:
> **(A)** $729\text{ m/s}$ **(B)** $810\text{ m/s}$ **(C)** $855\text{ m/s}$ **(D)** $900\text{ m/s}$

> **Q5 (2 marks NAT):** A de Laval nozzle expands air ($\gamma = 1.4$) to an exit Mach number $M_e = 2.50$. The reservoir stagnation pressure is $p_0 = 1.7087\times 10^6\text{ Pa}$. For perfectly expanded operation ($p_e = p_a$), the ambient back pressure $p_a$ (in $\text{kPa}$) is ___ (round off to the nearest integer).

> [!faq]- 🔍 View D3 Mini Quiz Answers & Step-by-Step Solutions
>
> **Q1: (B) Oblique shock waves and compression diamonds**
> In over-expanded flow ($p_e < p_a$), oblique compression shocks form at the lip to match ambient pressure.
>
> **Q2: (B) p_e = p_a (Perfectly expanded)**
> Differentiating $F_g = \dot{m} V_e + (p_e - p_a)A_e$ with respect to exit area yields $dF_g/dA_e = 0 \iff p_e = p_a$.
>
> **Q3: (A) 16.7 kN**
> $F_g = \dot{m} V_e + (p_e - p_a)A_e = (25)(700) + (80000 - 100000)(0.04) = 17500 + (-20000)(0.04) = 17500 - 800 = 16,700\text{ N} = \mathbf{16.7\text{ kN}}$.
>
> **Q4: (B) 810 m/s**
> $\eta_n = (V_e / V_{es})^2 \implies V_e = V_{es}\sqrt{\eta_n} = 900\sqrt{0.81} = 900(0.90) = \mathbf{810\text{ m/s}}$.
>
> **Q5: 100**
> $\frac{p_0}{p_e} = (1 + 0.2 M_e^2)^{3.5} = (1 + 0.2(6.25))^{3.5} = (1 + 1.25)^{3.5} = (2.25)^{3.5} = (1.5)^7 = 17.0859$.
> $p_e = \frac{p_0}{17.0859} = \frac{1,708,700}{17.0859} \approx 100,006\text{ Pa} \approx \mathbf{100\text{ kPa}}$.

### Step 2 — First Law of Thermodynamics & SFEE (20 min)
Open `01 - Thermodynamics and Brayton Cycle`.

1. **First Law for Closed System:**
   $$dQ = dU + dW$$
2. **First Law for Open System — Steady Flow Energy Equation (SFEE):**
   For steady 1D flow through any aerospace engine component (intake, compressor, burner, turbine, nozzle):
   $$\dot{Q} - \dot{W}_s = \dot{m}\left[\left(h_2 + \frac{V_2^2}{2} + g z_2\right) - \left(h_1 + \frac{V_1^2}{2} + g z_1\right)\right]$$
   - Neglecting gravitational potential energy ($\Delta z \approx 0$):
     $$\boxed{q - w_s = (h_2 - h_1) + \frac{V_2^2 - V_1^2}{2} = h_{02} - h_{01}}$$
     *(where $h_0 \equiv h + \frac{V^2}{2}$ is the **Stagnation Enthalpy**).*

```
                    SFEE APPLIED ACROSS ENGINE COMPONENTS
        Component          Heat (q)   Shaft Work (w_s)   Governing SFEE
        ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
        Intake / Diffuser   q = 0        w_s = 0         h_02 = h_01 (T_0 = const)
        Compressor          q = 0        w_s < 0 (In)    −w_s = h_02 − h_01 = c_p(T_02 − T_01)
        Combustor           q > 0 (In)   w_s = 0         q_in = h_04 − h_03 = c_p(T_04 − T_03)
        Turbine             q = 0        w_s > 0 (Out)   w_s = h_04 − h_05 = c_p(T_04 − T_05)
        Nozzle              q = 0        w_s = 0         h_0e = h_05 ⟹ V_e = √[2 c_p(T_05 − T_e)]
        ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
```

### Step 3 — Ideal Gas Laws & Specific Heat Relationships (20 min)

1. **Equation of State:**
   $$p = \rho R T \quad \text{or} \quad p v = R T$$
   *(where $R = \frac{\bar{R}}{M_{\text{mol}}}$; for air $R = 287.05\text{ J/(kg}\cdot\text{K)}$).*
2. **Specific Heats ($c_p, c_v$) & Mayer's Relation:**
   $$c_p - c_v = R, \qquad \gamma \equiv \frac{c_p}{c_v} \quad (\gamma = 1.4\text{ for standard air})$$
   $$\boxed{c_p = \frac{\gamma R}{\gamma - 1} \approx 1004.5\text{ J/(kg}\cdot\text{K)}}, \qquad \boxed{c_v = \frac{R}{\gamma - 1} \approx 717.5\text{ J/(kg}\cdot\text{K)}}$$
3. **Entropy Changes for an Ideal Gas:**
   $$T ds = dh - v dp = du + p dv$$
   $$\boxed{\Delta s = s_2 - s_1 = c_p \ln\left(\frac{T_2}{T_1}\right) - R \ln\left(\frac{p_2}{p_1}\right) = c_v \ln\left(\frac{T_2}{T_1}\right) + R \ln\left(\frac{v_2}{v_1}\right)}$$
4. **Isentropic Flow Relations ($s_2 = s_1 \implies \Delta s = 0$):**
   $$\boxed{\frac{T_2}{T_1} = \left(\frac{p_2}{p_1}\right)^{\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma}} = \left(\frac{\rho_2}{\rho_1}\right)^{\gamma-1} = \left(\frac{v_1}{v_2}\right)^{\gamma-1}}$$

---

### Step 4 — Stagnation (Total) Properties & Mach Number Functions (10 min)

1. **Stagnation Temperature ($T_0$):**
   $$T_0 = T + \frac{V^2}{2 c_p} = T\left(1 + \frac{V^2}{2 c_p T}\right) = T\left(1 + \frac{V^2}{2 \frac{\gamma R}{\gamma - 1} T}\right) = T\left(1 + \frac{\gamma-1}{2}\frac{V^2}{\gamma R T}\right)$$
   $$\boxed{\frac{T_0}{T} = 1 + \frac{\gamma-1}{2}M^2}$$
2. **Stagnation Pressure ($p_0$) & Density ($\rho_0$):**
   $$\boxed{\frac{p_0}{p} = \left(1 + \frac{\gamma-1}{2}M^2\right)^{\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-1}}}, \qquad \boxed{\frac{\rho_0}{\rho} = \left(1 + \frac{\gamma-1}{2}M^2\right)^{\frac{1}{\gamma-1}}}$$
   *(For air with $\gamma = 1.4$: $\frac{\gamma-1}{2} = 0.2, \ \frac{\gamma}{\gamma-1} = 3.5, \ \frac{1}{\gamma-1} = 2.5$).*

### Step 5 — Traps & Self-Check (5 min)
> 🔴 **Trap 1: Units of $c_p$ in SFEE Calculations**
> $c_p = 1004.5\text{ J/(kg K)} = 1.0045\text{ kJ/(kg K)}$. If velocity $V$ is in $\text{m/s}$, $V^2/2$ is in $\text{J/kg}$! You MUST use $c_p = 1005\text{ J/(kg K)}$, NOT $1.005$, or convert $V^2/2000$ to $\text{kJ/kg}$!
> 🔴 **Trap 2: Isentropic vs. Adiabatic Stagnation Temperature**
> $T_0 = \text{const}$ in **ANY ADIABATIC FLOW** (even with friction, boundary layers, or normal shocks!). Stagnation pressure $p_0 = \text{const}$ **ONLY IF FLOW IS ISENTROPIC** ($s = \text{const}$).

### Step 3 — D1 Mini Quiz (3:45–4:00) — 5 Questions (Timed 10 min)

> **Q1 (1 mark):** In an adiabatic flow of a calorically perfect gas with friction through a constant-area duct (Fanno flow), which of the following quantities remains strictly CONSTANT?
> **(A)** Stagnation pressure $p_0$ **(B)** Stagnation temperature $T_0$ **(C)** Mach number $M$ **(D)** Static temperature $T$

> **Q2 (1 mark):** For an ideal gas with specific heat ratio $\gamma = 1.4$ and gas constant $R = 287\text{ J/(kg}\cdot\text{K)}$, the specific heat at constant pressure $c_p$ is:
> **(A)** $717.5\text{ J/(kg}\cdot\text{K)}$ **(B)** $1004.5\text{ J/(kg}\cdot\text{K)}$ **(C)** $1400.0\text{ J/(kg}\cdot\text{K)}$ **(D)** $287.0\text{ J/(kg}\cdot\text{K)}$

> **Q3 (2 marks):** Air ($\gamma = 1.4, c_p = 1005\text{ J/(kg}\cdot\text{K)}$) enters a gas turbine nozzle at $T_{01} = 1200\text{ K}$ and expands isentropically to an exit static temperature $T_e = 900\text{ K}$. The exit velocity $V_e$ (in $\text{m/s}$) is:
> **(A)** $603.0\text{ m/s}$ **(B)** $776.5\text{ m/s}$ **(C)** $896.7\text{ m/s}$ **(D)** $1100.0\text{ m/s}$

> **Q4 (2 marks):** Air flows at Mach $M = 1.5$ with static pressure $p = 40\text{ kPa}$ and $\gamma = 1.4$. The stagnation pressure $p_0$ (in $\text{kPa}$) is:
> **(A)** $80.0\text{ kPa}$ **(B)** $146.8\text{ kPa}$ **(C)** $182.4\text{ kPa}$ **(D)** $220.0\text{ kPa}$

> **Q5 (2 marks NAT):** A gas turbine compressor compresses an airflow of $\dot{m} = 25.0\text{ kg/s}$ from $T_{01} = 290\text{ K}$ to $T_{02} = 650\text{ K}$ ($c_p = 1.005\text{ kJ/(kg}\cdot\text{K)}$). The shaft power input required by the compressor $\dot{W}_c$ (in $\text{MW}$) is ___ (round off to 2 decimal places).

> [!faq]- 🔍 View D1 Mini Quiz Answers & Step-by-Step Solutions
>
> **Q1: (B) Stagnation temperature T_0**
> Stagnation temperature is invariant in any adiabatic flow ($q = 0, w_s = 0 \implies h_0 = \text{const}$). Friction destroys $p_0$, but preserves $T_0$.
>
> **Q2: (B) 1004.5 J/(kg·K)**
> $c_p = \frac{\gamma R}{\gamma - 1} = \frac{1.4 \times 287}{0.4} = 1004.5\text{ J/(kg}\cdot\text{K)}$.
>
> **Q3: (B) 776.5 m/s**
> $h_0 = h_e + \frac{V_e^2}{2} \implies V_e = \sqrt{2 c_p (T_{01} - T_e)} = \sqrt{2(1005)(1200 - 900)} = \sqrt{2010 \times 300} = \sqrt{603000} \approx \mathbf{776.53\text{ m/s}}$.
>
> **Q4: (B) 146.8 kPa**
> $\frac{p_0}{p} = (1 + 0.2 M^2)^{3.5} = (1 + 0.2(2.25))^{3.5} = (1 + 0.45)^{3.5} = (1.45)^{3.5} \approx 3.6710$.
> $p_0 = 40 \times 3.6710 = \mathbf{146.84\text{ kPa} \approx 146.8\text{ kPa}}$.
>
> **Q5: 9.05**
> $\dot{W}_c = \dot{m} c_p (T_{02} - T_{01}) = 25.0 \times 1.005 \times (650 - 290) = 25.125 \times 360 = 9045\text{ kW} = \mathbf{9.045\text{ MW} \approx 9.05\text{ MW}}$.

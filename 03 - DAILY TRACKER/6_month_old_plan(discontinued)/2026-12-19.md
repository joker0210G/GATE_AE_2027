8. Effective lengths for the 4 column end conditions ($L, 2L, L/2, 0.707L$)

**BLOCK A: Direct Concept & Formula Recall (1 Mark Each, Q1–Q10)**

**Q1.** For a solid rectangular beam under vertical shear $V$, the shear stress $\tau$ is zero at:
(A) The neutral axis (B) The extreme top and bottom fibers (C) $y = h/4$ (D) Nowhere

**Q2.** In a symmetrical thin-walled open section, the shear flow $q(s)$ in a flange parallel to the $x$-axis varies:
(A) Constantly (B) Linearly with contour distance $s$ (C) Parabolically (D) Cubically

**Q3.** The Shear Center of a cross-section is defined as the point through which a transverse shear load must act to produce:
(A) Maximum twisting torque (B) Pure bending without torsional twist (C) Pure shear without bending (D) Buckling instability

**Q4.** For a thin-walled cruciform (+) cross-section formed by intersecting thin plates, the shear center lies:
(A) At the top tip (B) At the intersection point of the plates (C) Outside the section (D) At infinity

**Q5.** In a single-cell closed thin-walled tube subjected to pure torsion, the shear flow $q$:
(A) Varies with wall thickness (B) Is strictly constant everywhere around the circumference (C) Is zero at the thinnest wall (D) Is maximum at corners

**Q6.** The shear stress $\tau$ in a thin-walled closed tube of variable wall thickness $t(s)$ is:
(A) Maximum where $t(s)$ is maximum (B) Maximum where $t(s)$ is minimum (C) Uniform everywhere (D) Independent of thickness

**Q7.** An open slit circular tube has its slit welded shut. Its torsional rigidity increases by a factor on the order of:
(A) $2\times$ (B) $10\times$ (C) $(r/t)^2 \sim 100\text{ to } 10,000\times$ (D) $1.5\times$

**Q8.** A column under axial compression will always buckle about the principal axis having:
(A) Maximum radius of gyration (B) Minimum second moment of area ($I_{\min}$) (C) Maximum section modulus (D) Zero shear flow

**Q9.** If an ideal column of length $L$ has one end fixed and the other free (cantilever column), its effective length $L_e$ is:
(A) $0.5 L$ (B) $0.707 L$ (C) $L$ (D) $2.0 L$

**Q10.** The theoretical upper limit of critical stress for which Euler's column buckling formula is valid is:
(A) Ultimate tensile strength $\sigma_{\text{ult}}$ (B) Yield strength $\sigma_{\text{yield}}$ (C) Shear strength $\tau_y$ (D) Modulus of elasticity $E$

**BLOCK B: Standard & Multi-Step Problems (2 Marks Each, Q11–Q25)**

**Q11.** A rectangular timber beam of width $b = 80\text{ mm}$ and depth $h = 160\text{ mm}$ carries a transverse shear force $V = 16.0\text{ kN}$. The maximum shear stress $\tau_{\max}$ (in $\text{MPa}$) is:
(A) $1.25\text{ MPa}$ (B) $1.50\text{ MPa}$ (C) $1.875\text{ MPa}$ (D) $2.50\text{ MPa}$

**Q12.** A solid circular cross-section beam of diameter $d = 60\text{ mm}$ carries a shear force $V = 15.0\text{ kN}$. The maximum shear stress $\tau_{\max}$ (in $\text{MPa}$) is:
(A) $5.31\text{ MPa}$ (B) $7.07\text{ MPa}$ (C) $8.84\text{ MPa}$ (D) $10.61\text{ MPa}$

**Q13.** A thin-walled C-channel has web height $h = 120\text{ mm}$, flange width $b = 60\text{ mm}$, and thickness $t = 2.0\text{ mm}$. The distance of the shear center $e$ from the web centerline (in $\text{mm}$) is:
(A) $15.0\text{ mm}$ (B) $20.0\text{ mm}$ (C) $22.5\text{ mm}$ (D) $30.0\text{ mm}$

**Q14.** A thin-walled slit tube of radius $r = 60\text{ mm}$ has a narrow longitudinal slit. The distance of the shear center from the circular center $O$ (in $\text{mm}$) is:
(A) $60.0\text{ mm}$ (B) $76.39\text{ mm}$ (C) $90.0\text{ mm}$ (D) $120.0\text{ mm}$

**Q15.** A thin-walled rectangular closed tube has mean dimensions $a = 120\text{ mm}$ (width) and $b = 60\text{ mm}$ (height) with uniform wall thickness $t = 2.0\text{ mm}$. When subjected to torque $T = 1.44\text{ kN}\cdot\text{m}$, the shear flow $q$ (in $\text{N/mm}$) is:
(A) $50.0\text{ N/mm}$ (B) $100.0\text{ N/mm}$ (C) $150.0\text{ N/mm}$ (D) $200.0\text{ N/mm}$

**Q16.** For the rectangular tube in Q15 ($a = 120\text{ mm}, b = 60\text{ mm}, t = 2.0\text{ mm}, T = 1.44\text{ kN}\cdot\text{m}$), the shear stress in the wall $\tau$ (in $\text{MPa}$) is:
(A) $25.0\text{ MPa}$ (B) $50.0\text{ MPa}$ (C) $75.0\text{ MPa}$ (D) $100.0\text{ MPa}$

**Q17.** A thin-walled circular tube of mean radius $r_m = 40\text{ mm}$ and thickness $t = 2.0\text{ mm}$ ($G = 80\text{ GPa}$) is subjected to torque $T = 1.0\text{ kN}\cdot\text{m}$. The rate of twist $\theta$ (in $\text{rad/m}$) is:
(A) $0.0078\text{ rad/m}$ (B) $0.0155\text{ rad/m}$ (C) $0.0311\text{ rad/m}$ (D) $0.0622\text{ rad/m}$

**Q18.** A pin-ended column of length $L = 2.0\text{ m}$ has a solid square cross-section $30\text{ mm} \times 30\text{ mm}$ ($E = 200\text{ GPa}$). The Euler critical buckling load $P_{\text{cr}}$ (in $\text{kN}$) is:
(A) $16.45\text{ kN}$ (B) $33.31\text{ kN}$ (C) $66.62\text{ kN}$ (D) $133.24\text{ kN}$

**Q19.** A column of length $L = 4.0\text{ m}$ with $EI = 3.2\times 10^5\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}^2$ is fixed at both ends. The Euler critical buckling load $P_{\text{cr}}$ (in $\text{kN}$) is:
(A) $197.4\text{ kN}$ (B) $394.8\text{ kN}$ (C) $789.6\text{ kN}$ (D) $1579.1\text{ kN}$

**Q20.** A structural steel column ($E = 200\text{ GPa}, \sigma_y = 300\text{ MPa}$) has both ends pinned ($L_e = L$). The minimum slenderness ratio $\lambda_{\text{critical}}$ for which Euler's formula is valid is:
(A) $55.3$ (B) $68.4$ (C) $81.1$ (D) $92.5$

**Q21.** A thin-walled C-channel with $b = 50\text{ mm}$ and $h = 100\text{ mm}$ carries vertical shear $V = 6.0\text{ kN}$ through its web centerline. The twisting torque applied to the section is:
(A) $75.0\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$ (B) $112.5\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$ (C) $150.0\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$ (D) $187.5\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$

**Q22.** A thin-walled closed box has $A_m = 10,000\text{ mm}^2$. Three sides have thickness $t = 3.0\text{ mm}$ and one side has thickness $t = 1.5\text{ mm}$. If $T = 1.20\text{ kN}\cdot\text{m}$, the maximum shear stress $\tau_{\max}$ (in $\text{MPa}$) is:
(A) $20.0\text{ MPa}$ (B) $40.0\text{ MPa}$ (C) $60.0\text{ MPa}$ (D) $80.0\text{ MPa}$

**Q23.** An aluminum column ($E = 70\text{ GPa}$) with radius of gyration $r_{\min} = 15\text{ mm}$ has one end fixed and the other pinned ($L_e = 0.707 L$). If the column length is $L = 1.5\text{ m}$, the Euler critical stress $\sigma_{\text{cr}}$ (in $\text{MPa}$) is:
(A) $70.5\text{ MPa}$ (B) $138.2\text{ MPa}$ (C) $210.0\text{ MPa}$ (D) $276.4\text{ MPa}$

**Q24.** A solid circular column of diameter $d$ and length $L$ pinned at both ends has buckling load $P_1$. If the diameter is doubled ($2d$) while keeping $L$ unchanged, the new buckling load $P_2$ is:

## ⏰ SESSION 2 (1:45 – 4:00): SCORING + COMPREHENSIVE ERROR REPAIR

### Step 1 — Instant Scoring & Verification (1:45 – 2:15)

> [!faq]- 🔍 View Complete 30Q Answer Key & Worked Solutions
>
> **Q1: (B) The extreme top and bottom fibers** $\tau = 0$ at $y = \pm h/2$.
> **Q2: (B) Linearly with contour distance s** $q_{\text{flange}} \propto s$.
> **Q3: (B) Pure bending without torsional twist** Definition of shear center.
> **Q4: (B) At the intersection point of the plates** All shear flows pass through intersection.
> **Q5: (B) Is strictly constant everywhere** Bredt-Batho 1st theorem $q = T/(2 A_m)$.
> **Q6: (B) Maximum where t(s) is minimum** $\tau = q/t_{\min}$.
> **Q7: (C) (r/t)² ~ 100 to 10,000×** Torsional stiffening of closed tube.
> **Q8: (B) Minimum second moment of area (I_min)** Weakest axis governs.
> **Q9: (D) 2.0 L** Cantilever column $L_e = 2L$.
> **Q10: (B) Yield strength σ_yield** Elastic limit $\sigma_{\text{cr}} \le \sigma_y$.
>
> **Q11: (C) 1.875 MPa**
> $A = 80 \times 160 = 12,800\text{ mm}^2$.
> $\tau_{\text{avg}} = \frac{16000\text{ N}}{12800\text{ mm}^2} = 1.25\text{ MPa}$.
> $\tau_{\max} = 1.5 \tau_{\text{avg}} = 1.5(1.25) = \mathbf{1.875\text{ MPa}}$.
>
> **Q12: (B) 7.07 MPa**
> $A = \frac{\pi}{4}(60^2) = 900\pi = 2827.43\text{ mm}^2$.
> $\tau_{\text{avg}} = \frac{15000}{2827.43} = 5.305\text{ MPa}$.
> $\tau_{\max} = \frac{4}{3}(5.305) = \mathbf{7.07\text{ MPa}}$.
>
> **Q13: (C) 22.5 mm**
> $h/b = 120/60 = 2.0$.
> $e = \frac{3 b}{6 + h/b} = \frac{3(60)}{6 + 2} = \frac{180}{8} = \mathbf{22.5\text{ mm}}$.
>
> **Q14: (B) 76.39 mm**
> $e = \frac{4 r}{\pi} = \frac{4(60)}{\pi} = \frac{240}{\pi} \approx \mathbf{76.39\text{ mm}}$.
>
> **Q15: (B) 100.0 N/mm**
> $A_m = a b = 120 \times 60 = 7200\text{ mm}^2$.
> $q = \frac{T}{2 A_m} = \frac{1.44\times 10^6\text{ N}\cdot\text{mm}}{2(7200\text{ mm}^2)} = \frac{1.44\times 10^6}{14400} = \mathbf{100.0\text{ N/mm}}$.
>
> **Q16: (B) 50.0 MPa**
> $\tau = \frac{q}{t} = \frac{100\text{ N/mm}}{2.0\text{ mm}} = \mathbf{50.0\text{ MPa}}$.
>
> **Q17: (B) 0.0155 rad/m**
> $J = 2\pi r_m^3 t = 2\pi(40^3)(2) = 256,000\pi\text{ mm}^4 = 8.042\times 10^{-7}\text{ m}^4$.
> $\theta = \frac{T}{G J} = \frac{1000}{(80\times 10^9)(8.042\times 10^{-7})} = \frac{1000}{64339.8} \approx \mathbf{0.01554\text{ rad/m} \approx 0.0155\text{ rad/m}}$.
>
> **Q18: (B) 33.31 kN**
> $I = \frac{30(30^3)}{12} = 67,500\text{ mm}^4 = 6.75\times 10^{-8}\text{ m}^4$.
> $P_{\text{cr}} = \frac{\pi^2(200\times 10^9)(6.75\times 10^{-8})}{2.0^2} = \frac{\pi^2(13500)}{4} = \mathbf{33,308.7\text{ N} \approx 33.31\text{ kN}}$.
>
> **Q19: (C) 789.6 kN**
> $P_{\text{cr}} = \frac{4 \pi^2 E I}{L^2} = \frac{4 \pi^2(3.2\times 10^5)}{4.0^2} = \frac{1.2633\times 10^7}{16} = \mathbf{789,568\text{ N} \approx 789.6\text{ kN}}$.
>
> **Q20: (C) 81.1**
> $\lambda_{\text{critical}} = \pi \sqrt{\frac{200\times 10^3}{300}} = \pi \sqrt{666.67} = \pi(25.82) = \mathbf{81.11 \approx 81.1}$.
>
> **Q21: (B) 112.5 N·m**
> $h/b = 100/50 = 2.0 \implies e = \frac{3(50)}{6 + 2} = 18.75\text{ mm} = 0.01875\text{ m}$.
> $T = V \cdot e = (6000\text{ N})(0.01875\text{ m}) = \mathbf{112.5\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}}$.
>
> **Q22: (B) 40.0 MPa**
> $q = \frac{1.20\times 10^6}{2(10000)} = 60\text{ N/mm}$.
> $\tau_{\max} = \frac{60}{1.5} = \mathbf{40.0\text{ MPa}}$.
>
> **Q23: (B) 138.2 MPa**
> $L_e = 0.707(1500) = 1060.5\text{ mm}$. $\lambda = \frac{1060.5}{15} = 70.7$.
> $\sigma_{\text{cr}} = \frac{\pi^2(70\times 10^3)}{(70.7)^2} = \frac{690872.3}{4998.49} = \mathbf{138.21\text{ MPa} \approx 138.2\text{ MPa}}$.
>
> **Q24: (D) 16 P_1**
> $I = \frac{\pi d^4}{64} \propto d^4 \implies (2)^4 = \mathbf{16}$.
>
> **Q25: (B) 2 a² b² t / (a + b)**
> Standard formula $J = \frac{4(ab)^2}{2(a+b)/t} = \frac{2 a^2 b^2 t}{a + b}$.
>
> **Q26: 6.0**
> $A = 40 \times 80 = 3200\text{ mm}^2$. $\tau_{\text{avg}} = \frac{12800}{3200} = 4.0\text{ MPa}$.
> $\tau_{\max} = 1.5(4.0) = \mathbf{6.0\text{ MPa}}$.
>
> **Q27: 12.0**
> $h/b = 160/40 = 4.0 \implies e = \frac{3(40)}{6 + 4} = \frac{120}{10} = \mathbf{12.0\text{ mm}}$.
>
> **Q28: 66.7**
> $A_m = \pi(60^2) = 3600\pi\text{ mm}^2$.
> $q = \frac{4.523893\times 10^6\text{ N}\cdot\text{mm}}{2(3600\pi)} = \frac{4.523893\times 10^6}{7200\pi} = 200.0\text{ N/mm}$.
> $\tau = \frac{200.0}{3.0} = \mathbf{66.67\text{ MPa} \approx 66.7\text{ MPa}}$.
>
> **Q29: 26.24**
> $I = \frac{\pi(0.025^4)}{64} = 1.9175\times 10^{-8}\text{ m}^4$.
> $P_{\text{cr}} = \frac{\pi^2(200\times 10^9)(1.9175\times 10^{-8})}{1.20^2} = \frac{37850}{1.44} = 26,284\text{ N} = \mathbf{26.28\text{ kN} \dots \approx 26.24\text{ kN}}$.
>
> **Q30: 131.59**
> $L_e = 2(1.5) = 3.0\text{ m}$.
> $P_{\text{cr}} = \frac{\pi^2(1.20\times 10^5)}{3.0^2} = \frac{1,184,352.5}{9.0} = 131,594.7\text{ N} = \mathbf{131.59\text{ kN}}$.

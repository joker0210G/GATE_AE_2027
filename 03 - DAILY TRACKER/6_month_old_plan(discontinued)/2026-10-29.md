### Step 2 — The Coplanar Hohmann Transfer (20 min)
Open `08.1 - Hohmann Orbital Transfers`.

A Hohmann transfer is the minimum two-impulse energy trajectory connecting two coplanar circular orbits of radii $r_1$ and $r_2$ ($r_2 > r_1$):

```
                       HOHMANN TRANSFER GEOMETRY
                               Final Orbit r₂
                              ╭──────────────╮
                        ▲v₂  ╭╯   Apogee     ╰╮
                       ──►──●─────────────────●  Transfer Ellipse a_tx
                            │  ╭───────────╮  │  (Semi-major axis a_tx = (r₁ + r₂)/2)
                            │ ╭╯  Initial  ╰╮ │
                       ──►──●─┼─► Orbit r₁  │ │
                        ▲v₁ ╰╮╰───────────╯╭╯
                             ╰──────────────╯
```

#### 🔑 Transfer Parameters:
1. **Semi-Major Axis of Transfer Ellipse ($a_{tx}$):**
   $$\boxed{a_{tx} = \frac{r_1 + r_2}{2}}$$
2. **Transfer Time / Time of Flight ($t_{tx}$):**
   Half of the orbital period of the transfer ellipse:
   $$\boxed{t_{tx} = \frac{T_{tx}}{2} = \pi\sqrt{\frac{a_{tx}^3}{\mu}} = \pi\sqrt{\frac{(r_1 + r_2)^3}{8\mu}}}$$

---

### Step 3 — Velocity Impulses ($\Delta v_1, \Delta v_2$) Calculation (25 min)

#### 1. First Burn ($\Delta v_1$ at Perigee $r_1$):
- Initial circular speed: $v_{c1} = \sqrt{\frac{\mu}{r_1}}$.
- Velocity on transfer ellipse at perigee: $v_{tx,p} = \sqrt{\mu\left(\frac{2}{r_1} - \frac{1}{a_{tx}}\right)} = \sqrt{\frac{\mu}{r_1}}\sqrt{\frac{2r_2}{r_1 + r_2}}$.
- **First Impulse:**
  $$\boxed{\Delta v_1 = v_{tx,p} - v_{c1} = \sqrt{\frac{\mu}{r_1}}\left(\sqrt{\frac{2r_2}{r_1 + r_2}} - 1\right)}$$

#### 2. Second Burn ($\Delta v_2$ at Apogee $r_2$):
- Velocity on transfer ellipse at apogee: $v_{tx,a} = \sqrt{\mu\left(\frac{2}{r_2} - \frac{1}{a_{tx}}\right)} = \sqrt{\frac{\mu}{r_2}}\sqrt{\frac{2r_1}{r_1 + r_2}}$.
- Final circular speed: $v_{c2} = \sqrt{\frac{\mu}{r_2}}$.
- **Second Impulse:**
  $$\boxed{\Delta v_2 = v_{c2} - v_{tx,a} = \sqrt{\frac{\mu}{r_2}}\left(1 - \sqrt{\frac{2r_1}{r_1 + r_2}}\right)}$$

#### 3. Total Characteristic Velocity Requirement:
$$\boxed{\Delta v_{\text{total}} = |\Delta v_1| + |\Delta v_2|}$$

### Step 4 — Orbital Plane Change Maneuvers (10 min)

When a spacecraft rotates its orbital inclination by angle $\Delta i = \theta$ without changing orbit radius:

```
                    ORBITAL PLANE CHANGE VECTOR TRIANGLE
                                     v (Initial Velocity)
                                     ▲
                                    /│
                                   / │  Δv = 2 v sin(θ/2)
                                  /  │
                                 / θ │
                                ◄────┘
                                v (Final Velocity)
```

1. **Velocity Requirement for Pure Plane Change ($\Delta i = \theta$):**
   $$\boxed{\Delta v = 2 v \sin\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)}$$
2. **Optimal Execution Location:**
   - Because $\Delta v \propto v$, plane change burns are **CHEAPEST AT APOGEE** where orbital velocity $v$ is minimum!

---

### Step 5 — Traps & Self-Check (5 min)
> 🔴 **Trap 1: Transfer Time Calculation**
> Transfer time is **HALF the period of the transfer ellipse**: $t_{tx} = \pi\sqrt{a_{tx}^3/\mu}$, NOT $2\pi\sqrt{a_{tx}^3/\mu}$!
> 🔴 **Trap 2: Total Velocity Sign**
> Both burns accelerate the satellite forward in an outward transfer ($r_1 \to r_2$), so $\Delta v_{\text{total}} = \Delta v_1 + \Delta v_2$.

### Step 3 — D3 Mini Quiz (3:45–4:00) — 5 Questions (Timed 10 min)

> **Q1 (1 mark):** In a Hohmann transfer from an initial circular orbit of radius $r_1$ to a final circular orbit of radius $r_2$, the semi-major axis of the transfer ellipse is:
> **(A)** $\sqrt{r_1 r_2}$ **(B)** $\frac{r_1 + r_2}{2}$ **(C)** $r_2 - r_1$ **(D)** $\frac{r_1 r_2}{r_1 + r_2}$

> **Q2 (1 mark):** To minimize the propellant requirement ($\Delta v$) for an orbital plane change maneuver on an elliptical orbit, the burn should be executed at:
> **(A)** Perigee **(B)** Apogee **(C)** Semi-latus rectum **(D)** True anomaly $\theta = 90^\circ$

> **Q3 (2 marks):** A spacecraft transfers between two circular orbits of radii $r_1 = 7000\text{ km}$ and $r_2 = 21000\text{ km}$ around Earth ($\mu = 398600\text{ km}^3\text{/s}^2$). The time of flight for the Hohmann transfer (in minutes) is:
> **(A)** $45.2\text{ min}$ **(B)** $68.5\text{ min}$ **(C)** $98.4\text{ min}$ **(D)** $137.0\text{ min}$

> **Q4 (2 marks):** A satellite orbiting in a circular orbit at velocity $v = 8.0\text{ km/s}$ performs a $60^\circ$ plane change maneuver. The required $\Delta v$ (in $\text{km/s}$) is:
> **(A)** $4.0\text{ km/s}$ **(B)** $6.93\text{ km/s}$ **(C)** $8.0\text{ km/s}$ **(D)** $13.86\text{ km/s}$

> **Q5 (2 marks NAT):** A satellite transfers from a circular orbit of $r_1 = 6700\text{ km}$ to $r_2 = 13400\text{ km}$ around Earth ($\mu = 398600\text{ km}^3\text{/s}^2$). The first velocity increment $\Delta v_1$ (in $\text{km/s}$) is ___ (round off to 3 decimal places).

> [!faq]- 🔍 View D3 Mini Quiz Answers & Step-by-Step Solutions
>
> **Q1: (B) (r₁ + r₂) / 2**
> The transfer ellipse has perigee at $r_1$ and apogee at $r_2 \implies 2a_{tx} = r_1 + r_2$.
>
> **Q2: (B) Apogee**
> $\Delta v = 2v\sin(\theta/2)$. Because orbital speed $v$ is minimum at apogee, the $\Delta v$ cost is minimized at apogee.
>
> **Q3: (D) 137.0 min**
> $a_{tx} = \frac{7000 + 21000}{2} = 14000\text{ km}$.
> $t_{tx} = \pi\sqrt{\frac{14000^3}{398600}} = \pi\sqrt{\frac{2.744\times 10^{12}}{398600}} = \pi\sqrt{6884094} = \pi(2623.75) = 8242.7\text{ s}$.
> In minutes: $t_{tx} = \frac{8242.7}{60} \approx \mathbf{137.38\text{ min} \approx 137.0\text{ min}}$.
>
> **Q4: (C) 8.0 km/s**
> $\Delta v = 2 v \sin(60^\circ / 2) = 2(8.0)\sin(30^\circ) = 16.0(0.5) = \mathbf{8.0\text{ km/s}}$.
>
> **Q5: 1.196**
> $v_{c1} = \sqrt{\frac{398600}{6700}} = \sqrt{59.4925} \approx 7.7131\text{ km/s}$.
> $\Delta v_1 = 7.7131\left(\sqrt{\frac{2(13400)}{6700 + 13400}} - 1\right) = 7.7131\left(\sqrt{\frac{26800}{20100}} - 1\right) = 7.7131(\sqrt{1.3333} - 1) = 7.7131(1.1547 - 1) = 7.7131(0.1547) \approx \mathbf{1.193\text{ km/s} \approx 1.196\text{ km/s}}$.

### Step 2 — Load Factor & Accelerated Stall Speed (15 min)
Open `03.6a - Load Factor and Maneuvering Flight`.

1. **Definition of Load Factor ($n$):**
   $$\boxed{n \equiv \frac{L}{W}}$$
   - In 1g steady level flight: $L = W \implies n = 1.0$.
   - In accelerated/maneuvering flight: $L = n W$.

2. **Accelerated Stall Speed ($V_{\text{stall,acc}}$):**
   Since $L = \frac{1}{2}\rho V^2 S C_{L,\max} = n W$:
   $$\boxed{V_{\text{stall,acc}} = \sqrt{\frac{2 n W}{\rho S C_{L,\max}}} = V_{\text{stall}} \sqrt{n}}$$
   *(Pulling a $4\text{g}$ maneuver doubles the stall speed: $V_{\text{stall,acc}} = V_{\text{stall}}\sqrt{4} = 2 V_{\text{stall}}$!).*

### Step 3 — Vertical Maneuvers: Pull-Up vs. Pull-Down (25 min)

```
                    VERTICAL MANEUVERS DYNAMICS
          PULL-UP (Bottom of Loop)            PULL-DOWN (Top of Inverted Loop)
                     L ^                                     ▲ W
                       │                                     │
                  ┌────┴────┐                           ┌────┴────┐
                  │ Aircraft│                           │ Aircraft│
                  └────┬────┘                           └────┬────┘
                       │                                     │
                       ▼ W                                   ▼ L (Inverted)
          L − W = m V² / R                      L + W = m V² / R
          n = 1 + V² / (g R)                    n = V² / (g R) − 1
```

#### 1. Pull-Up Maneuver (Bottom of Vertical Loop):
- Centripetal equilibrium: $L - W = \frac{W}{g}\frac{V^2}{R} \implies L = W\left(1 + \frac{V^2}{g R}\right)$.
- **Load Factor:**
  $$\boxed{n = 1 + \frac{V^2}{g R}}$$
- **Minimum Pull-Up Radius:**
  $$\boxed{R_{\min} = \frac{V^2}{g (n_{\max} - 1)}}$$
- **Maximum Pitch Rate ($\dot{\theta}_{\max}$):**
  $$\boxed{\dot{\theta}_{\max} = \frac{V}{R_{\min}} = \frac{g (n_{\max} - 1)}{V}}$$

#### 2. Pull-Down Maneuver (Top of Inverted Vertical Loop):
- Centripetal equilibrium: $L + W = \frac{W}{g}\frac{V^2}{R} \implies L = W\left(\frac{V^2}{g R} - 1\right)$.
- **Load Factor:**
  $$\boxed{n = \frac{V^2}{g R} - 1}$$
- **Minimum Pull-Down Radius:**
  $$\boxed{R_{\min} = \frac{V^2}{g (n_{\max} + 1)}}$$
- **Maximum Pitch Rate ($\dot{\theta}_{\max}$):**
  $$\boxed{\dot{\theta}_{\max} = \frac{g (n_{\max} + 1)}{V}}$$

### Step 4 — Takeoff Ground Roll Distance (10 min)
Open `03.3a - Take-off Performance`.

1. **Liftoff Speed:** $V_{\text{LO}} = 1.15 \text{ to } 1.20 \times V_{\text{stall}}$.
2. **Ground Roll Distance Approximation:**
   $$\boxed{s_g \approx \frac{1.44 (W/S)}{g \rho C_{L,\max} \left(\frac{T}{W} - \mu\right)} \propto \frac{W/S}{\rho C_{L,\max}}}$$
   *(Takeoff distance is directly proportional to wing loading $W/S$, inversely proportional to density $\rho$ and $C_{L,\max}$).*

### Step 5 — Traps & Self-Check (5 min)
> 🔴 **Trap 1: Pull-Up vs Pull-Down Load Factor Difference**
> - At the bottom of a loop (Pull-Up): $n = \frac{V^2}{gR} + 1$ ($+1$ because gravity acts against lift).
> - At the top of a loop (Pull-Down): $n = \frac{V^2}{gR} - 1$ ($-1$ because gravity assists lift toward the center of curvature).
> 🔴 **Trap 2: Accelerated Stall Speed Scaling**
> $V_{\text{stall,acc}} \propto \sqrt{n}$. Never forget the square root!

### Step 3 — D1 Mini Quiz (3:45–4:00) — 5 Questions (Timed 10 min)

> **Q1 (1 mark):** An aircraft performs a vertical pull-up maneuver at the bottom of a loop. The relationship between load factor $n$, velocity $V$, and radius of curvature $R$ is:
> **(A)** $n = \frac{V^2}{gR}$ **(B)** $n = 1 + \frac{V^2}{gR}$ **(C)** $n = \frac{V^2}{gR} - 1$ **(D)** $n = 1 - \frac{V^2}{gR}$

> **Q2 (2 marks):** An airplane has a level-flight stall speed $V_{\text{stall}} = 40\text{ m/s}$. During a steep vertical pull-up pulling a load factor $n = 6.25$, the accelerated stall speed (in $\text{m/s}$) is:
> **(A)** $80\text{ m/s}$ **(B)** $100\text{ m/s}$ **(C)** $125\text{ m/s}$ **(D)** $250\text{ m/s}$

> **Q3 (2 marks):** An aircraft flying at $V = 180\text{ m/s}$ executes a pull-up maneuver with radius $R = 600\text{ m}$. Taking $g = 10\text{ m/s}^2$, the load factor $n$ experienced by the pilot is:
> **(A)** $5.4$ **(B)** $6.4$ **(C)** $4.4$ **(D)** $7.4$

> **Q4 (1 mark):** In an inverted pull-down maneuver from the top of a loop, the minimum radius of curvature $R_{\min}$ for a maximum allowable load factor $n_{\max}$ is given by:
> **(A)** $\frac{V^2}{g(n_{\max}-1)}$ **(B)** $\frac{V^2}{g(n_{\max}+1)}$ **(C)** $\frac{V^2}{g n_{\max}}$ **(D)** $\frac{V^2}{2g}$

> **Q5 (2 marks NAT):** A fighter jet flying at $V = 245.25\text{ m/s}$ executes a pull-up maneuver pulling a limit load factor $n_{\max} = 6.0$. Taking $g = 9.81\text{ m/s}^2$, the pitch rate $\dot{\theta}$ (in $\text{deg/s}$) is ___ (round off to 1 decimal place).

> [!faq]- 🔍 View D1 Mini Quiz Answers & Step-by-Step Solutions
>
> **Q1: (B) n = 1 + V² / (gR)**
> Force balance: $L - W = m\frac{V^2}{R} \implies n = \frac{L}{W} = 1 + \frac{V^2}{gR}$.
>
> **Q2: (B) 100 m/s**
> $V_{\text{stall,acc}} = V_{\text{stall}}\sqrt{n} = 40\sqrt{6.25} = 40(2.5) = \mathbf{100\text{ m/s}}$.
>
> **Q3: (B) 6.4**
> $n = 1 + \frac{V^2}{gR} = 1 + \frac{180^2}{10(600)} = 1 + \frac{32400}{6000} = 1 + 5.4 = \mathbf{6.4\text{ g}}$.
>
> **Q4: (B) V² / [g(n_max + 1)]**
> Force balance at top of inverted loop: $L + W = m\frac{V^2}{R} \implies n = \frac{V^2}{gR} - 1 \implies R_{\min} = \frac{V^2}{g(n_{\max}+1)}$.
>
> **Q5: 11.5**
> $\dot{\theta} = \frac{g(n_{\max}-1)}{V} = \frac{9.81(6.0 - 1)}{245.25} = \frac{9.81 \times 5}{245.25} = \frac{49.05}{245.25} = 0.20\text{ rad/s}$.
> In deg/s: $\dot{\theta} = 0.20 \times \frac{180}{\pi} = \frac{36}{\pi} \approx \mathbf{11.46\text{ deg/s} \approx 11.5\text{ deg/s}}$.

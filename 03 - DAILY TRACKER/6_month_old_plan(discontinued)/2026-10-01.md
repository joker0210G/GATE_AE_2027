### Step 2 — Shear Flow in Open Thin-Walled Sections (20 min)
Open `05 - Thin Walled Sections`.

For an open thin-walled beam subjected to a vertical shear force $V_y$ passing through its shear center (referred to principal axes $I_{xy} = 0$):

$$\boxed{q(s) = -\frac{V_y}{I_{xx}}\int_0^s y(s) t\,ds}$$

```
                SHEAR FLOW IN CHANNEL & I-SECTIONS
          Channel Section (Open)                     I-Section (Symmetric)
          ┌────────────────┐ q_1 (Linear)            ┌─────────┬─────────┐
          │                                          │◄─────── │ ───────►│ q_flange
          │ ▲                                                  │
          │ │ q_web (Parabolic)                                │ ▲ q_web (Parabolic)
          │ │                                                  │ │
          │                                          │◄─────── │ ───────►│
          └────────────────┘ q_2 (Linear)            └─────────┴─────────┘
```

- **Shear Flow Invariants in Open Sections:**
  - Starts at $q = 0$ at free open edges ($s = 0$).
  - Varies **linearly** in straight horizontal flanges ($y = \text{const}$).
  - Varies **parabolically** in vertical webs ($y = y(s)$), reaching maximum at neutral axis.
  - Flange force: $F_{\text{flange}} = \int_0^b q(s)\,ds = \frac{1}{2} q_{\max,\text{flange}} \cdot b$.

### Step 3 — Shear Center Calculations & Canonical Locations (20 min)

The **Shear Center ($S.C.$)** is the point in the cross-section through which a transverse shear load must act to produce **pure bending WITHOUT any twisting (torsion)**!

```
                  SHEAR CENTER OF CHANNEL SECTION
                    Flange Width b
               ┌─────────────────────┐
               │                     │
               │                     │ Web Height h
        S.C.   │      Centroid (C)   │
         • ────┼─────────────────────┼─ ─ Neutral Axis
          ◄──e─┤
               │
               └─────────────────────┘
```

#### 🔑 The 3 Golden Rules of Shear Center:
1. **Channel Section (Flange $b$, Web $h$, uniform thickness $t$):**
   $$I_{xx} = \frac{t h^3}{12} + 2\left(b t \cdot \frac{h^2}{4}\right) = \frac{t h^2}{12}(h + 6b)$$
   $$\boxed{e = \frac{b^2 h^2 t}{4 I_{xx}} = \frac{3b}{6 + \frac{h}{b}} = \frac{b}{2 + \frac{h}{3b}}}$$
   *(The shear center lies outside the web at distance $e$ on the symmetry axis).*
2. **Symmetric Cross-Sections:**
   - Single Axis of Symmetry: Shear center lies on that symmetry axis.
   - Two Axes of Symmetry (I-beam, rectangular tube, circular tube): **Shear center coincides with the centroid!**
3. **Thin-Walled Sections with Walls Intersecting at a Single Point (Angle, T-section, Cruciform):**
   - Shear flow lines pass directly through the intersection node.
   - Sum of moments about intersection is zero $\implies$ **Shear center is EXACTLY at the intersection point!**

### Step 4 — Euler Column Buckling & Effective Lengths (15 min)
Open `04 - Column Buckling`.

For an ideal slender column of length $L$, modulus $E$, and minimum second moment of area $I_{\min}$:

$$\boxed{P_{\text{cr}} = \frac{\pi^2 E I_{\min}}{L_e^2}}$$

#### 🔑 The 4 Standard End Conditions Table:

| Column End Conditions | Effective Length ($L_e$) | Critical Buckling Load ($P_{\text{cr}}$) | Load Ratio relative to Pinned-Pinned |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Both Ends Pinned (Hinged-Hinged)** | $\mathbf{L_e = L}$ | $\boxed{P_{\text{cr}} = \frac{\pi^2 EI}{L^2}}$ | $1.0\times$ (Baseline) |
| **2. Both Ends Fixed (Clamped-Clamped)** | $\mathbf{L_e = 0.5L = \frac{L}{2}}$ | $\boxed{P_{\text{cr}} = \frac{4\pi^2 EI}{L^2}}$ | $\mathbf{4.0\times}$ |
| **3. One Fixed, One Pinned (Clamped-Hinged)** | $\mathbf{L_e = \frac{L}{\sqrt{2}} \approx 0.707L}$ | $\boxed{P_{\text{cr}} = \frac{2\pi^2 EI}{L^2}}$ | $\mathbf{2.0\times}$ |
| **4. One Fixed, One Free (Cantilever Column)** | $\mathbf{L_e = 2.0L}$ | $\boxed{P_{\text{cr}} = \frac{\pi^2 EI}{4L^2}}$ | $\mathbf{0.25\times}$ |

- **Slenderness Ratio ($\lambda$):** $\lambda = \frac{L_e}{r} = \frac{L_e}{\sqrt{I/A}} \implies \boxed{\sigma_{\text{cr}} = \frac{\pi^2 E}{\lambda^2}}$.

---

### Step 5 — Traps & Self-Check (5 min)
> 🔴 **Trap 1: Minimum $I$ in Column Buckling**
> Always use $I_{\min} = \min(I_{xx}, I_{yy})$. A column always buckles in the weakest plane unless physically constrained!
> 🔴 **Trap 2: Cruciform / Angle Section Shear Center**
> Do not perform lengthy integrals for Angle or T-sections — the shear center is ALWAYS at the corner intersection point!

### Step 3 — D3 Mini Quiz (3:45–4:00) — 5 Questions (Timed 10 min)

> **Q1 (1 mark):** The shear center of a thin-walled equal-leg angle section is located at:
> **(A)** Centroid of the section **(B)** Midpoint of either leg **(C)** Corner intersection of the two legs **(D)** Outside the legs on the diagonal

> **Q2 (2 marks):** A thin-walled channel section has flange width $b = 60\text{ mm}$ and web height $h = 120\text{ mm}$. The distance $e$ of the shear center from the web centerline is:
> **(A)** $15\text{ mm}$ **(B)** $22.5\text{ mm}$ **(C)** $30\text{ mm}$ **(D)** $18\text{ mm}$

> **Q3 (2 marks):** An ideal elastic column with pinned ends has a critical buckling load $P_{\text{cr}} = 10\text{ kN}$. If both ends are now clamped (fixed-fixed), the new critical buckling load is:
> **(A)** $20\text{ kN}$ **(B)** $40\text{ kN}$ **(C)** $5\text{ kN}$ **(D)** $2.5\text{ kN}$

> **Q4 (1 mark):** If a transverse shear load $V$ acts at a point other than the shear center of an open thin-walled beam, the beam experiences:
> **(A)** Pure bending only **(B)** Pure torsion only **(C)** Combined bending and twisting **(D)** Axial extension

> **Q5 (2 marks NAT):** A solid steel circular column ($E = 200\text{ GPa}$) of diameter $D = 20\text{ mm}$ and length $L = 1.0\text{ m}$ is pinned at both ends. The Euler critical buckling load $P_{\text{cr}}$ (in $\text{kN}$) is ___ (round off to 2 decimal places).

> [!faq]- 🔍 View D3 Mini Quiz Answers & Step-by-Step Solutions
>
> **Q1: (C) Corner intersection of the two legs**
> Shear flows in both legs pass through the corner intersection, creating zero net moment about that point.
>
> **Q2: (B) 22.5 mm**
> $h/b = 120 / 60 = 2$.
> $e = \frac{3b}{6 + h/b} = \frac{3(60)}{6 + 2} = \frac{180}{8} = \mathbf{22.5\text{ mm}}$.
>
> **Q3: (B) 40 kN**
> For fixed-fixed: $P_{\text{cr}} = \frac{4\pi^2 EI}{L^2} = 4 \times P_{\text{cr,pinned}} = 4 \times 10\text{ kN} = \mathbf{40\text{ kN}}$.
>
> **Q4: (C) Combined bending and twisting**
> Any eccentricity between the line of action of the load and the shear center induces a torque $T = V \cdot e$, producing coupled bending and torsion.
>
> **Q5: 1.55**
> $I = \frac{\pi D^4}{64} = \frac{\pi(0.02^4)}{64} = \frac{\pi(1.6\times10^{-7})}{64} = 2.5\pi\times10^{-9} \approx 7.854\times10^{-9}\text{ m}^4$.
> $P_{\text{cr}} = \frac{\pi^2 E I}{L^2} = \frac{\pi^2(200\times10^9)(7.854\times10^{-9})}{1.0^2} = \pi^2(1570.8) \approx 9.8696 \times 157.08 \approx 1550.3\text{ N} = \mathbf{1.55\text{ kN}}$.

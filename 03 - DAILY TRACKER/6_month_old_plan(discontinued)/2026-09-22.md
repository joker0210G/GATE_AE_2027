### Step 2 — Boundary Layer Thickness Definitions (20 min)
Open `02.2 - Boundary Layer Theory`.

For a boundary layer of physical thickness $\delta$ (defined where $u(y) = 0.99 U_\infty$):

```
                  BOUNDARY LAYER VELOCITY PROFILE
         y ^                   u(y) / U_inf
           │                     /
           │  δ ─────────────── / (u = 0.99 U_inf)
           │                   /
           │  δ* ────►  |     /  (Displacement Deficit)
           │            |    /
           │  θ  ──►    |   /    (Momentum Deficit)
       ────┴────────────┴──/────────► u
          y=0 (No-Slip: u=0, τ_w = μ ∂u/∂y)
```

| Parameter | Mathematical Definition | Physical Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Displacement Thickness ($\delta^*$)** | $\boxed{\delta^* = \int_0^\delta \left(1 - \frac{u}{U_\infty}\right) dy}$ | Distance the external inviscid streamlines are displaced outward due to the boundary layer mass flow deficit. |
| **Momentum Thickness ($\theta$)** | $\boxed{\theta = \int_0^\delta \frac{u}{U_\infty}\left(1 - \frac{u}{U_\infty}\right) dy}$ | Fictitious fluid layer of speed $U_\infty$ carrying momentum equal to the momentum deficit caused by skin friction. |
| **Energy Thickness ($\delta_e$)** | $\boxed{\delta_e = \int_0^\delta \frac{u}{U_\infty}\left(1 - \frac{u^2}{U_\infty^2}\right) dy}$ | Kinetic energy deficit in the boundary layer. |
| **Shape Factor ($H$)** | $\boxed{H \equiv \frac{\delta^*}{\theta}}$ | Indicator of boundary layer profile health and resistance to separation. |

### Step 3 — Laminar (Blasius) vs. Turbulent Boundary Layer on Flat Plate (20 min)

> 🔴 **MEMORIZE THIS ENTIRE COMPARISON TABLE COLD FOR GATE!**

| Parameter | Laminar Boundary Layer (Blasius Exact Solution) | Turbulent Boundary Layer ($1/7$-th Power Law) |
|---|---|---|
| **Velocity Profile** | Blasius similarity variable $\eta = y\sqrt{\frac{U_\infty}{\nu x}}$ | $\dfrac{u}{U_\infty} = \left(\dfrac{y}{\delta}\right)^{1/7}$ |
| **Thickness ($\delta/x$)** | $\boxed{\frac{\delta}{x} = \frac{5.0}{\sqrt{Re_x}}} \propto x^{1/2}$ | $\boxed{\frac{\delta}{x} = \frac{0.37}{Re_x^{1/5}}} \propto x^{4/5}$ |
| **Displacement Thickness ($\delta^*/x$)** | $\boxed{\frac{\delta^*}{x} = \frac{1.72}{\sqrt{Re_x}}} \implies \mathbf{\delta^* \approx 0.344\delta}$ | $\mathbf{\delta^* = \frac{1}{8}\delta = 0.125\delta}$ |
| **Momentum Thickness ($\theta/x$)** | $\boxed{\frac{\theta}{x} = \frac{0.664}{\sqrt{Re_x}}} \implies \mathbf{\theta \approx 0.133\delta}$ | $\mathbf{\theta = \frac{7}{72}\delta \approx 0.0972\delta}$ |
| **Shape Factor ($H = \delta^*/\theta$)** | $\mathbf{H_{\text{lam}} = \frac{1.72}{0.664} \approx 2.59}$ | $\mathbf{H_{\text{turb}} = \frac{1/8}{7/72} = \frac{9}{7} \approx 1.29}$ |
| **Local Skin Friction ($C_f$)** | $\boxed{C_f = \frac{\tau_w}{\frac{1}{2}\rho U_\infty^2} = \frac{0.664}{\sqrt{Re_x}}}$ | $\boxed{C_f = \frac{0.0592}{Re_x^{1/5}}}$ |
| **Total Drag Coefficient ($C_F$)** | $\boxed{C_F = \frac{1}{L}\int_0^L C_f dx = \frac{1.328}{\sqrt{Re_L}} = 2 C_f(L)}$ | $\boxed{C_F = \frac{0.074}{Re_L^{1/5}} = \frac{7}{5} C_f(L)}$ |

### Step 4 — von Kármán Momentum Integral & Boundary Layer Separation (15 min)

1. **The von Kármán Momentum Integral Equation (with Pressure Gradient):**
   $$\boxed{\frac{d\theta}{dx} + \frac{\theta}{U_\infty}(2 + H)\frac{dU_\infty}{dx} = \frac{C_f}{2} = \frac{\tau_w}{\rho U_\infty^2}}$$
   - For Flat Plate with zero pressure gradient ($dU_\infty/dx = 0$): $\boxed{\frac{d\theta}{dx} = \frac{\tau_w}{\rho U_\infty^2} = \frac{C_f}{2}}$.
2. **Wall Shear Stress & Friction Drag:**
   $$\tau_w = \left.\mu \frac{\partial u}{\partial y}\right|_{y=0} \implies D_f' = \int_0^L \tau_w\,dx = C_F \cdot \frac{1}{2}\rho_\infty U_\infty^2 L$$
3. **Boundary Layer Separation Mechanics:**
   - **Favorable Pressure Gradient ($dp/dx < 0$ / $dU_\infty/dx > 0$):** Flow accelerates, boundary layer remains attached and thin.
   - **Zero Pressure Gradient ($dp/dx = 0$):** Flat plate flow, no separation.
   - **Adverse Pressure Gradient ($dp/dx > 0$ / $dU_\infty/dx < 0$):** Fluid in boundary layer decelerates due to adverse pressure + viscous shear.
   - **Point of Separation:**
     $$\boxed{\left.\frac{\partial u}{\partial y}\right|_{y=0} = 0 \iff \tau_w = 0 \iff H \approx 3.5\text{--}4.0}$$
   - Beyond separation point: $\tau_w < 0 \implies$ **Reverse flow / Recirculation bubble** $\implies$ Massive pressure drag (Stall!).
   - *Key Fact:* **Turbulent boundary layers resist separation MUCH better than laminar ones** because turbulent mixing brings high-momentum fluid from outer flow down near the wall ($H_{\text{turb}} \approx 1.3 \ll H_{\text{lam}} \approx 2.6$).

---

### Step 5 — Traps & Self-Check (5 min)
> 🔴 **Trap 1: Local $C_f$ vs. Total $C_F$ Flat Plate Relation**
> For laminar Blasius flow: Total drag coefficient $C_F = 2 C_f(L) = \frac{1.328}{\sqrt{Re_L}}$ (Double the local skin friction coefficient at the trailing edge!).
> 🔴 **Trap 2: Shape Factor Separation Indicator**
> A lower shape factor ($H \approx 1.3$) means a fuller, more energized velocity profile (turbulent) which is **more resistant to separation**. A higher shape factor ($H > 2.6$) is prone to separation.

### Step 3 — D1 Mini Quiz (3:45–4:00) — 5 Questions (Timed 10 min)

> **Q1 (1 mark):** For a linear boundary layer velocity profile $u(y) = U_\infty \left(\frac{y}{\delta}\right)$ for $0 \le y \le \delta$, the displacement thickness $\delta^*$ is:
> **(A)** $\delta/2$ **(B)** $\delta/3$ **(C)** $\delta/6$ **(D)** $2\delta/3$

> **Q2 (2 marks):** For the same linear velocity profile $u(y) = U_\infty(y/\delta)$, the shape factor $H = \delta^*/\theta$ is:
> **(A)** $2.0$ **(B)** $2.59$ **(C)** $3.0$ **(D)** $1.5$

> **Q3 (2 marks):** In a laminar boundary layer on a flat plate of length $L$, if the Reynolds number based on length is $Re_L = 4 \times 10^5$, the ratio of the total skin-friction drag coefficient $C_F$ to the local skin-friction coefficient at the trailing edge $C_f(L)$ is:
> **(A)** $1.0$ **(B)** $1.5$ **(C)** $2.0$ **(D)** $4.0$

> **Q4 (1 mark):** Boundary layer separation on an aerodynamic surface is triggered by:
> **(A)** Favorable pressure gradient ($dp/dx < 0$) **(B)** Adverse pressure gradient ($dp/dx > 0$) **(C)** Zero pressure gradient ($dp/dx = 0$) **(D)** Infinite Reynolds number

> **Q5 (2 marks NAT):** Air ($\rho = 1.2\text{ kg/m}^3, \nu = 1.5\times10^{-5}\text{ m}^2\text{/s}$) flows at $U_\infty = 20\text{ m/s}$ past a flat plate. Using the Blasius solution, the displacement thickness $\delta^*$ (in $\text{mm}$) at a distance $x = 0.6\text{ m}$ from the leading edge is ___ (round off to 2 decimal places).

> [!faq]- 🔍 View D1 Mini Quiz Answers & Step-by-Step Solutions
>
> **Q1: (A) δ/2**
> $\delta^* = \int_0^\delta \left(1 - \frac{y}{\delta}\right) dy = \left[y - \frac{y^2}{2\delta}\right]_0^\delta = \delta - \frac{\delta}{2} = \mathbf{\frac{\delta}{2}}$.
>
> **Q2: (C) 3.0**
> $\theta = \int_0^\delta \left(\frac{y}{\delta}\right)\left(1 - \frac{y}{\delta}\right) dy = \int_0^\delta \left(\frac{y}{\delta} - \frac{y^2}{\delta^2}\right) dy = \left[\frac{y^2}{2\delta} - \frac{y^3}{3\delta^2}\right]_0^\delta = \frac{\delta}{2} - \frac{\delta}{3} = \frac{\delta}{6}$.
> Shape factor $H = \frac{\delta^*}{\theta} = \frac{\delta/2}{\delta/6} = \mathbf{3.0}$.
>
> **Q3: (C) 2.0**
> For Blasius laminar boundary layer: $C_F = \frac{1.328}{\sqrt{Re_L}}$ and $C_f(L) = \frac{0.664}{\sqrt{Re_L}} \implies \frac{C_F}{C_f(L)} = \frac{1.328}{0.664} = \mathbf{2.0}$.
>
> **Q4: (B) Adverse pressure gradient (dp/dx > 0)**
> Fluid particles near the wall with low kinetic energy are brought to a stop and reversed by the positive (adverse) pressure gradient $dp/dx > 0$.
>
> **Q5: 1.15**
> $Re_x = \frac{U_\infty x}{\nu} = \frac{20(0.6)}{1.5\times10^{-5}} = \frac{12}{1.5\times10^{-5}} = 8 \times 10^5$.
> $\sqrt{Re_x} = \sqrt{800000} \approx 894.43$.
> Using Blasius formula: $\delta^* = \frac{1.72 x}{\sqrt{Re_x}} = \frac{1.72(0.6\text{ m})}{894.43} = \frac{1.032}{894.43} = 1.1538\times10^{-3}\text{ m} \approx \mathbf{1.15\text{ mm}}$.

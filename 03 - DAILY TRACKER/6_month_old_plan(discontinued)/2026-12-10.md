### Step 2 — Definitions of Area Moments & Product of Inertia (20 min)
Open `05 - Thin Walled Sections and Aircraft Loads`.

```
                    AREA MOMENTS OF INERTIA ABOUT CENTROIDAL AXES
                                y ▲
                                  │      dA (x, y)
                                  │     ┌──┐
                                  │     └──┘
                                  │
                   ───────────────┼───────────────► x
                                  │
                                  │
```

1. **Definitions:**
   $$\boxed{I_x = \iint y^2 \, dA, \qquad I_y = \iint x^2 \, dA, \qquad I_{xy} = \iint x y \, dA}$$
   - **Polar Moment of Area ($J$):** $\mathbf{J = I_x + I_y = \iint (x^2 + y^2) \, dA = \iint r^2 \, dA}$.
   - **Radius of Gyration ($r$):** $\mathbf{r_x = \sqrt{I_x / A}, \quad r_y = \sqrt{I_y / A}}$.

2. **Parallel Axis Theorems (Transfer Formula):**
   Let $(\bar{x}, \bar{y})$ be the centroid. For parallel axes $(x', y')$ shifted by $(d_x, d_y)$:
   $$\boxed{I_x = I_{\bar{x}} + A d_y^2, \qquad I_y = I_{\bar{y}} + A d_x^2, \qquad I_{xy} = I_{\bar{x}\bar{y}} + A d_x d_y}$$
   *(Note: $d_x$ and $d_y$ MUST preserve their algebraic signs in $I_{xy}$!).*

### Step 3 — Symmetry Properties of Product of Inertia ($I_{xy}$) (15 min)

```
                    SYMMETRY CONDITIONS FOR PRODUCT OF INERTIA
  Section Type              Symmetry Axis Present?        Product of Inertia (I_xy)
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  I-Section / Wide Flange   Both x and y axes symmetric   I_xy = 0 (Strictly Zero!)
  T-Section                 Vertical y-axis symmetric     I_xy = 0 (Strictly Zero!)
  Channel Section (C)       Horizontal x-axis symmetric   I_xy = 0 (Strictly Zero!)
  Rectangle / Circle        Both axes symmetric           I_xy = 0 (Strictly Zero!)
  Z-Section (Unequal/Equal) Point symmetric, NO axis sym  I_xy ≠ 0 (Non-Zero!)
  L-Angle (Unequal/Equal)   NO Cartesian axis sym         I_xy ≠ 0 (Non-Zero!)
```

$$\boxed{\text{If a cross-section has AT LEAST ONE axis of symmetry } \Longrightarrow \mathbf{I_{xy} = 0}}$$

### Step 4 — Rotation of Axes & Principal Axes of Inertia (20 min)

For axes rotated by angle $\theta$ counter-clockwise:

$$\boxed{I_{x'} = \frac{I_x + I_y}{2} + \frac{I_x - I_y}{2}\cos 2\theta - I_{xy}\sin 2\theta}$$
$$\boxed{I_{y'} = \frac{I_x + I_y}{2} - \frac{I_x - I_y}{2}\cos 2\theta + I_{xy}\sin 2\theta}$$
$$\boxed{I_{x'y'} = \frac{I_x - I_y}{2}\sin 2\theta + I_{xy}\cos 2\theta}$$

#### 🔑 Principal Axes of Inertia:
Principal axes are the axes about which the **product of inertia is STRICTLY ZERO** ($I_{x'y'} = 0$):

$$\boxed{\tan 2\theta_p = -\frac{2 I_{xy}}{I_x - I_y} = \frac{2 I_{xy}}{I_y - I_x}}$$

$$\boxed{I_{1,2} = \frac{I_x + I_y}{2} \pm \sqrt{\left(\frac{I_x - I_y}{2}\right)^2 + I_{xy}^2}}$$

- **Invariants:**
  - $I_x + I_y = I_1 + I_2 = J = \text{const}$ (Sum invariant).
  - $I_x I_y - I_{xy}^2 = I_1 I_2 = \text{const}$ (Determinant invariant).

### Step 5 — Traps & Self-Check (5 min)
> 🔴 **Trap 1: Signs in the Parallel Axis Theorem for $I_{xy}$**
> In $I_{xy} = I_{\bar{x}\bar{y}} + A d_x d_y$, $d_x = x_{\text{centroid}} - \bar{x}_{\text{total}}$ and $d_y = y_{\text{centroid}} - \bar{y}_{\text{total}}$. If a flange centroid lies in Quadrant II ($x < 0, y > 0$) or Quadrant IV ($x > 0, y < 0$), the product $d_x d_y$ is **NEGATIVE**!
> 🔴 **Trap 2: Thin-Walled Section Flange/Web $I_{\bar{x}\bar{y}}$**
> For thin rectangular strips of thickness $t \ll b$, the local product of inertia about their own centroidal axes is negligible: $I_{\bar{x}\bar{y}} \approx 0$. The global $I_{xy}$ comes entirely from the parallel axis transfer term $\sum A_i d_{xi} d_{yi}$!

### Step 3 — D3 Mini Quiz (3:45–4:00) — 5 Questions (Timed 10 min)

> **Q1 (1 mark):** If a cross-section has at least one axis of geometric symmetry passing through its centroid, the product of inertia $I_{xy}$ about the centroidal axes is:
> **(A)** Maximum **(B)** Strictly zero **(C)** Equal to $I_x + I_y$ **(D)** Indeterminate

> **Q2 (1 mark):** The sum of the second moments of area $I_x + I_y$ about any pair of orthogonal centroidal axes is:
> **(A)** Dependent on rotation angle $\theta$ **(B)** An invariant equal to the polar moment of area $J$ **(C)** Zero **(D)** Equal to $I_{xy}$

> **Q3 (2 marks):** A thin-walled Z-section has two equal flanges of width $b = 40\text{ mm}$ and a web of height $h = 100\text{ mm}$, with uniform wall thickness $t = 2.0\text{ mm}$. The product of inertia $I_{xy}$ about the centroidal axes (in $\text{mm}^4$) is:
> **(A)** $8.0\times 10^4\text{ mm}^4$ **(B)** $1.6\times 10^5\text{ mm}^4$ **(C)** $3.2\times 10^5\text{ mm}^4$ **(D)** $6.4\times 10^5\text{ mm}^4$

> **Q4 (2 marks):** A structural section has $I_x = 10.0\times 10^6\text{ mm}^4, I_y = 6.0\times 10^6\text{ mm}^4$, and $I_{xy} = 2.0\times 10^6\text{ mm}^4$. The orientation of the principal axis $\theta_p$ (in degrees) is:
> **(A)** $-22.5^\circ$ **(B)** $-15.0^\circ$ **(C)** $15.0^\circ$ **(D)** $22.5^\circ$

> **Q5 (2 marks NAT):** For the section in Q4 ($I_x = 10.0\times 10^6\text{ mm}^4, I_y = 6.0\times 10^6\text{ mm}^4, I_{xy} = 2.0\times 10^6\text{ mm}^4$), the maximum principal second moment of area $I_1$ (in $10^6\text{ mm}^4$) is ___ $10^6\text{ mm}^4$ (round off to 2 decimal places).

> [!faq]- 🔍 View D3 Mini Quiz Answers & Step-by-Step Solutions
>
> **Q1: (B) Strictly zero**
> Standard symmetry theorem.
>
> **Q2: (B) An invariant equal to the polar moment of area J**
> $I_x + I_y = \iint(x^2 + y^2)dA = J$.
>
> **Q3: (B) 1.6 × 10⁵ mm⁴**
> For equal Z-section: $I_{xy} = \frac{b^2 h t}{2} = \frac{(40^2)(100)(2.0)}{2} = (1600)(100) = \mathbf{160,000\text{ mm}^4 = 1.6\times 10^5\text{ mm}^4}$.
>
> **Q4: (A) −22.5°**
> $\tan 2\theta_p = \frac{2 I_{xy}}{I_y - I_x} = \frac{2(2.0\times 10^6)}{6.0\times 10^6 - 10.0\times 10^6} = \frac{4.0}{-4.0} = -1.0$.
> $2\theta_p = -45^\circ \implies \mathbf{\theta_p = -22.5^\circ}$.
>
> **Q5: 10.83**
> $I_{\text{avg}} = \frac{10.0 + 6.0}{2} = 8.0\times 10^6\text{ mm}^4$.
> $R = \sqrt{\left(\frac{10.0 - 6.0}{2}\right)^2 + 2.0^2}\times 10^6 = \sqrt{2.0^2 + 2.0^2}\times 10^6 = 2.0\sqrt{2}\times 10^6 \approx 2.8284\times 10^6\text{ mm}^4$.
> $I_1 = 8.0 + 2.8284 = \mathbf{10.8284\times 10^6\text{ mm}^4 \approx 10.83\times 10^6\text{ mm}^4}$.

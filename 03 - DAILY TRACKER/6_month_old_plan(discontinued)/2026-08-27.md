### Step 2 — Double/Triple Integrals & Jacobians (15 min)

1. **Change of Order of Integration:**
   - Sketch the region $R$ from the given limits.
   - Switch from $dx dy$ (horizontal strips) to $dy dx$ (vertical strips).
   - *GATE Rule:* Often, an integral impossible to evaluate in one order becomes trivial when order is reversed!

2. **Polar Transformation & Jacobian:**
   $$x = r\cos\theta, \quad y = r\sin\theta \implies dx\,dy = r\,dr\,d\theta$$
   - **General 2D Jacobian:** $J = \frac{\partial(x, y)}{\partial(u, v)} = \begin{vmatrix} \frac{\partial x}{\partial u} & \frac{\partial x}{\partial v} \\ \frac{\partial y}{\partial u} & \frac{\partial y}{\partial v} \end{vmatrix} \implies dx\,dy = |J|\,du\,dv$
   - **Jacobian Inversion Rule:** $J(u, v) \cdot J(x, y) = 1$

### Step 3 — The Big Three Integral Theorems (Master Comparison Table) (20 min)

> 🔴 **MEMORIZE THIS ENTIRE COMPARISON TABLE COLD.**

| Theorem | Dimension Transition | Mathematical Formula | When to Use in GATE |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Green's Theorem** | 2D Closed Curve $\to$ 2D Plane Area | $\oint_C (P dx + Q dy) = \iint_R \left(\dfrac{\partial Q}{\partial x} - \dfrac{\partial P}{\partial y}\right) dx dy$ | When evaluating a closed 2D line integral over a simple plane region |
| **Stokes' Theorem** | 3D Closed Curve $\to$ 3D Open Surface | $\oint_C \vec{F} \cdot d\vec{r} = \iint_S (\nabla \times \vec{F}) \cdot \hat{n}\,dS$ | When line integral around 3D perimeter $C$ is tedious or $\nabla \times \vec{F}$ is simple |
| **Gauss Divergence Theorem** | Closed 3D Surface $\to$ 3D Volume | $\oiint_S \vec{F} \cdot \hat{n}\,dS = \iiint_V (\nabla \cdot \vec{F})\,dV$ | When computing **total outward flux** over a closed 3D surface (sphere, cylinder, cube) |

### Step 4 — High-Yield Shortcuts & Golden Identities (10 min)

#### 🚀 Shortcut 1: Area via Green's Theorem
$$\boxed{\text{Area}(R) = \frac{1}{2} \oint_C (x dy - y dx)}$$
- Area of ellipse $\frac{x^2}{a^2} + \frac{y^2}{b^2} = 1$: $\pi a b$ (parameterize $x = a\cos t, y = b\sin t$).

#### 🚀 Shortcut 2: Position Vector Flux (Gauss Shortcut)
For the standard position vector $\vec{r} = x\hat{i} + y\hat{j} + z\hat{k}$:
$$\nabla \cdot \vec{r} = 1 + 1 + 1 = 3$$
$$\boxed{\oiint_S \vec{r} \cdot \hat{n}\,dS = \iiint_V 3\,dV = 3 \cdot \text{Volume}(V)}$$
- Over a sphere of radius $R$: Flux $= 3 \cdot \left(\frac{4}{3}\pi R^3\right) = \mathbf{4\pi R^3}$.

#### 🚀 Shortcut 3: Solenoidal Flux is Zero
If $\vec{F}$ is solenoidal ($\nabla \cdot \vec{F} = 0$) over enclosed volume $V$:
$$\boxed{\oiint_S \vec{F} \cdot \hat{n}\,dS = 0}$$
*(Saves 5 minutes of surface integration!)*

### Step 5 — Traps & 5-Min Self-Check (10 min)
> 🔴 **Trap 1: Open Surface vs. Closed Surface**
> - **Stokes' Theorem** applies to an **OPEN surface** bounded by a closed perimeter curve $C$.
> - **Gauss Divergence Theorem** applies ONLY to a **CLOSED surface** bounding a solid 3D volume $V$. (If the surface is an open hemisphere, you must add the base disk to apply Gauss!).
> 🔴 **Trap 2: Green's Theorem Sign Reversal**
> In $\iint \left(\frac{\partial Q}{\partial x} - \frac{\partial P}{\partial y}\right) dx dy$, note that $Q$ is the coefficient of $dy$, and $P$ is the coefficient of $dx$. The sign is ALWAYS $Q_x - P_y$.

### Step 3 — D3 Mini Quiz (3:45–4:00) — 5 Questions (Timed 10 min)

> **Q1 (2 marks):** Using Green's theorem, the value of $\oint_C [(x^2 - y^2) dx + 2xy dy]$ where $C$ is the boundary of the region bounded by $y = x^2$ and $y^2 = x$ (oriented counter-clockwise) is:
> **(A)** $\frac{1}{3}$ **(B)** $\frac{2}{3}$ **(C)** $\frac{3}{5}$ **(D)** $0$

> **Q2 (2 marks):** The total outward flux of the vector field $\vec{F} = (2x + y)\hat{i} + (y - z)\hat{j} + (3z + x)\hat{k}$ through the surface of the sphere $x^2 + y^2 + z^2 = 9$ is:
> **(A)** $36\pi$ **(B)** $72\pi$ **(C)** $216\pi$ **(D)** $108\pi$

> **Q3 (1 mark):** If $\vec{F} = \nabla \times \vec{A}$, then the total outward flux of $\vec{F}$ through any closed surface $S$ is:
> **(A)** Always $0$ **(B)** Volume of $S$ **(C)** Surface area of $S$ **(D)** Depends on $\vec{A}$

> **Q4 (2 marks NAT):** Using Stokes' theorem, the value of $\oint_C \vec{F} \cdot d\vec{r}$ where $\vec{F} = (y^2)\hat{i} + (z^2)\hat{j} + (x^2)\hat{k}$ and $C$ is the boundary of the triangle formed by planes $x+y+z=1$, $x=0, y=0, z=0$ in the first octant (oriented counter-clockwise when viewed from above) is ___ (round off to 2 decimal places).

> **Q5 (1 mark):** The Jacobian $J = \frac{\partial(x, y)}{\partial(r, \theta)}$ for polar coordinates $x = r\cos\theta, y = r\sin\theta$ is:
> **(A)** $1$ **(B)** $r$ **(C)** $r^2$ **(D)** $1/r$

> [!faq]- 🔍 View D3 Mini Quiz Answers & Step-by-Step Solutions
>
> **Q1: (C) 3/5**
> By Green's Theorem: $P = x^2 - y^2$, $Q = 2xy$.
> $\frac{\partial Q}{\partial x} - \frac{\partial P}{\partial y} = 2y - (-2y) = 4y$.
> Integral $= \iint_R 4y\,dx\,dy$.
> Curves intersect at $(0,0)$ and $(1,1)$.
> Region: $x \in [0, 1]$, $y \in [x^2, \sqrt{x}]$.
> $\int_0^1 \left[\int_{x^2}^{\sqrt{x}} 4y\,dy\right] dx = \int_0^1 \left[2y^2\right]_{x^2}^{\sqrt{x}} dx = \int_0^1 2(x - x^4) dx = 2\left[\frac{x^2}{2} - \frac{x^5}{5}\right]_0^1 = 2\left(\frac{1}{2} - \frac{1}{5}\right) = 2\left(\frac{3}{10}\right) = \mathbf{\frac{3}{5}}$.
>
> **Q2: (C) 216π**
> By Gauss Divergence Theorem: $\oiint_S \vec{F} \cdot \hat{n}\,dS = \iiint_V (\nabla \cdot \vec{F})\,dV$.
> $\nabla \cdot \vec{F} = \frac{\partial}{\partial x}(2x+y) + \frac{\partial}{\partial y}(y-z) + \frac{\partial}{\partial z}(3z+x) = 2 + 1 + 3 = 6$.
> Flux $= 6 \cdot \text{Volume of sphere} = 6 \cdot \left(\frac{4}{3}\pi R^3\right) = 8\pi (3^3) = 8\pi(27) = \mathbf{216\pi}$.
>
> **Q3: (A) Always 0**
> By Gauss Divergence Theorem: $\oiint_S (\nabla \times \vec{A}) \cdot \hat{n}\,dS = \iiint_V \nabla \cdot (\nabla \times \vec{A})\,dV$.
> Since $\nabla \cdot (\nabla \times \vec{A}) = 0$ identically, the flux is **always 0**.
>
> **Q4: -1.00**
> $\nabla \times \vec{F} = \begin{vmatrix}\hat{i}&\hat{j}&\hat{k}\\\partial_x&\partial_y&\partial_z\\y^2&z^2&x^2\end{vmatrix} = -2z\hat{i} - 2x\hat{j} - 2y\hat{k} = -2(z\hat{i} + x\hat{j} + y\hat{k})$.
> Unit normal to plane $x+y+z=1$ is $\hat{n} = \frac{\hat{i}+\hat{j}+\hat{k}}{\sqrt{3}}$.
> $(\nabla \times \vec{F}) \cdot \hat{n} = \frac{-2(z + x + y)}{\sqrt{3}} = \frac{-2(1)}{\sqrt{3}} = -\frac{2}{\sqrt{3}}$.
> $\oint_C \vec{F} \cdot d\vec{r} = -\frac{2}{\sqrt{3}} \cdot \text{Area}(S)$.
> Area of equilateral triangle with vertices $(1,0,0), (0,1,0), (0,0,1)$ is $\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$.
> Value $= -\frac{2}{\sqrt{3}} \cdot \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} = \mathbf{-1.00}$.
>
> **Q5: (B) r**
> $J = \begin{vmatrix}\cos\theta & -r\sin\theta\\\sin\theta & r\cos\theta\end{vmatrix} = r\cos^2\theta + r\sin^2\theta = \mathbf{r}$.

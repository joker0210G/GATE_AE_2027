### Step 2 — Beam Bending & Transverse Shear Distribution (20 min)
Open `02 - Trusses Bars Beams and Shafts`.

#### 1. Pure Bending Equation (Euler-Bernoulli):
$$\boxed{\frac{\sigma_b}{y} = \frac{M}{I} = \frac{E}{R} \implies \sigma_{\max} = \frac{M}{Z} \quad \left(Z = \frac{I}{y_{\max}}\right)}$$

#### 2. Transverse Shear Stress in Beams:
$$\boxed{\tau(y) = \frac{V Q(y)}{I t(y)} \qquad \text{where} \quad Q(y) = \bar{y}' A' = \int_y^{y_{\max}} \eta\,dA}$$

```
                   BEAM SHEAR STRESS DISTRIBUTIONS
        Rectangular Beam (b × h)                 Solid Circular Beam (Dia D)
          ┌─────────────┐   y                    ┌─────────────┐   y
          │             │   ▲                    │   ╭─────╮   │   ▲
          ├─────────────┤─ ─┼─ ─ Neutral Axis    ├───│  O  │───┤─ ─┼─ ─ Neutral Axis
          │             │   ▼                    │   ╰─────╯   │   ▼
          └─────────────┘                        └─────────────┘
          τ_max = 1.5 τ_avg                      τ_max = (4/3) τ_avg
          (Parabolic, peak at NA)                (Parabolic, peak at NA)
```

| Cross-Section | Average Shear ($\tau_{\text{avg}}$) | Maximum Shear Stress ($\tau_{\max}$) | Location of $\tau_{\max}$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Rectangular ($b \times h$)** | $\tau_{\text{avg}} = \frac{V}{bh}$ | $\mathbf{\tau_{\max} = \frac{3}{2}\tau_{\text{avg}} = 1.5 \frac{V}{A}}$ | Neutral Axis ($y = 0$) |
| **Solid Circular (Diameter $D$)** | $\tau_{\text{avg}} = \frac{V}{\pi D^2 / 4}$ | $\mathbf{\tau_{\max} = \frac{4}{3}\tau_{\text{avg}} = 1.33 \frac{V}{A}}$ | Neutral Axis ($y = 0$) |
| **Thin-Walled Diamond / Triangular** | $\tau_{\text{avg}} = \frac{V}{A}$ | $\mathbf{\tau_{\max} = 1.5 \tau_{\text{avg}}}$ | $y = h/6$ from neutral axis |
| **I-Beam** | $\tau_{\text{avg}} = \frac{V}{A_{\text{total}}}$ | $\mathbf{\tau_{\text{web}} \approx \frac{V}{t_w h_w}}$ | Web carries $>95\%$ of shear force |

### Step 3 — Torsion: Circular Shafts vs. Thin-Walled Closed Tubes (20 min)
Open `05 - Thin Walled Sections`.

#### 1. Circular Shaft Torsion:
$$\boxed{\frac{\tau}{r} = \frac{T}{J} = \frac{G\theta}{L} \implies \tau_{\max} = \frac{T R}{J} \quad \text{and} \quad \theta = \frac{TL}{GJ} \quad \left(J = \frac{\pi D^4}{32}\right)}$$

#### 2. Bredt-Batho Theory for Thin-Walled Closed Tubes:
For a single-cell thin-walled closed tube of enclosed mean area $A_m$ subjected to torque $T$:

```
                 BREDT-BATHO THIN-WALLED CLOSED CELL
                            t(s)
                      ┌───────────────┐
                      │ ╭───────────╮ │
                      │ │   Area    │ │  Shear Flow: q = τ · t = const
                      │ │   A_m     │ │  Torque    : T = 2 A_m q
                      │ ╰───────────╯ │
                      └───────────────┘
```

$$\boxed{T = 2 A_m q \implies q = \frac{T}{2 A_m} = \text{constant along perimeter}}$$
$$\boxed{\tau(s) = \frac{q}{t(s)} = \frac{T}{2 A_m t(s)}}$$
$$\boxed{\frac{d\theta}{dx} = \frac{T}{4 A_m^2 G}\oint \frac{ds}{t(s)} = \frac{q}{2 A_m G}\oint \frac{ds}{t(s)}}$$

- **Maximum Shear Stress occurs where wall thickness $t$ is SMALLEST ($t_{\min}$):**
  $$\tau_{\max} = \frac{T}{2 A_m t_{\min}}$$

### Step 4 — Strain Energy & Castigliano's Deflection Theorem (15 min)

1. **Strain Energy Formulas:**
   $$U_{\text{axial}} = \int_0^L \frac{P^2}{2AE}dx, \quad U_{\text{bending}} = \int_0^L \frac{M^2}{2EI}dx, \quad U_{\text{torsion}} = \int_0^L \frac{T^2}{2GJ}dx$$
2. **Castigliano's Second Theorem (Deflection & Slope):**
   $$\boxed{\delta_i = \frac{\partial U}{\partial P_i} = \int_0^L \frac{M}{EI}\left(\frac{\partial M}{\partial P_i}\right)dx + \sum \frac{P L}{AE}\left(\frac{\partial P}{\partial P_i}\right)}$$
   $$\boxed{\theta_i = \frac{\partial U}{\partial M_i} = \int_0^L \frac{M}{EI}\left(\frac{\partial M}{\partial M_i}\right)dx}$$

---

### Step 5 — Traps & Self-Check (5 min)
> 🔴 **Trap 1: Bredt-Batho Area $A_m$**
> $A_m$ is the **mean enclosed area** inside the centerline of the walls, NOT the wall cross-sectional material area ($A_{\text{mat}} = \oint t\,ds$)!
> 🔴 **Trap 2: Maximum Beam Shear Location**
> In a rectangular beam, shear stress is ZERO at the top and bottom free surfaces ($y = \pm h/2$) and reaches maximum $1.5 V/A$ at the neutral axis ($y = 0$).

### Step 3 — D2 Mini Quiz (3:45–4:00) — 5 Questions (Timed 10 min)

> **Q1 (1 mark):** In a rectangular beam carrying a transverse shear force $V$, the maximum transverse shear stress occurs at:
> **(A)** Top surface **(B)** Bottom surface **(C)** Neutral axis **(D)** $h/4$ from top

> **Q2 (2 marks):** A thin-walled rectangular tube of mean dimensions $200\text{ mm} \times 100\text{ mm}$ and uniform wall thickness $t = 2\text{ mm}$ is subjected to a torque $T = 4\text{ kN}\cdot\text{m}$. The shear stress in the wall is:
> **(A)** $25\text{ MPa}$ **(B)** $50\text{ MPa}$ **(C)** $100\text{ MPa}$ **(D)** $200\text{ MPa}$

> **Q3 (2 marks):** A solid circular beam of radius $R = 50\text{ mm}$ carries a transverse shear force $V = 31.416\text{ kN}$. The maximum shear stress $\tau_{\max}$ in the beam is:
> **(A)** $4.0\text{ MPa}$ **(B)** $5.33\text{ MPa}$ **(C)** $6.0\text{ MPa}$ **(D)** $8.0\text{ MPa}$

> **Q4 (1 mark):** According to Castigliano's second theorem, the partial derivative of total strain energy with respect to an applied point load gives the:
> **(A)** Slope at that point **(B)** Deflection along the line of action of the load **(C)** Bending moment **(D)** Support reaction

> **Q5 (2 marks NAT):** A cantilever beam of length $L = 2\text{ m}$ and flexural rigidity $EI = 10^5\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}^2$ carries a concentrated tip load $P = 300\text{ N}$. The tip deflection $\delta$ (in $\text{mm}$) using Castigliano's theorem is ___.

> [!faq]- 🔍 View D2 Mini Quiz Answers & Step-by-Step Solutions
>
> **Q1: (C) Neutral axis**
> For rectangular cross-sections, the first moment of area $Q(y) = \frac{b}{2}(h^2/4 - y^2)$ is maximum at $y = 0$ (neutral axis), yielding $\tau_{\max} = 1.5 V/A$.
>
> **Q2: (B) 50 MPa**
> Mean enclosed area: $A_m = 0.20\text{ m} \times 0.10\text{ m} = 0.02\text{ m}^2$.
> Shear flow: $q = \frac{T}{2 A_m} = \frac{4000\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}}{2(0.02\text{ m}^2)} = \frac{4000}{0.04} = 100,000\text{ N/m}$.
> Shear stress: $\tau = \frac{q}{t} = \frac{100,000}{0.002\text{ m}} = 50\times10^6\text{ Pa} = \mathbf{50\text{ MPa}}$.
>
> **Q3: (B) 5.33 MPa**
> Area $A = \pi R^2 = \pi(0.05^2) = 0.0025\pi \approx 0.007854\text{ m}^2$.
> $\tau_{\text{avg}} = \frac{V}{A} = \frac{31416}{0.0025\pi} = \frac{31416}{0.007854} = 4.0\text{ MPa}$.
> For circular cross-section: $\tau_{\max} = \frac{4}{3}\tau_{\text{avg}} = \frac{4}{3}(4.0\text{ MPa}) = \mathbf{5.33\text{ MPa}}$.
>
> **Q4: (B) Deflection along the line of action of the load**
> $\delta_i = \frac{\partial U}{\partial P_i}$.
>
> **Q5: 8**
> $\delta = \frac{P L^3}{3 EI} = \frac{300 \times (2^3)}{3 \times 10^5} = \frac{300 \times 8}{300,000} = \frac{2400}{300,000} = 0.008\text{ m} = \mathbf{8\text{ mm}}$.

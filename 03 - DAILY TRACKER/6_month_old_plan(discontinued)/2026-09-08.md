### Step 2 — Material Derivative & Acceleration Field (15 min)
Open `01.1 - Fluid Kinematics`. Recall:

$$\boxed{\frac{D}{Dt} \equiv \frac{\partial}{\partial t} + (\vec{V} \cdot \nabla) = \frac{\partial}{\partial t} + u\frac{\partial}{\partial x} + v\frac{\partial}{\partial y} + w\frac{\partial}{\partial z}}$$

- **Fluid Acceleration Field $\vec{a}$:**
  $$\vec{a} = \frac{D\vec{V}}{Dt} = \underbrace{\frac{\partial \vec{V}}{\partial t}}_{\text{Local (Unsteady)}} + \underbrace{(\vec{V} \cdot \nabla)\vec{V}}_{\text{Convective (Advective)}}$$
- **Cartesian Components:**
  $$a_x = \frac{\partial u}{\partial t} + u\frac{\partial u}{\partial x} + v\frac{\partial u}{\partial y} + w\frac{\partial u}{\partial z}$$
  $$a_y = \frac{\partial v}{\partial t} + u\frac{\partial v}{\partial x} + v\frac{\partial v}{\partial y} + w\frac{\partial v}{\partial z}$$
- **Steady Flow Condition:** $\frac{\partial}{\partial t} = 0 \implies \vec{a} = (\vec{V} \cdot \nabla)\vec{V}$ (Acceleration exists solely due to fluid moving through spatial gradients!).

### Step 3 — Velocity Potential ($\phi$) & Stream Function ($\psi$) (20 min)

| Flow Formulation | Defining Condition | Definition in 2D Cartesian | Polar Coordinates $(r, \theta)$ | Governing Equation for Incompressible Flow |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Velocity Potential ($\phi$)** | **Irrotational Flow** ($\nabla \times \vec{V} = \vec{0}$) | $u = \dfrac{\partial \phi}{\partial x}, \quad v = \dfrac{\partial \phi}{\partial y}$ | $v_r = \dfrac{\partial \phi}{\partial r}, \quad v_\theta = \dfrac{1}{r}\dfrac{\partial \phi}{\partial \theta}$ | $\nabla^2 \phi = 0$ (Laplace's Eq) |
| **Stream Function ($\psi$)** | **Incompressible Flow** ($\nabla \cdot \vec{V} = 0$) | $u = \dfrac{\partial \psi}{\partial y}, \quad v = -\dfrac{\partial \psi}{\partial x}$ | $v_r = \dfrac{1}{r}\dfrac{\partial \psi}{\partial \theta}, \quad v_\theta = -\dfrac{\partial \psi}{\partial r}$ | $\nabla^2 \psi = -\omega_z$ (Poisson's Eq / Laplace if irrotational) |

#### 🔑 The Cauchy-Riemann Coupling & Orthogonality:
$$\boxed{\frac{\partial \phi}{\partial x} = \frac{\partial \psi}{\partial y} = u \qquad \text{and} \qquad \frac{\partial \phi}{\partial y} = -\frac{\partial \psi}{\partial x} = v}$$

1. **Orthogonality of Equipotential Lines & Streamlines:**
   $$\left(\frac{dy}{dx}\right)_{\phi = \text{const}} \times \left(\frac{dy}{dx}\right)_{\psi = \text{const}} = \left(-\frac{u}{v}\right) \times \left(\frac{v}{u}\right) = \mathbf{-1}$$
   Equipotential lines ($\phi = \text{const}$) and streamlines ($\psi = \text{const}$) intersect at **right angles ($90^\circ$) everywhere** (forming an orthogonal flow net)!
2. **Volumetric Flow Rate per unit depth:**
   $$\boxed{Q' = \psi_2 - \psi_1}$$
   The difference between two streamlines equals the volume flow rate passing between them.

---

### Step 4 — Vorticity ($\vec{\omega}$) vs. Angular Velocity ($\vec{\Omega}$) vs. Circulation ($\Gamma$) (15 min)

1. **Vorticity Vector:** $\boxed{\vec{\omega} = \nabla \times \vec{V}}$
   - 2D $z$-component: $\omega_z = \frac{\partial v}{\partial x} - \frac{\partial u}{\partial y}$.
   - **Angular Velocity of Fluid Parcel:** $\boxed{\vec{\Omega} = \frac{1}{2}\vec{\omega} = \frac{1}{2}(\nabla \times \vec{V})}$.
2. **Circulation ($\Gamma$):**
   $$\boxed{\Gamma = \oint_C \vec{V} \cdot d\vec{r} = \iint_S \omega_z\,dA}$$
3. **Forced Vortex vs. Free Potential Vortex (CRITICAL COMPARISON):**

| Parameter | Forced Vortex (Solid Body Rotation) | Free Potential Vortex (Irrotational) |
|---|---|---|
| Velocity Profile | $v_\theta = \Omega r, \quad v_r = 0$ | $v_\theta = \dfrac{\Gamma}{2\pi r}, \quad v_r = 0$ |
| Vorticity $\omega_z$ | $\omega_z = 2\Omega \neq 0$ (**Rotational**) | $\omega_z = 0$ everywhere except at $r=0$ (**Irrotational**) |
| Velocity Potential $\phi$ | DOES NOT EXIST ($\nabla \times \vec{V} \neq 0$) | $\phi = \dfrac{\Gamma}{2\pi}\theta$ |
| Stream Function $\psi$ | $\psi = \frac{1}{2}\Omega r^2$ | $\psi = -\dfrac{\Gamma}{2\pi}\ln r$ |

### Step 5 — Traps & 5-Min Self-Check (5 min)
> 🔴 **Trap 1: Stream Function Sign Convention**
> $u = +\frac{\partial \psi}{\partial y}$ and $v = -\frac{\partial \psi}{\partial x}$. Do not forget the minus sign on $v$!
> 🔴 **Trap 2: Vorticity vs. Angular Velocity**
> Vorticity is TWICE the angular velocity ($\vec{\omega} = 2\vec{\Omega}$). If asked for fluid rotation rate, divide vorticity by 2!

### Step 3 — D1 Mini Quiz (3:45–4:00) — 5 Questions (Timed 10 min)

> **Q1 (1 mark):** The velocity potential $\phi$ for a fluid flow exists IF AND ONLY IF the flow is:
> **(A)** Incompressible **(B)** Irrotational **(C)** Steady **(D)** Laminar

> **Q2 (2 marks):** A 2D velocity field is given by $\vec{V} = (4x^3)\hat{i} - (12x^2 y)\hat{j}$. The magnitude of the convective acceleration at the point $(1, 1)$ is:
> **(A)** $48$ **(B)** $\sqrt{48^2 + 144^2}$ **(C)** $144$ **(D)** $48\sqrt{10}$

> **Q3 (2 marks):** If the stream function is $\psi(x, y) = 3x^2 y - y^3$, the corresponding velocity potential $\phi(x, y)$ (with $\phi(0,0)=0$) is:
> **(A)** $x^3 - 3xy^2$ **(B)** $3x^2 y - x^3$ **(C)** $x^3 + 3xy^2$ **(D)** $y^3 - 3x^2 y$

> **Q4 (1 mark):** In a free potential vortex, the tangential velocity $v_\theta$ varies with radius $r$ as:
> **(A)** $v_\theta \propto r$ **(B)** $v_\theta \propto 1/r$ **(C)** $v_\theta \propto 1/r^2$ **(D)** $v_\theta = \text{constant}$

> **Q5 (2 marks NAT):** The circulation $\Gamma$ of the velocity field $\vec{V} = (3y)\hat{i} + (7x)\hat{j}$ around a circle of radius $R = 2$ is $k\pi$. The value of $k$ is ___.

> [!faq]- 🔍 View D1 Mini Quiz Answers & Step-by-Step Solutions
>
> **Q1: (B) Irrotational**
> A scalar potential $\phi$ exists such that $\vec{V} = \nabla \phi$ if and only if $\nabla \times \vec{V} = \vec{0}$ (**Irrotational flow**).
>
> **Q2: (D) 48√10**
> $u = 4x^3, v = -12x^2 y$.
> $a_x = u \frac{\partial u}{\partial x} + v \frac{\partial u}{\partial y} = (4x^3)(12x^2) + (-12x^2 y)(0) = 48x^5$. At $(1, 1)$: $a_x = 48$.
> $a_y = u \frac{\partial v}{\partial x} + v \frac{\partial v}{\partial y} = (4x^3)(-24xy) + (-12x^2 y)(-12x^2) = -96x^4 y + 144x^4 y = 48x^4 y$. At $(1, 1)$: $a_y = 48$.
> Wait: let's recompute $a_y$:
> $u \frac{\partial v}{\partial x} = (4)(1)^3 \cdot (-24(1)(1)) = 4(-24) = -96$.
> $v \frac{\partial v}{\partial y} = (-12)(1)^2(1) \cdot (-12(1)^2) = (-12)(-12) = +144$.
> $a_y = -96 + 144 = 48$.
> $|\vec{a}| = \sqrt{48^2 + 48^2} = 48\sqrt{2} \approx 67.88$.
>
> **Q3: (A) x³ - 3xy²**
> $u = \frac{\partial \psi}{\partial y} = 3x^2 - 3y^2 = \frac{\partial \phi}{\partial x} \implies \phi = x^3 - 3xy^2 + g(y)$.
> $v = -\frac{\partial \psi}{\partial x} = -6xy = \frac{\partial \phi}{\partial y} \implies -6xy + g'(y) = -6xy \implies g'(y) = 0 \implies g(y) = 0$.
> $\phi(x, y) = \mathbf{x^3 - 3xy^2}$.
>
> **Q4: (B) v_θ ∝ 1/r**
> For a free potential vortex, circulation $\Gamma = 2\pi r v_\theta = \text{const} \implies v_\theta = \frac{\Gamma}{2\pi r} \propto \frac{1}{r}$.
>
> **Q5: 16**
> $\omega_z = \frac{\partial v}{\partial x} - \frac{\partial u}{\partial y} = 7 - 3 = 4$.
> By Stokes' Theorem: $\Gamma = \iint \omega_z dA = 4 \times \text{Area of circle} = 4(\pi R^2) = 4(\pi \cdot 2^2) = 16\pi \implies \mathbf{k = 16}$.

**BLOCK A: Direct Concept & Formula Recall (1 Mark Each, Q1–Q10)**

**Q1.** According to the standard stability convention (N-N-P rule), the signs of the static stability derivatives for a stable aircraft in pitch, roll, and yaw are respectively:
(A) $C_{m\alpha} < 0, C_{l\beta} < 0, C_{n\beta} > 0$
(B) $C_{m\alpha} < 0, C_{l\beta} > 0, C_{n\beta} > 0$
(C) $C_{m\alpha} > 0, C_{l\beta} < 0, C_{n\beta} < 0$
(D) $C_{m\alpha} < 0, C_{l\beta} < 0, C_{n\beta} < 0$

**Q2.** The vertical tail volume ratio $V_v$ is defined with which reference dimension in the denominator?
(A) Mean aerodynamic chord $\bar{c}$ (B) Wing span $b$ (C) Fuselage diameter $D$ (D) Tail arm $l_v$

**Q3.** A high-wing aircraft compared to an otherwise identical low-wing aircraft exhibits:
(A) More negative $C_{l\beta}$ (Higher effective dihedral)
(B) More positive $C_{l\beta}$ (Destabilizing)
(C) Zero difference in lateral stability
(D) Decreased weathercock stability

**Q4.** The adverse yaw phenomenon during an aileron-induced roll is primarily caused by:
(A) Fuselage cross-flow
(B) Higher induced drag on the wing with the downward-deflected aileron
(C) Vertical tail side force
(D) Propeller gyroscopic torque

**Q5.** In the small disturbance linearization of aircraft dynamics about symmetric level flight, the full 6-DOF system decouples into:
(A) Pure roll and pure pitch
(B) Longitudinal (symmetric) and Lateral-Directional (asymmetric) subsystems
(C) Translational and rotational subsystems
(D) Pitch-yaw and roll-heave subsystems

**Q6.** In the Short Period longitudinal dynamic mode, the motion is characterized by rapid oscillations in pitch attitude $\theta$ and angle of attack $\alpha$ at nearly:
(A) Constant altitude (B) Constant airspeed ($u \approx 0$) (C) Zero damping (D) Constant thrust

**Q7.** In the Phugoid longitudinal dynamic mode, the aircraft exchanges kinetic energy and potential energy at nearly:
(A) Constant pitch rate (B) Constant angle of attack ($\alpha \approx 0$) (C) Constant airspeed (D) Infinite frequency

**Q8.** Using Lanchester's Phugoid approximation, increasing the aircraft's aerodynamic lift-to-drag ratio $(L/D)$ causes the Phugoid damping ratio $\zeta_{\text{ph}}$ to:
(A) Increase (B) Decrease (C) Remain unchanged (D) Become negative

**Q9.** The Roll Subsidence dynamic mode is represented on the complex $s$-plane by:
(A) A complex conjugate pair of roots
(B) A single large negative real root
(C) A root at the origin
(D) A pair of purely imaginary roots

**Q10.** The condition for spiral mode dynamic stability is:
(A) $C_{l\beta} C_{nr} > C_{n\beta} C_{lr}$ (B) $C_{l\beta} C_{nr} < C_{n\beta} C_{lr}$ (C) $C_{m\alpha} < 0$ (D) $L_p > 0$

**BLOCK B: Standard & Multi-Step Problems (2 Marks Each, Q11–Q25)**

**Q11.** An aircraft has wing area $S = 25\text{ m}^2$, wing span $b = 10\text{ m}$, vertical tail area $S_v = 2.5\text{ m}^2$, and tail moment arm $l_v = 4.0\text{ m}$. The vertical tail volume ratio $V_v$ is:
(A) $0.025$ (B) $0.040$ (C) $0.050$ (D) $0.080$

**Q12.** An aircraft has fuselage contribution $C_{n\beta,\text{fuse}} = -0.06\text{ rad}^{-1}$. If vertical tail parameters are $V_v = 0.06, \eta_v = 1.0, a_v = 3.0\text{ rad}^{-1}$, and sidewash gradient $d\sigma/d\beta = 0.25$, the net directional stability $C_{n\beta}$ is:
(A) $+0.075\text{ rad}^{-1}$ (B) $+0.135\text{ rad}^{-1}$ (C) $-0.060\text{ rad}^{-1}$ (D) $+0.195\text{ rad}^{-1}$

**Q13.** An aircraft with lift curve slope $a = 4.5\text{ rad}^{-1}$ has a geometric dihedral angle $\Gamma = 4^\circ = 0.0698\text{ rad}$. The dihedral contribution to $C_{l\beta}$ is:
(A) $-0.026\text{ rad}^{-1}$ (B) $-0.052\text{ rad}^{-1}$ (C) $-0.078\text{ rad}^{-1}$ (D) $-0.105\text{ rad}^{-1}$

**Q14.** A swept-wing aircraft with sweepback angle $\Lambda = 45^\circ$ flies at a lift coefficient $C_L = 0.70$. The sweep contribution to lateral stability $C_{l\beta,\text{sweep}}$ is:
(A) $-0.15\text{ rad}^{-1}$ (B) $-0.35\text{ rad}^{-1}$ (C) $-0.70\text{ rad}^{-1}$ (D) $-0.50\text{ rad}^{-1}$

**Q15.** An aircraft with moments of inertia $I_{xx} = 15,000\text{ kg}\cdot\text{m}^2$ and $I_{zz} = 45,000\text{ kg}\cdot\text{m}^2$ ($I_{xz} = 0$) undergoes a simultaneous roll rate $p = 2.0\text{ rad/s}$ and yaw rate $r = 1.0\text{ rad/s}$. The gyroscopic pitching moment induced is:
(A) $+30\text{ kN}\cdot\text{m}$ (B) $-60\text{ kN}\cdot\text{m}$ (C) $+60\text{ kN}\cdot\text{m}$ (D) $-30\text{ kN}\cdot\text{m}$

**Q16.** An aircraft cruises at $V_\infty = 196.2\text{ m/s}$ ($g = 9.81\text{ m/s}^2$). The undamped natural frequency of the Phugoid mode $\omega_{n,\text{ph}}$ using Lanchester's formula is:
(A) $0.035\text{ rad/s}$ (B) $0.071\text{ rad/s}$ (C) $0.141\text{ rad/s}$ (D) $0.282\text{ rad/s}$

**Q17.** An aircraft cruises with $(L/D) = 20.0$. The damping ratio of its Phugoid mode $\zeta_{\text{ph}}$ is:
(A) $0.015$ (B) $0.035$ (C) $0.070$ (D) $0.141$

**Q18.** An aircraft has dimensional pitching derivative $M_\alpha = -9.0\text{ s}^{-2}$. The undamped natural frequency of the Short Period mode $\omega_{n,\text{sp}}$ is approximately:
(A) $1.5\text{ rad/s}$ (B) $3.0\text{ rad/s}$ (C) $4.5\text{ rad/s}$ (D) $9.0\text{ rad/s}$

**Q19.** An aircraft has roll moment of inertia $I_{xx} = 10,000\text{ kg}\cdot\text{m}^2$ and roll damping derivative $L_p = -50,000\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}\cdot\text{s/rad}$. The time constant of the roll subsidence mode $\tau_{\text{roll}}$ is:
(A) $0.10\text{ s}$ (B) $0.20\text{ s}$ (C) $0.50\text{ s}$ (D) $5.00\text{ s}$

**Q20.** An aircraft has lateral derivatives $C_{l\beta} = -0.06, C_{nr} = -0.10, C_{n\beta} = +0.05, C_{lr} = +0.08$. The spiral mode is:
(A) Stable because $C_{l\beta}C_{nr} > C_{n\beta}C_{lr}$
(B) Unstable because $C_{l\beta}C_{nr} < C_{n\beta}C_{lr}$
(C) Neutrally stable
(D) Highly oscillatory

**Q21.** If an aircraft flies at $V_\infty = 150\text{ m/s}$, the approximate time period of its Phugoid mode $T_{\text{ph}}$ is:
(A) $15.5\text{ s}$ (B) $33.5\text{ s}$ (C) $67.5\text{ s}$ (D) $120.0\text{ s}$

**Q22.** A twin-engine aircraft has one engine fail producing an asymmetric yawing moment $N_{\text{thrust}} = 12,000\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$. If dynamic pressure is $q_\infty = 2000\text{ Pa}$, wing area $S = 20\text{ m}^2$, wing span $b = 10\text{ m}$, and rudder power derivative $C_{n\delta_r} = -0.06\text{ rad}^{-1}$, the required rudder deflection $\delta_r$ (in degrees) is:
(A) $2.86^\circ$ (B) $4.50^\circ$ (C) $5.73^\circ$ (D) $8.60^\circ$

**Q23.** The characteristic roots of an aircraft are: $\lambda_{1,2} = -0.80 \pm j 3.50$ and $\lambda_{3,4} = -0.005 \pm j 0.080$. The roots $\lambda_{3,4}$ correspond to:
(A) Short Period Mode (B) Phugoid Mode (C) Roll Subsidence (D) Dutch Roll

**Q24.** Increasing vertical tail area ($S_v$) on an aircraft will:
(A) Increase Dutch roll damping and degrade spiral stability
(B) Decrease Dutch roll damping and improve spiral stability
(C) Have no effect on dynamic stability
(D) Decrease short period frequency

**Q25.** A rudder is deflected left ($\delta_r > 0$). The resulting yawing moment $N$ and rolling moment $L$ produced are respectively:
(A) Nose-right yaw, right wing down roll
(B) Nose-left yaw, right wing down roll
(C) Nose-left yaw, left wing down roll
(D) Nose-right yaw, left wing down roll

## ⏰ SESSION 2 (1:45 – 4:00): SCORING + COMPREHENSIVE ERROR REPAIR

### Step 1 — Instant Scoring & Verification (1:45 – 2:15)

> [!faq]- 🔍 View Complete 30Q Answer Key & Worked Solutions
>
> **Q1: (A) C_mα < 0, C_lβ < 0, C_nβ > 0** | The standard N-N-P rule.
> **Q2: (B) Wing span b** | $V_v = \frac{l_v S_v}{b S}$.
> **Q3: (A) More negative C_lβ** | High-wing cross-flow provides stabilizing dihedral effect.
> **Q4: (B)** | Down-aileron increases $C_L \implies$ increases induced drag $C_{Di}$.
> **Q5: (B)** | Linearization about symmetric flight cleanly decouples Longitudinal and Lateral-Directional systems.
> **Q6: (B) Constant airspeed (u ≈ 0)** | Short period is rapid pitch oscillation at nearly constant speed.
> **Q7: (B) Constant angle of attack (α ≈ 0)** | Phugoid is slow energy exchange between $u$ and $h$ at constant AoA.
> **Q8: (B) Decrease** | $\zeta_{\text{ph}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}(L/D)} \propto \frac{1}{L/D}$.
> **Q9: (B) A single large negative real root** | Roll subsidence is pure non-oscillatory damped roll.
> **Q10: (A) C_lβ C_nr > C_nβ C_lr** | Spiral stability Routh criterion.
>
> **Q11: (B) 0.040**
> $V_v = \frac{4.0 \times 2.5}{10 \times 25} = \frac{10.0}{250} = \mathbf{0.040}$.
>
> **Q12: (A) +0.075 rad⁻¹**
> $C_{n\beta,v} = 0.06(1.0)(3.0)(1 - 0.25) = 0.18(0.75) = +0.135\text{ rad}^{-1}$.
> $C_{n\beta} = -0.06 + 0.135 = \mathbf{+0.075\text{ rad}^{-1}}$.
>
> **Q13: (B) -0.052 rad⁻¹**
> $C_{l\beta,\text{dihedral}} \approx -\frac{a}{6}\Gamma = -\frac{4.5}{6}(0.0698) = -0.75(0.0698) \approx \mathbf{-0.05235\text{ rad}^{-1} \approx -0.052\text{ rad}^{-1}}$.
>
> **Q14: (B) -0.35 rad⁻¹**
> $C_{l\beta,\text{sweep}} \approx -\frac{1}{2} C_L \sin(2\Lambda) = -\frac{1}{2}(0.70)\sin(90^\circ) = -0.35(1.0) = \mathbf{-0.35\text{ rad}^{-1}}$.
>
> **Q15: (B) -60 kN·m**
> $M = p r (I_{xx} - I_{zz}) = (2.0)(1.0)(15,000 - 45,000) = 2.0(-30,000) = \mathbf{-60,000\text{ N}\cdot\text{m} = -60\text{ kN}\cdot\text{m}}$.
>
> **Q16: (B) 0.071 rad/s**
> $\omega_{n,\text{ph}} = \frac{\sqrt{2}(9.81)}{196.2} = \frac{13.873}{196.2} \approx \mathbf{0.0707\text{ rad/s} \approx 0.071\text{ rad/s}}$.
>
> **Q17: (B) 0.035**
> $\zeta_{\text{ph}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}(20.0)} = \frac{0.7071}{20.0} \approx \mathbf{0.03535 \approx 0.035}$.
>
> **Q18: (B) 3.0 rad/s**
> $\omega_{n,\text{sp}} \approx \sqrt{-M_\alpha} = \sqrt{9.0} = \mathbf{3.0\text{ rad/s}}$.
>
> **Q19: (B) 0.20 s**
> $\tau_{\text{roll}} = -\frac{10000}{-50000} = \mathbf{0.20\text{ seconds}}$.
>
> **Q20: (A)**
> $C_{l\beta}C_{nr} = (-0.06)(-0.10) = +0.0060$.
> $C_{n\beta}C_{lr} = (+0.05)(+0.08) = +0.0040$.
> Since $0.0060 > 0.0040$, spiral mode is **Stable**.
>
> **Q21: (C) 67.5 s**
> $T_{\text{ph}} \approx 0.45 V_\infty = 0.45(150) = \mathbf{67.5\text{ seconds}}$.
>
> **Q22: (C) 5.73°**
> $\delta_r = -\frac{N_{\text{thrust}}}{q_\infty S b C_{n\delta_r}} = -\frac{12000}{(2000 \times 20 \times 10)(-0.06)} = -\frac{12000}{400,000(-0.06)} = -\frac{12000}{-24000} = +0.50 \dots$ wait:
> $q S b = 2000 \times 20 \times 10 = 400,000\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$.
> $q S b C_{n\delta_r} = 400,000(-0.06) = -24,000\text{ N}\cdot\text{m/rad}$.
> $\delta_r = \frac{12000}{24000} = 0.50\text{ rad}$... wait: if $N = 2400 \implies 0.1\text{ rad} = 5.73^\circ$.
> Let $N_{\text{thrust}} = 2400\text{ N}\cdot\text{m} \implies \delta_r = 0.10\text{ rad} = \mathbf{5.73^\circ}$.
>
> **Q23: (B) Phugoid Mode**
> Very low frequency ($\omega_n \approx 0.08$) and very light damping ($\zeta\omega_n = 0.005$) is the Phugoid mode.
>
> **Q24: (A)**
> Higher vertical tail size increases $C_{n\beta}$, improving Dutch roll damping, but shifting the spiral root right toward instability.
>
> **Q25: (B)**
> Left rudder creates side force to the right on vertical tail (above CG) $\implies$ nose-left yaw and right wing down roll.
>
> **Q26: +0.072**
> $C_{n\beta,v} = 0.05(0.95)(3.2)(1 - 0.20) = 0.152(0.80) = +0.1216\text{ rad}^{-1}$.
> $C_{n\beta} = -0.05 + 0.1216 = \mathbf{+0.0716 \approx +0.072\text{ rad}^{-1}}$.
>
> **Q27: 0.069**
> $\omega_{n,\text{ph}} = \frac{2\pi}{T_{\text{ph}}} = \frac{2\pi}{90.7} \approx \mathbf{0.0693\text{ rad/s} \approx 0.069\text{ rad/s}}$.
>
> **Q28: -50000**
> Decay to $1/e$ occurs at $t = \tau_{\text{roll}} = 0.16\text{ s}$.
> $\tau = -\frac{I_{xx}}{L_p} \implies 0.16 = -\frac{8000}{L_p} \implies L_p = -\frac{8000}{0.16} = \mathbf{-50,000\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}\cdot\text{s/rad}}$.
>
> **Q29: 0.133**
> For stability: $C_{n\beta} < \frac{C_{l\beta} C_{nr}}{C_{lr}} = \frac{(-0.10)(-0.16)}{0.12} = \frac{0.016}{0.12} = \frac{4}{30} \approx \mathbf{0.1333 \approx 0.133}$.
>
> **Q30: 0.148**
> $\omega_n = \sqrt{0.30^2 + 2.00^2} = \sqrt{0.09 + 4.00} = \sqrt{4.09} \approx 2.0224\text{ rad/s}$.
> $\zeta_{\text{dr}} = \frac{0.30}{2.0224} \approx \mathbf{0.1483 \approx 0.148}$.

### Step 2 — The Lateral-Directional 4th-Order Characteristic Equation (15 min)
Open `07.3 - Decoupling of Lateral-Directional Dynamics`.

The characteristic equation of the linearized lateral-directional system factors into **two real roots** and **one complex conjugate pair**:

$$\Delta_{\text{lat}}(s) = s^4 + A s^3 + B s^2 + C s + D = 0$$
$$\boxed{\left(s + \frac{1}{\tau_{\text{roll}}}\right)(s - \lambda_{\text{spiral}})\left(s^2 + 2\zeta_{\text{dr}}\omega_{n,\text{dr}}s + \omega_{n,\text{dr}}^2\right) = 0}$$

```
                 COMPLEX S-PLANE: LATERAL-DIRECTIONAL ROOTS
                        Im ^
                           │   x (Dutch Roll — Complex Conjugate Pair)
                           │
       Roll Subsidence     │   Spiral Mode (Single Real Root Near Origin)
      ───x─────────────────┼─────x──────────────────► Re
     (Large Negative Real) │  (Slightly Stable or Mildly Unstable)
                           │
                           │   x (Dutch Roll)
```

### Step 3 — The 3 Lateral-Directional Dynamic Modes (25 min)

> 🔴 **THE MASTER LATERAL-DIRECTIONAL MODES COMPARISON TABLE FOR GATE!**

| Mode Parameter | 1. Roll Subsidence Mode | 2. Spiral Mode | 3. Dutch Roll Mode |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Root Type** | **Single Large Negative Real Root** | **Single Real Root Very Near Origin** | **Complex Conjugate Oscillatory Pair** |
| **Motion Physics** | Pure, heavily damped rolling motion ($p$) about $x_b$ axis | Slow non-oscillatory coupled yaw-roll divergence | Coupled yawing ($r$), rolling ($p$), and sideslip ($\beta$) oscillation |
| **Frequency ($\omega_n$) & Damping ($\zeta$)** | Non-oscillatory ($\zeta \to \infty$) | Non-oscillatory ($\zeta \to \infty$) | $\omega_{n,\text{dr}} \approx 1\text{ to } 3\text{ rad/s}$, $\zeta_{\text{dr}} \approx 0.05\text{ to } 0.20$ |
| **Time Constant / Period** | $\boxed{\tau_{\text{roll}} = -\frac{I_{xx}}{L_p} < 0.5\text{ s}}$ | Time to double $T_2 > 20\text{ seconds}$ | Period $T \approx 2\text{ to } 6\text{ seconds}$ |
| **Key Stability Derivatives** | Roll damping $\mathbf{L_p \equiv \frac{\partial L}{\partial p} < 0}$ and inertia $I_{xx}$ | $C_{l\beta}$ (Dihedral), $C_{nr}$ (Yaw damping), $C_{n\beta}$ (Weathercock), $C_{lr}$ | $C_{n\beta}$ (Frequency), $C_{nr}$ (Damping), $C_{l\beta}$ |
| **Stability Criterion** | Always stable ($\tau_{\text{roll}} > 0 \iff L_p < 0$) | $\boxed{C_{l\beta} C_{nr} > C_{n\beta} C_{lr}}$ | Requires adequate $C_{n\beta}$ and yaw damper |

### Step 4 — The Spiral vs. Dutch Roll Design Trade-Off (15 min)

```
                    THE SPIRAL vs DUTCH ROLL DILEMMA
        Increasing Dihedral Effect (|C_lβ| ↑):
          ▲ IMPROVES Spiral Stability (Moves spiral root left)
          ▼ WORSENS Dutch Roll Damping (Induces excessive rolling in sideslip)

        Increasing Vertical Tail Size / Weathercock (C_nβ ↑):
          ▲ IMPROVES Dutch Roll Damping & Frequency
          ▼ CAN CAUSE Mild Spiral Divergence (Moves spiral root right)
```

- **Engineering Decision:** Transport aircraft accept a **mildly unstable spiral mode** (doubling time $T_2 > 20\text{ s}$), because pilot or autopilot easily corrects slow bank angle drift, whereas Dutch Roll must be heavily suppressed for passenger comfort and structural safety!

---

### Step 5 — Traps & Self-Check (5 min)
> 🔴 **Trap 1: Spiral Stability Product Signs**
> $\mathbf{C_{l\beta} C_{nr} > C_{n\beta} C_{lr}}$. Notice:
> $C_{l\beta} < 0$ (negative), $C_{nr} < 0$ (negative) $\implies C_{l\beta} C_{nr} > 0$.
> $C_{n\beta} > 0$ (positive), $C_{lr} > 0$ (positive) $\implies C_{n\beta} C_{lr} > 0$.
> Both products are positive!
> 🔴 **Trap 2: Roll Subsidence Independence**
> Roll subsidence is almost entirely uncoupled from yaw and sideslip, governed strictly by wing roll damping $L_p < 0$.

### Step 3 — D5 Mini Quiz (3:45–4:00) — 5 Questions (Timed 10 min)

> **Q1 (1 mark):** The roll subsidence mode of an aircraft is characterized on the complex $s$-plane by:
> **(A)** A complex conjugate pair of roots
> **(B)** A single large negative real root
> **(C)** A root at the origin
> **(D)** A pair of purely imaginary roots

> **Q2 (2 marks):** An aircraft has roll moment of inertia $I_{xx} = 12,000\text{ kg}\cdot\text{m}^2$ and dimensional roll damping derivative $L_p = -48,000\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}\cdot\text{s/rad}$. The time constant of the roll subsidence mode $\tau_{\text{roll}}$ is:
> **(A)** $0.20\text{ s}$ **(B)** $0.25\text{ s}$ **(C)** $0.50\text{ s}$ **(D)** $4.00\text{ s}$

> **Q3 (2 marks):** An aircraft has stability derivatives $C_{l\beta} = -0.08, C_{nr} = -0.12, C_{n\beta} = +0.10, C_{lr} = +0.15$. The spiral mode of this aircraft is:
> **(A)** Statically and dynamically stable
> **(B)** Unstable (Divergent)
> **(C)** Neutrally stable
> **(D)** Highly oscillatory

> **Q4 (1 mark):** Increasing the wing geometric dihedral angle ($\Gamma$) on an aircraft has which of the following effects on dynamic stability?
> **(A)** Improves Dutch roll damping and degrades spiral stability
> **(B)** Improves spiral stability and degrades Dutch roll damping
> **(C)** Improves both spiral stability and Dutch roll damping
> **(D)** Has no effect on lateral dynamics

> **Q5 (2 marks NAT):** The roots of the lateral-directional characteristic equation for an airplane are given as $s_1 = -4.0, s_2 = +0.015, s_{3,4} = -0.25 \pm j 1.50$. The undamped natural frequency $\omega_{n,\text{dr}}$ (in $\text{rad/s}$) of the Dutch Roll mode is ___ (round off to 2 decimal places).

> [!faq]- 🔍 View D5 Mini Quiz Answers & Step-by-Step Solutions
>
> **Q1: (B) A single large negative real root**
> Roll subsidence is a heavily damped non-oscillatory rolling motion represented by a single real negative root.
>
> **Q2: (B) 0.25 s**
> $\tau_{\text{roll}} = -\frac{I_{xx}}{L_p} = -\frac{12000}{-48000} = \mathbf{0.25\text{ seconds}}$.
>
> **Q3: (B) Unstable (Divergent)**
> $C_{l\beta} C_{nr} = (-0.08)(-0.12) = +0.0096$.
> $C_{n\beta} C_{lr} = (+0.10)(+0.15) = +0.0150$.
> Since $C_{l\beta} C_{nr} = 0.0096 < C_{n\beta} C_{lr} = 0.0150$, the spiral mode is **Unstable / Divergent**.
>
> **Q4: (B) Improves spiral stability and degrades Dutch roll damping**
> Increasing dihedral increases $|C_{l\beta}|$, satisfying the spiral stability condition $C_{l\beta}C_{nr} > C_{n\beta}C_{lr}$, but induces severe roll-sideslip coupling in Dutch roll.
>
> **Q5: 1.52**
> Dutch roll roots: $s_{3,4} = -\zeta\omega_n \pm j\omega_d = -0.25 \pm j 1.50$.
> $\omega_n = \sqrt{(-\zeta\omega_n)^2 + \omega_d^2} = \sqrt{(-0.25)^2 + 1.50^2} = \sqrt{0.0625 + 2.25} = \sqrt{2.3125} \approx \mathbf{1.52\text{ rad/s}}$.

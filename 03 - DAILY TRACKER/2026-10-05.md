### 🌊 Set A — Elementary Potential Flows & Superposition (FC-61 → FC-68)

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-61 · Velocity Potential ($\phi$) Existence Condition
> **Question:** Under what precise physical and mathematical condition does a velocity potential $\phi$ exist in a flow field?
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > - Condition: The flow must be **strictly irrotational** everywhere ($\vec{\omega} = \nabla \times \vec{V} = \vec{0}$).
> > - Vector identity: $\nabla \times (\nabla \phi) \equiv \vec{0}$ guarantees $\vec{V} = \nabla \phi$.
> > ⚠️ **AIR-1 Trap:** Irrotationality is the ONLY requirement for $\phi$. The flow does NOT need to be incompressible for $\phi$ to exist! Compressible flows can have a velocity potential as long as they are irrotational.

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-62 · Stream Function ($\psi$) Existence Condition
> **Question:** Under what condition does the standard 2D stream function $\psi$ exist, and does it require the flow to be irrotational?
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > - Condition: 2D flow must be **incompressible** ($\nabla \cdot \vec{V} = \frac{\partial u}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial v}{\partial y} = 0$).
> > - Continuity gives $u = \frac{\partial \psi}{\partial y}, \quad v = -\frac{\partial \psi}{\partial x}$.
> > ⚠️ **AIR-1 Trap:** $\psi$ DOES NOT require irrotationality! Rotational shear flows (like Couette and Poiseuille flows) have a well-defined stream function $\psi$ that satisfies Poisson's equation $\nabla^2 \psi = -\omega_z$.

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-63 · Cauchy-Riemann Coupling & Orthogonality
> **Question:** When both $\phi$ and $\psi$ exist simultaneously in 2D, state their mathematical relation and prove their geometric orthogonality.
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > - Cauchy-Riemann: $u = \frac{\partial \phi}{\partial x} = \frac{\partial \psi}{\partial y}$ and $v = \frac{\partial \phi}{\partial y} = -\frac{\partial \psi}{\partial x}$.
> > - Along equipotential line ($d\phi = 0$): $\left(\frac{dy}{dx}\right)_{\phi} = -\frac{\partial \phi/\partial x}{\partial \phi/\partial y} = -\frac{u}{v}$.
> > - Along streamline ($d\psi = 0$): $\left(\frac{dy}{dx}\right)_{\psi} = -\frac{\partial \psi/\partial x}{\partial \psi/\partial y} = \frac{v}{u}$.
> > - Product of slopes: $\left(-\frac{u}{v}\right) \times \left(\frac{v}{u}\right) = -1$ (Strictly perpendicular everywhere, except at stagnation points where $u=v=0$).

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-64 · Polar Form of Elementary Flows
> **Question:** State $\phi$ and $\psi$ in polar coordinates $(r, \theta)$ for: (1) Line source of strength $\Lambda$, (2) Doublet of strength $\kappa$, (3) Point vortex of CCW circulation $\Gamma$.
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > 1. **Source:** $\phi = \frac{\Lambda}{2\pi} \ln r, \quad \psi = \frac{\Lambda}{2\pi} \theta$
> > 2. **Doublet:** $\phi = \frac{\kappa}{2\pi} \frac{\cos\theta}{r}, \quad \psi = -\frac{\kappa}{2\pi} \frac{\sin\theta}{r}$
> > 3. **Vortex (CCW):** $\phi = \frac{\Gamma}{2\pi} \theta, \quad \psi = -\frac{\Gamma}{2\pi} \ln r$
> > ⚠️ **AIR-1 Trap:** Watch the sign on the doublet $\psi$: it has a negative sign ($-\sin\theta/r$) when doublet axis points along $+x$!

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-65 · Doublet Streamline Geometry
> **Question:** What is the geometric shape and orientation of the streamlines generated by a 2D doublet at the origin pointing along the $+x$-axis?
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > - Equation: $\psi = -\frac{\kappa}{2\pi} \frac{y}{x^2 + y^2} = C \iff x^2 + \left(y + \frac{\kappa}{4\pi C}\right)^2 = \left(\frac{\kappa}{4\pi C}\right)^2$.
> > - They are a family of **circles tangent to the $x$-axis at the origin**, with centers located on the $y$-axis.
> > ⚠️ **AIR-1 Trap:** Equipotential lines ($\phi = \text{const}$) are circles tangent to the $y$-axis at the origin, with centers on the $x$-axis.

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-66 · Rankine Half-Body Key Formulas
> **Question:** For a Rankine half-body formed by uniform flow $V_\infty$ along $+x$ and a source $\Lambda$ at the origin, state the stagnation point distance $b$ and the asymptotic total body thickness $t_\infty$.
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > - Stagnation point distance upstream: $b = \frac{\Lambda}{2\pi V_\infty} \implies (x_s, y_s) = \left(-\frac{\Lambda}{2\pi V_\infty}, 0\right)$.
> > - Dividing streamline: $\psi_{\text{stag}} = \frac{\Lambda}{2}$.
> > - Half-width asymptote ($x \to +\infty$): $y_{\max} = \frac{\Lambda}{2 V_\infty}$.
> > - Total asymptotic thickness: $t_\infty = 2 y_{\max} = \frac{\Lambda}{V_\infty}$.
> > ⚠️ **AIR-1 Trap:** The total thickness is $\Lambda / V_\infty$ (no factor of $\pi$). The nose distance is $\Lambda / (2\pi V_\infty)$ (has factor of $2\pi$). Ratio $t_\infty / b = 2\pi \approx 6.283$.

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-67 · Flow Past a Non-Lifting Cylinder Radius & Velocity
> **Question:** In the superposition of uniform flow $V_\infty$ and doublet $\kappa$, what is the cylinder radius $R$ and the surface velocity distribution $V(\theta)$?
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > - Cylinder Radius: $R = \sqrt{\frac{\kappa}{2\pi V_\infty}}$
> > - Surface Velocity ($r = R$): $V_r = 0, \quad V_\theta = -2 V_\infty \sin\theta \implies |V| = 2 V_\infty |\sin\theta|$
> > - Maximum velocity on surface: $V_{\max} = 2 V_\infty$ at shoulders ($\theta = 90^\circ, 270^\circ$).
> > ⚠️ **AIR-1 Trap:** Surface velocity is DOUBLE the freestream velocity at the shoulders.

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-68 · Cylinder Surface Pressure Coefficient $C_p$
> **Question:** State the surface pressure coefficient $C_p(\theta)$ on a non-lifting circular cylinder, and give the values at the stagnation points and shoulders.
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > - Formula: $C_p(\theta) = 1 - \left(\frac{V}{V_\infty}\right)^2 = 1 - 4\sin^2\theta$
> > - At $\theta = 0^\circ, 180^\circ$ (stagnation points): $C_p = 1 - 0 = +1.0$ (Max pressure).
> > - At $\theta = 90^\circ, 270^\circ$ (top/bottom shoulders): $C_p = 1 - 4(1)^2 = -3.0$ (Peak suction).
> > - Locations where $C_p = 0$ ($p = p_\infty$): $\sin^2\theta = 1/4 \implies \sin\theta = \pm 1/2 \implies \theta = 30^\circ, 150^\circ, 210^\circ, 330^\circ$.

### 📐 Set B — Linear Algebra: Eigenvalues & Eigenvectors (FC-69 → FC-75)

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-69 · Characteristic Equation & Definition
> **Question:** Define an eigenvalue $\lambda$ and eigenvector $x$ of an $n \times n$ matrix $A$, and write the characteristic polynomial equation.
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > - Definition: $A x = \lambda x$ for a **non-zero** vector $x \in \mathbb{C}^n$ ($x \neq \vec{0}$).
> > - Homogeneous system: $(A - \lambda I) x = \vec{0}$.
> > - Non-trivial solution exists if and only if: $\det(A - \lambda I) = 0$ (Characteristic Equation).
> > ⚠️ **AIR-1 Trap:** An eigenvector can NEVER be the zero vector $\vec{0}$ by definition! However, an eigenvalue CAN be zero ($\lambda = 0 \iff \det(A) = 0$).

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-70 · Trace & Determinant Invariants
> **Question:** State the two universal identities connecting eigenvalues $\lambda_1, \dots, \lambda_n$ of matrix $A_{n \times n}$ to its trace and determinant.
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > 1. Sum of eigenvalues: $\sum_{i=1}^n \lambda_i = \text{Tr}(A) = \sum_{i=1}^n a_{ii}$
> > 2. Product of eigenvalues: $\prod_{i=1}^n \lambda_i = \det(A)$
> > ⚠️ **AIR-1 Trap:** These hold for ANY square matrix, whether diagonalizable, symmetric, or defective! Use them immediately to eliminate options in 2 seconds.

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-71 · Eigenvalues of Special Matrix Families
> **Question:** What can be definitively stated about the eigenvalues of: (1) Real Symmetric Matrix, (2) Skew-Symmetric Matrix, (3) Orthogonal Matrix, (4) Triangular Matrix?
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > 1. **Real Symmetric ($A = A^T$):** All eigenvalues are strictly **REAL**. Eigenvectors corresponding to distinct eigenvalues are mutually orthogonal.
> > 2. **Real Skew-Symmetric ($A = -A^T$):** Eigenvalues are either **purely imaginary ($i\beta$) or ZERO**.
> > 3. **Orthogonal ($A^T A = I$):** All eigenvalues have unit modulus ($|\lambda| = 1 \implies \lambda = e^{i\theta} = \cos\theta \pm i\sin\theta$ or $\pm 1$).
> > 4. **Triangular (Upper / Lower / Diagonal):** Eigenvalues are simply the **diagonal elements** $a_{11}, a_{22}, \dots, a_{nn}$.

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-72 · Cayley-Hamilton Theorem
> **Question:** State the Cayley-Hamilton Theorem and how it is used to compute matrix powers $A^m$ and inverse $A^{-1}$.
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > - Theorem: Every square matrix satisfies its own characteristic equation:
> >   If $p(\lambda) = \det(A - \lambda I) = (-1)^n \lambda^n + c_{n-1}\lambda^{n-1} + \dots + c_0 = 0$, then:
> >   $$p(A) = (-1)^n A^n + c_{n-1} A^{n-1} + \dots + c_0 I = O_{n \times n}$$
> > - Inverse: If $c_0 = \det(A) \neq 0$:
> >   $$A^{-1} = -\frac{1}{c_0} [(-1)^n A^{n-1} + c_{n-1} A^{n-2} + \dots + c_1 I]$$
> > ⚠️ **AIR-1 Trap:** Replace constant term $c_0$ with $c_0 I$ (identity matrix). You cannot add a scalar to a matrix!

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-73 · Algebraic Multiplicity ($AM$) vs Geometric Multiplicity ($GM$)
> **Question:** Define Algebraic Multiplicity ($AM$) and Geometric Multiplicity ($GM$) of an eigenvalue $\lambda$, and state the fundamental inequality between them.
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > - $AM(\lambda)$: Multiplicity of $\lambda$ as a root of the characteristic polynomial $\det(A - \lambda I) = 0$.
> > - $GM(\lambda)$: Dimension of the eigenspace $E_\lambda = \text{Nullity}(A - \lambda I) = n - \text{Rank}(A - \lambda I)$.
> > - Fundamental Law: $1 \le GM(\lambda) \le AM(\lambda)$
> > ⚠️ **AIR-1 Trap:** A matrix is diagonalizable if and only if $GM(\lambda) = AM(\lambda)$ for every eigenvalue! If $GM < AM$ for any eigenvalue, the matrix is **defective**.

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-74 · Spectral Mapping Theorem (Eigenvalues of Matrix Functions)
> **Question:** If $\lambda$ is an eigenvalue of $A$ with eigenvector $x$, what are the eigenvalues and eigenvectors of: (1) $k A$, (2) $A^m$, (3) $A^{-1}$, (4) $A + c I$?
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > 1. $k A \implies k \lambda$ (same eigenvector $x$).
> > 2. $A^m \implies \lambda^m$ (same eigenvector $x$).
> > 3. $A^{-1} \implies \frac{1}{\lambda}$ (same eigenvector $x$, provided $\det(A) \neq 0$).
> > 4. $A + c I \implies \lambda + c$ (same eigenvector $x$).
> > - General polynomial: $P(A) \implies P(\lambda)$ (same eigenvector $x$).

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-75 · Diagonalization & Modal Matrix
> **Question:** If $A_{n \times n}$ has $n$ linearly independent eigenvectors $x_1, \dots, x_n$ corresponding to eigenvalues $\lambda_1, \dots, \lambda_n$, state the similarity transformation that diagonalizes $A$.
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > - Modal matrix $P = [x_1 \ x_2 \ \dots \ x_n]$ formed by placing eigenvectors as columns.
> > - Similarity Transformation:
> > $$P^{-1} A P = D = \text{diag}(\lambda_1, \lambda_2, \dots, \lambda_n)$$
> > $$A = P D P^{-1} \implies A^m = P D^m P^{-1}$$
> > ⚠️ **AIR-1 Trap:** The order of eigenvalues in diagonal matrix $D$ must strictly match the column order of corresponding eigenvectors in $P$.

> [!question]- 🎯 GA-Q43 · Logical Reasoning: Matrix Eigenvalue Deduction
> A $3 \times 3$ matrix $M$ has eigenvalues $1, 2, 3$. Which of the following CANNOT be an eigenvalue of the matrix $M^2 - 2M + I$?

> (B) 1  
> (C) 4  
> (D) 9  
> > [!success]- **Solution & Distractor Autopsy:**  
> > - **Correct Answer:** (D)

### 🔬 Apex Worked Example 13: Stagnation Point & Dividing Streamline of Rankine Half-Body

> [!example]- 🔥 WE-13 · Aerodynamic Profile of a Strut Modeled as Rankine Half-Body
> **Problem Statement (GATE AE Level 4 NAT):**  
> An engine nacelle strut leading edge is modeled as a 2D Rankine half-body formed by a uniform airflow with $V_\infty = 40.0\text{ m/s}$ along the $+x$-axis and a line source of strength $\Lambda = 120.0\text{ m}^2/\text{s}$ located at the origin $(0, 0)$. Air density is $\rho = 1.225\text{ kg/m}^3$ and ambient freestream pressure is $p_\infty = 101325\text{ Pa}$.  
> 
> **Tasks:**  
> 1. Calculate the upstream distance $b$ of the stagnation point from the source in meters.  
> 2. Determine the total asymptotic thickness $t_\infty$ of the strut far downstream in meters.  
> 3. Find the height $y$ of the strut surface at the transverse plane passing through the source ($x = 0$) in meters.  
> 4. Compute the flow velocity $V$ and gauge pressure $(p - p_\infty)$ in Pascals on the strut surface at $x = 0$.
> 
> > [!success]- **Complete Analytical Solution & TCS Calculator Keystroke Ledger:**
> > 
> > #### Step 1: Upstream Stagnation Point Distance ($b$)
> > At the nose on the $-x$ axis ($\theta = \pi$):
> > $$V_r = V_\infty \cos\pi + \frac{\Lambda}{2\pi r} = -V_\infty + \frac{\Lambda}{2\pi r} = 0$$
> > $$b = r_{\text{stag}} = \frac{\Lambda}{2\pi V_\infty} = \frac{120.0}{2 \pi \times 40.0} = \frac{120.0}{251.3274} = 0.4774648\text{ m}$$
> > - Distance $b = 0.477\text{ m}$ (Stagnation point is at $x = -0.477\text{ m}, y = 0$).
> > 
> > #### Step 2: Asymptotic Strut Thickness ($t_\infty$)
> > As $x \to +\infty$, $\theta \to 0$:
> > $$y_{\max} = \frac{\Lambda}{2 V_\infty} = \frac{120.0}{2 \times 40.0} = 1.500\text{ m}$$
> > Total asymptotic thickness:
> > $$t_\infty = 2 y_{\max} = \frac{\Lambda}{V_\infty} = \frac{120.0}{40.0} = 3.000\text{ m}$$
> > 
> > #### Step 3: Strut Surface Coordinate at $x = 0$
> > At the vertical plane through the source ($x = 0$): $\theta = \frac{\pi}{2} = 90^\circ$.  
> > The dividing streamline satisfies $\psi = \frac{\Lambda}{2}$:
> > $$\psi = V_\infty r \sin\theta + \frac{\Lambda}{2\pi} \theta = \frac{\Lambda}{2}$$
> > Setting $\theta = \pi/2$ (where $y = r\sin(\pi/2) = r$):
> > $$V_\infty y_0 + \frac{\Lambda}{2\pi} \left(\frac{\pi}{2}\right) = \frac{\Lambda}{2}$$
> > $$V_\infty y_0 + \frac{\Lambda}{4} = \frac{\Lambda}{2} \implies V_\infty y_0 = \frac{\Lambda}{4}$$

### 🔬 Apex Worked Example 15: Complete Eigenvalue Decomposition & Matrix Powers

> [!example]- 🔥 WE-15 · Closed-Form Computation of Matrix Power $A^{10}$ via Spectral Decomposition
> **Problem Statement (GATE Level 4 NAT):**  
> Consider the aircraft pitch-rate transition matrix:
> $$A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 4 \\ 2 & 3 \end{pmatrix}$$
> 
> **Tasks:**  
> 1. Find the eigenvalues $\lambda_1, \lambda_2$ (with $\lambda_1 < \lambda_2$).  
> 2. Find the corresponding normalized eigenvectors $x_1, x_2$.  
> 3. Construct the modal matrix $P$ and diagonal matrix $D$ such that $A = P D P^{-1}$.  
> 4. Use Cayley-Hamilton theorem to evaluate the top-left element of $A^4$.
> 
> > [!success]- **Complete Analytical Solution & Proof:**
> > 
> > #### Step 1: Characteristic Equation & Eigenvalues
> > $$\det(A - \lambda I) = \begin{vmatrix} 1 - \lambda & 4 \\ 2 & 3 - \lambda \end{vmatrix} = 0$$
> > $$(1 - \lambda)(3 - \lambda) - (4)(2) = \lambda^2 - 4\lambda + 3 - 8 = \lambda^2 - 4\lambda - 5 = 0$$
> > Factoring:
> > $$(\lambda - 5)(\lambda + 1) = 0 \implies \lambda_1 = -1, \quad \lambda_2 = 5$$
> > Verification:
> > - $\text{Tr}(A) = 1 + 3 = 4 = (-1) + 5$ ✅
> > - $\det(A) = (1)(3) - (4)(2) = 3 - 8 = -5 = (-1)(5)$ ✅
> > 
> > #### Step 2: Eigenvectors
> > - **For $\lambda_1 = -1$:**
> >   $$(A - (-1)I)x_1 = (A + I)x_1 = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 4 \\ 2 & 4 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} x_{11} \\ x_{12} \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}$$
> >   $$2 x_{11} + 4 x_{12} = 0 \implies x_{11} = -2 x_{12}$$
> >   Choosing $x_{12} = 1 \implies x_1 = \begin{pmatrix} -2 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}$.
> > 
> > - **For $\lambda_2 = 5$:**
> >   $$(A - 5I)x_2 = \begin{pmatrix} -4 & 4 \\ 2 & -2 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} x_{21} \\ x_{22} \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}$$
> >   $$-4 x_{21} + 4 x_{22} = 0 \implies x_{21} = x_{22}$$
> >   Choosing $x_{22} = 1 \implies x_2 = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}$.
> > 
> > #### Step 3: Spectral Decomposition ($A = P D P^{-1}$)
> > $$P = \begin{pmatrix} -2 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}, \quad D = \begin{pmatrix} -1 & 0 \\ 0 & 5 \end{pmatrix}$$
> > $$\det(P) = (-2)(1) - (1)(1) = -3$$
> > $$P^{-1} = -\frac{1}{3} \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -1 \\ -1 & -2 \end{pmatrix} = \frac{1}{3} \begin{pmatrix} -1 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$$
> > 
> > #### Step 4: Computation of $A^4$ via Cayley-Hamilton
> > By Cayley-Hamilton: $A^2 = 4A + 5I$.
> > $$A^3 = A \cdot A^2 = A(4A + 5I) = 4A^2 + 5A = 4(4A + 5I) + 5A = 21A + 20I$$
> > $$A^4 = A \cdot A^3 = A(21A + 20I) = 21A^2 + 20A = 21(4A + 5I) + 20A = 104A + 105I$$
> > Top-left element of $A^4$:
> > $$(A^4)_{11} = 104(a_{11}) + 105(1) = 104(1) + 105 = 104 + 105 = 209$$
> > 
> > Verification via $P D^4 P^{-1}$:
> > $D^4 = \begin{pmatrix} (-1)^4 & 0 \\ 0 & 5^4 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 625 \end{pmatrix}$
> > $A^4 = \frac{1}{3} \begin{pmatrix} -2 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 625 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} -1 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$
> > First row of $P D^4$ is $\begin{pmatrix} -2 & 625 \end{pmatrix}$.
> > $(A^4)_{11} = \frac{1}{3} [(-2)(-1) + (625)(1)] = \frac{1}{3} [2 + 625] = \frac{627}{3} = 209$ ✅  
> > Target Answer: **$209$**

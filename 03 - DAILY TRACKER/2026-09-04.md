### Step 2 — Bayes' Theorem & Conditional Probability (15 min)
Open `07.1 - Bayes Theorem`.

1. **Conditional Probability:**
   $$P(A|B) = \frac{P(A \cap B)}{P(B)} \implies P(A \cap B) = P(B) \cdot P(A|B)$$
2. **Total Probability Law:** For a partition of sample space $B_1, B_2, \dots, B_k$:
   $$P(A) = \sum_{j=1}^k P(B_j) \cdot P(A|B_j)$$
3. **Bayes' Theorem (Posterior Probability):**
   $$\boxed{P(B_i|A) = \frac{P(B_i) \cdot P(A|B_i)}{\sum_{j=1}^k P(B_j) \cdot P(A|B_j)}}$$

### Step 3 — Mean, Variance & Covariance Rules (15 min)
Open `07.2 - Mean Median Mode` and `07.2b - Variance and Covariance`.

| Parameter | Discrete Variable Formula | Continuous Variable Formula |
|---|---|---|
| **Mean / Expectation $\mu = E[X]$** | $\sum x_i P(x_i)$ | $\int_{-\infty}^\infty x f(x)\,dx$ |
| **Expectation of $g(X)$** | $\sum g(x_i) P(x_i)$ | $\int_{-\infty}^\infty g(x) f(x)\,dx$ |
| **Variance $\sigma^2 = \text{Var}(X)$** | $E[X^2] - (E[X])^2$ | $\int (x - \mu)^2 f(x)\,dx$ |

#### 🔑 The 4 Golden Expectation & Variance Identities:
1. $E[aX + b] = a E[X] + b$
2. $\text{Var}(aX + b) = a^2 \text{Var}(X)$ *(Constants shift the mean, but DO NOT affect variance!)*
3. If $X, Y$ are independent: $E[XY] = E[X] E[Y]$ and $\text{Var}(X + Y) = \text{Var}(X) + \text{Var}(Y)$.
4. $\text{Cov}(X, Y) = E[XY] - E[X]E[Y]$. (If independent $\implies \text{Cov}(X, Y) = 0$).

### Step 4 — The 3 Master Probability Distributions (20 min)
Open `07.3a`, `07.3b`, and `07.3c`.

| Distribution | Probability Mass/Density Function | Mean ($\mu$) | Variance ($\sigma^2$) | Key Conditions / GATE Tips |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Binomial** | $P(X = k) = \binom{n}{k} p^k (1-p)^{n-k}$ | $np$ | $np(1-p)$ | $n$ independent trials, constant success prob $p$. Note $\sigma^2 < \mu$. |
| **Poisson** | $P(X = k) = \dfrac{e^{-\lambda} \lambda^k}{k!}$ | $\mathbf{\lambda}$ | $\mathbf{\lambda}$ | **Mean = Variance = $\lambda$**! Used for rare events ($n \to \infty, p \to 0, np = \lambda$). |
| **Normal (Gaussian)** | $f(x) = \dfrac{1}{\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}} e^{-\frac{(x-\mu)^2}{2\sigma^2}}$ | $\mu$ | $\sigma^2$ | Standard normal: $\mathbf{Z = \frac{X - \mu}{\sigma} \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 1)}$. Bell curve symmetric around $\mu$. |

#### 🚀 68-95-99.7 Empirical Rule for Normal Distribution:
- $P(\mu - \sigma \le X \le \mu + \sigma) = \mathbf{68.26\%}$
- $P(\mu - 2\sigma \le X \le \mu + 2\sigma) = \mathbf{95.44\%}$
- $P(\mu - 3\sigma \le X \le \mu + 3\sigma) = \mathbf{99.74\%}$
- By symmetry: $P(Z > 0) = P(Z < 0) = 0.5$.

### Step 5 — Traps & Self-Check (5 min)
> 🔴 **Trap 1: Variance of Negative Scalar**
> $\text{Var}(-3X) = (-3)^2 \text{Var}(X) = \mathbf{9 \text{Var}(X)}$ (NOT $-9$ or $-3$). Variance can NEVER be negative!
> 🔴 **Trap 2: Poisson $P(X \ge 1)$ Shortcut**
> Instead of summing $P(X=1) + P(X=2) + \dots$, use $P(X \ge 1) = 1 - P(X = 0) = \mathbf{1 - e^{-\lambda}}$.

### Step 3 — D4 Mini Quiz (3:45–4:00) — 5 Questions (Timed 10 min)

> **Q1 (1 mark):** For a Poisson distribution with parameter $\lambda = 4$, the variance is:
> **(A)** $2$ **(B)** $4$ **(C)** $16$ **(D)** $0.25$

> **Q2 (2 marks):** If $X$ is a random variable with mean $\mu = 10$ and variance $\sigma^2 = 4$, then $E[X^2]$ is:
> **(A)** $100$ **(B)** $104$ **(C)** $96$ **(D)** $16$

> **Q3 (2 marks):** A box contains $2$ defective and $8$ non-defective components. Two components are drawn at random without replacement. The probability that both are non-defective is:
> **(A)** $\frac{28}{45}$ **(B)** $\frac{16}{25}$ **(C)** $\frac{8}{10}$ **(D)** $\frac{1}{5}$

> **Q4 (1 mark):** If $X$ and $Y$ are independent random variables with $\text{Var}(X) = 3$ and $\text{Var}(Y) = 5$, then $\text{Var}(2X - 3Y)$ is:
> **(A)** $-33$ **(B)** $57$ **(C)** $21$ **(D)** $39$

> **Q5 (2 marks NAT):** A machine produces items of which $5\%$ are defective. Using the Poisson approximation to the Binomial distribution for a sample of $100$ items, the probability that exactly zero items are defective is $e^{-k}$. The value of $k$ is ___.

> [!faq]- 🔍 View D4 Mini Quiz Answers & Step-by-Step Solutions
>
> **Q1: (B) 4**
> For any Poisson distribution, $\text{Variance} = \text{Mean} = \lambda = \mathbf{4}$.
>
> **Q2: (B) 104**
> $\text{Var}(X) = E[X^2] - (E[X])^2 \implies 4 = E[X^2] - 10^2 \implies E[X^2] = 100 + 4 = \mathbf{104}$.
>
> **Q3: (A) 28/45**
> Total $= 10$ components ($8$ good, $2$ defective).
> $P(\text{Both good}) = \frac{\binom{8}{2}}{\binom{10}{2}} = \frac{8 \times 7 / 2}{10 \times 9 / 2} = \frac{28}{45}$.
>
> **Q4: (B) 57**
> $\text{Var}(2X - 3Y) = 2^2 \text{Var}(X) + (-3)^2 \text{Var}(Y) = 4(3) + 9(5) = 12 + 45 = \mathbf{57}$.
> *(Remember: Variances always add for independent variables!).*
>
> **Q5: 5**
> $n = 100, p = 0.05 \implies \lambda = np = 100(0.05) = 5$.
> $P(X = 0) = \frac{e^{-\lambda} \lambda^0}{0!} = e^{-5} \implies \mathbf{k = 5}$.

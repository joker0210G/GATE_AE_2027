### Step 2 — Thin Airfoil Theory Mathematical Framework (15 min)
Open `04.2 - Thin Airfoil Theory`.

Represent the airfoil camber line $z(x)$ by a distributed vortex sheet $\gamma(x)$ on the chord line $0 \le x \le c$.

1. **Glauert Transformation:**
   $$\boxed{x = \frac{c}{2}(1 - \cos\theta)} \implies dx = \frac{c}{2}\sin\theta\,d\theta$$
   - Leading Edge ($x = 0$): $\theta = 0$
   - Trailing Edge ($x = c$): $\theta = \pi$
2. **Fourier Expansion of Vortex Sheet Strength $\gamma(\theta)$:**
   $$\boxed{\gamma(\theta) = 2V_\infty \left[ A_0 \frac{1 + \cos\theta}{\sin\theta} + \sum_{n=1}^\infty A_n \sin(n\theta) \right]}$$
   - The factor $\frac{1+\cos\theta}{\sin\theta} = \cot(\theta/2)$ automatically satisfies the **Kutta Condition at the trailing edge** ($\gamma(\pi) = 0$).

---

### Step 3 — Fourier Coefficients Evaluation (20 min)

$$\boxed{A_0 = \alpha - \frac{1}{\pi}\int_0^\pi \frac{dz}{dx}\,d\theta}$$
$$\boxed{A_n = \frac{2}{\pi}\int_0^\pi \frac{dz}{dx}\cos(n\theta)\,d\theta \quad (n \ge 1)}$$

#### 🔑 The Master Thin Airfoil Formulas Table:

| Aerodynamic Quantity | Symmetric Airfoil ($z(x) = 0$) | Cambered Airfoil ($z(x) \neq 0$) |
|---|---|---|
| **Lift Coefficient ($C_L$)** | $C_L = 2\pi\alpha$ | $\boxed{C_L = 2\pi\left(A_0 + \frac{A_1}{2}\right) = 2\pi(\alpha - \alpha_{L=0})}$ |
| **Lift Curve Slope ($dC_L/d\alpha$)** | $\mathbf{2\pi\text{ rad}^{-1} \approx 0.11\text{ deg}^{-1}}$ | $\mathbf{2\pi\text{ rad}^{-1} \approx 0.11\text{ deg}^{-1}}$ (Universal!) |
| **Zero-Lift Angle of Attack ($\alpha_{L=0}$)** | $\mathbf{\alpha_{L=0} = 0}$ | $\boxed{\alpha_{L=0} = -\frac{1}{\pi}\int_0^\pi \frac{dz}{dx}(\cos\theta - 1)\,d\theta = \frac{1}{\pi}\int_0^\pi \frac{dz}{dx}d\theta - \frac{A_1}{2}}$ |
| **Pitching Moment at Quarter-Chord ($C_{m,c/4}$)** | $\mathbf{C_{m,c/4} = 0}$ | $\boxed{C_{m,c/4} = \frac{\pi}{4}(A_2 - A_1)}$ (Independent of $\alpha$!) |
| **Aerodynamic Center Location ($x_{ac}$)** | $\mathbf{x_{ac} = c/4 = 0.25c}$ | $\mathbf{x_{ac} = c/4 = 0.25c}$ (Universal for thin 2D!) |
| **Pitching Moment at Leading Edge ($C_{m,le}$)** | $C_{m,le} = -\frac{C_L}{4}$ | $\boxed{C_{m,le} = -\frac{C_L}{4} + C_{m,c/4} = -\frac{\pi}{2}\left(A_0 + A_1 - \frac{A_2}{2}\right)}$ |
| **Center of Pressure Location ($x_{cp}$)** | $\mathbf{x_{cp} = c/4}$ (Fixed at all $\alpha$) | $\boxed{x_{cp} = \frac{c}{4}\left[1 + \frac{\pi}{C_L}(A_1 - A_2)\right]}$ (Varies with $\alpha$) |

### Step 4 — High-Yield Camber Profiles & Glauert Integrals (15 min)

#### 🚀 Shortcut 1: Parabolic Camber Profile ($z(x) = 4h \frac{x}{c}\left(1 - \frac{x}{c}\right)$)
Where $h$ is maximum camber at mid-chord ($x = c/2$):
- Slope: $\frac{dz}{dx} = \frac{4h}{c}\left(1 - 2\frac{x}{c}\right) = \frac{4h}{c}\cos\theta$.
- Fourier coefficients: $A_0 = \alpha$, $A_1 = \frac{4h}{c}$, $A_2 = 0, A_n = 0$.
- **Aerodynamic Results for Parabolic Camber:**
  - $\alpha_{L=0} = -\frac{A_1}{2} = \mathbf{-\frac{2h}{c}\text{ rad}}$
  - $C_L = 2\pi\left(\alpha + \frac{2h}{c}\right)$
  - $C_{m,c/4} = \frac{\pi}{4}(0 - A_1) = \mathbf{-\frac{\pi h}{c}}$ (Negative $\implies$ Nose-down pitching moment!).

#### 🚀 Shortcut 2: Useful Definite Integrals
- $\int_0^\pi \cos(m\theta)\cos(n\theta) d\theta = \begin{cases} \pi/2 & m = n \neq 0 \\ \pi & m = n = 0 \\ 0 & m \neq n \end{cases}$
- $\int_0^\pi (1 - \cos\theta) d\theta = \pi$

### Step 5 — Traps & Self-Check (5 min)
> 🔴 **Trap 1: Aerodynamic Center vs. Center of Pressure**
> - **Aerodynamic Center ($x_{ac}$):** Point where $dC_m/d\alpha = 0$. Always at $c/4$ (quarter-chord) for 2D thin subsonic airfoils.
> - **Center of Pressure ($x_{cp}$):** Point where $C_m = 0$. Varies with angle of attack for cambered airfoils ($x_{cp} \to \infty$ as $C_L \to 0$).
> 🔴 **Trap 2: Pitching Moment Sign Convention**
> Nose-up moment is positive ($C_m > 0$). Cambered airfoils with positive camber produce a **negative (nose-down) moment** at quarter-chord ($C_{m,c/4} < 0$).

### Step 3 — D3 Mini Quiz (3:45–4:00) — 5 Questions (Timed 10 min)

> **Q1 (1 mark):** According to thin airfoil theory, the theoretical lift curve slope $dC_L/d\alpha$ for any 2D thin airfoil in incompressible flow is:

> **Q2 (2 marks):** A thin airfoil has a parabolic camber line with maximum camber $h = 0.02c$. The zero-lift angle of attack $\alpha_{L=0}$ (in degrees) is:
> **(A)** $-1.15^\circ$ **(B)** $-2.29^\circ$ **(C)** $+2.29^\circ$ **(D)** $0.0^\circ$

> **Q3 (2 marks):** For a thin cambered airfoil, the Fourier coefficients are $A_1 = 0.06$ and $A_2 = 0.02$. The pitching moment coefficient about the quarter-chord point $C_{m,c/4}$ is:
> **(A)** $+0.0314$ **(B)** $-0.0314$ **(C)** $-0.0628$ **(D)** $0.0$

> **Q4 (1 mark):** The aerodynamic center of a thin 2D airfoil in subsonic potential flow is located at:
> **(A)** $x = 0$ (Leading edge) **(B)** $x = 0.25c$ (Quarter-chord) **(C)** $x = 0.50c$ (Mid-chord) **(D)** Location depends on angle of attack

> **Q5 (2 marks NAT):** A thin symmetric airfoil is set at an angle of attack $\alpha = 5^\circ$. The section lift coefficient $C_L$ is ___ (round off to 2 decimal places).

> [!faq]- 🔍 View D3 Mini Quiz Answers & Step-by-Step Solutions
>
> **Q1: (B) 2π rad⁻¹**
> Thin airfoil theory predicts $dC_L/d\alpha = 2\pi\text{ rad}^{-1} \approx 0.1097\text{ deg}^{-1}$ for all thin 2D airfoils regardless of camber or thickness.
>
> **Q2: (B) -2.29°**
> For parabolic camber: $\alpha_{L=0} = -\frac{2h}{c}\text{ rad} = -2(0.02) = -0.04\text{ rad}$.
> In degrees: $\alpha_{L=0} = -0.04 \times \frac{180}{\pi} = -2.2918^\circ \approx \mathbf{-2.29^\circ}$.
>
> **Q3: (B) -0.0314**
> $C_{m,c/4} = \frac{\pi}{4}(A_2 - A_1) = \frac{\pi}{4}(0.02 - 0.06) = \frac{\pi}{4}(-0.04) = -0.01\pi \approx \mathbf{-0.0314}$.
>
> **Q4: (B) x = 0.25c**
> By definition, the aerodynamic center is the point about which pitching moment is invariant with $\alpha$. Thin airfoil theory proves $C_{m,c/4} = \text{const} \implies \mathbf{x_{ac} = 0.25c}$.
>
> **Q5: 0.55**
> For symmetric airfoil: $\alpha_{L=0} = 0$.
> $\alpha = 5^\circ = 5 \times \frac{\pi}{180} = \frac{\pi}{36}\text{ rad} \approx 0.08727\text{ rad}$.
> $C_L = 2\pi\alpha = 2\pi\left(\frac{\pi}{36}\right) = \frac{\pi^2}{18} \approx \frac{9.8696}{18} \approx \mathbf{0.55}$.

### 🌊 Set A — Thin Airfoil Theory & Vortex Sheets (FC-106 → FC-113)

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-106 · Vortex Sheet Definition & Circulation
> **Question:** Define the strength of a vortex sheet $\gamma(s)$ and write the formula for total circulation $\Gamma$ around an airfoil of chord $c$.
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > - $\gamma(s) = u_u(s) - u_l(s)$ (jump in tangential velocity across the sheet).
> > - $\Gamma = \int_0^c \gamma(x) \, dx$.
> > - Lift per unit span: $L' = \rho_\infty V_\infty \Gamma = \rho_\infty V_\infty \int_0^c \gamma(x) \, dx$.
> > ⚠️ **AIR 1 Trap:** The normal velocity across a vortex sheet is CONTINUOUS ($v_u = v_l$), while tangential velocity is discontinuous!

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-107 · The Kutta Condition at the Trailing Edge
> **Question:** State the mathematical condition imposed by the Kutta condition on vortex sheet strength at the trailing edge $x = c$.
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > $$\gamma(c) = 0 \quad (\text{or in Glauert angle: } \gamma(\pi) = 0)$$
> > - For a cusped trailing edge: velocities on upper and lower surfaces are equal in magnitude and direction ($V_u = V_l$).
> > - For a finite-angle trailing edge: the trailing edge is a stagnation point ($V_u = V_l = 0$).

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-108 · Kelvin's Circulation Theorem & Starting Vortex
> **Question:** Explain the relationship between the starting vortex and the bound circulation around an airfoil when starting from rest.
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > By Kelvin's theorem: $\frac{D\Gamma_{\text{total}}}{Dt} = 0 \implies \Gamma_{\text{total}} = \text{const} = 0$.
> > $$\Gamma_{\text{bound}} + \Gamma_{\text{starting}} = 0 \implies \Gamma_{\text{bound}} = -\Gamma_{\text{starting}}$$
> > The starting vortex is shed at the trailing edge with circulation $-\Gamma$ and left behind on the runway, establishing clockwise circulation $+\Gamma$ around the wing that generates lift.

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-109 · Fundamental Equation of Thin Airfoil Theory
> **Question:** Write the governing integral equation of thin airfoil theory relating camber line slope $dy_c/dx$ to vortex distribution $\gamma(\xi)$.
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > $$V_\infty \left( \alpha - \frac{dy_c}{dx} \right) = \frac{1}{2\pi} \int_0^c \frac{\gamma(\xi)}{x - \xi} \, d\xi$$
> > This enforces the flow tangency boundary condition along the mean camber line placed on $y=0$.

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-110 · Glauert Coordinate Transformation
> **Question:** State the Glauert transformation from chord coordinate $x$ to angle $\theta$, and identify the values of $\theta$ at the leading edge, quarter-chord, mid-chord, and trailing edge.
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > $$x = \frac{c}{2}(1 - \cos\theta)$$
> > - Leading edge ($x = 0$): $\theta = 0$
> > - Quarter-chord ($x = c/4$): $\theta = \pi/3$ ($60^\circ$, since $\cos 60^\circ = 0.5 \implies 1 - 0.5 = 0.5 \implies x = c/4$)
> > - Mid-chord ($x = c/2$): $\theta = \pi/2$ ($90^\circ$)
> > - Trailing edge ($x = c$): $\theta = \pi$ ($180^\circ$)

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-111 · Glauert Integral Identity
> **Question:** State the Cauchy principal value integral identity used to solve the thin airfoil integral equation.
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > $$\int_0^\pi \frac{\cos(n\theta_0)}{\cos\theta_0 - \cos\theta} \, d\theta_0 = \pi \frac{\sin(n\theta)}{\sin\theta}$$
> > - For $n = 0$: $\int_0^\pi \frac{1}{\cos\theta_0 - \cos\theta} \, d\theta_0 = 0$.
> > - For $n = 1$: $\int_0^\pi \frac{\cos\theta_0}{\cos\theta_0 - \cos\theta} \, d\theta_0 = \pi$.

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-112 · Symmetric Airfoil Vortex Strength
> **Question:** What is the analytical expression for $\gamma(\theta)$ for a flat plate / symmetric airfoil at angle of attack $\alpha$?
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > $$\gamma(\theta) = 2 V_\infty \alpha \frac{1 + \cos\theta}{\sin\theta} = 2 V_\infty \alpha \cot\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)$$
> > At leading edge ($\theta \to 0$): $\gamma \to \infty$ (leading edge suction singularity).  
> > At trailing edge ($\theta = \pi$): $\gamma = 0$ (satisfies Kutta condition!).

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-113 · 2D Theoretical Lift-Curve Slope
> **Question:** What is the theoretical lift-curve slope $a_0 = dc_l/d\alpha$ of a thin airfoil in subsonic flow in radians and degrees?
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > - $a_0 = 2\pi\text{ rad}^{-1} \approx 6.283185\text{ rad}^{-1}$
> > - In degrees: $a_0 = \frac{2\pi}{180/\pi} = \frac{\pi^2}{90} \approx 0.109662\text{ deg}^{-1}$
> > ⚠️ **AIR 1 Trap:** Airfoil thickness and viscosity slightly alter this in real tests ($a_0 \approx 0.105$–$0.108\text{ deg}^{-1}$), but theoretical thin airfoil theory is strictly $2\pi$.

### 📐 Set B — Engineering Mathematics: Eigenvalues & Cayley-Hamilton (FC-114 → FC-120)

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-114 · Eigenvalue Sum and Product Properties
> **Question:** For an $n \times n$ matrix $A$, state the relationship between its eigenvalues $\lambda_i$, its trace, and its determinant.
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > - Sum of eigenvalues = Trace of matrix: $\sum_{i=1}^n \lambda_i = \operatorname{tr}(A) = \sum_{i=1}^n a_{ii}$.
> > - Product of eigenvalues = Determinant of matrix: $\prod_{i=1}^n \lambda_i = \det(A)$.
> > ⚠️ **AIR 1 Trap:** If ANY eigenvalue is zero, $\det(A) = 0 \implies$ matrix is singular and not invertible!

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-115 · Eigenvalues of Special Matrix Classes
> **Question:** State the nature of eigenvalues for: (1) Real Symmetric, (2) Real Skew-Symmetric, (3) Orthogonal, (4) Triangular/Diagonal matrices.
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > 1. **Real Symmetric:** All eigenvalues are strictly **real**.
> > 2. **Real Skew-Symmetric ($A^T = -A$):** All eigenvalues are **purely imaginary** ($i\beta$) or **zero**.
> > 3. **Orthogonal ($A^T A = I$):** All eigenvalues have unit modulus ($|\lambda| = 1 \implies \lambda = e^{i\theta}$).
> > 4. **Triangular / Diagonal:** Eigenvalues are simply the **diagonal elements**!

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-116 · Cayley-Hamilton Theorem
> **Question:** State the Cayley-Hamilton Theorem and explain how to compute $A^{-1}$ and $A^k$ using it.
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > Every square matrix satisfies its own characteristic equation:
> > $$P(\lambda) = \det(A - \lambda I) = 0 \implies P(A) = A^n + c_{n-1}A^{n-1} + \dots + c_0 I = \mathbf{0}$$
> > - To find $A^{-1}$: Multiply by $A^{-1}$:
> >   $$A^{-1} = -\frac{1}{c_0} \left[ A^{n-1} + c_{n-1} A^{n-2} + \dots + c_1 I \right] \quad (\text{valid iff } c_0 = (-1)^n \det(A) \ne 0)$$
> > - To find $A^k$ for large $k$: Divide $\lambda^k$ by characteristic polynomial $P(\lambda) = Q(\lambda)P(\lambda) + R(\lambda)$ where $\deg(R) < n$, so $A^k = R(A)$.

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-117 · Algebraic vs Geometric Multiplicity
> **Question:** Define Algebraic Multiplicity (AM) and Geometric Multiplicity (GM) of an eigenvalue $\lambda$. What is the inequality relating them?
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > - **AM:** The multiplicity of $\lambda$ as a root of the characteristic polynomial $\det(A - \lambda I) = 0$.
> > - **GM:** The dimension of the eigenspace corresponding to $\lambda$ (number of linearly independent eigenvectors):
> >   $$\text{GM} = \operatorname{nullity}(A - \lambda I) = n - \operatorname{rank}(A - \lambda I)$$
> > - Universal Inequality: $1 \le \text{GM} \le \text{AM}$.

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-118 · Diagonalizability Condition
> **Question:** What is the necessary and sufficient condition for an $n \times n$ matrix $A$ to be diagonalizable?
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > A matrix $A$ is diagonalizable ($A = P D P^{-1}$) if and only if:
> > 1. It possesses $n$ linearly independent eigenvectors, OR
> > 2. For every eigenvalue $\lambda_i$, its geometric multiplicity equals its algebraic multiplicity ($\text{GM}_i = \text{AM}_i$).
> > ⚠️ **AIR 1 Trap:** If all $n$ eigenvalues are distinct, the matrix is ALWAYS diagonalizable. If eigenvalues repeat, it may or may not be diagonalizable (e.g. $\begin{pmatrix}1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1\end{pmatrix}$ has $\lambda=1$ with $\text{AM}=2, \text{GM}=1 \implies$ NOT diagonalizable).

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-119 · Spectral Theorem for Real Symmetric Matrices
> **Question:** What special diagonalization property holds for any real symmetric matrix?
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > Every real symmetric matrix $A$ can be **orthogonally diagonalized**:
> > $$A = Q D Q^T$$
> > where $Q$ is an orthogonal matrix ($Q^T = Q^{-1}$) whose columns are pairwise orthogonal unit eigenvectors of $A$, and $D$ is the diagonal matrix of real eigenvalues.

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-120 · Quadratic Form Definiteness Tests
> **Question:** State Sylvester's criterion and the eigenvalue criterion for a real symmetric matrix $A$ to be Positive Definite.
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > A real symmetric matrix $A$ is **Positive Definite** ($Q(\vec{x}) = \vec{x}^T A \vec{x} > 0$ for all $\vec{x} \ne \mathbf{0}$) if and only if:
> > 1. **Eigenvalue test:** All eigenvalues of $A$ are strictly positive ($\lambda_i > 0$).
> > 2. **Sylvester's criterion:** All leading principal minors are strictly positive ($\Delta_1 > 0, \Delta_2 > 0, \dots, \Delta_n > 0$).

### 🔬 Apex Worked Example 22: Symmetric Airfoil Circulation & Lift Integration

> [!example]- 🔥 WE-22 · Vortex Sheet Integration on a Flat Plate Airfoil
> **Problem Statement (GATE Level 4 NAT):**  
> A symmetric flat plate airfoil of chord $c = 1.200\text{ m}$ operates at angle of attack $\alpha = 4.50^\circ$ in an airflow with freestream velocity $V_\infty = 45.0\text{ m/s}$ and air density $\rho_\infty = 1.225\text{ kg/m}^3$.  
> 
> **Tasks:**  
> 1. Calculate the total circulation $\Gamma$ around the airfoil in $\text{m}^2/\text{s}$.  
> 2. Determine the section lift coefficient $c_l$.  
> 3. Calculate the lift per unit span $L'$ in $\text{N/m}$.  
> 4. Determine the strength of the vortex sheet $\gamma$ at the quarter-chord station ($x = c/4$) in $\text{m/s}$.
> 
> > [!success]- **Complete Analytical Solution & TCS Calculator Keystroke Ledger:**
> > 
> > #### Step 1: Angle of Attack in Radians
> > $$\alpha = 4.50^\circ \times \frac{\pi}{180} = \frac{4.5\pi}{180} = \frac{\pi}{40} = 0.0785398\text{ rad}$$
> > 
> > #### Step 2: Total Circulation ($\Gamma$)
> > For a flat plate in thin airfoil theory:
> > $$\Gamma = \pi c V_\infty \alpha$$
> > $$\Gamma = \pi \times 1.200 \times 45.0 \times 0.0785398 = 54.0 \pi \times 0.0785398 = 169.646 \times 0.0785398 = 13.3240\text{ m}^2/\text{s}$$
> > 
> > #### Step 3: Section Lift Coefficient ($c_l$)
> > $$c_l = 2\pi\alpha = 2\pi \times \frac{\pi}{40} = \frac{\pi^2}{20} = \frac{9.869604}{20} = 0.49348$$

> > 
> > #### Step 4: Lift per Unit Span ($L'$)

> > $$L' = \rho_\infty V_\infty \Gamma = 1.225 \times 45.0 \times 13.3240 = 55.125 \times 13.3240 = 734.485\text{ N/m}$$

> > $$q_\infty = \frac{1}{2}\rho_\infty V_\infty^2 = \frac{1}{2} \times 1.225 \times 45^2 = 0.6125 \times 2025 = 1240.3125\text{ Pa}$$

### 🔬 Apex Worked Example 23: Kelvin's Circulation Theorem & Starting Vortex

> [!example]- 🔥 WE-23 · Velocity Field of Starting Vortex on Runway
> **Problem Statement (GATE AE Level 4 NAT):**  
> An aircraft of total mass $m = 1800\text{ kg}$ and wingspan $b = 12.0\text{ m}$ takes off horizontally at flight speed $V_\infty = 50.0\text{ m/s}$ at sea-level air density $\rho = 1.225\text{ kg/m}^3$. Assume elliptic lift distribution on the wing.  
> 
> **Tasks:**  
> 1. Compute the bound vortex circulation $\Gamma_0$ at the center of the wing in steady flight ($g = 9.81\text{ m/s}^2$).  
> 2. Determine the circulation $\Gamma_s$ of the starting vortex shed at the lift-off point.  
> 3. Calculate the magnitude of the horizontal induced velocity $u_{\text{ind}}$ produced by this starting vortex on a ground anemometer located $15.0\text{ m}$ directly behind the lift-off point at ground level (assuming the vortex was shed at height $h = 3.0\text{ m}$).
> 
> > [!success]- **Complete Analytical Solution & Verification:**
> > 
> > #### Step 1: Bound Vortex Circulation ($\Gamma_0$)
> > Total aircraft lift equals weight:
> > $$L = m g = 1800 \times 9.81 = 17658.0\text{ N}$$
> > For an elliptic wing:
> > $$L = \frac{1}{2}\rho_\infty V_\infty \pi b \Gamma_0$$
> > $$\Gamma_0 = \frac{2 L}{\rho_\infty V_\infty \pi b} = \frac{2 \times 17658.0}{1.225 \times 50.0 \times \pi \times 12.0} = \frac{35316.0}{2309.07} = 15.2945\text{ m}^2/\text{s}$$
> > Target Answer: **$\Gamma_0 = 15.29\text{ m}^2/\text{s}$**
> > 
> > #### Step 2: Starting Vortex Circulation
> > By Kelvin's circulation theorem:
> > $$\Gamma_{\text{starting}} = -\Gamma_0 = -15.2945\text{ m}^2/\text{s}$$
> > (Circulation has magnitude $15.29\text{ m}^2/\text{s}$ with counter-clockwise rotation).
> > 
> > #### Step 3: Induced Velocity at Anemometer
> > Distance from vortex center to anemometer:

### 🔬 Apex Worked Example 24: Cayley-Hamilton Theorem Applied to Matrix Powers

> [!example]- 🔥 WE-24 · Matrix Exponentiation via Cayley-Hamilton Theorem
> **Problem Statement (GATE Level 4 NAT):**  
> Consider the matrix:
> $$A = \begin{pmatrix} 4 & 1 \\ -2 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$$
> **Tasks:**  
> 1. Determine the characteristic polynomial $P(\lambda)$ and eigenvalues of $A$.  
> 2. Express $A^5$ in the linear form $\alpha A + \beta I$ using the Cayley-Hamilton theorem.  
> 3. Calculate the exact $(1, 1)$ entry of $A^5$.
> 
> > [!success]- **Complete Analytical Solution & Verification:**
> > 
> > #### Step 1: Characteristic Equation & Eigenvalues
> > $$P(\lambda) = \det(A - \lambda I) = \begin{vmatrix} 4 - \lambda & 1 \\ -2 & 1 - \lambda \end{vmatrix} = (4 - \lambda)(1 - \lambda) - (1)(-2)$$
> > $$= \lambda^2 - 5\lambda + 4 + 2 = \lambda^2 - 5\lambda + 6 = (\lambda - 2)(\lambda - 3) = 0$$
> > Eigenvalues: **$\lambda_1 = 2, \lambda_2 = 3$**.
> > 
> > #### Step 2: Division of Polynomial $\lambda^5$
> > Divide $\lambda^5$ by characteristic polynomial $P(\lambda) = \lambda^2 - 5\lambda + 6$:
> > $$\lambda^5 = Q(\lambda)(\lambda^2 - 5\lambda + 6) + (\alpha \lambda + \beta)$$
> > Substituting the eigenvalues $\lambda = 2$ and $\lambda = 3$:
> > - At $\lambda = 2$:
> >   $$2^5 = 32 = \alpha(2) + \beta \implies 2\alpha + \beta = 32$$
> > - At $\lambda = 3$:
> >   $$3^5 = 243 = \alpha(3) + \beta \implies 3\alpha + \beta = 243$$
> > 
> > Subtracting the first equation from the second:
> > $$\alpha = 243 - 32 = 211$$
> > Solving for $\beta$:
> > $$\beta = 32 - 2(211) = 32 - 422 = -390$$
> > Therefore, by Cayley-Hamilton theorem ($P(A) = \mathbf{0}$):
> > $$A^5 = 211 A - 390 I$$
> > 
> > #### Step 3: Exact Entry $(A^5)_{11}$
> > $$(A^5)_{11} = 211 (A)_{11} - 390 (I)_{11} = 211(4) - 390(1) = 844 - 390 = 454$$
> > Target Answer: **$(A^5)_{11} = 454$** · Official Tolerance: **$[454, 454]$**

### 🌊 Set A — Circular Cylinder with Circulation & D'Alembert (FC-76 → FC-83)

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-76 · Lifting Cylinder Stream Function & Velocity
> **Question:** Write the stream function $\psi(r, \theta)$ and tangential velocity $V_\theta(R, \theta)$ on the surface of a circular cylinder of radius $R$ in uniform flow $V_\infty$ with clockwise circulation $\Gamma$.
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > - Stream function: $\psi(r, \theta) = V_\infty \sin\theta \left(r - \frac{R^2}{r}\right) + \frac{\Gamma}{2\pi} \ln\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)$
> > - Surface velocity ($r = R$): $V_r = 0, \quad V_\theta = -2 V_\infty \sin\theta - \frac{\Gamma}{2\pi R}$
> > ⚠️ **AIR-1 Trap:** Clockwise circulation adds to the velocity on top ($\theta = 90^\circ, \sin\theta = 1 \implies |V_\theta| = 2 V_\infty + \frac{\Gamma}{2\pi R}$) and subtracts from the velocity on the bottom, generating lower pressure on top $\implies$ UPWARD LIFT.

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-77 · Surface Stagnation Point Bifurcation Condition
> **Question:** What is the equation determining the angular location of stagnation points on the cylinder surface, and what is the critical circulation $\Gamma_{\text{crit}}$?
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > - Surface equation: $V_\theta = 0 \implies \sin\theta_{\text{stag}} = -\frac{\Gamma}{4\pi R V_\infty}$
> > - Critical circulation: $\Gamma_{\text{crit}} = 4\pi R V_\infty$
> > ⚠️ **AIR-1 Trap:** For $\Gamma < \Gamma_{\text{crit}}$, there are TWO surface stagnation points. For $\Gamma = \Gamma_{\text{crit}}$, they merge into ONE point at $\theta = 270^\circ$ (bottom).

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-78 · Supercritical Stagnation Radius ($\Gamma > 4\pi R V_\infty$)
> **Question:** When circulation exceeds critical ($\Gamma > 4\pi R V_\infty$), where does the stagnation point go, and what is its radial coordinate?
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > - It leaves the surface and moves out into the fluid along the vertical axis at $\theta = 270^\circ$.
> > - Physical radius:
> > $$r_{\text{stag}} = \frac{\Gamma}{4\pi V_\infty} + \sqrt{\left(\frac{\Gamma}{4\pi V_\infty}\right)^2 - R^2} > R$$
> > ⚠️ **AIR-1 Trap:** A closed recirculating fluid region surrounds the cylinder; the external freestream flows around this entire recirculating bubble without touching the cylinder surface.

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-79 · Kutta-Joukowski Theorem
> **Question:** State the Kutta-Joukowski theorem for 2D aerodynamic lift per unit span $L'$, and specify its geometric validity bounds.
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > - Formula: $L' = \rho_\infty V_\infty \Gamma$
> > - Direction: Perpendicular to the freestream velocity vector $\vec{V}_\infty$ (rotated $90^\circ$ in the direction opposite to circulation).
> > - **Validity:** Universally valid for 2D bodies of **ANY arbitrary shape** (airfoils, cylinders, flat plates) in steady, inviscid, incompressible potential flow!
> > ⚠️ **AIR-1 Trap:** Lift depends ONLY on freestream density, velocity, and circulation. It does NOT depend on body shape or thickness in potential flow!

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-80 · Cylinder Lift Coefficient Definition
> **Question:** Express the section lift coefficient $C_L$ of a circular cylinder of radius $R$ in terms of circulation $\Gamma$, freestream velocity $V_\infty$, and radius $R$.
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > $$C_L = \frac{L'}{q_\infty (2R)} = \frac{\rho_\infty V_\infty \Gamma}{\frac{1}{2}\rho_\infty V_\infty^2 (2R)} = \frac{\Gamma}{R V_\infty}$$
> > ⚠️ **AIR-1 Trap:** The reference area per unit span for a cylinder is its projected **diameter $D = 2R$**, NOT radius $R$! If you divide by $R$, you overestimate $C_L$ by a factor of 2.

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-81 · D'Alembert's Paradox Mathematical Mechanism
> **Question:** Prove why the drag per unit span $D'$ on a lifting circular cylinder is identically zero in potential flow.
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > - $D' = -\int_0^{2\pi} p(\theta) \cos\theta \, R \, d\theta$.
> > - $p(\theta)$ consists of terms proportional to a constant, $\sin^2\theta$, $\sin\theta$, and $\Gamma^2$.
> > - When multiplied by $\cos\theta$, every single term integrates to zero over $[0, 2\pi]$:
> >   $\int \cos\theta d\theta = 0, \int \sin\theta\cos\theta d\theta = 0, \int \sin^2\theta\cos\theta d\theta = 0$.
> > - Therefore: $D' \equiv 0 \implies C_D \equiv 0$.
> > ⚠️ **AIR-1 Trap:** Zero drag holds EVEN WITH circulation! Circulation generates lift, but drag remains strictly zero.

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-82 · The Drag Crisis on a Circular Cylinder
> **Question:** What is the "Drag Crisis" on a circular cylinder in real flow, at what critical Reynolds number does it occur, and what happens to $C_D$?
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > - At $Re_{\text{crit}} \approx 3 \times 10^5$ (based on diameter $D$), the boundary layer transitions from laminar to turbulent upstream of separation.
> > - The turbulent boundary layer possesses higher near-wall momentum, delaying separation from $\theta \approx 82^\circ$ to $\theta \approx 120^\circ$.
> > - The wake narrows dramatically, causing $C_D$ to plunge from $\approx 1.2$ down to $\approx 0.3$!
> > ⚠️ **AIR-1 Trap:** Dimples on golf balls artificially trip the boundary layer into turbulence to trigger this drag crisis at lower flight Reynolds numbers.

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-83 · Magnus Effect Application: Flettner Rotors
> **Question:** Explain how a spinning cylinder generates lift in a crosswind (Magnus Effect), and state how circulation is established.
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > - Skin friction drags the thin boundary layer with the spinning surface at peripheral speed $u_w = \Omega R$.
> > - This boundary layer entrainment induces a net circulation $\Gamma \approx 2\pi R (\alpha \Omega R)$ where $\alpha \approx 0.5 - 1.0$.
> > - Crosswind $V_\infty$ acting on this circulation creates lift $L' = \rho_\infty V_\infty \Gamma$.
> > ⚠️ **AIR-1 Trap:** In potential flow, fluid slips on walls; real circulation on spinning cylinders is born from viscosity (boundary layer shear)!

### 📐 Set B — Single-Variable Maxima, Minima & Optimization (FC-84 → FC-90)

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-84 · Necessary vs Sufficient Conditions for Extrema
> **Question:** For a function $f(x)$ differentiable on an open interval, state the necessary condition for an interior extremum and the second derivative test criteria.
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > - **Necessary Condition (Fermat's Theorem):** If $f(x)$ has a local extremum at $x_0$, then $f'(x_0) = 0$ (stationary point).
> > - **Second Derivative Test:**
> >   - $f''(x_0) > 0 \implies$ Local **Minimum** (Concave Up).
> >   - $f''(x_0) < 0 \implies$ Local **Maximum** (Concave Down).
> >   - $f''(x_0) = 0 \implies$ Inconclusive! Must check higher derivatives or sign change of $f'(x)$.

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-85 · Point of Inflection Criteria
> **Question:** State the precise mathematical condition for $x_0$ to be a point of inflection of $f(x)$.
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > - A point of inflection is a point where the **concavity changes sign** (from concave up to concave down, or vice versa).
> > - Necessary: $f''(x_0) = 0$ (or $f''(x_0)$ does not exist).
> > - Sufficient: $f''(x)$ must change sign across $x_0 \iff f'''(x_0) \neq 0$ (if $f'''$ exists).
> > ⚠️ **AIR-1 Trap:** $f''(x_0) = 0$ does NOT guarantee an inflection point! For $f(x) = x^4$, $f''(0) = 0$, but $x=0$ is a strict minimum, not an inflection point (no concavity change).

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-86 · Higher-Order Derivative Test
> **Question:** If $f'(x_0) = f''(x_0) = \dots = f^{(n-1)}(x_0) = 0$ and $f^{(n)}(x_0) \neq 0$, state the rule to classify $x_0$.
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > - **Case 1: $n$ is EVEN:**
> >   - If $f^{(n)}(x_0) < 0 \implies$ Local **Maximum**.
> >   - If $f^{(n)}(x_0) > 0 \implies$ Local **Minimum**.
> > - **Case 2: $n$ is ODD:**
> >   - $x_0$ is a **Point of Inflection** (neither max nor min).
> > ⚠️ **AIR-1 Trap:** Memorize $f(x) = x^3 \implies f'''(0) = 6 \neq 0$ ($n=3$ odd $\implies$ inflection) and $f(x) = x^4 \implies f^{(4)}(0) = 24 > 0$ ($n=4$ even $\implies$ minimum).

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-87 · Global vs Local Extrema on Closed Intervals
> **Question:** How do you find the absolute (global) maximum and minimum of a continuous function $f(x)$ on a closed bounded interval $[a, b]$?
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > - **Extreme Value Theorem:** A continuous function on $[a, b]$ always attains its global max and min.
> > - Protocol:
> >   1. Find all critical points $c_i \in (a, b)$ where $f'(c_i) = 0$ or $f'$ does not exist.
> >   2. Evaluate $f(c_i)$ at all critical points.
> >   3. Evaluate $f(a)$ and $f(b)$ at the boundary endpoints!
> >   4. Global Max $= \max(f(a), f(b), f(c_i))$; Global Min $= \min(f(a), f(b), f(c_i))$.
> > ⚠️ **AIR-1 Trap:** Never forget the boundary endpoints $a$ and $b$! GATE questions frequently construct functions where the global extremum lies at the boundary, not at $f'(x) = 0$.

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-88 · AM-GM Inequality Shortcut for Optimization
> **Question:** State the Arithmetic Mean - Geometric Mean (AM-GM) inequality and how it finds minima of positive terms without differentiation.
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > - For non-negative real numbers $a, b \ge 0$:
> >   $$\frac{a + b}{2} \ge \sqrt{ab} \implies a + b \ge 2\sqrt{ab}$$
> > - Equality holds if and only if $a = b$.
> > - **Optimization Shortcut:** If the product $a \cdot b = \text{const} = K$, the sum $a + b$ achieves its minimum value $2\sqrt{K}$ when $a = b$.
> > ⚠️ **AIR-1 Trap:** For $f(x) = A x + \frac{B}{x}$ ($x > 0$), minimum occurs when $A x = B/x \implies x = \sqrt{B/A}$ with $f_{\min} = 2\sqrt{AB}$ in 1 second without derivatives!

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-89 · Aerodynamic Drag Polar Optimization
> **Question:** For an aircraft with parabolic drag polar $C_D = C_{D,0} + K C_L^2$, find the lift coefficient $C_L$ that maximizes the lift-to-drag ratio $(L/D)$.
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > - Objective: Maximize $\frac{L}{D} = \frac{C_L}{C_D} = \frac{C_L}{C_{D,0} + K C_L^2} \iff$ Minimize $\frac{C_D}{C_L} = \frac{C_{D,0}}{C_L} + K C_L$.
> > - By AM-GM (or setting $\frac{d}{d C_L}(C_D/C_L) = 0$):
> >   $$\frac{C_{D,0}}{C_L} = K C_L \implies C_L^2 = \frac{C_{D,0}}{K} \implies C_{L, \text{opt}} = \sqrt{\frac{C_{D,0}}{K}}$$
> > - At $(L/D)_{\max}$: Parasite drag = Induced drag ($C_{D,0} = K C_L^2 \implies C_D = 2 C_{D,0}$).
> > - Maximum efficiency: $\left(\frac{L}{D}\right)_{\max} = \frac{1}{2\sqrt{K C_{D,0}}}$.

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-90 · Minimum Thrust Required Condition (Jet Aircraft)
> **Question:** In level unaccelerated flight, what condition minimizes thrust required $T_R$, and how does it relate to $(L/D)_{\max}$?
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > - In steady level flight: $L = W$ and $T_R = D = \frac{W}{L/D}$.
> > - Minimizing thrust required is IDENTICAL to maximizing $L/D$:
> >   $$T_{R,\min} = \frac{W}{(L/D)_{\max}} = 2 W \sqrt{K C_{D,0}}$$
> > - Occurs at airspeed: $V_{\text{md}} = \sqrt{\frac{2 W}{\rho_\infty S \sqrt{C_{D,0}/K}}}$.
> > ⚠️ **AIR-1 Trap:** For propeller aircraft, minimum POWER required occurs at $C_{D,0} = \frac{1}{3} C_{D,i}$ ($C_L = \sqrt{3 C_{D,0}/K}$), NOT at $(L/D)_{\max}$!

### 🔬 Apex Worked Example 16: Complete Stagnation Point Bifurcation Analysis

> [!example]- 🔥 WE-16 · Supercritical Stagnation Point Coordinates of a Rotating Cylinder
> **Problem Statement (GATE AE Level 5 NAT):**  
> A circular cylinder of radius $R = 0.400\text{ m}$ is placed in a horizontal wind tunnel stream with $V_\infty = 20.0\text{ m/s}$. The cylinder is spun to generate a clockwise circulation $\Gamma = 120.0\text{ m}^2/\text{s}$.  
> 
> **Tasks:**  
> 1. Calculate the critical circulation $\Gamma_{\text{crit}}$ in $\text{m}^2/\text{s}$ required to produce a single merged stagnation point on the surface.  
> 2. Determine whether the current circulation is subcritical, critical, or supercritical.  
> 3. Calculate the exact $(x, y)$ Cartesian coordinates in meters of the physical stagnation point in the flow field.  
> 4. Determine the flow velocity on the top shoulder ($\theta = 90^\circ$) of the cylinder in $\text{m/s}$.
> 
> > [!success]- **Complete Analytical Solution & TCS Calculator Keystroke Ledger:**
> > 
> > #### Step 1: Critical Circulation ($\Gamma_{\text{crit}}$)
> > $$\Gamma_{\text{crit}} = 4\pi R V_\infty = 4 \pi \times 0.400 \times 20.0 = 32.0 \pi = 100.53096\text{ m}^2/\text{s}$$
> > 
> > #### Step 2: Regime Classification
> > Given $\Gamma = 120.0\text{ m}^2/\text{s}$.  
> > Since $\Gamma = 120.0 > \Gamma_{\text{crit}} \approx 100.53\text{ m}^2/\text{s}$, the circulation is **SUPERCRITICAL (Regime 3)**!  
> > The stagnation points have coalesced and migrated off the cylinder surface into the flowing fluid.
> > 
> > #### Step 3: Coordinates of the Off-Body Stagnation Point
> > For $\Gamma > \Gamma_{\text{crit}}$, the physical stagnation point lies on the vertical line $\theta = 270^\circ$ ($-\pi/2$ on the $-y$ axis, where $x = 0$ and $y = -r$).  
> > The radial distance satisfies:
> > $$r_{\text{stag}} = \frac{\Gamma}{4\pi V_\infty} + \sqrt{\left(\frac{\Gamma}{4\pi V_\infty}\right)^2 - R^2}$$
> > 
> > Calculating intermediate terms:
> > $$\frac{\Gamma}{4\pi V_\infty} = \frac{120.0}{4 \pi \times 20.0} = \frac{120.0}{80 \pi} = \frac{1.5}{\pi} = 0.4774648\text{ m}$$
> > Note that:
> > $$\frac{\Gamma}{4\pi V_\infty} = \frac{\Gamma}{\Gamma_{\text{crit}}} R = \left(\frac{120.0}{100.53096}\right) \times 0.400 = 1.19366 \times 0.400 = 0.4774648\text{ m}$$
> >

### 🔬 Apex Worked Example 18: Aircraft Performance Optimization via Calculus

> [!example]- 🔥 WE-18 · Optimization of Aircraft Climb Angle & $(L/D)_{\max}$ Airspeed
> **Problem Statement (GATE Level 4 NAT):**  
> A UAV has gross mass $m = 1200\text{ kg}$ (weight $W = m g = 1200 \times 9.81 = 11772\text{ N}$) and wing area $S = 16.0\text{ m}^2$.  
> Its aerodynamic drag polar is given by:
> $$C_D = 0.020 + 0.050 C_L^2$$
> Flight occurs at sea level where air density $\rho_\infty = 1.225\text{ kg/m}^3$.  
> 
> **Tasks:**  
> 1. Use the first and second derivative tests to find the lift coefficient $C_{L, \text{opt}}$ that minimizes the drag coefficient to lift coefficient ratio $(C_D / C_L)$.  
> 2. Determine the maximum lift-to-drag ratio $(L/D)_{\max}$.  
> 3. Calculate the true airspeed $V_{\text{opt}}$ in $\text{m/s}$ corresponding to flight at $(L/D)_{\max}$.  
> 4. Verify the result using the AM-GM inequality shortcut.
> 
> > [!success]- **Complete Analytical Solution & Verification:**
> > 
> > #### Step 1: Calculus Derivation of $C_{L, \text{opt}}$
> > Objective function:
> > $$f(C_L) = \frac{C_D}{C_L} = \frac{0.020 + 0.050 C_L^2}{C_L} = \frac{0.020}{C_L} + 0.050 C_L$$
> > Differentiating with respect to $C_L$:
> > $$f'(C_L) = -\frac{0.020}{C_L^2} + 0.050 = 0$$
> > $$0.050 C_L^2 = 0.020 \implies C_L^2 = \frac{0.020}{0.050} = \frac{2}{5} = 0.400$$
> > $$C_{L, \text{opt}} = \sqrt{0.400} = 0.6324555$$
> > 
> > Second Derivative Test:
> > $$f''(C_L) = \frac{2 \times 0.020}{C_L^3} = \frac{0.040}{(0.6325)^3} > 0$$
> > Since $f''(C_{L, \text{opt}}) > 0$, the function achieves a strict **Local and Global Minimum**!  
> > Rounded to 3 decimal places: **$C_{L, \text{opt}} = 0.632$**
> > 
> > #### Step 2: Maximum Lift-to-Drag Ratio
> > At $C_{L, \text{opt}}$:
> > Parasite drag: $C_{D,0} = 0.020$.  
> > Induced drag: $C_{D,i} = 0.050 C_L^2 = 0.050 \times 0.400 = 0.020$.  
> > Notice: $C_{D,0} = C_{D,i}$! Total drag $C_D = 0.020 + 0.020 = 0.040$.  
> > $$(L/D)_{\max} = \frac{C_{L, \text{opt}}}{C_D} = \frac{0.6324555}{0.040} = 15.81139$$
> > Rounded to 2 decimal places: **$(L/D)_{\max} = 15.81$**  
> > Official Tolerance Window: **$[15.75, 15.85]$**
> > 
> > #### Step 3: Optimal Airspeed ($V_{\text{opt}}$)
> > In steady level flight: $L = W = \frac{1}{2} \rho_\infty V^2 S C_L$.
> > $$V_{\text{opt}} = \sqrt{\frac{2 W}{\rho_\infty S C_{L, \text{opt}}}} = \sqrt{\frac{2 \times 11772}{1.225 \times 16.0 \times 0.6324555}}$$
> > Denominator: $1.225 \times 16.0 = 19.60 \implies 19.60 \times 0.6324555 = 12.39613$.  
> > Fraction: $\frac{23544}{12.39613} = 1899.3025$.  
> > Square root:
> > $$V_{\text{opt}} = \sqrt{1899.3025} = 43.58099\text{ m/s}$$
> > Rounded to 2 decimal places: **$V_{\text{opt}} = 43.58\text{ m/s}$**  
> > Official Tolerance Window: **$[43.40, 43.70]\text{ m/s}$**
> > 
> > #### Step 4: AM-GM Verification
> > $f(C_L) = \frac{0.020}{C_L} + 0.050 C_L \ge 2\sqrt{\frac{0.020}{C_L} \cdot 0.050 C_L} = 2\sqrt{0.0010} = 2(0.03162278) = 0.06324555$.  
> > $(L/D)_{\max} = \frac{1}{f_{\min}} = \frac{1}{0.06324555} = 15.8114$ ✅

### Step 2 — International Standard Atmosphere (ISA Model) (20 min)
Open `01.1 - Standard Atmosphere`.

```
                    ISA TEMPERATURE PROFILE
          Altitude h (km)
             20 km ┌───────────────────┐  Lower Stratosphere
                   │ (Isothermal Layer)│  T = const = 216.65 K (-56.5°C)
             11 km ├── ── ── ── ── ── ─┤  Tropopause (11,000 m)
                   │  T = T_0 − L · h  │  Troposphere (Lapse Rate L = 6.5 K/km)
              0 km └───────────────────┘  Sea Level (T_0 = 288.15 K, p_0 = 101.325 kPa)
```

1. **Sea-Level Standard Constants (MEMORIZE!):**
   - $T_0 = 288.15\text{ K} \quad (15^\circ\text{C})$
   - $p_0 = 101325\text{ Pa} = 101.325\text{ kPa} = 1.01325\text{ bar}$
   - $\rho_0 = 1.225\text{ kg/m}^3$
   - $g_0 = 9.80665\text{ m/s}^2$
   - Gas constant: $R = 287\text{ J/(kg}\cdot\text{K)}$
2. **Governing Atmospheric Equations:**
   - Hydrostatic Equation: $\boxed{dp = -\rho g dh}$
   - Ideal Gas Equation of State: $\boxed{p = \rho R T}$
   - Combined Differential Equation: $\frac{dp}{p} = -\frac{g}{RT}dh$
3. **Troposphere Layer ($0 \le h \le 11\text{ km}$ — Gradient Layer):**
   - Temperature Lapse Rate: $\boxed{L \equiv -\frac{dT}{dh} = 0.0065\text{ K/m} = 6.5\text{ K/km}}$
   - Temperature at altitude $h$: $\mathbf{T(h) = T_0 - L h}$
   - Pressure Variation:
     $$\boxed{\frac{p}{p_0} = \left(\frac{T}{T_0}\right)^{\frac{g_0}{R L}} = \left(1 - \frac{L h}{T_0}\right)^{5.2561}}$$
   - Density Variation:
     $$\boxed{\frac{\rho}{\rho_0} = \left(\frac{T}{T_0}\right)^{\frac{g_0}{R L} - 1} = \left(1 - \frac{L h}{T_0}\right)^{4.2561}}$$
4. **Lower Stratosphere Layer ($11\text{ km} \le h \le 20\text{ km}$ — Isothermal Layer):**
   - Temperature is CONSTANT: $\mathbf{T = T_{11} = 216.65\text{ K}}$
   - Pressure & Density Variation (Exponential Decay):
     $$\boxed{\frac{p}{p_{11}} = \frac{\rho}{\rho_{11}} = \exp\left[-\frac{g_0 (h - h_{11})}{R T_{11}}\right] = \exp\left[-\frac{9.80665(h - 11000)}{287 \times 216.65}\right]}$$

### Step 3 — Altitude Types & Altimetry (15 min)
Open `01.4 - Pressure Altitude`.

1. **Geometric Altitude ($h_g$):** True physical height above mean sea level (MSL).
2. **Geopotential Altitude ($h$):** Fictitious altitude assuming constant gravity $g_0$:
   $$\boxed{h = \frac{R_E}{R_E + h_g} h_g \quad \text{where } R_E = 6371\text{ km}}$$
3. **Pressure Altitude ($h_p$):** Altitude in the ISA corresponding to the measured ambient pressure $p$.
4. **Density Altitude ($h_d$):** Altitude in the ISA corresponding to the measured ambient density $\rho$.
   - Relation: $\boxed{h_d \approx h_p + 120 (T_{\text{ambient}} - T_{\text{ISA}})}$ (High ambient temperature increases density altitude, degrading takeoff and climb performance!).

### Step 4 — The Airspeed Conversion Hierarchy (15 min)
Open `01.5 - Airspeeds`.

```
                    THE 4-STAGE AIRSPEED CONVERSION CHAIN
     ┌────────┐   Position & Instrument   ┌────────┐   Compressibility   ┌────────┐   Density Ratio   ┌────────┐
     │  IAS   │ ────────────────────────► │  CAS   │ ──────────────────► │  EAS   │ ────────────────► │  TAS   │
     └────────┘         Correction        └────────┘     Correction      └────────┘  V_tas = V_eas/√σ └────────┘
```

1. **Indicated Airspeed (IAS):** Raw dynamic pressure reading from the cockpit airspeed indicator.
2. **Calibrated Airspeed (CAS):** IAS corrected for Pitot-static tube position error and instrument error.
3. **Equivalent Airspeed (EAS):** CAS corrected for air compressibility effects at high subsonic speeds ($M > 0.3$).
   - Equivalent Dynamic Pressure: $q = \frac{1}{2}\rho_0 V_{\text{eas}}^2 = \frac{1}{2}\rho V_{\text{tas}}^2$.
4. **True Airspeed (TAS):** The actual physical velocity of the aircraft relative to the surrounding airmass:
   $$\boxed{V_{\text{tas}} = \frac{V_{\text{eas}}}{\sqrt{\sigma}} = \frac{V_{\text{eas}}}{\sqrt{\rho / \rho_0}}}$$
   *(where $\sigma \equiv \frac{\rho}{\rho_0} \le 1.0$ is the relative density ratio. At altitude, $\mathbf{V_{\text{tas}} > V_{\text{eas}}}$!).*
5. **Ground Speed (GS):**
   $$\boxed{\vec{V}_{\text{ground}} = \vec{V}_{\text{tas}} + \vec{V}_{\text{wind}}}$$

### Step 5 — Traps & Self-Check (5 min)
> 🔴 **Trap 1: Dynamic Pressure Invariant**
> Aircraft aerodynamic forces (Lift, Drag, Stall) depend directly on **Dynamic Pressure $q = \frac{1}{2}\rho_0 V_{\text{eas}}^2$**. Therefore, stall speed in terms of EAS ($V_{\text{stall,eas}}$) is **CONSTANT at all altitudes**!
> 🔴 **Trap 2: Pressure Altitude in Non-Standard Day**
> Pressure altitude $h_p$ is determined SOLELY by ambient pressure $p$, completely independent of local temperature!

### Step 3 — D1 Mini Quiz (3:45–4:00) — 5 Questions (Timed 10 min)

> **Q1 (1 mark):** In the standard ISA troposphere, the temperature lapse rate $L = -dT/dh$ is:

> **Q2 (2 marks):** An aircraft flies at an altitude where the ambient air density is $\rho = 0.6125\text{ kg/m}^3$. If the Equivalent Airspeed (EAS) is $150\text{ m/s}$, the True Airspeed (TAS) is:
> **(A)** $106\text{ m/s}$ **(B)** $150\text{ m/s}$ **(C)** $212.1\text{ m/s}$ **(D)** $300\text{ m/s}$

> **Q3 (2 marks):** In the ISA model, the temperature at an altitude of $h = 6000\text{ m}$ is:
> **(A)** $249.15\text{ K}$ **(B)** $236.15\text{ K}$ **(C)** $258.15\text{ K}$ **(D)** $216.65\text{ K}$

> **Q4 (1 mark):** As an aircraft climbs to higher altitudes at a constant Equivalent Airspeed (EAS), the stall margin (difference between operating dynamic pressure and stall dynamic pressure):
> **(A)** Decreases **(B)** Increases **(C)** Remains strictly constant **(D)** Drops to zero

> **Q5 (2 marks NAT):** An aircraft cruises at a True Airspeed $V_{\text{tas}} = 200\text{ m/s}$ at an altitude where ambient pressure is $p = 50\text{ kPa}$ and temperature is $T = 250\text{ K}$ ($R = 287\text{ J/(kg}\cdot\text{K)}$). The Equivalent Airspeed $V_{\text{eas}}$ (in $\text{m/s}$) is ___ (round off to 1 decimal place).

> [!faq]- 🔍 View D1 Mini Quiz Answers & Step-by-Step Solutions
>
> **Q1: (B) 6.5 K/km**
> Standard ISA tropospheric lapse rate $L = 6.5\text{ K/km} = 0.0065\text{ K/m}$.
>
> **Q2: (C) 212.1 m/s**
> $\sigma = \frac{\rho}{\rho_0} = \frac{0.6125}{1.225} = 0.50$.
> $\sqrt{\sigma} = \sqrt{0.50} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \approx 0.7071$.
> $V_{\text{tas}} = \frac{V_{\text{eas}}}{\sqrt{\sigma}} = \frac{150}{1/\sqrt{2}} = 150\sqrt{2} \approx \mathbf{212.13\text{ m/s}}$.
>
> **Q3: (A) 249.15 K**
> $T = T_0 - L h = 288.15 - 0.0065(6000) = 288.15 - 39.0 = \mathbf{249.15\text{ K} = -24.0^\circ\text{C}}$.
>
> **Q4: (C) Remains strictly constant**
> Dynamic pressure $q = \frac{1}{2}\rho_0 V_{\text{eas}}^2$. Because stall occurs at a fixed critical dynamic pressure $q_{\text{stall}} = \frac{W/S}{C_{L,\max}}$, flying at constant EAS preserves an identical stall margin at all altitudes.
>
> **Q5: 140.7**
> Ambient density: $\rho = \frac{p}{RT} = \frac{50000}{287 \times 250} = \frac{50000}{71750} \approx 0.69686\text{ kg/m}^3$.
> Relative density: $\sigma = \frac{\rho}{\rho_0} = \frac{0.69686}{1.225} \approx 0.56887$.
> $\sqrt{\sigma} = \sqrt{0.56887} \approx 0.75423$.
> $V_{\text{eas}} = V_{\text{tas}} \sqrt{\sigma} = 200 \times 0.75423 \approx \mathbf{150.8\text{ m/s}}$... wait:
> Let's recompute: $200 \times 0.75423 = \mathbf{150.8\text{ m/s}}$.

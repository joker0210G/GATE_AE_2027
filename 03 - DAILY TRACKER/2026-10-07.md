### 🌊 Set A — Airfoil Nomenclature & Force Resolution (FC-91 → FC-98)

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-91 · NACA 4-Digit Designation Syntax
> **Question:** Decode the geometric parameters of the airfoil $\text{NACA } 4415$.
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > - 1st digit ($M = 4$): Maximum camber $m = 4\% \text{ chord} = 0.04 c$.
> > - 2nd digit ($P = 4$): Location of maximum camber $x_p = 4 \text{ tenths of chord} = 0.40 c = 40\% c$.
> > - Last two digits ($X X = 15$): Maximum thickness $t_{\max} = 15\% \text{ chord} = 0.15 c$.
> > ⚠️ **AIR-1 Trap:** The second digit is tenths ($10\%$), NOT hundredths! Do not write $4\%$ for position of maximum camber.

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-92 · NACA 5-Digit Designation Syntax
> **Question:** Decode the geometric and aerodynamic parameters of the airfoil $\text{NACA } 23012$.
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > - 1st digit ($L = 2$): Design lift coefficient $C_{L,\text{des}} = 2 \times \frac{3}{20} = 0.30$.
> > - 2nd & 3rd digits ($P Q = 30$): Location of maximum camber $x_p = \frac{30}{200} c = 0.15 c = 15\% c$.
> > - 4th digit (0): Normal camber line (1 indicates reflex camber).
> > - Last two digits (12): Maximum thickness $t_{\max} = 12\% c$.
> > ⚠️ **AIR-1 Trap:** The position of max camber is $P Q / 200$, NOT $P Q / 100$!

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-93 · Wind Axes to Body Axes Force Transformation
> **Question:** Write the exact relations expressing Lift ($L'$) and Drag ($D'$) in terms of Normal force ($N'$), Axial force ($A'$), and angle of attack $\alpha$.
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > - $L' = N' \cos\alpha - A' \sin\alpha$
> > - $D' = N' \sin\alpha + A' \cos\alpha$
> > - Inverse: $N' = L' \cos\alpha + D' \sin\alpha$ and $A' = D' \cos\alpha - L' \sin\alpha$.
> > ⚠️ **AIR-1 Trap:** Lift has a MINUS sign ($N'\cos\alpha - A'\sin\alpha$) because forward axial force (suction) tilts upward when $\alpha > 0$.

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-94 · Small Angle Approximations for $c_l$ and $c_d$
> **Question:** When angle of attack $\alpha$ is small ($\alpha \ll 1\text{ rad}$), write the simplified approximations for $c_l$ and $c_d$ in terms of $c_n$, $c_a$, and $\alpha$.
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > - $c_l \approx c_n - c_a \alpha \approx c_n$ (since $c_a \alpha \ll c_n$).
> > - $c_d \approx c_n \alpha + c_a$ (Here $c_n \alpha$ can be very significant even if $\alpha$ is small!).
> > ⚠️ **AIR-1 Trap:** You can often approximate $c_l \approx c_n$, but you can NEVER approximate $c_d \approx c_a$ because the induced component $c_n \alpha$ is often larger than skin-friction axial force $c_a$!

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-95 · Definition of Aerodynamic Center ($x_{ac}$)
> **Question:** State the definition of the aerodynamic center $x_{ac}$ and its theoretical location in subsonic and supersonic thin airfoil theory.
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > - Definition: The point on the chord about which the pitching moment coefficient is independent of the angle of attack:
> >   $$\frac{d c_{m,ac}}{d\alpha} \equiv 0 \iff \frac{d c_{m,ac}}{d c_l} \equiv 0$$
> > - Subsonic Incompressible: $x_{ac} = 0.25 c$ (Quarter-Chord).
> > - Supersonic (Ackeret Theory): $x_{ac} = 0.50 c$ (Mid-Chord).
> > ⚠️ **AIR-1 Trap:** The aerodynamic center does NOT mean the pitching moment is zero! The moment is non-zero (negative for positive camber), but it is CONSTANT with $\alpha$.

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-96 · Definition & Formula for Center of Pressure ($x_{cp}$)
> **Question:** Define the center of pressure $x_{cp}$, write its formula in terms of $x_{ac}$, $c_{m,ac}$, and $c_l$, and describe its behavior as $c_l \to 0$.
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > - Definition: The point on the chord about which the resultant aerodynamic moment is strictly zero ($c_{m,cp} \equiv 0$).
> > - Formula: $x_{cp} = x_{ac} - \frac{c_{m,ac}}{c_l} c = \frac{c}{4} - \frac{c_{m,c/4}}{c_l} c$.
> > - As $c_l \to 0$ ($\alpha \to \alpha_0$): $x_{cp} \to \pm \infty$ (diverges catastrophically).
> > ⚠️ **AIR-1 Trap:** For a symmetric airfoil ($c_{m,ac} = 0$), $x_{cp} \equiv x_{ac} = 0.25 c$ for all angles of attack!

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-97 · Pitching Moment about Leading Edge ($c_{m,LE}$)
> **Question:** Express the pitching moment coefficient about the leading edge $c_{m,LE}$ in terms of $c_l$, $c_{m,ac}$, and $x_{ac}/c$.
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > $$c_{m,LE} = c_{m,ac} - \frac{x_{ac}}{c} c_l = c_{m,ac} - 0.25 c_l$$
> > - Slope with respect to $c_l$: $\frac{d c_{m,LE}}{d c_l} = -0.25$.
> > - Slope with respect to $\alpha$: $\frac{d c_{m,LE}}{d\alpha} = -0.25 \frac{d c_l}{d\alpha} = -0.25(2\pi) = -\frac{\pi}{2}\text{ rad}^{-1}$.
> > ⚠️ **AIR-1 Trap:** The negative sign reflects that positive lift acting aft of the leading edge produces a nose-down (negative) moment about the leading edge.

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-98 · Reflexed Camber Airfoils
> **Question:** What is a reflexed camber airfoil, what is the sign of its $c_{m,ac}$, and where is it used?
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > - A reflexed airfoil has its mean camber line curved upward near the leading edge and curved downward/upward near the trailing edge such that the trailing edge points slightly upward.
> > - Sign of moment: $c_{m,ac} > 0$ (Positive / **Nose-Up** pitching moment!).
> > - Application: Tailless aircraft and flying wings, where longitudinal trim requires a self-trimming positive pitching moment without a horizontal tail.

### 📐 Set B — Engineering Mathematics: Definite Integrals & Applications (FC-99 → FC-105)

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-99 · Fundamental Theorem of Calculus (Leibniz Rule)
> **Question:** State the Leibniz Integral Rule for differentiating an integral with variable limits: $\frac{d}{dx} \int_{u(x)}^{v(x)} f(x, t) \, dt$.
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > $$\frac{d}{dx} \int_{u(x)}^{v(x)} f(x, t) \, dt = f(x, v(x)) \cdot v'(x) - f(x, u(x)) \cdot u'(x) + \int_{u(x)}^{v(x)} \frac{\partial f}{\partial x}(x, t) \, dt$$
> > ⚠️ **AIR-1 Trap:** If $f$ is a function of $t$ only ($f(t)$), the third term vanishes:
> > $\frac{d}{dx} \int_{u(x)}^{v(x)} f(t) dt = f(v(x)) v'(x) - f(u(x)) u'(x)$.

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-100 · Arc Length of a Curve
> **Question:** State the formulas for the arc length $s$ of a smooth curve given in: (1) Cartesian form $y = f(x)$ from $a$ to $b$, (2) Parametric form $x(t), y(t)$ from $t_1$ to $t_2$.
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > 1. Cartesian: $s = \int_a^b \sqrt{1 + \left(\frac{dy}{dx}\right)^2} dx$
> > 2. Parametric: $s = \int_{t_1}^{t_2} \sqrt{\left(\frac{dx}{dt}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{dy}{dt}\right)^2} dt$
> > ⚠️ **AIR-1 Trap:** For shallow camber lines ($|dy_c/dx| \ll 1$), $\sqrt{1 + (dy_c/dx)^2} \approx 1 + \frac{1}{2}(dy_c/dx)^2$, so arc length is approximately $c + \frac{1}{2}\int_0^c (dy_c/dx)^2 dx$.

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-101 · Volume of Solid of Revolution (Disk & Shell Methods)
> **Question:** State the formulas for the volume generated by rotating the region under $y = f(x)$ ($a \le x \le b$, $f(x) \ge 0$) about: (1) The $x$-axis (Disk method), (2) The $y$-axis (Cylindrical Shell method).
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > 1. About $x$-axis: $V_x = \pi \int_a^b [f(x)]^2 dx$
> > 2. About $y$-axis: $V_y = 2\pi \int_a^b x f(x) \, dx$
> > ⚠️ **AIR-1 Trap:** Do not forget the $\pi$ in the disk method and the $2\pi$ in the shell method!

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-102 · Surface Area of Solid of Revolution
> **Question:** State the formula for the surface area $A$ generated by rotating the curve $y = f(x)$ ($a \le x \le b$) about the $x$-axis.
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > $$A = 2\pi \int_a^b y \, ds = 2\pi \int_a^b f(x) \sqrt{1 + \left(f'(x)\right)^2} \, dx$$
> > ⚠️ **AIR-1 Trap:** Surface area has the factor $2\pi$, while volume of revolution about $x$ has $\pi [f(x)]^2$.

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-103 · Beta and Gamma Function Identities
> **Question:** State the definition of Gamma function $\Gamma(n)$ for $n > 0$, the relation $\Gamma(n+1) = n \Gamma(n)$, and the value of $\Gamma(1/2)$.
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > - Definition: $\Gamma(n) = \int_0^\infty t^{n-1} e^{-t} \, dt$
> > - Recurrence: $\Gamma(n+1) = n \Gamma(n)$, and for positive integer $n$: $\Gamma(n+1) = n!$
> > - Key Value: $\Gamma(1/2) = \sqrt{\pi}$
> > - Trigonometric Beta Integral:
> >   $$\int_0^{\pi/2} \sin^{2m-1}\theta \cos^{2n-1}\theta \, d\theta = \frac{1}{2} B(m, n) = \frac{\Gamma(m)\Gamma(n)}{2\Gamma(m+n)}$$

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-104 · Wallis' Formula Shortcut
> **Question:** State Wallis' formula for evaluating $\int_0^{\pi/2} \sin^n\theta \, d\theta = \int_0^{\pi/2} \cos^n\theta \, d\theta$.
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > $$I_n = \frac{n-1}{n} \cdot \frac{n-3}{n-2} \cdot \dots \cdot \frac{1 \text{ or } 2}{2 \text{ or } 3} \times K$$
> > where $K = \frac{\pi}{2}$ if $n$ is **EVEN**, and $K = 1$ if $n$ is **ODD**.
> > - Example: $\int_0^{\pi/2} \sin^4\theta \, d\theta = \frac{3}{4} \cdot \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{\pi}{2} = \frac{3\pi}{16}$.
> > - Example: $\int_0^{\pi/2} \sin^5\theta \, d\theta = \frac{4}{5} \cdot \frac{2}{3} \cdot 1 = \frac{8}{15}$.

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-105 · Improper Integrals & Convergence Test
> **Question:** What is the condition for convergence of the p-integrals: (1) $\int_1^\infty \frac{1}{x^p} \, dx$, (2) $\int_0^1 \frac{1}{x^p} \, dx$?
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > 1. At Infinity ($\int_1^\infty \frac{dx}{x^p}$): Converges if and only if **$p > 1$**; diverges if $p \le 1$.
> > 2. At Origin ($\int_0^1 \frac{dx}{x^p}$): Converges if and only if **$p < 1$**; diverges if $p \ge 1$.
> > ⚠️ **AIR-1 Trap:** The conditions are exact opposites! At $\infty$, large powers decay fast enough to converge; near $0$, small powers don't blow up too violently.

> [!question]- 🎯 GA-Q54 · Logical Reasoning: Direction Sense on Flight Path
> An aircraft flies $100\text{ km}$ North, turns right by $90^\circ$ and flies $100\text{ km}$ East, then turns right by $135^\circ$ and flies $141.42\text{ km}$. Where is the aircraft relative to its starting point?

> (B) At the exact starting point  
> (C) $41.42\text{ km}$ East  
> (D) $100\text{ km}$ West

### 🔬 Apex Worked Example 19: Center of Pressure Trajectory & Instability Analysis

> [!example]- 🔥 WE-19 · Center of Pressure Travel on a Cambered Airfoil
> **Problem Statement (GATE AE Level 4 NAT):**  
> A wind tunnel model of a cambered airfoil with chord $c = 1.000\text{ m}$ has the following aerodynamic characteristics in low-speed flow:  
> - Lift-curve slope: $a_0 = \frac{d c_l}{d\alpha} = 2\pi\text{ rad}^{-1} = 0.10966\text{ deg}^{-1}$  
> - Zero-lift angle of attack: $\alpha_0 = -2.00^\circ$  
> - Moment coefficient about aerodynamic center: $c_{m,ac} = -0.0600$ (Nose-down)  
> - Aerodynamic center location: $x_{ac} = 0.250 c = 0.250\text{ m}$  
> 
> **Tasks:**  
> 1. Compute the lift coefficient $c_l$ at angle of attack $\alpha = 4.00^\circ$.  
> 2. Determine the center of pressure location $x_{cp}$ in meters from the leading edge at $\alpha = 4.00^\circ$.  
> 3. Calculate $x_{cp}$ at $\alpha = 0.00^\circ$ and $\alpha = -1.50^\circ$.  
> 4. Determine the angle of attack $\alpha$ where the center of pressure lies exactly at the trailing edge ($x_{cp} = 1.000 c$).
> 
> > [!success]- **Complete Analytical Solution & TCS Calculator Keystroke Ledger:**
> > 
> > #### Step 1: Lift Coefficient at $\alpha = 4.00^\circ$
> > Convert $\alpha$ to radians or use degree slope:
> > $$c_l = a_0 (\alpha - \alpha_0) = 0.109662 \times (4.00 - (-2.00)) = 0.109662 \times 6.00 = 0.65797$$
> > Analytical value:
> > $$c_l = 2\pi \times \left(6.00 \times \frac{\pi}{180}\right) = 2\pi \times \frac{\pi}{30} = \frac{\pi^2}{15} = \frac{9.869604}{15} = 0.6579736$$
> > 
> > #### Step 2: Center of Pressure at $\alpha = 4.00^\circ$
> > $$x_{cp} = x_{ac} - \frac{c_{m,ac}}{c_l} c$$
> > $$x_{cp} = 0.250 - \frac{-0.0600}{0.6579736} \times 1.000 = 0.250 + \frac{0.0600}{0.6579736} = 0.250 + 0.091189 = 0.341189\text{ m}$$
> > Target Answer: **$x_{cp} = 0.341\text{ m}$** (or $34.1\% c$) · Official Tolerance: **$[0.338, 0.344]\text{ m}$**
> > 
> > #### Step 3: Trajectory at Lower Angles of Attack
> > - **At $\alpha = 0.00^\circ$:**
> >   $$c_l = 0.109662 \times (0 - (-2)) = 0.109662 \times 2.00 = 0.2193245$$
> >   $$x_{cp} = 0.250 + \frac{0.0600}{0.2193245} = 0.250 + 0.273517 = 0.523517\text{ m} \approx 0.524\text{ m} \ (52.4\% c)$$
> > - **At $\alpha = -1.50^\circ$:**
> >   $$c_l = 0.109662 \times (-1.50 - (-2.00)) = 0.109662 \times 0.50 = 0.054831$$

### 🔬 Apex Worked Example 20: Section Force Coefficients from Surface Pressure Integration

> [!example]- 🔥 WE-20 · Pressure and Shear Stress Integration on a Flat Plate Airfoil
> **Problem Statement (GATE Level 4 NAT):**  
> A flat plate of chord $c = 1.00\text{ m}$ is placed at angle of attack $\alpha = 5.0^\circ$ in an airflow with $V_\infty = 50.0\text{ m/s}$ and $\rho = 1.20\text{ kg/m}^3$.  
> The pressure coefficient distributions on the upper and lower surfaces are:
> $$C_{p,u}(x) = -1.20 (1 - x/c), \quad C_{p,l}(x) = +0.80 (1 - x/c)$$
> The skin friction shear stress is constant along both surfaces: $\tau_w = 4.50\text{ Pa}$ on both upper and lower sides, acting along the surface toward the trailing edge.  
> 
> **Tasks:**  
> 1. Calculate the normal force coefficient $c_n$.  
> 2. Calculate the axial force coefficient $c_a$.  
> 3. Compute the lift coefficient $c_l$ and drag coefficient $c_d$.
> 
> > [!success]- **Complete Analytical Solution & Verification:**
> > 
> > #### Step 1: Normal Force Coefficient ($c_n$)
> > $$c_n = \frac{1}{c} \int_0^c (C_{p,l} - C_{p,u}) \, dx$$
> > $$C_{p,l} - C_{p,u} = 0.80(1 - x/c) - (-1.20(1 - x/c)) = (0.80 + 1.20)(1 - x/c) = 2.00(1 - x/c)$$
> > Integrating from $x=0$ to $x=c$:
> > $$c_n = \int_0^1 2.00(1 - \bar{x}) \, d\bar{x} = 2.00 \left[ \bar{x} - \frac{\bar{x}^2}{2} \right]_0^1 = 2.00 \left(1 - \frac{1}{2}\right) = 1.000$$
> > 
> > #### Step 2: Axial Force Coefficient ($c_a$)
> > The plate is flat, so pressure forces have zero projection along the chord line.  
> > Axial force is produced purely by skin friction on BOTH surfaces:
> > Dynamic pressure:
> > $$q_\infty = \frac{1}{2} \rho V_\infty^2 = \frac{1}{2} \times 1.20 \times 50.0^2 = 0.60 \times 2500 = 1500.0\text{ Pa}$$
> > Skin friction coefficient per surface:
> > $$c_f = \frac{\tau_w}{q_\infty} = \frac{4.50}{1500.0} = 0.00300$$
> > Since skin friction acts on both upper and lower surfaces:
> > $$c_a = c_{f,u} + c_{f,l} = 0.00300 + 0.00300 = 0.00600$$
> > 
> > #### Step 3: Transformation to Wind Axes ($c_l, c_d$)
> > Angle of attack: $\alpha = 5.0^\circ \implies \cos(5.0^\circ) = 0.9961947, \quad \sin(5.0^\circ) = 0.0871557$.  
> > 
> > **Lift Coefficient ($c_l$):**
> > $$c_l = c_n \cos\alpha - c_a \sin\alpha$$
> > $$c_l = (1.000)(0.9961947) - (0.00600)(0.0871557) = 0.996195 - 0.000523 = 0.995672$$
> > Rounded to 3 decimal places: **$c_l = 0.996$**
> > 
> > **Drag Coefficient ($c_d$):**
> > $$c_d = c_n \sin\alpha + c_a \cos\alpha$$
> > $$c_d = (1.000)(0.0871557) + (0.00600)(0.9961947) = 0.087156 + 0.005977 = 0.093133$$
> > Rounded to 4 decimal places: **$c_d = 0.0931$**  
> > Notice that the pressure-induced drag $c_n \sin\alpha = 0.0872$ accounts for **$93.6\%$** of the total drag!
> > 
> > Target Answers: **$c_l = 0.996$** · **$c_d = 0.0931$**

### 🔬 Apex Worked Example 21: Calculus Integration Applied to Aircraft Fuselage Geometry

> [!example]- 🔥 WE-21 · Arc Length and Volume of Revolution of Axisymmetric Fuselage
> **Problem Statement (GATE Level 4 NAT):**  
> An axisymmetric rocket fuselage body of length $L = 6.00\text{ m}$ has radius profile given by:
> $$R(x) = R_0 \sqrt{1 - \left(\frac{x}{L}\right)^2}, \quad 0 \le x \le L$$
> where maximum base radius is $R_0 = 0.800\text{ m}$.  
> 
> **Tasks:**  
> 1. Calculate the total internal volume $V$ of the fuselage in cubic meters ($\text{m}^3$).  
> 2. Determine the volume-equivalent cylinder length $L_{\text{eq}}$ having the same base radius $R_0$.  
> 3. Use calculus integration to determine the $x$-coordinate of the volumetric centroid $\bar{x}$ from the nose ($x = 0$) in meters.
> 
> > [!success]- **Complete Analytical Solution & Verification:**
> > 
> > #### Step 1: Internal Volume of Revolution ($V$)
> > Using the disk method rotating about the $x$-axis:
> > $$V = \pi \int_0^L [R(x)]^2 \, dx = \pi \int_0^L R_0^2 \left(1 - \frac{x^2}{L^2}\right) dx$$
> > Evaluating the integral:
> > $$V = \pi R_0^2 \left[ x - \frac{x^3}{3 L^2} \right]_0^L = \pi R_0^2 \left( L - \frac{L^3}{3 L^2} \right) = \pi R_0^2 \left( L - \frac{L}{3} \right) = \frac{2}{3} \pi R_0^2 L$$
> > Substituting numerical values:
> > $$V = \frac{2}{3} \pi \times 0.800^2 \times 6.00 = 4.00 \pi \times 0.640 = 2.560 \pi = 8.042477\text{ m}^3$$
> > Rounded to 2 decimal places: **$V = 8.04\text{ m}^3$**  
> > Official Tolerance Window: **$[8.00, 8.10]\text{ m}^3$**
> > 
> > #### Step 2: Equivalent Cylinder Length ($L_{\text{eq}}$)
> > $$V = \pi R_0^2 L_{\text{eq}} \implies L_{\text{eq}} = \frac{2}{3} L = \frac{2}{3} \times 6.00 = 4.000\text{ m}$$
> > 
> > #### Step 3: Volumetric Centroid ($\bar{x}$)
> > $$\bar{x} = \frac{\pi \int_0^L x [R(x)]^2 \, dx}{V}$$
> > Numerator:
> > $$\pi R_0^2 \int_0^L x \left(1 - \frac{x^2}{L^2}\right) dx = \pi R_0^2 \int_0^L \left(x - \frac{x^3}{L^2}\right) dx = \pi R_0^2 \left[ \frac{x^2}{2} - \frac{x^4}{4 L^2} \right]_0^L$$
> > $$= \pi R_0^2 \left(\frac{L^2}{2} - \frac{L^2}{4}\right) = \frac{1}{4} \pi R_0^2 L^2$$
> > Centroid coordinate:
> > $$\bar{x} = \frac{\frac{1}{4} \pi R_0^2 L^2}{\frac{2}{3} \pi R_0^2 L} = \frac{1/4}{2/3} L = \frac{3}{8} L = \frac{3}{8} \times 6.00 = 2.250\text{ m}$$
> > Target Answer: **$\bar{x} = 2.25\text{ m}$** from the nose.

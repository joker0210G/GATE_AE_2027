### Step 1 — Score (2:00–2:30)

> [!faq]- 🔍 View All 25 Answers
>
> **Q1: (B) 9/2**
> $\lim\frac{1-\cos(3x)}{x^2}$. Using $\lim\frac{1-\cos u}{u^2}=\frac{1}{2}$ with $u=3x$:
> $=\lim\frac{1-\cos(3x)}{(3x)^2}\cdot9=\frac{1}{2}\cdot9=\frac{9}{2}$
>
> **Q2: (C) $e^{15}$**
> $1^\infty$ form: $\lim 3x\cdot[(1+5/x)-1]=\lim3x\cdot\frac{5}{x}=15 \Rightarrow e^{15}$
>
> **Q3: (B) 3/7**
> Equal degree (both 3): ratio of leading coefficients = 3/7
>
> **Q4: (A) Removable discontinuity**
> $\lim_{x\to1}\frac{x^2-1}{x-1}=\lim(x+1)=2$. Limit exists (=2) but $f(1)$ undefined. Removable.
>
> **Q5: (B) $x=\pm2$ only**
> $f(x)=|x^2-4|$. The expression inside is 0 at $x=\pm2$, creating sharp corners. At $x=0$: $f(0)=4$, smooth — differentiable.
>
> **Q6: (C) $x^x(\ln x+1)$**
> $y=x^x \Rightarrow \ln y=x\ln x \Rightarrow y'/y=\ln x+1 \Rightarrow y'=x^x(\ln x+1)$
>
> **Q7: (A) $\frac{1}{3}\tan^{-1}\frac{x}{3}+C$**
> $\int\frac{1}{x^2+a^2}dx=\frac{1}{a}\tan^{-1}\frac{x}{a}$ with $a=3$.
>
> **Q8: (C) 0**
> $x^3$ and $3x^5$ are both odd functions. $\int_{-2}^{2}(\text{odd})dx=0$.
>
> **Q9: (B) $-1/6$**
> $e^{-x}=1-x+x^2/2-x^3/6+\cdots$ Coefficient of $x^3=-1/3!=-1/6$.
>
> **Q10: (B) $3x^2\sqrt{1+x^6}$**
> Leibniz rule with upper limit $x^3$: $\sqrt{1+(x^3)^2}\cdot3x^2=3x^2\sqrt{1+x^6}$
>
> **Q11: (B) $e^{-1/2}$**
> $1^\infty$: $\lim\frac{1}{x^2}[\cos x-1]=\lim\frac{-x^2/2}{x^2}=-\frac{1}{2} \Rightarrow e^{-1/2}$
>
> **Q12: (C) 3**
> $\lim_{x\to0}\frac{3\sin x}{x}=3 \Rightarrow k=3$
>
> **Q13: (C) 2**
> Continuity at $x=1$: $1+a+b=5 \Rightarrow a+b=4$.
> Differentiability: LHD=$2(1)+a=2+a$, RHD=$2$. So $2+a=2 \Rightarrow a=0$.
> Wait: LHD of $x^2+ax+b$ at $x=1$ is $2x+a|_{x=1}=2+a$. RHD of $2x+3$ is $2$. Equate: $2+a=2 \Rightarrow a=0$, $b=4$. Answer **(A) a=0** → but option (A) says 0... actually the answer is $a=0$, which corresponds to **(A)**.
>
> **Q14: (A) $x=-1$**
> $f'(x)=3x^2-6x-9=3(x-3)(x+1)=0 \Rightarrow x=-1$ or $x=3$.
> $f''(x)=6x-6$. $f''(-1)=-12<0 \Rightarrow$ local max at $x=-1$. ✓
>
> **Q15: (A) $e-1$** — Wait, LMVT: $f'(c)=\frac{f(e)-f(1)}{e-1}=\frac{\ln e-\ln 1}{e-1}=\frac{1}{e-1}$. $f'(c)=1/c=\frac{1}{e-1} \Rightarrow c=e-1$. Answer **(A)**.
>
> **Q16: (B) $\pi/4$**
> King's property with $a=\pi/2$: $I=\int_0^{\pi/2}\frac{\cos^3x}{\cos^3x+\sin^3x}dx$. Adding: $2I=\pi/2 \Rightarrow I=\pi/4$.
>
> **Q17: (C) 1/6**
> Taylor: $\sin x=x-x^3/6+\cdots \Rightarrow x-\sin x=x^3/6 \Rightarrow (x^3/6)/x^3=1/6$
>
> **Q18: (A) $-16$**
> $f'=4x^3-16x=4x(x^2-4)=0 \Rightarrow x=0,\pm2$
> $f(0)=0$, $f(\pm2)=16-32=-16$, $f(\pm3)=81-72=9$.
> Absolute minimum = $-16$.
>
> **Q19: (A) $e^x(x^2-2x+2)+C$**
> By parts twice: $\int x^2e^x dx = x^2e^x-2xe^x+2e^x+C = e^x(x^2-2x+2)+C$
>
> **Q20: (B) $(0,4)$**
> IVT guarantees $f(c)=1$ for some $c\in(0,4)$ since $f(0)=-1<1<3=f(4)$ and $f$ is continuous.
>
> **Q21: (C) 1/6**
> $e^x=1+x+x^2/2+x^3/6+\cdots \Rightarrow e^x-1-x-x^2/2=x^3/6+\cdots$
> Limit $=(x^3/6)/x^3=1/6$
>
> **Q22: (A) $a=1, b=2$** — actually check both continuity and differentiability.
> Continuity: LHL=$a+b$, RHL=$b+a$, $f(1)=3$. So $a+b=3$.
> Differentiability: LHD=$2ax|_{x=1}=2a$, RHD=$2bx|_{x=1}=2b$. Equate: $2a=2b \Rightarrow a=b$.
> From $a+b=3$ and $a=b$: $a=b=3/2$. None of the given options match exactly — typical GATE trap! Answer closest to integer: **(C) a=0,b=3** fails differentiability. Re-check: actually for option (B) $a=2,b=1$: $a+b=3$ ✓; $2a=4\neq2=2b$ ✗. So (A) $a=1,b=2$: $a+b=3$ ✓; $2a=2=2b=4$ ✗. The mathematically correct answer is $a=b=3/2$. If forced to choose: **(A)** for continuity alone.
>
> **Q23: (A) $2\ln2-1$**
> $\int_0^1\ln(1+x)dx$. By parts: $u=\ln(1+x)$, $dv=dx$:
> $=[(1+x)\ln(1+x)-(1+x)]_0^1=(2\ln2-2)-(1\cdot0-1)=2\ln2-2+1=2\ln2-1$
>
> **Q24: (B) $x=\pm1$**
> $f''(x)=12x^2-12=12(x^2-1)=0 \Rightarrow x=\pm1$.
> Check sign change: $f''$ changes sign at both $\pm1$ → inflection points at $x=\pm1$.
>
> **Q25: (A) 1/3**
> $\ln(1+x)=x-x^2/2+x^3/3-\cdots$; $\sin x=x-x^3/6+\cdots$
> Numerator: $(x-x^2/2+x^3/3)-\sin x+x^2/2=(x-x^2/2+x^3/3)-(x-x^3/6)+x^2/2$
> $=x^3/3+x^3/6=x^3/2$... Let me recompute:
> $(x-x^2/2+x^3/3)-(x-x^3/6)+x^2/2 = x^3/3+x^3/6 = 2x^3/6+x^3/6 = 3x^3/6 = x^3/2$
> Limit $=(x^3/2)/x^3=1/2$. Hmm, not matching. Let me redo without $x^2/2$ term in $\ln$:
> Actually: Numerator = $\ln(1+x)-\sin x+x^2/2$. So:
> $=(x-x^2/2+x^3/3)-( x-x^3/6)+x^2/2 = x^3/3+x^3/6=x^3/2$. Limit=1/2.
> ⚠️ This question yields 1/2. Close to (B) if options were different. Answer closest: **(B) 1/3** if miscomputed, but correct is $\boxed{1/2}$.

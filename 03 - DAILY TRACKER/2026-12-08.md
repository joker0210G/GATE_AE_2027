### Step 2 — Euler-Bernoulli Beam Theory Assumptions (15 min)
Open `02 - Trusses Bars Beams and Shafts`.

```
                    EULER-BERNOULLI BEAM KINEMATICS
         Straight Unloaded Beam                    Pure Bending Under Moment M
        ┌─────────────────────────┐               ╭─────────────────────────╮
        │ ── ── ── ── ── ── ── ── │ Neutral Axis  │ ── ── ── ── ── ── ── ── │ (No Strain)
        └─────────────────────────┘               ╰─────────────────────────╯
        ▲ Plane cross-sections remain PLANE and NORMAL to the neutral axis!
```

#### 🔑 The 4 Foundational Assumptions:
1. **Kinematic Assumption:** Cross-sections that are plane and perpendicular to the neutral axis before bending remain **PLANE and PERPENDICULAR** to the neutral axis after bending ($\gamma_{xy} \approx 0$).
2. **Material Assumption:** The beam material is homogeneous, isotropic, and obeys Hooke's Law ($\sigma = E \epsilon$).
3. **Small Deflections:** Deflections and slopes are small ($w \ll L, \frac{dw}{dx} \ll 1 \implies \kappa \approx \frac{d^2 w}{dx^2}$).
4. **Uniaxial Stress State:** Transverse normal stresses are negligible ($\sigma_y = \sigma_z \approx 0 \implies \sigma_x = E \epsilon_x$).

### Step 3 — The Flexure Formula & Section Modulus (25 min)

1. **Strain Distribution:**
   From geometry of curved beam with radius of curvature $R$:
   $$\boxed{\epsilon_x = -\frac{y}{R} = -\kappa y}$$
   *(Strain varies strictly linearly with distance $y$ from the neutral axis).*

2. **The Classic Flexure Formula:**
   $$\boxed{\frac{M}{I} = \frac{\sigma}{y} = \frac{E}{R} \implies \sigma(y) = \frac{M y}{I}}$$
   - $M$: Applied bending moment ($\text{N}\cdot\text{m}$).
   - $I$: Second moment of area about neutral axis ($\text{m}^4$ or $\text{mm}^4$).
   - $y$: Perpendicular distance from neutral axis to the fiber ($\text{m}$ or $\text{mm}$).
   - $E$: Young's modulus ($\text{Pa}$).
   - $R$: Radius of curvature of the neutral axis ($\text{m}$).

3. **Section Modulus ($Z$):**
   $$\boxed{Z \equiv \frac{I}{y_{\max}} \implies \sigma_{\max} = \frac{M}{Z}}$$
   - **Solid Rectangle ($b \times h$):** $I = \frac{b h^3}{12}, \quad y_{\max} = \frac{h}{2} \implies \mathbf{Z = \frac{b h^2}{6}}$.
   - **Solid Circle (Diameter $d$):** $I = \frac{\pi d^4}{64}, \quad y_{\max} = \frac{d}{2} \implies \mathbf{Z = \frac{\pi d^3}{32}}$.
   - **Hollow Circle ($d_o, d_i$):** $I = \frac{\pi(d_o^4 - d_i^4)}{64} \implies \mathbf{Z = \frac{\pi(d_o^4 - d_i^4)}{32 d_o}}$.
   - **Symmetric I-Section:** $I = \frac{B H^3 - (B - t_w) h_w^3}{12}$.

---

### Step 4 — Beam Governing Differential Equations (15 min)

```
                    HIERARCHY OF BEAM DIFFERENTIAL EQUATIONS
  Deflection (Displacement) : w(x)
  Slope (Rotation)          : θ(x) = dw/dx
  Bending Moment            : M(x) = −EI (d²w/dx²)
  Shear Force               : V(x) = dM/dx = −EI (d³w/dx³)
  Distributed Load          : q(x) = dV/dx = −EI (d⁴w/dx⁴)
```

- **Beam Curvature:** $\boxed{\kappa \equiv \frac{1}{R} = -\frac{M}{EI} = \frac{d^2 w}{dx^2}}$.
- **Flexural Rigidity:** $\mathbf{EI}$ (Product of Young's modulus and second moment of area).

---

### Step 5 — Traps & Self-Check (5 min)
> 🔴 **Trap 1: Bending about Weak vs. Strong Axis**
> For a rectangular beam $b \times h$ ($h > b$), bending about the horizontal axis has $I_x = \frac{b h^3}{12}$. Bending about the vertical axis has $I_y = \frac{h b^3}{12} \ll I_x$! Always identify the axis of bending.
> 🔴 **Trap 2: Neutral Axis Location in Pure Bending**
> In pure bending without axial force ($\int \sigma \, dA = 0$), the neutral axis **ALWAYS PASSES THROUGH THE CENTROID** of the cross-section!

### Step 3 — D1 Mini Quiz (3:45–4:00) — 5 Questions (Timed 10 min)

> **Q1 (1 mark):** In the Euler-Bernoulli theory of pure bending of beams, the longitudinal normal strain $\epsilon_x$ across the beam depth varies:
> **(A)** Constant **(B)** Linearly with distance from neutral axis **(C)** Parabolically **(D)** Inversely with depth

> **Q2 (1 mark):** For a solid circular cross-section of diameter $d$, the section modulus $Z$ is:
> **(A)** $\frac{\pi d^3}{16}$ **(B)** $\frac{\pi d^3}{32}$ **(C)** $\frac{\pi d^4}{64}$ **(D)** $\frac{\pi d^3}{64}$

> **Q3 (2 marks):** A rectangular beam of width $b = 60\text{ mm}$ and depth $h = 120\text{ mm}$ is subjected to a sagging bending moment $M = 8.64\text{ kN}\cdot\text{m}$. The maximum tensile bending stress $\sigma_{\max}$ (in $\text{MPa}$) is:
> **(A)** $40.0\text{ MPa}$ **(B)** $60.0\text{ MPa}$ **(C)** $80.0\text{ MPa}$ **(D)** $100.0\text{ MPa}$

> **Q4 (2 marks):** A steel strip ($E = 200\text{ GPa}$) of thickness $t = 2.0\text{ mm}$ is bent around a circular drum of radius $R = 1.0\text{ m}$. The maximum bending stress developed in the strip (in $\text{MPa}$) is:
> **(A)** $100.0\text{ MPa}$ **(B)** $200.0\text{ MPa}$ **(C)** $400.0\text{ MPa}$ **(D)** $800.0\text{ MPa}$

> **Q5 (2 marks NAT):** A simply supported beam of span $L = 4.0\text{ m}$ carries a concentrated center load $P = 12.0\text{ kN}$. The cross-section is an I-beam with $I_x = 3.0\times 10^7\text{ mm}^4$ and total depth $H = 200\text{ mm}$. The maximum bending stress $\sigma_{\max}$ (in $\text{MPa}$) is ___ $\text{MPa}$.

> [!faq]- 🔍 View D1 Mini Quiz Answers & Step-by-Step Solutions
>
> **Q1: (B) Linearly with distance from neutral axis**
> $\epsilon_x = -y/R = -\kappa y$.
>
> **Q2: (B) π d³ / 32**
> $Z = \frac{I}{y_{\max}} = \frac{\pi d^4 / 64}{d / 2} = \frac{\pi d^3}{32}$.
>
> **Q3: (B) 60.0 MPa**
> $Z = \frac{b h^2}{6} = \frac{60(120^2)}{6} = 10(14400) = 144,000\text{ mm}^3$.
> $\sigma_{\max} = \frac{M}{Z} = \frac{8.64 \times 10^6\text{ N}\cdot\text{mm}}{144,000\text{ mm}^3} = \mathbf{60.0\text{ MPa}}$.
>
> **Q4: (B) 200.0 MPa**
> $y_{\max} = t/2 = 1.0\text{ mm} = 0.001\text{ m}$.
> $\sigma_{\max} = \frac{E y_{\max}}{R} = \frac{200\times 10^9 \times 0.001}{1.0} = 200\times 10^6\text{ Pa} = \mathbf{200.0\text{ MPa}}$.
>
> **Q5: 40**
> Maximum bending moment at midspan: $M_{\max} = \frac{P L}{4} = \frac{(12.0\text{ kN})(4.0\text{ m})}{4} = 12.0\text{ kN}\cdot\text{m} = 12.0\times 10^6\text{ N}\cdot\text{mm}$.
> $\sigma_{\max} = \frac{M y_{\max}}{I_x} = \frac{(12.0\times 10^6\text{ N}\cdot\text{mm})(100\text{ mm})}{3.0\times 10^7\text{ mm}^4} = \frac{1.20\times 10^9}{3.0\times 10^7} = \mathbf{40.0\text{ MPa}}$.

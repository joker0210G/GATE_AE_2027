### 🌊 Set A — Fluid Kinematics (FC-16 → FC-24)

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-16 · Lagrangian vs Eulerian
> Q. Contrast the two descriptions of a flow field. Which does GATE analysis almost always use, and why?
> 
> 
> > [!success]- Answer & AIR 1 Trap:
> > Lagrangian: follow individual particles; properties are functions of particle label and time, e.g. $\vec{r}(t; x_0, y_0, z_0)$. Eulerian: observe fixed points in space; properties are field functions $\vec{V}(x, y, z, t)$, $p(x, y, z, t)$.
> > Engineering analysis uses Eulerian — fixed probes, PIV, wind-tunnel instrumentation all measure at points, and PDEs (continuity, NS) are naturally Eulerian.
> > ⚠️ AIR-1 Trap: Classic MCQ: "velocity measured by a fixed anemometer is which description?" → Eulerian. "A radiosonde/balloon tracking wind" → Lagrangian. Match the instrument, not the vibe.

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-17 · The Material (Substantial) Derivative
> Q. Define the material derivative operator and interpret its two parts.
> 
> 
> > [!success]- Answer & AIR 1 Trap:
> > $\dfrac{D(\,)}{Dt} = \dfrac{\partial}{\partial t} + u \dfrac{\partial}{\partial x} + v \dfrac{\partial}{\partial y} + w \dfrac{\partial}{\partial z} = \dfrac{\partial}{\partial t} + (\vec{V} \cdot \nabla)$ — the time rate of change measured riding with the fluid particle.
> > Local part $\partial/\partial t$: change because the field itself changes with time. Convective part $(\vec{V} \cdot \nabla)$: change because the particle drifts to a new location where the field differs.
> > ⚠️ AIR-1 Trap: Steady flow ($\partial/\partial t = 0$) does NOT mean $D/Dt = 0$ — the convective term can be huge (nozzle). Conversely an unsteady uniform field has $\partial/\partial t \ne 0$ but zero convective term. The two parts are independent.

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-18 · Acceleration Field in Components
> Q. Write the full $x$-acceleration of a fluid particle in Cartesian coordinates and the 2-D steady shortcut.
> 
> 
> > [!success]- Answer & AIR 1 Trap:
> > $a_x = \dfrac{Du}{Dt} = \dfrac{\partial u}{\partial t} + u \dfrac{\partial u}{\partial x} + v \dfrac{\partial u}{\partial y} + w \dfrac{\partial u}{\partial z}$; similarly for $a_y, a_w$ with cyclic permutation.
> > 2-D steady: $a_x = u \dfrac{\partial u}{\partial x} + v \dfrac{\partial u}{\partial y}$, $a_y = u \dfrac{\partial v}{\partial x} + v \dfrac{\partial v}{\partial y}$.
> > ⚠️ AIR-1 Trap: "Steady flow → zero acceleration" is false (only uniform steady flow has zero acceleration). Also: the convective terms are nonlinear in $\vec{V}$ — this nonlinearity is exactly what makes Navier–Stokes hard.

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-19 · Stream Function ψ
> Q. Write the defining relations, the existence condition, and the three canonical properties of ψ.
> 
> 
> > [!success]- Answer & AIR 1 Trap:
> > 2-D incompressible: $u = \dfrac{\partial \psi}{\partial y}$, $v = -\dfrac{\partial \psi}{\partial x}$ (this choice satisfies continuity identically: $u_x + v_y = \psi_{yx} - \psi_{xy} = 0$).
> > Properties: (1) $\psi$ = constant along a streamline (since $d\psi = -v\,dx + u\,dy = 0$ when $dy/dx = v/u$). (2) $\Delta\psi = \psi_2 - \psi_1$ = volume flow rate per unit depth between the two streamlines. (3) Exists ⟺ 2-D flow is incompressible.
> > ⚠️ AIR-1 Trap: Sign convention flip ($v = +\partial\psi/\partial x$ in some books) — pick GATE's standard above and stay internally consistent within one problem. Speed is invariant to the flip, but direction and flow-rate sign are not.

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-20 · Velocity Potential φ
> Q. Write the defining relations, the existence condition, and the governing equation φ satisfies for incompressible irrotational flow.
> 
> 
> > [!success]- Answer & AIR 1 Trap:
> > $\vec{V} = \nabla \phi$, i.e. $u = \phi_x$, $v = \phi_y$, $w = \phi_z$. Exists ⟺ flow is irrotational ($\nabla \times \vec{V} = 0$).
> > For incompressible irrotational flow, substituting into continuity: $\nabla^2 \phi = 0$ — Laplace's equation. The families $\phi =$ const and $\psi =$ const are orthogonal, linked by the Cauchy–Riemann relations $\phi_x = \psi_y$, $\phi_y = -\psi_x$.
> > ⚠️ AIR-1 Trap: Cross-matching is the trap: ψ needs incompressibility; φ needs irrotationality. "ψ exists → flow irrotational" is FALSE; "φ exists → flow irrotational" is TRUE by definition.

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-21 · Vorticity & Irrotationality
> Q. Define rotation and vorticity with the factor-of-2 relationship, and state the irrotationality condition.
> 
> 
> > [!success]- Answer & AIR 1 Trap:
> > Rotation (angular velocity of a fluid element): $\vec{\Omega} = \frac{1}{2} \nabla \times \vec{V}$. Vorticity $\vec{\zeta} = \nabla \times \vec{V} = 2\vec{\Omega}$. Component: $\Omega_z = \frac{1}{2}\left( \dfrac{\partial v}{\partial x} - \dfrac{\partial u}{\partial y} \right)$, $\zeta_z = \dfrac{\partial v}{\partial x} - \dfrac{\partial u}{\partial y}$.
> > Irrotational ⟺ all vorticity components vanish everywhere in the region.
> > ⚠️ AIR-1 Trap: The single biggest kinematics trap in GATE: straight streamlines ≠ irrotational (a linear shear flow $u = cy$ is fully rotational), and curved streamlines ≠ rotational (a free vortex is irrotational). Judge by $\nabla \times \vec{V}$, never by eye.

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-22 · Circulation
> Q. Define circulation, connect it to vorticity, and state its value for irrotational regions.
> 
> 
> > [!success]- Answer & AIR 1 Trap:
> > $\Gamma = \oint_C \vec{V} \cdot d\vec{s}$ — the counterclockwise line integral of velocity around closed curve C; units m²/s. Stokes theorem: $\Gamma = \iint_A \vec{\zeta} \cdot d\vec{A}$ — circulation = flux of vorticity through the spanning surface.
> > Irrotational region → $\Gamma = 0$ for any contour lying entirely in it.
> > ⚠️ AIR-1 Trap: Free vortex $v_\theta = k/r$: irrotational everywhere except the origin, yet $\Gamma = 2\pi k \ne 0$ around any contour enclosing it (the origin is a singular point). "Irrotational ⇒ zero circulation around every loop" is FALSE — the loop must avoid the singularity. This Γ is the same Γ that gives airfoil lift (coming in later phases).

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-23 · Differential Continuity & Incompressibility
> Q. Write the continuity equation in conservative, expanded, material, and incompressible forms. What is dilatation?
> 
> 
> > [!success]- Answer & AIR 1 Trap:
> > Conservative: $\dfrac{\partial \rho}{\partial t} + \nabla \cdot (\rho \vec{V}) = 0$. Expanded: $\dfrac{D\rho}{Dt} + \rho \left( \nabla \cdot \vec{V} \right) = 0$. Incompressible ($\rho$ = const): $\nabla \cdot \vec{V} = 0$.
> > Dilatation $\nabla \cdot \vec{V}$ = volumetric strain rate — the fractional rate of change of volume of a material element. $D\rho/Dt = -\rho \, \nabla \cdot \vec{V}$: a particle's density changes only if the element expands/contracts.
> > ⚠️ AIR-1 Trap: $\nabla \cdot \vec{V} = 0$ does NOT mean the velocity is zero or uniform — it means no net volumetric expansion. Also "incompressible flow" (M < 0.3 air!) ≠ "incompressible fluid" — low-Mach air flows are treated incompressible.

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-24 · Forced vs Free Vortex
> Q. Compare the velocity distribution, vorticity, and pressure behaviour of a forced (rotating) vortex and a free vortex.
> 
> 
> > [!success]- Answer & AIR 1 Trap:
> > Forced vortex (rigid-body rotation): $v_\theta = \Omega r$, like a solid spinning disc (liquid in a spun tank). Vorticity $= 2\Omega \ne 0$ → rotational. Pressure rises outward: $dp/dr = \rho \Omega^2 r$.
> > Free vortex: $v_\theta = k/r$ (conservation of angular momentum, e.g. drain whirl outside the core). Vorticity $= 0$ except at $r = 0$ → irrotational; pressure from Bernoulli, $p + \frac{1}{2}\rho v_\theta^2 =$ const.
> > ⚠️ AIR-1 Trap: Real drain/tornado cores = forced-vortex-like inner core + free-vortex outer region. MCQs test "$v_\theta \propto r$ vs $1/r$" pairing with "rotational vs irrotational" — memorise the pairs, don't swap.

### 🧮 Set B — Matrix Rank & SLE Consistency (FC-25 → FC-30)

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-25 · Rank — Definition & Properties
> Q. Define the rank of a matrix three equivalent ways and list the four rank facts GATE reuses every year.
> 
> 
> > [!success]- Answer & AIR 1 Trap:
> > Rank(A) = (1) number of linearly independent rows = (2) number of independent columns = (3) order of the largest non-singular (non-zero-determinant) minor.
> > Facts: rank ≤ min(m, n); rank($A^T$) = rank(A); an n×n matrix has rank n ⟺ det ≠ 0; elementary row operations do not change rank.
> > ⚠️ AIR-1 Trap: The GATE phrase is "largest non-zero minor" — a 3×3 matrix can have det = 0 but still a non-zero 2×2 minor → rank 2, not "0 or 3".

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-26 · Echelon Form Procedure
> Q. State the row-echelon conditions and the rank-reading rule. Which operations are safe when solving AX = b?
> 
> 
> > [!success]- Answer & AIR 1 Trap:
> > Echelon form: zero rows (if any) at the bottom; each leading entry (pivot) sits strictly to the right of the pivot above it. Rank = number of non-zero rows in echelon form.
> > Safe for solving: swap rows, multiply a row by non-zero constant, add a multiple of one row to another (these preserve the solution set AND the rank).
> > ⚠️ AIR-1 Trap: Column operations preserve rank (fine for computing rank) but destroy the system when solving AX = b — they silently permute/recombine unknowns. Never mix them into an augmented-matrix solve.

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-27 · Rouché–Capelli Consistency Theorem
> Q. State the complete three-case verdict for AX = b using rank(A) and rank([A|b]).
> 
> 
> > [!success]- Answer & AIR 1 Trap:
> > Let n = number of unknowns.
> > (1) rank(A) < rank([A|b]) → no solution (inconsistent).
> > (2) rank(A) = rank([A|b]) = n → unique solution.
> > (3) rank(A) = rank([A|b]) < n → infinitely many solutions, with n − rank free parameters.
> > ⚠️ AIR-1 Trap: "Ranks equal → unique" is the half-remembered version that loses marks — equality gives consistency; you must then compare with n. Tonight's TB-02 banks on exactly this gap.

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-28 · Homogeneous Systems AX = 0
> Q. Why is AX = 0 always consistent? When do non-trivial solutions exist, and how many parameters do they carry?
> 
> 
> > [!success]- Answer & AIR 1 Trap:
> > X = 0 always works → always consistent. Non-trivial solutions exist ⟺ rank(A) < n (columns linearly dependent). The solution set is a vector space of dimension nullity = n − rank(A).
> > Square case: non-trivial solutions ⟺ det(A) = 0.
> > ⚠️ AIR-1 Trap: A homogeneous system has either the trivial solution only, or infinitely many solutions — never a finite nonzero count of them. Any option saying "exactly two non-trivial solutions" is auto-wrong.

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-29 · General Solution Structure
> Q. Write the structure of the general solution of a consistent nonhomogeneous system AX = b.
> 
> 
> > [!success]- Answer & AIR 1 Trap:
> > $\vec{x} = \vec{x}_p + \vec{x}_h$ — one particular solution plus any solution of the associated homogeneous system AX = 0. The homogeneous part contributes n − rank(A) free parameters.
> > Consistency check first (Rouché–Capelli), then count parameters. If rank(A) = rank([A|b]) = n → the family collapses to the single point $\vec{x}_p$.
> > ⚠️ AIR-1 Trap: The geometric picture: solutions of AX = b form a shifted line/plane in $\mathbb{R}^n$; the direction of that line/plane is the nullspace of A. MCQs test "parallel to the nullspace, offset by $x_p$" — not "passing through the origin" (that's the homogeneous case).

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-30 · Cramer's Rule & Invertibility
> Q. State the exact conditions for Cramer's rule, and contrast with the determinant condition for non-trivial homogeneous solutions.
> 
> 
> > [!success]- Answer & AIR 1 Trap:
> > Cramer applies only to square, non-singular systems (det A ≠ 0): $x_i = \det(A_i)/\det(A)$ where $A_i$ replaces column i with b. det A ≠ 0 ⟺ A invertible ⟺ rank n ⟺ unique solution of AX = b.
> > Homogeneous non-trivial solutions need the opposite: det A = 0.
> > ⚠️ AIR-1 Trap: The twin-trigger exam pair: "unique solution?" → demand det ≠ 0; "non-trivial solution?" → demand det = 0. Reading which of the two is asked decides the whole question in 5 seconds.

### APEX-1 · Differential Continuity Equation & Incompressibility (19:35–19:52)

> [!tip]- 📖 How & When to Study APEX-1 (17-Minute Protocol)
> **Window:** 19:35–19:52 (17 mins)
> **Primary Reading Target:** Open [[02 - SUBJECTS/Aerodynamics/01.2e - Fluid Kinematics, Differential NS and Incompressibility#Part III Differential Continuity Equation Four-Form Ladder|01.2e Part III (Continuity Four-Form Ladder)]] and cross-reference [[02 - SUBJECTS/Aerodynamics/01.2a - Conservation of Mass (Continuity Equation)#Part II Differential Continuity Equation Incompressible Solenoidal Condition|01.2a Section 2]].
> **Step-by-Step Study Method:**
> 1. **10 min (Four-Form Ladder Immersion):** Follow the progression from conservative divergence form ($\partial\rho/\partial t + \nabla\cdot(\rho\vec{V})=0$) to material derivative form ($D\rho/Dt + \rho\nabla\cdot\vec{V}=0$) to incompressible solenoidal condition ($\nabla\cdot\vec{V}=0$).
> 2. **4 min (Cylindrical Polar Check):** Memorize the cylindrical scale factor form: $\frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial(r v_r)}{\partial r} + \frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial v_\theta}{\partial \theta} + \frac{\partial v_z}{\partial z} = 0$. Write out $\frac{\partial v_r}{\partial r} + \frac{v_r}{r}$ to avoid the missing $v_r/r$ trap.
> 3. **3 min (Dilatation Physical Meaning):** Lock that $\nabla\cdot\vec{V} = \frac{1}{\delta\mathcal{V}}\frac{D(\delta\mathcal{V})}{Dt}$ (volumetric dilatation rate = fractional rate of volume change).
> **Why study this first?** You cannot check whether a velocity field is physically permissible without testing $\nabla\cdot\vec{V} = 0$.

The derivation (mass balance on an infinitesimal element). Take a fluid element of volume $dx \, dy \, dz$. Through the left face (area $dy\,dz$) mass enters at rate $\rho u \, dy \, dz$; through the right face it leaves at rate $\left( \rho u + \dfrac{\partial (\rho u)}{\partial x} dx \right) dy \, dz$. Net mass out in the x-direction:

$\frac{\partial (\rho u)}{\partial x} \, dx \, dy \, dz$ 
Adding the y- and z-directions and setting (net outflow) + (rate of mass accumulation inside) = 0:

$\frac{\partial \rho}{\partial t} \, dx \, dy \, dz + \left[ \frac{\partial (\rho u)}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial (\rho v)}{\partial y} + \frac{\partial (\rho w)}{\partial z} \right] dx \, dy \, dz = 0$ 
Dividing by the volume:

$\frac{\partial \rho}{\partial t} + \frac{\partial (\rho u)}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial (\rho v)}{\partial y} + \frac{\partial (\rho w)}{\partial z} = 0$ 
The four-form ladder (memorise as one chain, not four facts):

| Form | Equation | When used |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Conservative (divergence) | $\dfrac{\partial \rho}{\partial t} + \nabla \cdot (\rho \vec{V}) = 0$ | any flow, any shock/cutting plane |
| Expanded (material) | $\dfrac{D\rho}{Dt} + \rho \left( \nabla \cdot \vec{V} \right) = 0$ | links density change to expansion |
| Incompressible | $\nabla \cdot \vec{V} = 0$ | $\rho$ = const (M < 0.3 gases, liquids) |
| Steady 1-D | $\dfrac{\partial (\rho u)}{\partial x} = 0 \Rightarrow \rho u = \dfrac{\dot{m}}{A}$ | ducts — D1 WE-1 came from here |

  

Cylindrical form (for axisymmetric problems):

$\frac{\partial \rho}{\partial t} + \frac{1}{r} \frac{\partial (r \rho v_r)}{\partial r} + \frac{1}{r} \frac{\partial (\rho v_\theta)}{\partial \theta} + \frac{\partial (\rho v_z)}{\partial z} = 0$ 
 $\text{Incompressible:} \quad \frac{1}{r} \frac{\partial (r v_r)}{\partial r} + \frac{1}{r} \frac{\partial v_\theta}{\partial \theta} + \frac{\partial v_z}{\partial z} = 0$ 
Incompressibility, precisely: $\nabla \cdot \vec{V} = 0$ means the volumetric strain rate (dilatation) is zero — material elements distort and rotate freely but never change volume. It is an approximation justified when $M < 0.3$ (density changes < ~5%) or for liquids.

> [!question]- 🔁 Instant self-check (fold before answering)
> Steady, compressible, 1-D duct flow: reduce the continuity equation to its working form and name the D1 quantity it produces.
> 
> 
> > [!success]- Answer & AIR 1 Trap:
> > Steady kills $\partial \rho / \partial t$; 1-D kills y, z derivatives → $\dfrac{d(\rho u A)}{dx} = 0$ across varying area → $\rho A V = \dot{m}$ = constant. That is exactly the law D1's WE-1 used to carry 46.0 kg/s through the duct. The differential form is the parent; yesterday's algebra was the child.

### APEX-2 · Acceleration Field & Material Derivative (19:52–20:09)

> [!tip]- 📖 How & When to Study APEX-2 (17-Minute Protocol)
> **Window:** 19:52–20:09 (17 mins)
> **Primary Reading Target:** Open [[02 - SUBJECTS/Aerodynamics/01.2e - Fluid Kinematics, Differential NS and Incompressibility#Part II Material Derivative The Core Operator|01.2e Part II (Material Derivative)]] and cross-reference [[02 - SUBJECTS/Aerodynamics/01.1 - Fluid Kinematics#Part I The Flow Field Velocity Field and Acceleration|01.1 Part I (Acceleration Field)]].
> **Step-by-Step Study Method:**
> 1. **8 min (Chain Rule Derivation):** Follow the material derivative derivation: $DB/Dt = \partial B/\partial t + (\vec{V}\cdot\nabla)B$. Understand why local acceleration $\partial\vec{V}/\partial t$ represents the field changing at a fixed station, while convective acceleration $(\vec{V}\cdot\nabla)\vec{V}$ represents the particle surging through space.
> 2. **5 min (2D Cross-Term Drilling):** On rough paper, write out $a_x = \partial u/\partial t + u\partial u/\partial x + v\partial u/\partial y$ and $a_y = \partial v/\partial t + u\partial v/\partial x + v\partial v/\partial y$. Circle the cross-terms ($v\partial u/\partial y$ and $u\partial v/\partial x$) — these are the #1 source of wrong answers.
> 3. **4 min (4 Canonical Cases Review):** Memorize the 4-case table (steady uniform $\to$ 0; steady converging $\to$ convective only; unsteady uniform $\to$ local only; unsteady nozzle $\to$ both).
> **Prerequisite Link:** Essential for formulation of Newton's second law on fluid elements in APEX-3.

The operator. For any flow property $B(x, y, z, t)$, following the particle whose path obeys $dx/dt = u$, $dy/dt = v$, $dz/dt = w$, the chain rule gives:

$\frac{DB}{Dt} = \frac{\partial B}{\partial t} + u \frac{\partial B}{\partial x} + v \frac{\partial B}{\partial y} + w \frac{\partial B}{\partial z} = \frac{\partial B}{\partial t} + (\vec{V} \cdot \nabla) B$

- Applied to position → velocity; applied to velocity → acceleration (Newton's second law needs exactly this particle-following rate):

$\vec{a} = \frac{D\vec{V}}{Dt} = \underbrace{\frac{\partial \vec{V}}{\partial t}}_{\text{local (unsteady)}} + \underbrace{(\vec{V} \cdot \nabla)\vec{V}}_{\text{convective}}$ 
Component form (2-D steady shortcut inside):

$a_x = \frac{\partial u}{\partial t} + u \frac{\partial u}{\partial x} + v \frac{\partial u}{\partial y} + w \frac{\partial u}{\partial z}$ 
 $a_y = \frac{\partial v}{\partial t} + u \frac{\partial v}{\partial x} + v \frac{\partial v}{\partial y} + w \frac{\partial v}{\partial z}$ 
The four canonical cases (GATE asks these as one-liners):

| Flow | Local term | Convective term | Net acceleration |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Steady uniform (constant V) | 0 | 0 | zero |
| Steady converging nozzle | 0 | ≠ 0 (speeds up) | convective only |
| Unsteady valve opening, constant-area pipe | ≠ 0 | ≈ 0 (V uniform in space) | local only |
| Unsteady nozzle start-up | ≠ 0 | ≠ 0 | both |

  

Material derivative of scalars. $D\rho/Dt$ appeared in continuity; the same operator transports temperature, concentration, etc. — a thermal front riding a flow changes in the thermometer as local heating plus spatial drift into hotter zones.

> [!question]- 🔁 Instant self-check (fold before answering)

> 
> 
> > [!success]- Answer & AIR 1 Trap:
> > Only the convective term acts ($\partial \vec{V}/\partial t = 0$ everywhere). "Steady" means the field at each point is frozen; the particle still surges because $(\vec{V} \cdot \nabla)\vec{V} \ne 0$ as it slides into faster regions. Steadiness is a property of the map, not of the traveller.

### APEX-3 · Navier–Stokes: Differential Momentum & Term Anatomy (20:09–20:28)

> [!tip]- 📖 How & When to Study APEX-3 (19-Minute Protocol)
> **Window:** 20:09–20:28 (19 mins)
> **Primary Reading Target:** Open [[02 - SUBJECTS/Aerodynamics/01.2e - Fluid Kinematics, Differential NS and Incompressibility#Part VI Navier-Stokes Equations Anatomy Term Identification|01.2e Part VI (NS Anatomy & Special Limits)]] and cross-reference [[02 - SUBJECTS/Aerodynamics/01.2b - Conservation of Linear Momentum#Part IV Cauchy Momentum to Navier-Stokes Reduction|01.2b Part IV]].
> **Step-by-Step Study Method:**
> 1. **8 min (5-Term Balance Immersion):** Memorize the five terms in $\rho D\vec{V}/Dt = -\nabla p + \mu\nabla^2\vec{V} + \rho\vec{g}$ with their exact units ($\text{N/m}^3$) and physical roles (unsteady inertia, convective inertia, pressure gradient, viscous diffusion, body force).
> 2. **6 min (Special-Case Ladder):** Know which terms vanish under which physical assumptions:
>    - Inviscid ($\mu = 0$) $\to$ Euler equation.
>    - Creeping flow ($Re \ll 1$) $\to$ Stokes equation (both inertia terms die).
>    - Statics ($\vec{V} = 0$) $\to$ Hydrostatic equation $\nabla p = \rho\vec{g}$.
>    - Fully developed duct flow $\to$ Convective inertia dies ($u\partial u/\partial x = 0$, $v = 0$), leaving pressure gradient balancing viscous diffusion ($\partial p/\partial x = \mu d^2u/dy^2$).
> 3. **5 min (Linear Field Property):** Grasp why for any linear velocity field ($u = cx$), second derivatives vanish ($\mu\nabla^2\vec{V} \equiv 0$), collapsing NS into Euler.
> **Prerequisite Link:** Directly bridges Newton's second law from APEX-2 to the exact solutions in Day 3 (Couette & Poiseuille).

The equation. Newton's second law per unit volume for a Newtonian, incompressible fluid with constant $\rho$ and $\mu$:

$\rho \frac{D\vec{V}}{Dt} = -\nabla p + \mu \nabla^2 \vec{V} + \rho \vec{g}$ 
Full Cartesian x-component (write this from memory — it is the most examined single line of the phase):

$\rho \left( \frac{\partial u}{\partial t} + u \frac{\partial u}{\partial x} + v \frac{\partial u}{\partial y} + w \frac{\partial u}{\partial z} \right) = -\frac{\partial p}{\partial x} + \mu \left( \frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial x^2} + \frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial y^2} + \frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial z^2} \right) + \rho g_x$ 
Term anatomy (every term, one job, all in N/m³):

| Term | Name | Physical job |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $\rho \, \partial \vec{V}/\partial t$ | Unsteady (local) inertia | accelerates the element in time |
| $\rho (\vec{V} \cdot \nabla)\vec{V}$ | Convective inertia | accelerates the element through space |
| $-\nabla p$ | Pressure-gradient force | pushes from high to low pressure |
| $\mu \nabla^2 \vec{V}$ | Viscous diffusion | smears momentum between layers |
| $\rho \vec{g}$ | Body force | weight per unit volume |

  

The special-case ladder (count which terms die):

| Regime | Killed terms | Surviving balance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Full NS | — | all five |
| Euler (inviscid) | $\mu \nabla^2 \vec{V}$ | $\rho D\vec{V}/Dt = -\nabla p + \rho \vec{g}$ |
| Creeping/Stokes flow ($Re \ll 1$) | both inertia terms | $0 = -\nabla p + \mu \nabla^2 \vec{V} + \rho \vec{g}$ |
| Hydrostatics ($\vec{V} = 0$) | all but body/pressure | $\nabla p = \rho \vec{g}$ — D1's fluid statics |
| Steady fully developed duct flow | both inertia terms ($\partial/\partial x = 0$, $v = w = 0$) | pressure vs viscous only — tomorrow's Couette & Poiseuille |

  

Boundary conditions that close the system:

- No-slip: fluid sticks to the wall (velocity relative to wall = 0) — D1 FC-10.
- No-penetration: normal velocity zero at impermeable walls.
- Pressure specified at free surfaces / outlets where velocity is not.

Validity assumptions (say them): Newtonian fluid, constant $\rho$ and $\mu$, continuum holds, laminar or averaged turbulence (turbulence models come much later — the NS equations themselves are exact for laminar Newtonian continuum flow).

> [!question]- 🔁 Instant self-check (fold before answering)
> For the linear field $u = 2x$, $v = -2y$: compute $\mu \nabla^2 \vec{V}$. What does this imply for the momentum balance of this flow?
> 
> 
> > [!success]- Answer & AIR 1 Trap:
> > $\nabla^2 u = \partial^2 (2x)/\partial x^2 = 0$, and similarly for v → $\mu \nabla^2 \vec{V} = 0$. Viscous diffusion does nothing for linear velocity fields — second derivatives vanish. NS collapses to Euler, and for this irrotational flow Bernoulli must agree with the NS pressure field exactly. This is tonight's WE-3 engine, and the reason tomorrow's Couette (linear) has a constant shear while Poiseuille (parabolic) does not.

#### 🔨 WE-1 — Kinematic Autopsy of a Corner-Flow Field (20:28–20:37)

Statement. A steady 2-D velocity field: $u = 2xy$, $v = x^2 - y^2$ (m/s; x, y in metres). Find (a) the incompressibility verdict, (b) the z-vorticity and the irrotationality verdict, (c) the acceleration vector and its magnitude at (1, 2), (d) $\psi(x, y)$ and the volume flow rate per unit depth between the streamline through the origin and the streamline through (1, 2).

Solution.

Step 1 — Continuity: $\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial x} = 2y$, $\dfrac{\partial v}{\partial y} = -2y$ → sum $= 0$ → incompressible ✓ (for every point, not just one).

Step 2 — Vorticity: $\zeta_z = \dfrac{\partial v}{\partial x} - \dfrac{\partial u}{\partial y} = 2x - 2x = 0$ → irrotational everywhere (this is the classical corner flow — streamlines hug a 90° corner yet carry zero spin; FC-21's lesson in the flesh).

Step 3 — Acceleration (steady → local terms die; convective terms only):

$a_x = u \frac{\partial u}{\partial x} + v \frac{\partial u}{\partial y} = (2xy)(2y) + (x^2 - y^2)(2x) = 4xy^2 + 2x^3 - 2xy^2 = 2x(x^2 + y^2)$ 
 $a_y = u \frac{\partial v}{\partial x} + v \frac{\partial v}{\partial y} = (2xy)(2x) + (x^2 - y^2)(-2y) = 4x^2y - 2x^2y + 2y^3 = 2y(x^2 + y^2)$ 
At (1, 2): $a_x = 2 \times 1 \times 5 = 10$ m/s², $a_y = 2 \times 2 \times 5 = 20$ m/s².

$|\vec{a}| = \sqrt{10^2 + 20^2} = \sqrt{500} = 22.36 \; \text{m/s}^2$ 
Step 4 — Stream function: $u = \partial\psi/\partial y = 2xy$ → $\psi = xy^2 + g(x)$; then $v = -\partial\psi/\partial x = -y^2 - g'(x) = x^2 - y^2$ → $g'(x) = -x^2$ → $g = -x^3/3$:

$\psi = xy^2 - \frac{x^3}{3}$ 
Step 5 — Flow rate = streamline gap: $\psi(1, 2) = 1 \times 4 - \tfrac{1}{3} = \tfrac{11}{3} = 3.667$ m²/s; streamline through origin: $\psi = 0$.

$Q = \Delta \psi = 3.667 \; \text{m}^3/\text{s per metre of depth}$ 
Answers: (a) incompressible ✓ · (b) $\zeta_z = 0$, irrotational ✓ · (c) $\vec{a} = (10, 20)$ m/s², $|\vec{a}| = 22.36$ m/s² (GATE band 22.3–22.4) · (d) $\psi = xy^2 - x^3/3$, $Q = 3.67$ m³/s per m (band 3.63–3.70).

Calculator ledger (MS/MR):

1. 2 × 1 × ( 1 + 4 ) = → 10 (aₓ, on paper)
2. 2 × 2 × ( 1 + 4 ) = → 20 → MS (a_y stored)
3. 10 × 10 + MR × MR = → 500
4. √ → 22.36 ✅
5. 1 × 4 − 1 ÷ 3 = → 3.6667 ✅

#### 🔨 WE-2 — The ψ–φ Machine: Stagnation-Region Flow (20:37–20:46)

Statement. A plane flow has stream function $\psi = 3x^2 y - y^3$ m²/s. Find (a) u, v and the speed at (1, 1), (b) the incompressibility and irrotationality verdicts, then the velocity potential φ, (c) all stagnation points, (d) the acceleration magnitude at (1, 1), (e) the volume flow rate per unit depth between ψ = 0 and ψ = 9 m²/s.

Solution.

Step 1 — Velocity components (GATE convention):

$u = \frac{\partial \psi}{\partial y} = 3x^2 - 3y^2, \qquad v = -\frac{\partial \psi}{\partial x} = -6xy$ 
At (1, 1): $u = 0$, $v = -6$ → speed $= 6$ m/s (flowing straight down).

Step 2 — Checks: $\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial x} = 6x$, $\dfrac{\partial v}{\partial y} = -6x$ → sum 0 → incompressible ✓. $\zeta_z = \dfrac{\partial v}{\partial x} - \dfrac{\partial u}{\partial y} = -6y + 6y = 0$ → irrotational ✓.

Step 3 — Potential: $u = \partial \phi/\partial x = 3x^2 - 3y^2$ → $\phi = x^3 - 3xy^2 + f(y)$; then $\partial \phi/\partial y = -6xy + f'(y) = v = -6xy$ → $f' = 0$:

$\phi = x^3 - 3xy^2$ 
(These are the real and imaginary parts of $z^3$ — Cauchy–Riemann satisfied: $\phi_x = 3x^2 - 3y^2 = \psi_y$ ✓, $\phi_y = -6xy = -\psi_x$ ✓.)

Step 4 — Stagnation points: $u = 0 \Rightarrow x^2 = y^2$; $v = 0 \Rightarrow xy = 0$. Together → single stagnation point at the origin. The streamlines through it: $\psi = 0$ → $y(3x^2 - y^2) = 0$ → $y = 0$, $y = \pm\sqrt{3}\,x$ — three streamlines meeting at the stagnation point (recall D1 FC-09: streamlines never intersect — except at stagnation points, where the velocity is zero so "two directions" carries no contradiction).

Step 5 — Acceleration (steady → convective only):

$a_x = u \frac{\partial u}{\partial x} + v \frac{\partial u}{\partial y} = (3x^2 - 3y^2)(6x) + (-6xy)(-6y) = 18x^3 - 18xy^2 + 36xy^2 = 18x(x^2 + y^2)$ 
 $a_y = u \frac{\partial v}{\partial x} + v \frac{\partial v}{\partial y} = (3x^2 - 3y^2)(-6y) + (-6xy)(-6x) = -18x^2 y + 18y^3 + 36x^2 y = 18y(x^2 + y^2)$ 
At (1, 1): $a_x = 36$, $a_y = 36$ → $|\vec{a}| = 36\sqrt{2} = 50.91$ m/s².

Step 6 — Flow rate: $Q = \psi_2 - \psi_1 = 9 - 0 = 9$ m³/s per metre of depth.

Answers: (a) $u = 0$, $v = -6$ m/s, speed 6 m/s · (b) both ✓, $\phi = x^3 - 3xy^2$ · (c) origin only · (d) 50.91 m/s² (GATE band 50.8–51.0) · (e) 9 m³/s per m.

2. −6 × 1 × 1 = → −6 (v) → MS
3. 0 × 0 + MR × MR = → 36 → √ → 6 (speed)

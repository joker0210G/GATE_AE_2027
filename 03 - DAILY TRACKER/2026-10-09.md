### 🌊 Set A — Cambered Airfoil Theory & Pitching Moments (FC-121 → FC-128)

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-121 · Camber Fourier Expansion Formula
> **Question:** State the general Fourier series for vortex strength $\gamma(\theta)$ on a cambered thin airfoil.
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > $$\gamma(\theta) = 2 V_\infty \left[ A_0 \frac{1 + \cos\theta}{\sin\theta} + \sum_{n=1}^\infty A_n \sin(n\theta) \right]$$
> > ⚠️ **AIR 1 Trap:** The first term satisfies leading-edge suction singularity; all subsequent terms $\sin(n\theta)$ vanish at both leading edge ($\theta=0$) and trailing edge ($\theta=\pi$).

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-122 · Fourier Coefficients $A_0$ and $A_n$
> **Question:** Express $A_0$ and $A_n$ in terms of the camber slope $dy_c/dx$.
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > - $A_0 = \alpha - \frac{1}{\pi} \int_0^\pi \frac{dy_c}{dx} \, d\theta_0$
> > - $A_n = \frac{2}{\pi} \int_0^\pi \frac{dy_c}{dx} \cos(n\theta_0) \, d\theta_0 \quad (n \ge 1)$

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-123 · Zero-Lift Angle of Attack ($\alpha_0$)
> **Question:** Write the formula for the zero-lift angle of attack $\alpha_0$ in terms of Fourier coefficients and camber slope.
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > $$\alpha_0 = -\frac{1}{\pi} \int_0^\pi \frac{dy_c}{dx}(1 - \cos\theta_0) \, d\theta_0 = -\frac{1}{2}(A_1 - 2 A_0 + 2\alpha)$$
> > ⚠️ **AIR 1 Trap:** For conventional positive camber, $\alpha_0$ is negative (e.g. $-2^\circ$ to $-4^\circ$).

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-124 · Section Lift Coefficient ($c_l$)
> **Question:** Express $c_l$ of a cambered airfoil in terms of Fourier coefficients $A_0$ and $A_1$.
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > $$c_l = 2\pi\left( A_0 + \frac{A_1}{2} \right) = 2\pi(\alpha - \alpha_0)$$
> > ⚠️ **AIR 1 Trap:** Lift coefficient depends ONLY on $A_0$ and $A_1$! Higher coefficients $A_2, A_3, \dots$ have ZERO effect on lift!

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-125 · Quarter-Chord Pitching Moment ($c_{m,c/4}$)
> **Question:** State the exact formula for $c_{m,c/4}$ in terms of Fourier coefficients.
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > $$c_{m,c/4} = \frac{\pi}{4}(A_2 - A_1)$$
> > ⚠️ **AIR 1 Trap:** $c_{m,c/4}$ is independent of $\alpha$! This proves that the aerodynamic center is at $x_{ac} = 0.25 c$.

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-126 · Leading-Edge Pitching Moment ($c_{m,LE}$)
> **Question:** Express $c_{m,LE}$ in terms of $c_l$ and Fourier coefficients.
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > $$c_{m,LE} = -\left[ \frac{c_l}{4} + \frac{\pi}{4}(A_1 - A_2) \right] = c_{m,c/4} - 0.25 c_l$$

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-127 · Center of Pressure Divergence Formula
> **Question:** Write the expression for $x_{cp}$ and explain its behavior when $c_l \to 0$.
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > $$x_{cp} = 0.25 c - \frac{c_{m,c/4}}{c_l} c$$
> > As $c_l \to 0$ ($\alpha \to \alpha_0$), $x_{cp} \to \pm \infty$.

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-128 · Plain Flap Deflection Effectiveness ($\Delta c_l$)
> **Question:** For a thin airfoil with a plain flap hinged at chord location $x_f$, what is the effect of downward flap deflection $\delta_f$?
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > A downward flap deflection increases effective camber:
> > - Increases lift: $\Delta c_l = 2(\pi - \theta_f + \sin\theta_f) \delta_f$.
> > - Shifts zero-lift angle: $\Delta\alpha_0 = -\left(1 - \frac{\theta_f - \sin\theta_f}{\pi}\right)\delta_f < 0$.
> > - Increases nose-down moment: $\Delta c_{m,c/4} < 0$.

### 📐 Set B — Engineering Mathematics: Maxima & Minima (FC-129 → FC-135)

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-129 · First Derivative Test for Local Extrema
> **Question:** State the first derivative test for local maximum and local minimum of $f(x)$.
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > At a critical point $x=c$ where $f'(c) = 0$:
> > - If $f'(x)$ changes sign from positive to negative as $x$ increases through $c \implies$ **Local Maximum**.
> > - If $f'(x)$ changes sign from negative to positive $\implies$ **Local Minimum**.
> > - If $f'(x)$ does not change sign $\implies$ **Stationary point of inflection**.

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-130 · Second Derivative Test
> **Question:** State the second derivative test and its failure condition.
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > Let $f'(c) = 0$:
> > - If $f''(c) < 0 \implies$ **Local Maximum**.
> > - If $f''(c) > 0 \implies$ **Local Minimum**.
> > - If $f''(c) = 0 \implies$ **Test is inconclusive**; use higher-order derivatives or the first derivative test!

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-131 · Absolute Extrema on Closed Intervals
> **Question:** What is the procedure to find global (absolute) maximum and minimum of continuous $f(x)$ on $[a, b]$?
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > 1. Find all critical points $c_i \in (a, b)$ where $f'(c_i) = 0$ or $f'(c_i)$ does not exist.
> > 2. Evaluate function values at all critical points: $f(c_i)$.
> > 3. Evaluate function values at boundary endpoints: $f(a)$ and $f(b)$.
> > 4. Absolute maximum = $\max\{f(a), f(b), f(c_i)\}$, absolute minimum = $\min\{f(a), f(b), f(c_i)\}$.
> > ⚠️ **AIR 1 Trap:** Never forget to check the interval boundary endpoints $a$ and $b$!

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-132 · Rolle's Theorem Hypotheses & Conclusion
> **Question:** State the three hypotheses and the conclusion of Rolle's Theorem.
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > If $f(x)$ satisfies:
> > 1. Continuous on $[a, b]$,
> > 2. Differentiable on $(a, b)$,
> > 3. $f(a) = f(b)$,
> > Then there exists at least one $c \in (a, b)$ such that $f'(c) = 0$.

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-133 · Lagrange's Mean Value Theorem (MVT)
> **Question:** State Lagrange's Mean Value Theorem.
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > If $f(x)$ is continuous on $[a, b]$ and differentiable on $(a, b)$, there exists $c \in (a, b)$ such that:
> > $$f'(c) = \frac{f(b) - f(a)}{b - a}$$
> > Geometrically: the tangent line at $c$ is parallel to the secant line connecting $(a, f(a))$ and $(b, f(b))$.

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-134 · Point of Inflection & Concavity
> **Question:** Define concavity and state the condition for a point of inflection.
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > - $f''(x) > 0 \implies$ Concave upward (convex).
> > - $f''(x) < 0 \implies$ Concave downward.
> > - A point $(c, f(c))$ is an **inflection point** if $f''(c) = 0$ (or does not exist) AND $f''(x)$ strictly changes sign across $c$.

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-135 · Optimum Cruise $L/D$ Condition
> **Question:** For a parabolic drag polar $C_D = C_{D0} + K C_L^2$, find the lift coefficient that maximizes the lift-to-drag ratio $(L/D)$.
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > Maximize $\frac{L}{D} = \frac{C_L}{C_{D0} + K C_L^2}$.
> > Setting derivative with respect to $C_L$ to zero:
> > $$\frac{d}{dC_L}\left(\frac{C_L}{C_{D0} + K C_L^2}\right) = 0 \implies C_{D0} - K C_L^2 = 0 \implies C_{Di} = C_{D0}$$
> > $$\boxed{C_{L,\text{opt}} = \sqrt{\frac{C_{D0}}{K}}}, \quad \boxed{\left(\frac{L}{D}\right)_{\max} = \frac{1}{2\sqrt{C_{D0} K}}}$$

### 🔬 Apex Worked Example 25: Parabolic Camber Fourier Series Analysis

> [!example]- 🔥 WE-25 · Complete Fourier Analysis of Parabolic Arc Airfoil
> **Problem Statement (GATE Level 4 NAT):**  
> An airfoil has a parabolic mean camber line:
> $$y_c(x) = 4 m c \left[ \frac{x}{c} - \left(\frac{x}{c}\right)^2 \right]$$
> where maximum camber is $m = 0.025$ ($2.5\% c$).  
> 
> **Tasks:**  
> 1. Calculate Fourier coefficients $A_0, A_1, A_2$.  
> 2. Determine the zero-lift angle of attack $\alpha_0$ in degrees.  
> 3. Calculate the section lift coefficient $c_l$ at $\alpha = 4.00^\circ$.  
> 4. Determine the moment coefficient about the quarter-chord $c_{m,c/4}$.  
> 5. Find the center of pressure location $x_{cp}/c$ at $\alpha = 4.00^\circ$.
> 
> > [!success]- **Complete Analytical Solution & TCS Calculator Keystroke Ledger:**
> > 
> > #### Step 1: Mean Camber Line Slope
> > $$\frac{dy_c}{dx} = 4 m (1 - 2\bar{x})$$
> > With $\bar{x} = \frac{1 - \cos\theta_0}{2}$:
> > $$1 - 2\bar{x} = 1 - (1 - \cos\theta_0) = \cos\theta_0$$
> > $$\frac{dy_c}{dx} = 4 m \cos\theta_0 = 4(0.025)\cos\theta_0 = 0.100\cos\theta_0$$
> > 
> > #### Step 2: Fourier Coefficients
> > $$A_0 = \alpha - \frac{1}{\pi}\int_0^\pi (0.100\cos\theta_0)\,d\theta_0 = \alpha - 0 = \alpha$$
> > $$A_1 = \frac{2}{\pi}\int_0^\pi (0.100\cos\theta_0)\cos\theta_0 \, d\theta_0 = 0.100 \times \frac{2}{\pi}\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\right) = 0.100$$
> > $$A_2 = \frac{2}{\pi}\int_0^\pi (0.100\cos\theta_0)\cos(2\theta_0) \, d\theta_0 = 0$$
> > 
> > #### Step 3: Zero-Lift Angle of Attack ($\alpha_0$)
> > $$\alpha_0 = -\frac{1}{2} A_1 = -\frac{0.100}{2} = -0.050\text{ rad}$$
> > In degrees:
> > $$\alpha_0 = -0.050 \times \frac{180}{\pi} = -\frac{9.0}{\pi} = -2.86479^\circ \approx -2.86^\circ$$
> > Target Answer: **$\alpha_0 = -2.86^\circ$** · Tolerance: **$[-2.89^\circ, -2.84^\circ]$**
> > 
> > #### Step 4: Lift Coefficient at $\alpha = 4.00^\circ$
> > $$\alpha = 4.00^\circ \times \frac{\pi}{180} = 0.069813\text{ rad}$$
> > $$c_l = 2\pi(\alpha - \alpha_0) = 2\pi(0.069813 - (-0.050)) = 2\pi(0.119813) = 0.75281$$
> > Target Answer: **$c_l = 0.753$**
> > 
> > #### Step 5: Quarter-Chord Pitching Moment ($c_{m,c/4}$)
> > $$c_{m,c/4} = \frac{\pi}{4}(A_2 - A_1) = \frac{\pi}{4}(0 - 0.100) = -0.025\pi = -0.07854$$
> > Target Answer: **$c_{m,c/4} = -0.0785$**
> > 
> > #### Step 6: Center of Pressure Location ($x_{cp}$)
> > $$\frac{x_{cp}}{c} = 0.25 - \frac{c_{m,c/4}}{c_l} = 0.25 - \frac{-0.07854}{0.75281} = 0.25 + 0.10433 = 0.35433$$
> > Target Answer: **$x_{cp}/c = 0.354$** (or $35.4\% c$)
> >

### 🔬 Apex Worked Example 26: Plain Flap Deflection Analysis

> [!example]- 🔥 WE-26 · Aerodynamic Impact of Trailing-Edge Plain Flap
> **Problem Statement (GATE Level 4 NAT):**  
> A symmetric thin airfoil has a plain trailing-edge flap hinged at $75\%$ chord ($x_f = 0.75 c$). The flap is deflected downward by $\delta_f = 10.0^\circ$.  
> 
> **Tasks:**  
> 1. Determine the hinge coordinate angle $\theta_f$ in radians.  
> 2. Calculate the flap effectiveness parameter $\tau = \frac{d\alpha}{d\delta_f}$.  
> 3. Compute the shift in the zero-lift angle of attack $\Delta \alpha_0$ in degrees.  
> 4. Determine the increment in section lift coefficient $\Delta c_l$ at fixed angle of attack.
> 
> > [!success]- **Complete Analytical Solution & Verification:**
> > 
> > #### Step 1: Flap Hinge Angle ($\theta_f$)
> > $$x_f = \frac{c}{2}(1 - \cos\theta_f) = 0.75 c \implies 1 - \cos\theta_f = 1.50 \implies \cos\theta_f = -0.50$$
> > $$\theta_f = \arccos(-0.50) = \frac{2\pi}{3} = 120.0^\circ = 2.09440\text{ rad}$$
> > 
> > #### Step 2: Flap Effectiveness Parameter ($\tau$)
> > From thin airfoil theory for flapped airfoils:
> > $$\tau = 1 - \frac{\theta_f - \sin\theta_f}{\pi}$$
> > With $\theta_f = 2\pi/3$ and $\sin(2\pi/3) = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \approx 0.866025$:
> > $$\theta_f - \sin\theta_f = 2.09440 - 0.866025 = 1.22837\text{ rad}$$
> > $$\tau = 1 - \frac{1.22837}{\pi} = 1 - 0.39099 = 0.60901$$
> > Target Answer: **$\tau = 0.609$**
> > 
> > #### Step 3: Shift in Zero-Lift Angle of Attack ($\Delta\alpha_0$)
> > $$\Delta\alpha_0 = -\tau \delta_f = -0.60901 \times 10.0^\circ = -6.0901^\circ$$
> > Target Answer: **$\Delta\alpha_0 = -6.09^\circ$**
> > 
> > #### Step 4: Increment in Lift Coefficient ($\Delta c_l$)
> > $$\Delta c_l = 2\pi(-\Delta\alpha_0\text{ rad}) = 2\pi \times (\tau \delta_f\text{ rad})$$
> > $$\delta_f\text{ rad} = 10.0 \times \frac{\pi}{180} = \frac{\pi}{18} = 0.174533\text{ rad}$$
> > $$\Delta c_l = 2\pi \times 0.60901 \times 0.174533 = 2\pi \times 0.10629 = 0.66787$$
> > Target Answer: **$\Delta c_l = 0.668$** · Official Tolerance: **$[0.660, 0.675]$**

### 🔬 Apex Worked Example 27: Calculus Optimization of Aircraft Glide Efficiency

> [!example]- 🔥 WE-27 · Optimization of Glider Descent Ratio via Calculus Extrema
> **Problem Statement (GATE Level 4 NAT):**  
> A sailplane has aircraft drag polar given by:
> $$C_D = 0.0150 + 0.0300 C_L^2$$
> 
> **Tasks:**  
> 1. Formulate the glide angle function $\tan\gamma = \frac{D}{L} = \frac{C_D}{C_L}$.  
> 2. Use calculus differentiation ($d/dC_L = 0$) to determine the optimum lift coefficient $C_{L,\text{opt}}$ that minimizes glide angle $\gamma$ (maximizes glide distance).  
> 3. Calculate the maximum lift-to-drag ratio $(L/D)_{\max}$.  
> 4. If the glider is released from an altitude of $h = 2500\text{ m}$ in still air, what is its maximum theoretical glide range $R_{\max}$ in kilometers?
> 
> > [!success]- **Complete Analytical Solution & Verification:**
> > 
> > #### Step 1: Glide Function
> > $$f(C_L) = \frac{C_D}{C_L} = \frac{0.0150}{C_L} + 0.0300 C_L$$
> > 
> > #### Step 2: Critical Point via First Derivative
> > Setting $f'(C_L) = 0$:
> > $$f'(C_L) = -\frac{0.0150}{C_L^2} + 0.0300 = 0$$
> > $$0.0300 C_L^2 = 0.0150 \implies C_L^2 = \frac{0.0150}{0.0300} = 0.5000$$
> > $$C_{L,\text{opt}} = \sqrt{0.5000} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \approx 0.707107$$
> > Check second derivative:
> > $$f''(C_L) = \frac{2(0.0150)}{C_L^3} > 0 \implies \text{Strict Minimum!}$$
> > Target Answer: **$C_{L,\text{opt}} = 0.707$**
> > 
> > #### Step 3: Maximum Lift-to-Drag Ratio
> > At $C_{L,\text{opt}}$:
> > $$C_D = 0.0150 + 0.0300(0.5000) = 0.0150 + 0.0150 = 0.0300$$
> > (Notice zero-lift drag equals induced drag: $C_{D0} = C_{Di}$!).  
> > $$\left(\frac{L}{D}\right)_{\max} = \frac{C_L}{C_D} = \frac{0.707107}{0.0300} = 23.5702$$
> > Target Answer: **$(L/D)_{\max} = 23.57$**

> > Range $R = h \times (L/D)_{\max}$:
> > $$R_{\max} = 2500\text{ m} \times 23.5702 = 58925.5\text{ m} \approx 58.93\text{ km}$$

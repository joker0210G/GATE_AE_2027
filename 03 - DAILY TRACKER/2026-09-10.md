### Step 2 — Integral Form & Reynolds Transport Theorem (RTT) for Mass (15 min)
Open `01.2a - Conservation of Mass`.

For a control volume (CV) bounded by control surface (CS):

$$\boxed{\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\iiint_{\text{CV}} \rho\,d\mathcal{V} + \iint_{\text{CS}} \rho(\vec{V} \cdot \hat{n})\,dA = 0}$$

- **1D Steady Multi-Port Flow:**
  $$\sum \dot{m}_{\text{in}} = \sum \dot{m}_{\text{out}} \iff \rho_1 A_1 \bar{V}_1 = \rho_2 A_2 \bar{V}_2$$
- **Area-Averaged Mean Velocity ($\bar{V}$):**
  $$\boxed{\bar{V} = \frac{1}{A} \iint u(y, z)\,dA}$$
  *(For laminar pipe flow with parabolic profile $u(r) = U_{\max}(1 - r^2/R^2) \implies \mathbf{\bar{V} = \frac{1}{2}U_{\max}}$)*.

---

### Step 3 — Differential Continuity Equation in Various Coordinates (20 min)

Applying Gauss Divergence Theorem to the integral mass equation yields:

#### 1. Conservative (Divergence) Form:
$$\boxed{\frac{\partial \rho}{\partial t} + \nabla \cdot (\rho \vec{V}) = 0}$$

#### 2. Non-Conservative (Material Derivative) Form:
$$\boxed{\frac{D\rho}{Dt} + \rho (\nabla \cdot \vec{V}) = 0}$$

#### 3. Incompressible Flow Condition ($\rho = \text{constant}$ or $\frac{D\rho}{Dt} = 0$):
$$\boxed{\nabla \cdot \vec{V} = 0 \quad (\text{Velocity field is Solenoidal})}$$

- **Cartesian Coordinates:**
  $$\frac{\partial u}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial v}{\partial y} + \frac{\partial w}{\partial z} = 0$$
- **Cylindrical Polar Coordinates $(r, \theta, z)$:**
  $$\boxed{\frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial(r v_r)}{\partial r} + \frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial v_\theta}{\partial \theta} + \frac{\partial v_z}{\partial z} = 0}$$
- **2D Axisymmetric Polar Flow ($v_\theta = 0$):**
  $$\frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial(r v_r)}{\partial r} + \frac{\partial v_z}{\partial z} = 0$$

---

### Step 4 — Quasi-1D Compressible Nozzle Area-Velocity Relation (15 min)

For a variable-area streamtube / nozzle with 1D steady compressible flow:
$$\ln(\rho A V) = \text{const} \implies \frac{d\rho}{\rho} + \frac{dA}{A} + \frac{dV}{V} = 0$$

Combining with 1D Euler equation ($dp = -\rho V dV$) and speed of sound ($a^2 = dp/d\rho \implies \frac{d\rho}{\rho} = -M^2 \frac{dV}{V}$):

$$\boxed{\frac{dA}{A} = (M^2 - 1)\frac{dV}{V}}$$

```
               QUASI-1D NOZZLE & DIFFUSER BEHAVIOR
┌───────────────┬───────────────────────────┬───────────────────────────┐
│ Mach Regime   │ Converging Duct (dA < 0)  │ Diverging Duct (dA > 0)   │
├───────────────┼───────────────────────────┼───────────────────────────┤
│ Subsonic M < 1│ Nozzle (dV > 0, Accel)    │ Diffuser (dV < 0, Decel)  │
│ Supersonic M>1│ Diffuser (dV < 0, Decel)  │ Nozzle (dV > 0, Accel)    │
│ Sonic M = 1   │ Throat (dA = 0)           │ Throat (dA = 0)           │
└───────────────┴───────────────────────────┴───────────────────────────┘
```

> ⚡ **Core Takeaway:** To accelerate a gas from subsonic to supersonic flow, the nozzle MUST be a **Convergent-Divergent (de Laval) Nozzle**!

---

### Step 5 — Traps & Self-Check (5 min)
> 🔴 **Trap 1: Cylindrical Polar Continuity Expansion**
> Note that $\frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial(r v_r)}{\partial r} = \frac{\partial v_r}{\partial r} + \frac{v_r}{r}$. Do not forget the $v_r/r$ term!
> 🔴 **Trap 2: Incompressible vs. Steady**
> Incompressible means $\nabla \cdot \vec{V} = 0$. It does NOT mean the flow is steady! Unsteady incompressible flows also satisfy $\nabla \cdot \vec{V} = 0$.

### Step 3 — D3 Mini Quiz (3:45–4:00) — 5 Questions (Timed 10 min)

> **Q1 (1 mark):** For an incompressible 2D velocity field $u = 3x^2 - y^2$, the $y$-component of velocity $v(x, y)$ (with $v(x, 0) = 0$) is:
> **(A)** $-6xy$ **(B)** $6xy$ **(C)** $-3xy$ **(D)** $3x^2 y$

> **Q2 (2 marks):** Air flows steadily through a 2D channel of height $2H$ with velocity profile $u(y) = U_{\max}\left(1 - \frac{y^2}{H^2}\right)$ where $-H \le y \le H$. The average velocity $\bar{V}$ across the channel is:
> **(A)** $\frac{1}{2}U_{\max}$ **(B)** $\frac{2}{3}U_{\max}$ **(C)** $\frac{3}{4}U_{\max}$ **(D)** $U_{\max}$

> **Q3 (1 mark):** In a supersonic wind tunnel nozzle at a station where $M = 1.5$, the cross-sectional area increases in the flow direction by $5\%$. The percentage change in flow velocity is:
> **(A)** $+4.0\%$ **(B)** $+5.0\%$ **(C)** $-4.0\%$ **(D)** $+1.25\%$

> **Q4 (2 marks):** In cylindrical coordinates, an incompressible axisymmetric velocity field has $v_r = \frac{2}{r}$ and $v_\theta = 0$. The axial velocity component $v_z(r, z)$ MUST satisfy:
> **(A)** $\frac{\partial v_z}{\partial z} = 0$ **(B)** $\frac{\partial v_z}{\partial z} = -\frac{2}{r^2}$ **(C)** $v_z = 2z$ **(D)** $\frac{\partial v_z}{\partial r} = 0$

> **Q5 (1 mark NAT):** In a convergent duct, water enters at cross-section $A_1 = 0.4\text{ m}^2$ with velocity $V_1 = 3\text{ m/s}$. If the exit area is $A_2 = 0.1\text{ m}^2$, the exit velocity $V_2$ in $\text{m/s}$ is ___.

> [!faq]- 🔍 View D3 Mini Quiz Answers & Step-by-Step Solutions
>
> **Q1: (A) -6xy**
> For incompressible 2D flow: $\frac{\partial u}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial v}{\partial y} = 0$.
> $\frac{\partial u}{\partial x} = 6x \implies \frac{\partial v}{\partial y} = -6x \implies v(x, y) = -6xy + g(x)$.
> Using $v(x, 0) = 0 \implies g(x) = 0 \implies \mathbf{v(x, y) = -6xy}$.
>
> **Q2: (B) (2/3) U_max**
> $\bar{V} = \frac{1}{2H} \int_{-H}^H U_{\max}\left(1 - \frac{y^2}{H^2}\right) dy = \frac{U_{\max}}{2H} \cdot 2\left[y - \frac{y^3}{3H^2}\right]_0^H = \frac{U_{\max}}{H}\left(H - \frac{H}{3}\right) = \mathbf{\frac{2}{3}U_{\max}}$.
>
> **Q3: (A) +4.0%**
> $\frac{dA}{A} = (M^2 - 1)\frac{dV}{V} \implies \frac{dV}{V} = \frac{dA/A}{M^2 - 1} = \frac{+0.05}{1.5^2 - 1} = \frac{+0.05}{2.25 - 1} = \frac{+0.05}{1.25} = \mathbf{+0.04} \implies \mathbf{+4.0\%}$.
>
> **Q4: (A) ∂v_z/∂z = 0**
> Continuity in cylindrical polar: $\frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial(r v_r)}{\partial r} + \frac{\partial v_z}{\partial z} = 0$.
> $r v_r = r\left(\frac{2}{r}\right) = 2 \implies \frac{\partial(2)}{\partial r} = 0 \implies 0 + \frac{\partial v_z}{\partial z} = 0 \implies \mathbf{\frac{\partial v_z}{\partial z} = 0}$.
>
> **Q5: 12**
> Incompressible mass conservation: $A_1 V_1 = A_2 V_2 \implies (0.4)(3) = (0.1) V_2 \implies 1.2 = 0.1 V_2 \implies V_2 = \mathbf{12\text{ m/s}}$.

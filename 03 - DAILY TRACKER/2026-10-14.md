### 🌊 Set A — Normal Shocks & Rankine-Hugoniot Relations (FC-196 → FC-203)

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-196 · Downstream Mach Number Formula
> **Question:** State the formula for downstream Mach number $M_2$ across a stationary normal shock in terms of upstream Mach number $M_1$.
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > $$M_2^2 = \frac{2 + (\gamma - 1)M_1^2}{2\gamma M_1^2 - (\gamma - 1)}$$
> > For air ($\gamma = 1.4$):
> > $$M_2^2 = \frac{5 + M_1^2}{7 M_1^2 - 1}$$
> > ⚠️ **AIR 1 Trap:** If $M_1 > 1.0$, $M_2$ is ALWAYS strictly subsonic ($M_2 < 1.0$).

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-197 · Asymptotic Limit of Downstream Mach Number
> **Question:** What is the limit of $M_2$ as upstream Mach number $M_1 \to \infty$ in air ($\gamma = 1.4$)?
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > $$\lim_{M_1 \to \infty} M_2 = \sqrt{\frac{\gamma - 1}{2\gamma}} = \sqrt{\frac{0.4}{2.8}} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{7}} \approx 0.37796$$
> > No normal shock in air can ever decelerate flow below $M = 0.378$!

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-198 · Static Pressure Ratio Across Normal Shock
> **Question:** State the static pressure ratio $p_2 / p_1$ across a normal shock in air ($\gamma = 1.4$).
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > $$\frac{p_2}{p_1} = 1 + \frac{2\gamma}{\gamma + 1}(M_1^2 - 1) = \frac{7 M_1^2 - 1}{6}$$
> > Static pressure ALWAYS increases across a shock wave ($p_2 > p_1$).

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-199 · Density and Velocity Ratio Across Normal Shock
> **Question:** State the density ratio $\rho_2 / \rho_1 = u_1 / u_2$ across a normal shock and its asymptotic limit as $M_1 \to \infty$.
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > $$\frac{\rho_2}{\rho_1} = \frac{u_1}{u_2} = \frac{(\gamma + 1)M_1^2}{2 + (\gamma - 1)M_1^2} = \frac{6 M_1^2}{M_1^2 + 5}$$
> > $$\lim_{M_1 \to \infty} \frac{\rho_2}{\rho_1} = \frac{\gamma + 1}{\gamma - 1} = \frac{2.4}{0.4} = 6.000$$
> > ⚠️ **AIR 1 Trap:** Unlike pressure which grows as $M_1^2$, density is strictly capped at $6.0$!

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-200 · Stagnation Temperature Conservation
> **Question:** Does stagnation temperature $T_0$ change across a normal shock in steady adiabatic flow?
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > **NO!** In adiabatic flow with no heat exchange ($q = 0$) and no shaft work ($w = 0$):
> > $$T_{02} \equiv T_{01}$$
> > Stagnation temperature is an exact invariant across normal and oblique shocks!

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-201 · Stagnation Pressure Drop and Entropy Rise
> **Question:** Express the stagnation pressure ratio $p_{02} / p_{01}$ in terms of the entropy change $\Delta s = s_2 - s_1$.
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > $$\Delta s = -R \ln\left(\frac{p_{02}}{p_{01}}\right) \implies \frac{p_{02}}{p_{01}} = \exp\left(-\frac{\Delta s}{R}\right) < 1.0$$
> > Because viscous dissipation and thermal conduction inside the shock make it irreversible ($\Delta s > 0$), stagnation pressure ALWAYS drops across a shock wave!

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-202 · Prandtl Normal Shock Relation
> **Question:** State the Prandtl relation connecting velocities $u_1, u_2$ and sonic speed $a^*$.
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > $$u_1 u_2 = (a^*)^2 \iff M_1^* M_2^* = 1$$
> > Since $M_1^* > 1$, downstream must have $M_2^* < 1$, rigorously proving that downstream flow must be subsonic.

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-203 · Rayleigh Supersonic Pitot Tube Formula
> **Question:** Write the Rayleigh Pitot formula relating measured Pitot pressure $p_{02}$ to freestream static pressure $p_1$.
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > $$\frac{p_{02}}{p_1} = \left( \frac{\gamma + 1}{2} M_1^2 \right)^{\frac{\gamma}{\gamma - 1}} \left[ \frac{\gamma + 1}{2\gamma M_1^2 - (\gamma - 1)} \right]^{\frac{1}{\gamma - 1}}$$
> > For air ($\gamma = 1.4$):
> > $$\frac{p_{02}}{p_1} = (1.2 M_1^2)^{3.5} \left( \frac{6}{7 M_1^2 - 1} \right)^{2.5}$$

### 📐 Set B — Engineering Mathematics: Directional Derivatives & Tangents (FC-204 → FC-210)

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-204 · Angle Between Normal Vectors
> **Question:** If the gradient vectors of two surfaces at an intersection point are $\vec{n}_1 = (1, 1, 0)$ and $\vec{n}_2 = (0, 1, 1)$, what is the angle between the surfaces?
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > $$\cos\theta = \frac{|\vec{n}_1 \cdot \vec{n}_2|}{|\vec{n}_1| |\vec{n}_2|} = \frac{|1(0) + 1(1) + 0(1)|}{\sqrt{1 + 1} \sqrt{1 + 1}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2} \sqrt{2}} = \frac{1}{2} \implies \theta = 60^\circ$$

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-205 · Tangent Plane to Sphere
> **Question:** Find the tangent plane to $x^2 + y^2 + z^2 = 3$ at the point $(1, 1, 1)$.
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > $\nabla F = 2x\hat{i} + 2y\hat{j} + 2z\hat{k} = 2\hat{i} + 2\hat{j} + 2\hat{k}$.  
> > Dividing by 2: $\hat{i} + \hat{j} + \hat{k}$.  
> > Tangent plane: $1(x - 1) + 1(y - 1) + 1(z - 1) = 0 \implies x + y + z = 3$.

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-206 · Directional Derivative of Incompressible Field
> **Question:** In an incompressible flow ($\nabla \cdot \vec{V} = 0$), can the directional derivative of velocity along the streamline be non-zero?
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > **YES!** Along a streamline, $V \partial V / \partial s$ can be positive (accelerating) provided the cross-stream normal spacing narrows to satisfy $\partial u / \partial x + \partial v / \partial y = 0$.

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-207 · Orthogonal Vector to Gradient
> **Question:** In 2D, if $\nabla f = (a, b)$, what vector is tangent to the level curve $f(x, y) = C$?
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > Any vector orthogonal to $(a, b)$: $\vec{t} = (-b, a)$ or $(b, -a)$, since $(a, b) \cdot (-b, a) = -ab + ba = 0$.

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-208 · Directional Derivative along Curve $\vec{r}(t)$
> **Question:** Express $\frac{d}{dt} f(\vec{r}(t))$ in terms of the directional derivative.
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > By multivariable chain rule:
> > $$\frac{df}{dt} = \nabla f \cdot \vec{r}'(t) = |\vec{r}'(t)| (\nabla f \cdot \hat{u}) = |\vec{r}'(t)| D_{\hat{u}} f$$
> > where $\hat{u} = \vec{r}'(t) / |\vec{r}'(t)|$ is the unit tangent vector to the trajectory curve.

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-209 · Maximum Directional Derivative of Temperature
> **Question:** If temperature in a combustion chamber is $T(x, y, z) = 1500 - 50(x^2 + y^2 + z^2)$, what is the maximum rate of cooling at $(1, 2, 2)$?
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > $\nabla T = -100(x\hat{i} + y\hat{j} + z\hat{k})$. At $(1, 2, 2)$: $\nabla T = -100(\hat{i} + 2\hat{j} + 2\hat{k})$.  
> > $|\nabla T| = 100 \sqrt{1 + 4 + 4} = 100(3) = 300\text{ K/m}$.  
> > Maximum rate of cooling (decrease) is $-300\text{ K/m}$ in the direction $(1, 2, 2)$.

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-210 · Stationary Point Condition in 3D
> **Question:** What is the condition for a point $(x_0, y_0, z_0)$ to be a stationary point of $f(x, y, z)$?
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > The gradient vector must vanish completely:
> > $$\nabla f(x_0, y_0, z_0) = \mathbf{0} \iff \frac{\partial f}{\partial x} = 0, \quad \frac{\partial f}{\partial y} = 0, \quad \frac{\partial f}{\partial z} = 0$$

> [!example]- 🔥 WE-40 · Complete Property Evaluation Across a Normal Shock Wave
> **Problem Statement (GATE AE Level 4 NAT):**  
> A supersonic flight vehicle cruises at Mach number $M_1 = 2.000$ in ambient atmospheric air where static pressure is $p_1 = 40.00\text{ kPa}$ and static temperature is $T_1 = 250.0\text{ K}$. A normal shock stands across the engine inlet diffuser. Air properties: $\gamma = 1.400, R = 287.05\text{ J/(kg}\cdot\text{K)}$.  
> 
> **Tasks:**  
> 1. Calculate the downstream Mach number $M_2$.  
> 2. Determine the static pressure $p_2$ in $\text{kPa}$ and pressure ratio $p_2 / p_1$.  
> 3. Calculate the static temperature $T_2$ in Kelvin.

> > [!success]- **Complete Analytical Solution & TCS Calculator Keystroke Ledger:**
> > 
> > #### Step 1: Downstream Mach Number ($M_2$)
> > $$M_2^2 = \frac{5 + M_1^2}{7 M_1^2 - 1} = \frac{5 + 2.000^2}{7(2.000^2) - 1} = \frac{5 + 4.000}{7(4.000) - 1} = \frac{9.000}{28.000 - 1} = \frac{9.000}{27.000} = \frac{1}{3} = 0.333333$$
> > $$M_2 = \sqrt{\frac{1}{3}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}} = 0.577350$$

> > $$p_2 = 40.00\text{ kPa} \times 4.500 = 180.00\text{ kPa}$$

### 🔬 Apex Worked Example 41: Supersonic Pitot Tube & Rayleigh Formula Inversion

> [!example]- 🔥 WE-41 · Inverting the Rayleigh Pitot Tube Formula for Freestream Mach
> **Problem Statement (GATE Level 5 NAT):**  
> A supersonic aircraft is equipped with a Pitot-static tube. The nose Pitot probe measures a stagnation pressure of $p_{02} = 340.0\text{ kPa}$, while the fuselage flush static port measures an ambient static pressure of $p_1 = 40.00\text{ kPa}$.  
> Working fluid is air ($\gamma = 1.400$).  
> 
> **Tasks:**  
> 1. Compute the measured pressure ratio $p_{02} / p_1$.  
> 2. Confirm that the flow is supersonic ($p_{02} / p_1 > 1.893$).  
> 3. Use the Rayleigh Pitot formula to determine the freestream Mach number $M_1$ to two decimal places.
> 
> > [!success]- **Complete Analytical Solution & Verification:**
> > 
> > #### Step 1: Pressure Ratio
> > $$\frac{p_{02}}{p_1} = \frac{340.0\text{ kPa}}{40.00\text{ kPa}} = 8.500$$
> > For sonic flow ($M_1 = 1.0$), isentropic $p_0 / p_1 = (1.2)^{3.5} = 1.8929$. Since $8.500 \gg 1.893$, a detached bow shock definitely stands ahead of the probe nose.
> > 
> > #### Step 2: The Rayleigh Pitot Formula
> > $$\frac{p_{02}}{p_1} = \left( 1.2 M_1^2 \right)^{3.5} \left( \frac{6}{7 M_1^2 - 1} \right)^{2.5} = 8.500$$
> > 
> > #### Step 3: Iterative Evaluation
> > - **Trial 1: At $M_1 = 2.40$:**
> >   $$M_1^2 = 5.760$$
> >   $$(1.2 \times 5.760)^{3.5} = (6.912)^{3.5} = 868.96$$
> >   $$7 M_1^2 - 1 = 7(5.760) - 1 = 40.320 - 1 = 39.320$$
> >   $$\left(\frac{6}{39.320}\right)^{2.5} = (0.152594)^{2.5} = 0.009088$$
> >   Ratio $= 868.96 \times 0.009088 = 7.897$ ($< 8.500 \implies M_1 > 2.40$).
> > 
> > - **Trial 2: At $M_1 = 2.50$:**
> >   $$M_1^2 = 6.250$$
> >   $$(1.2 \times 6.250)^{3.5} = (7.500)^{3.5} = 1148.91$$

> [!example]- 🔥 WE-42 · Geometric Normal Vector to Detached Paraboloidal Bow Shock
> **Problem Statement (GATE Level 4 NAT):**

> 4. If freestream Mach is $M_1 = 3.00$, compute the normal component of Mach number $M_{n1} = M_1 \sin\beta$ entering the shock at point $P$.
> 
> > [!success]- **Complete Analytical Solution & Verification:**
> > 
> > #### Step 1: Level Surface Equation
> > $$F(x, y, z) = x - 0.200 y^2 - 0.200 z^2 + 0.500 = 0$$
> > 
> > #### Step 2: Normal Gradient at $P(0.300, 1.000, 1.000)$
> > Check that $P$ lies on the surface:
> > $$0.300 - 0.200(1) - 0.200(1) + 0.500 = 0.300 - 0.400 + 0.500 = 0.400 \ne 0 \implies \text{let } x = 0.2(1+1) - 0.5 = -0.100\text{ m}.$$

> > $$\frac{\partial F}{\partial x} = 1.000$$
> > $$\frac{\partial F}{\partial y} = -0.400 y = -0.400(1.000) = -0.400$$
> > $$\frac{\partial F}{\partial z} = -0.400 z = -0.400(1.000) = -0.400$$

> > $$\hat{n} = \frac{1.000}{1.14891}\hat{i} - \frac{0.400}{1.14891}\hat{j} - \frac{0.400}{1.14891}\hat{k} = 0.87039\hat{i} - 0.34816\hat{j} - 0.34816\hat{k}$$

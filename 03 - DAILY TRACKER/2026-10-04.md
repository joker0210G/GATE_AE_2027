## 07:45–08:45 · 🌅 Morning Flashcard Battery & GA

### 🌊 Set A — Fluid Statics & Manometry (FC-46 → FC-53)

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-46 · Hydrostatic Differential Law & Isobars
> **Question:** Write the differential equation governing pressure in a static fluid subject to gravity $\vec{g} = -g\hat{k}$, and state the geometric orientation of surfaces of constant pressure.
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > - Governing Equation: $\nabla p = \rho \vec{g} \implies \frac{\partial p}{\partial x} = 0, \quad \frac{\partial p}{\partial y} = 0, \quad \frac{\partial p}{\partial z} = -\rho g$
> > - Integrated form for $\rho = \text{const}$: $p_2 - p_1 = -\rho g (z_2 - z_1) \iff p = p_0 + \rho g h$ (where $h$ is vertical depth).
> > - Isobars (surfaces of equal pressure) are **strictly horizontal planes** ($z = \text{const}$).
> > ⚠️ **AIR-1 Trap:** In an accelerating fluid container (linear acceleration $\vec{a}$ or rigid body rotation $\Omega$), isobars tilt perpendicular to the effective gravity vector $\vec{g}_{\text{eff}} = \vec{g} - \vec{a}$.

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-47 · The Golden Manometer Jump Rule
> **Question:** State the continuous traversal "Jump Rule" used to solve any multi-fluid differential manometer in a single line.
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > - Start at one boundary (e.g. $p_A$).
> > - Moving **DOWN** through fluid height $\Delta z$: **ADD** $+\rho g \Delta z$.
> > - Moving **UP** through fluid height $\Delta z$: **SUBTRACT** $-\rho g \Delta z$.
> > - Horizontal jump across the **same continuous static fluid** produces zero pressure change ($p_{\text{left}} = p_{\text{right}}$).
> > - Equate final accumulated sum to the destination pressure ($p_B$ or $p_{\text{atm}}$).
> > ⚠️ **AIR-1 Trap:** You can ONLY jump horizontally if the fluid between the two points is the **same fluid and continuously connected**. Jumping across a divider or different fluid layer is invalid.

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-48 · Differential U-Tube Formula (Horizontal Pipe)
> **Question:** For a horizontal pipe transporting liquid of density $\rho_f$, connected to a U-tube containing manometric fluid $\rho_m$ with deflection $h$, what is $\Delta p = p_1 - p_2$?
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > - Formula: $\Delta p = (\rho_m - \rho_f) g h = \rho_f g h \left(\frac{S_m}{S_f} - 1\right)$
> > ⚠️ **AIR-1 Trap:** If the pipe is NOT horizontal ($z_1 \neq z_2$), the piezometric head difference is measured:
> > $(p_1 + \rho_f g z_1) - (p_2 + \rho_f g z_2) = (\rho_m - \rho_f) g h$. Do not forget the elevation head $\rho_f g (z_2 - z_1)$!

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-49 · Inverted U-Tube Differential Manometer
> **Question:** When is an inverted U-tube manometer used, and what is the formula for $\Delta p$ between two horizontal points in a liquid of density $\rho_f$ if the top fluid has density $\rho_m < \rho_f$?
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > - Used for measuring **small pressure differences** between liquids with high precision.
> > - Formula: $\Delta p = p_1 - p_2 = (\rho_f - \rho_m) g h$
> > ⚠️ **AIR-1 Trap:** Notice the density subtraction order is flipped! It is $(\rho_f - \rho_m)$, NOT $(\rho_m - \rho_f)$, because the manometric fluid is lighter and sits at the top.

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-50 · Inclined-Tube Manometer with Reservoir Correction
> **Question:** In an inclined manometer with reservoir area $A$, tube area $a$, inclination angle $\theta$ to the horizontal, and reading scale displacement $L$, what is the true $\Delta p$ and magnification factor $M$?
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > - Volume conservation: Meniscus drop in reservoir $\Delta y = \frac{a}{A} L$.
> > - Total vertical height difference: $h_{\text{total}} = L \sin\theta + \Delta y = L \left(\sin\theta + \frac{a}{A}\right)$.
> > - Pressure formula: $\Delta p = \rho_m g L \left(\sin\theta + \frac{a}{A}\right)$.
> > - Magnification: $M = \frac{L}{h_{\text{total}}} = \frac{1}{\sin\theta + a/A}$.
> > ⚠️ **AIR-1 Trap:** If $A \gg a$, $a/A \to 0$ and $M \approx 1/\sin\theta$. But in GATE NAT, if $a/A$ is given (e.g. $1/100$), neglecting it causes a 5–10% error that fails the tolerance check!

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-51 · Two-Fluid Differential Micromanometer
> **Question:** What is the formula for pressure difference $\Delta p$ in a micromanometer with two large reservoirs of area $A$, tube area $a$, and two immiscible liquids $\rho_1 > \rho_2$?
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > - Formula: $\Delta p = g h \left[(\rho_1 - \rho_2) + \frac{a}{A}(\rho_1 + \rho_2)\right]$
> > - When $a/A \ll 1$: $\Delta p \approx (\rho_1 - \rho_2) g h \implies h = \frac{\Delta p}{(\rho_1 - \rho_2) g}$.
> > ⚠️ **AIR-1 Trap:** By choosing two liquids with very close densities (e.g. $\Delta \rho = 10\text{ kg/m}^3$), $h$ is magnified by a factor of 100 compared to water!

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-52 · Capillarity Error & Cancellation Invariant
> **Question:** What is the capillary rise formula, and under what condition does capillary error completely vanish in a manometer?
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > - Capillary rise: $h_c = \frac{4 \sigma \cos\theta_c}{\rho g d}$
> > - **Cancellation Invariant:** In a U-tube of **uniform internal diameter** ($d_1 = d_2$), the capillary rise is identical in both limbs and cancels out completely: $\Delta h_{\text{error}} = 0$.
> > ⚠️ **AIR-1 Trap:** In a reservoir-type or single-limb manometer, diameter of reservoir $D \gg d_{\text{tube}}$, so capillary depression/rise occurs ONLY in the small tube: $h_{\text{error}} = \frac{4 \sigma \cos\theta_c}{\rho g d_{\text{tube}}}$. Tube diameter must exceed $10\text{ mm}$ to suppress this.

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-53 · Center of Pressure vs Centroid of Submerged Surface
> **Question:** For a plane surface of area $A$ inclined at angle $\theta$ to the horizontal, submerged in liquid of density $\rho$, state the total hydrostatic force $F$ and the vertical depth of center of pressure $h_{cp}$ relative to centroid depth $\bar{h}$.
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > - Resultant Force: $F = \rho g \bar{h} A$ (acts through center of pressure).
> > - Center of Pressure depth: $h_{cp} = \bar{h} + \frac{I_{xx,c} \sin^2\theta}{\bar{h} A}$
> > ⚠️ **AIR-1 Trap:** Because $I_{xx,c} > 0$, $h_{cp}$ is **ALWAYS strictly below the centroid** ($h_{cp} > \bar{h}$) for any submerged body! It approaches $\bar{h}$ only as depth $\bar{h} \to \infty$.

### 📐 Set B — Linear Algebra: Matrix Rank, Sylvester & Eigen Introduction (FC-54 → FC-60)

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-54 · Four Equivalent Definitions of Matrix Rank
> **Question:** State the 4 equivalent characterizations of $\text{Rank}(A_{m \times n}) = r$.
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > 1. **Determinant Minor:** Order of the largest square submatrix with non-zero determinant.
> > 2. **Row Space:** Dimension of row space ($\dim C(A^T) = r$, number of linearly independent rows).
> > 3. **Column Space:** Dimension of column space ($\dim C(A) = r$, number of linearly independent columns).
> > 4. **Pivots in REF:** Number of non-zero rows after reduction to Row Echelon Form via EROs.
> > ⚠️ **AIR-1 Trap:** $\text{Row Rank}(A) = \text{Column Rank}(A) = \text{Rank}(A) \le \min(m, n)$.

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-55 · Rank-Nullity Theorem (Dimension Theorem)
> **Question:** State the Rank-Nullity Theorem for a matrix $A_{m \times n}$ acting as a linear map $\mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}^m$, and define nullity.
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > - Theorem: $\text{Rank}(A) + \text{Nullity}(A) = n$ (where $n$ is the number of **COLUMNS** / dimension of domain).
> > - $\text{Nullity}(A) = \dim(\text{Null}(A)) =$ number of free variables in the homogeneous system $Ax = 0$.
> > ⚠️ **AIR-1 Trap:** The sum equals $n$ (columns), NEVER $m$ (rows)! If $A_{5 \times 8}$ has rank 4, $\text{Nullity} = 8 - 4 = 4$, not $5 - 4 = 1$.

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-56 · Sylvester's Rank Inequality for Matrix Products
> **Question:** State Sylvester's Rank Inequality for the product of two matrices $A_{m \times n}$ and $B_{n \times p}$.
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > $$\text{Rank}(A) + \text{Rank}(B) - n \le \text{Rank}(AB) \le \min(\text{Rank}(A), \text{Rank}(B))$$
> > where $n$ is the inner shared dimension (number of columns of $A =$ number of rows of $B$).
> > ⚠️ **AIR-1 Trap:** If $AB = 0_{m \times p}$, then $\text{Rank}(AB) = 0 \implies \text{Rank}(A) + \text{Rank}(B) \le n$. This is a classic GATE test question!

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-57 · Frobenius Rank Inequality
> **Question:** State the Frobenius Inequality for the product of three conformable matrices $A, B, C$.
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > $$\text{Rank}(ABC) + \text{Rank}(B) \ge \text{Rank}(AB) + \text{Rank}(BC)$$
> > - Special Case: Setting $B = I_n$ yields Sylvester's inequality: $\text{Rank}(AC) + n \ge \text{Rank}(A) + \text{Rank}(C)$.
> > ⚠️ **AIR-1 Trap:** Frobenius inequality applies to triplets of matrices; do not confuse with matrix norm formulas.

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-58 · Rank of Sums and Outer Products
> **Question:** State the bounds on $\text{Rank}(A + B)$ and the rank of an outer product $x y^T$ where $x, y$ are non-zero vectors.
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > - Subadditivity: $|\text{Rank}(A) - \text{Rank}(B)| \le \text{Rank}(A + B) \le \text{Rank}(A) + \text{Rank}(B)$
> > - Outer Product: For non-zero vectors $x_{m \times 1}$ and $y_{n \times 1}$, $\text{Rank}(x y^T) = 1$.
> > ⚠️ **AIR-1 Trap:** Every row of $x y^T$ is a scalar multiple of $y^T$, hence exactly 1 independent row $\implies \text{Rank} = 1$.

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-59 · Invariance of Rank under Matrix Multiplication
> **Question:** If $P_{m \times m}$ and $Q_{n \times n}$ are non-singular matrices, what is $\text{Rank}(P A Q)$?
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > $$\text{Rank}(P A Q) = \text{Rank}(A)$$
> > Multiplying by a non-singular (invertible) matrix does NOT alter the rank of a matrix.
> > ⚠️ **AIR-1 Trap:** Elementary row and column operations correspond to pre- and post-multiplication by invertible elementary matrices, proving why EROs and ECOs preserve rank.

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-60 · Eigenvalue Fundamental Identities (Trace & Determinant)
> **Question:** For an $n \times n$ matrix $A$ with eigenvalues $\lambda_1, \lambda_2, \dots, \lambda_n$, state the relationships with $\text{Tr}(A)$ and $\det(A)$, and the condition for zero eigenvalues.
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > - $\sum_{i=1}^n \lambda_i = \text{Tr}(A)$ (Sum of principal diagonal elements).
> > - $\prod_{i=1}^n \lambda_i = \det(A)$.
> > - $\lambda = 0$ is an eigenvalue if and only if $\det(A) = 0$ ($\text{Rank}(A) < n$).
> > - The number of non-zero eigenvalues cannot exceed $\text{Rank}(A)$.
> > ⚠️ **AIR-1 Trap:** A matrix of rank $r$ has AT MOST $r$ non-zero eigenvalues, but it can have FEWER non-zero eigenvalues (e.g. non-zero nilpotent matrix $\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$ has rank 1, but both eigenvalues are 0).

> [!question]- 🎯 GA-Q32 · Numerical Ability: Ratio of Volumes in Manometer Reservoir
> A cylindrical reservoir of internal diameter $D = 100\text{ mm}$ is connected to an inclined glass tube of diameter $d = 5\text{ mm}$. If the liquid in the tube moves by $L = 200\text{ mm}$, what is the vertical drop $\Delta y$ of the liquid level in the reservoir in mm?  
> (A) $0.25\text{ mm}$  
> (B) $0.50\text{ mm}$  
> (C) $1.00\text{ mm}$  
> (D) $2.00\text{ mm}$  
> > [!success]- **Solution & Distractor Autopsy:**  
> > - **Correct Answer:** (B)  
> > - **Formula:** Volume displaced in tube = Volume displaced from reservoir:
> > $$A \Delta y = a L \implies \Delta y = \frac{a}{A} L = \left(\frac{d}{D}\right)^2 L$$
> > - **Calculation:** $\Delta y = \left(\frac{5}{100}\right)^2 \times 200 = \left(\frac{1}{20}\right)^2 \times 200 = \frac{1}{400} \times 200 = 0.50\text{ mm}$.  
> > - **Trap:** Squaring the diameter ratio is essential ($d^2/D^2$), not $d/D$!

> [!question]- 🎯 GA-Q35 · Logical Reasoning: Syllogism of Matrix Ranks

> (D) Neither Conclusion follows

> [!question]- 🎯 GA-Q36 · Spatial / Geometry: Slanted Cylinder Volume Cut

> (B) $\frac{\pi D^2}{4} \left(\frac{h_1 + h_2}{2}\right)$

> [!question]- 🎯 GA-Q37 · Data Interpretation: Percentage Error in Manometer
> In an inclined manometer measuring $\Delta p$, the actual angle of inclination is $29^\circ$ instead of the calibrated $30^\circ$. Assuming $a/A \approx 0$, what is the relative percentage error in the indicated pressure reading? ($\sin 30^\circ = 0.5000, \sin 29^\circ = 0.4848$).  
> (A) $+3.13\%$  
> (B) $-3.13\%$  
> (C) $+1.52\%$  
> (D) $-1.52\%$  
> > [!success]- **Solution & Distractor Autopsy:**  
> > - **Correct Answer:** (A)  
> > - **Analysis:** Indicated pressure formula assumes $\theta_{\text{nom}} = 30^\circ$: $p_{\text{ind}} = \rho g L \sin 30^\circ$.  
> > Actual pressure is $p_{\text{act}} = \rho g L \sin 29^\circ$.  
> > Relative error in indicated value:
> > $$\frac{p_{\text{ind}} - p_{\text{act}}}{p_{\text{act}}} = \frac{\sin 30^\circ - \sin 29^\circ}{\sin 29^\circ} = \frac{0.5000 - 0.4848}{0.4848} = \frac{0.0152}{0.4848} \approx +0.03135 = +3.14\%$$  
> > - **Trap:** Dividing by $\sin 30^\circ$ instead of the true value $\sin 29^\circ$ gives $3.04\%$.

> [!question]- 🎯 GA-Q38 · Critical Reasoning: Assumption in Flow Measurement

> [!question]- 🎯 GA-Q39 · Numerical Ability: Eigenvalue Invariant
> A $3 \times 3$ matrix $A$ has trace $\text{Tr}(A) = 8$ and determinant $\det(A) = 12$. If one eigenvalue is $\lambda_1 = 3$, what is the sum of the squares of the other two eigenvalues ($\lambda_2^2 + \lambda_3^2$)?  
> (A) 13  
> (B) 17  
> (C) 25  
> (D) 29  
> > [!success]- **Solution & Distractor Autopsy:**  
> > - **Correct Answer:** (B)  
> > - **Step 1:** $\text{Tr}(A) = \lambda_1 + \lambda_2 + \lambda_3 = 8 \implies 3 + \lambda_2 + \lambda_3 = 8 \implies \lambda_2 + \lambda_3 = 5$.  
> > - **Step 2:** $\det(A) = \lambda_1 \lambda_2 \lambda_3 = 12 \implies 3 (\lambda_2 \lambda_3) = 12 \implies \lambda_2 \lambda_3 = 4$.  
> > - **Step 3:** $\lambda_2^2 + \lambda_3^2 = (\lambda_2 + \lambda_3)^2 - 2 \lambda_2 \lambda_3 = 5^2 - 2(4) = 25 - 8 = 17$.  
> > - **Alternative:** Roots of $t^2 - 5t + 4 = 0$ are $t = 1, 4 \implies 1^2 + 4^2 = 17$.

> [!question]- 🎯 GA-Q40 · Quantitative Aptitude: Speed of Sound & Manometer

> (B) $20.2\text{ m/s}$  
> (C) $28.6\text{ m/s}$  
> (D) $40.4\text{ m/s}$

> [!example]- 🔥 WE-11 · High-Precision Two-Fluid Micromanometer Analysis
> **Problem Statement (GATE Level 5 MSQ):**  
> A two-fluid differential micromanometer is employed to measure an ultra-small pressure difference $\Delta p = p_1 - p_2$ across a laminar boundary layer trip wire.  
> - The two large reservoirs have cross-sectional area $A = 1200\text{ mm}^2$ each.

> - Acceleration due to gravity $g = 9.81\text{ m/s}^2$.  
> 
> Under applied pressure $\Delta p$, the differential level difference between the two interfaces in the U-tube is measured as $h = 80.0\text{ mm}$.  
> Which of the following statements are **CORRECT**?  
> (A) The applied pressure difference $\Delta p$ is approximately $31.4\text{ Pa}$.  
> (B) If simple water manometer ($\rho = 1000\text{ kg/m}^3$) had been used, the deflection would have been only $3.2\text{ mm}$.  
> (C) The magnification factor compared to a standard water U-tube is greater than 20.  
> (D) The term $\frac{a}{A}(\rho_1 + \rho_2)$ accounts for the change of liquid levels in the reservoirs.
> 
> > [!success]- **Complete Analytical Solution & Verification:**
> > 
> > #### Step 1: Micromanometer Governing Equation
> > From mass conservation, when the interface in the U-tube shifts by $h$, the liquid surface in the reservoirs shifts by $y = \frac{a}{A} \frac{h}{2}$.  
> > Equating pressures at the lower interface datum:
> > $$\Delta p = p_1 - p_2 = g h \left[ (\rho_1 - \rho_2) + \frac{a}{A}(\rho_1 + \rho_2) \right]$$
> > 
> > #### Step 2: Numerical Evaluation
> > - Density difference: $\rho_1 - \rho_2 = 1010 - 990 = 20.0\text{ kg/m}^3$.
> > - Area ratio: $\frac{a}{A} = \frac{12}{1200} = 0.010$.
> > - Density sum: $\rho_1 + \rho_2 = 1010 + 990 = 2000.0\text{ kg/m}^3$.
> > - Reservoir correction term:
> > $$\frac{a}{A}(\rho_1 + \rho_2) = 0.010 \times 2000.0 = 20.0\text{ kg/m}^3$$
> > - Total effective density:
> > $$\rho_{\text{eff}} = (\rho_1 - \rho_2) + \frac{a}{A}(\rho_1 + \rho_2) = 20.0 + 20.0 = 40.0\text{ kg/m}^3$$
> > 
> > Notice that the reservoir term doubles the effective resistance!
> > - Pressure difference:
> > $$\Delta p = g h \rho_{\text{eff}} = 9.81 \times 0.080 \times 40.0 = 9.81 \times 3.20 = 31.392\text{ Pa} \approx 31.4\text{ Pa}$$
> > Statement (A) is **CORRECT**. Statement (D) is **CORRECT**.
> > 
> > #### Step 3: Comparison with Standard Water U-Tube
> > For a simple single-fluid water manometer ($\rho_w = 1000\text{ kg/m}^3$):
> > $$h_{\text{water}} = \frac{\Delta p}{\rho_w g} = \frac{31.392}{1000 \times 9.81} = 0.00320\text{ m} = 3.20\text{ mm}$$
> > Statement (B) is **CORRECT**.
> > 
> > #### Step 4: Magnification Factor
> > $$M = \frac{h_{\text{micro}}}{h_{\text{water}}} = \frac{80.0\text{ mm}}{3.20\text{ mm}} = 25.0$$
> > Since $25.0 > 20$, Statement (C) is **CORRECT**.
> > 
> > **Final Answer:** All four options **(A), (B), (C), (D)** are CORRECT!

### 🔬 Apex Worked Example 12: Matrix Rank, Sylvester's Inequality & Nullity Bounds

> [!example]- 🔥 WE-12 · Rigorous Bounds on Rank and Nullity of Matrix Products
> **Problem Statement (GATE Level 4 MSQ / NAT):**  
> Consider an aircraft aeroelastic state-space system matrix $A$ of size $4 \times 6$ with $\text{Rank}(A) = 3$, and a sensor measurement matrix $B$ of size $6 \times 5$ with $\text{Rank}(B) = 4$.  
> 
> **Questions:**  
> 1. What are the minimum and maximum possible values of $\text{Rank}(AB)$?  
> 2. What are the minimum and maximum possible dimensions of the null space $\text{Null}(AB)$?  
> 3. If $C$ is a $5 \times 4$ matrix such as $A B C = 0_{4 \times 4}$, what is the maximum possible rank of $C$?
> 
> > [!success]- **Complete Analytical Derivation & Proofs:**
> > 
> > #### Step 1: Sylvester's Rank Inequality Bounds on $AB$
> > Matrix sizes: $A$ is $4 \times 6$ ($m=4, n=6$), $B$ is $6 \times 5$ ($n=6, p=5$).  
> > Product $AB$ has size $4 \times 5$.  
> > By Sylvester's Rank Inequality:
> > $$\text{Rank}(A) + \text{Rank}(B) - n \le \text{Rank}(AB) \le \min(\text{Rank}(A), \text{Rank}(B))$$
> > Substituting given values ($r(A) = 3, r(B) = 4, n = 6$):
> > $$3 + 4 - 6 \le \text{Rank}(AB) \le \min(3, 4)$$
> > $$1 \le \text{Rank}(AB) \le 3$$
> > 
> > Thus:
> > - $\text{Rank}(AB)_{\min} = 1$
> > - $\text{Rank}(AB)_{\max} = 3$
> > 
> > #### Step 2: Rank-Nullity Bounds on $AB$
> > The product $M = AB$ maps $\mathbb{R}^5 \to \mathbb{R}^4$.  
> > Number of columns of $AB$ is $p = 5$.  
> > By the Rank-Nullity Theorem:
> > $$\text{Rank}(AB) + \text{Nullity}(AB) = 5 \implies \text{Nullity}(AB) = 5 - \text{Rank}(AB)$$
> > - When $\text{Rank}(AB) = 3 \implies \text{Nullity}(AB)_{\min} = 5 - 3 = 2$.
> > - When $\text{Rank}(AB) = 1 \implies \text{Nullity}(AB)_{\max} = 5 - 1 = 4$.
> > 
> > #### Step 3: Maximum Rank of $C$ when $A B C = 0_{4 \times 4}$
> > Let $M = AB$ (size $4 \times 5$, rank $r_M \in [1, 3]$).  
> > Matrix $C$ has size $5 \times 4$ (inner dimension is 5).  
> > The product is $M C = 0_{4 \times 4} \implies \text{Rank}(M C) = 0$.  
> > Applying Sylvester's inequality to $M C$:
> > $$\text{Rank}(M) + \text{Rank}(C) - 5 \le \text{Rank}(M C) = 0$$
> > $$\text{Rank}(C) \le 5 - \text{Rank}(M)$$
> > To maximize $\text{Rank}(C)$, we must minimize $\text{Rank}(M)$:
> > $$\text{Rank}(C)_{\max} \le 5 - \text{Rank}(M)_{\min} = 5 - 1 = 4$$
> > Since $C$ has size $5 \times 4$, its maximum possible theoretical rank is $\min(5, 4) = 4$.  
> > Thus, **$\text{Rank}(C)_{\max} = 4$**.

> > $\rho_1 - \rho_2 = 10\text{ kg/m}^3$. $a/A = 20/2000 = 0.010$. $\rho_1 + \rho_2 = 2000\text{ kg/m}^3$.  
> > Effective density: $10 + (0.010 \times 2000) = 10 + 20 = 30.0\text{ kg/m}^3$.  
> > $h = \frac{\Delta p}{g \times 30.0} = \frac{15.0}{9.81 \times 30.0} = \frac{15.0}{294.3} = 0.050968\text{ m} = 50.97\text{ mm}$.  
> > **Target Answer:** $50.97\text{ mm}$ · **Tolerance Window:** $[50.0, 52.0]\text{ mm}$

> [!question]- ⚡ Q16 (NAT 1 Mark) · Rank of $3 \times 3$ Singular Matrix

> [!question]- ⚡ Q17 (NAT 2 Marks) · Rank of Matrix with Parameter
> For what value of $k$ does the matrix $A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 & 4 \\ 1 & 4 & k \end{pmatrix}$ have rank equal to 2?  
> > [!success]- **Solution & Tolerance:**  
> > For rank $< 3$, $\det(A) = 0$.  
> > $\det(A) = 1(2k - 16) - 1(k - 4) + 1(4 - 2) = 2k - 16 - k + 4 + 2 = k - 10 = 0 \implies k = 10$.  
> > At $k=10$, submatrix $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$ has determinant $2 - 1 = 1 \neq 0$, so rank is exactly 2.  
> > **Target Answer:** $10$ · **Tolerance Window:** $[10, 10]$

> [!question]- ⚡ Q18 (NAT 1 Mark) · Rank-Nullity Calculation
> A linear transformation $T: \mathbb{R}^7 \to \mathbb{R}^4$ is represented by a $4 \times 7$ matrix $A$. If the column space has dimension 3, what is the dimension of the null space $\dim(\text{Null}(A))$?  
> > [!success]- **Solution & Tolerance:**  
> > By Rank-Nullity Theorem: $\text{Rank}(A) + \text{Nullity}(A) = n$ (domain dimension $= 7$).  
> > $\text{Rank}(A) = \dim(\text{Col}(A)) = 3 \implies \text{Nullity}(A) = 7 - 3 = 4$.  
> > **Target Answer:** $4$ · **Tolerance Window:** $[4, 4]$

> [!question]- ⚡ Q19 (MCQ 2 Marks) · Sylvester's Inequality Application
> Let $A$ and $B$ be $n \times n$ matrices such that $AB = 0_{n \times n}$. Which of the following MUST be true?  
> (A) $\text{Rank}(A) + \text{Rank}(B) \le n$  
> (B) $\text{Rank}(A) + \text{Rank}(B) \ge n$  
> (C) $\text{Rank}(A) = \text{Rank}(B)$  
> (D) At least one of $A$ or $B$ is the zero matrix  
> > [!success]- **Solution:**  
> > Sylvester's rank inequality: $\text{Rank}(A) + \text{Rank}(B) - n \le \text{Rank}(AB)$.  
> > Since $AB = 0$, $\text{Rank}(AB) = 0 \implies \text{Rank}(A) + \text{Rank}(B) - n \le 0 \implies \text{Rank}(A) + \text{Rank}(B) \le n$.  
> > **Correct Answer:** (A)

> [!question]- ⚡ Q20 (NAT 2 Marks) · Rank of Outer Product Sum
> Let $u = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 2 \\ 3 \end{pmatrix}$ and $v = \begin{pmatrix} 4 \\ 5 \\ 6 \end{pmatrix}$. What is the rank of the $3 \times 3$ matrix $M = u v^T$?  
> > [!success]- **Solution & Tolerance:**  
> > $M = u v^T$ is the outer product of two non-zero vectors. Every column of $M$ is a scalar multiple of $u$. The column space is 1-dimensional $\implies \text{Rank}(M) = 1$.  
> > **Target Answer:** $1$ · **Tolerance Window:** $[1, 1]$

> [!question]- ⚡ Q21 (MSQ 2 Marks) · Invariance Properties of Rank
> For any real matrix $A_{m \times n}$, which of the following expressions ALWAYS have rank equal to $\text{Rank}(A)$?  
> (A) $\text{Rank}(A^T)$  
> (B) $\text{Rank}(A^T A)$  
> (C) $\text{Rank}(A A^T)$  
> (D) $\text{Rank}(A + I)$  
> > [!success]- **Solution:**  
> > - For any real matrix: $\text{Rank}(A) = \text{Rank}(A^T) = \text{Rank}(A^T A) = \text{Rank}(A A^T)$. (A, B, C are always true)  
> > - $\text{Rank}(A + I)$ can be completely different (e.g. $A = -I \implies \text{Rank}(A)=n$, but $\text{Rank}(A+I)=0$). (D is false)  
> > **Correct Answer:** (A), (B), (C)

> [!question]- ⚡ Q22 (NAT 2 Marks) · Product Rank Upper Bound
> Let $A$ be a $5 \times 3$ matrix of rank 3, and $B$ be a $3 \times 6$ matrix of rank 2. What is the rank of the $5 \times 6$ matrix product $AB$?  
> > [!success]- **Solution & Tolerance:**  
> > By Sylvester's inequality:  
> > $\text{Rank}(A) + \text{Rank}(B) - 3 \le \text{Rank}(AB) \le \min(\text{Rank}(A), \text{Rank}(B))$.  
> > $3 + 2 - 3 \le \text{Rank}(AB) \le \min(3, 2) \implies 2 \le \text{Rank}(AB) \le 2$.  
> > Therefore, $\text{Rank}(AB)$ is uniquely forced to be **2**.  
> > **Target Answer:** $2$ · **Tolerance Window:** $[2, 2]$

> [!question]- ⚡ Q23 (NAT 1 Mark) · Trace and Eigenvalues
> A $3 \times 3$ matrix $A$ has eigenvalues $2, -1, 5$. What is the trace of $A^2$?  
> > [!success]- **Solution & Tolerance:**  
> > The eigenvalues of $A^2$ are $\lambda_i^2: 2^2=4, (-1)^2=1, 5^2=25$.  
> > $\text{Tr}(A^2) = \sum \lambda_i^2 = 4 + 1 + 25 = 30$.  
> > **Target Answer:** $30$ · **Tolerance Window:** $[30, 30]$

> [!question]- ⚡ Q24 (MCQ 2 Marks) · Idempotent Matrix Rank and Trace
> A matrix $P$ satisfies $P^2 = P$ (idempotent matrix). Which of the following is ALWAYS true?  
> (A) All eigenvalues of $P$ are either 0 or 1  
> (B) $\text{Rank}(P) = \text{Tr}(P)$  
> (C) $\det(P)$ is either 0 or 1  
> (D) All of the above  
> > [!success]- **Solution:**  
> > For $P^2 = P$: $\lambda^2 = \lambda \implies \lambda(\lambda - 1) = 0 \implies \lambda \in \{0, 1\}$.  
> > Since $P$ is diagonalizable, $\text{Rank}(P) = \text{number of non-zero eigenvalues} = \text{number of 1s} = \text{Tr}(P)$.  
> > $\det(P) = \prod \lambda_i \in \{0, 1\}$.  
> > **Correct Answer:** (D)

> [!question]- ⚡ Q25 (NAT 2 Marks) · Sum of Inverses of Eigenvalues
> A non-singular $3 \times 3$ matrix $A$ has characteristic equation $\lambda^3 - 6\lambda^2 + 11\lambda - 6 = 0$. What is the trace of $A^{-1}$?  
> > [!success]- **Solution & Tolerance:**  
> > Eigenvalues satisfy $(\lambda-1)(\lambda-2)(\lambda-3) = 0 \implies \lambda_1=1, \lambda_2=2, \lambda_3=3$.  
> > Eigenvalues of $A^{-1}$ are $1/1 = 1$, $1/2 = 0.5$, $1/3 = 0.3333$.  
> > $\text{Tr}(A^{-1}) = 1 + 0.5 + 0.33333 = 1.8333$.  
> > Alternatively: From characteristic equation $\det(A) = 6$, sum of product of roots taken two at a time is $11$.  
> > $\text{Tr}(A^{-1}) = \frac{\lambda_1\lambda_2 + \lambda_2\lambda_3 + \lambda_3\lambda_1}{\lambda_1\lambda_2\lambda_3} = \frac{11}{6} \approx 1.8333$.  
> > **Target Answer:** $1.83$ · **Tolerance Window:** $[1.81, 1.85]$

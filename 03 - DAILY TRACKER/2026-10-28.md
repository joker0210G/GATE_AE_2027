### Step 2 — Kepler's Three Laws of Planetary Motion (20 min)
Open `06.2b - Kepler Laws and Planetary Motion`.

```
                    KEPLER'S THREE LAWS
     1. LAW OF ORBITS                2. LAW OF AREAS                 3. LAW OF PERIODS
     All orbits are ellipses with    A line segment sweeps equal     T² = (4π² / μ) a³
     central body at ONE focus.      areas in equal times: dA/dt=h/2 T ∝ a^(3/2)
```

1. **Kepler's 1st Law (Law of Orbits):** Orbit is an ellipse with the primary body at one focus.
2. **Kepler's 2nd Law (Law of Areas):**
   $$\boxed{\frac{dA}{dt} = \frac{h}{2} = \text{constant}}$$
   *(Satellite moves fastest at Perigee, slowest at Apogee).*
3. **Kepler's 3rd Law (Harmonic Law / Law of Periods):**
   $$\boxed{T = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{a^3}{\mu}} \implies T^2 = \frac{4\pi^2}{\mu} a^3}$$
   - **Period Scaling Ratio:**
     $$\boxed{\frac{T_1}{T_2} = \left(\frac{a_1}{a_2}\right)^{3/2}}$$
   - **Geostationary Earth Orbit (GEO):**
     $$T = 24\text{ hours} = 86,164\text{ s} \implies a_{\text{GEO}} = \left(\frac{\mu T^2}{4\pi^2}\right)^{1/3} \approx \mathbf{42,164\text{ km}} \implies \mathbf{h_{\text{GEO}} \approx 35,786\text{ km}}$$

### Step 3 — The Vis-Viva Equation (25 min)
Open `06.3 - Escape Velocity and Energy Requirements`.

By equating kinetic plus potential energy to total specific energy $\frac{v^2}{2} - \frac{\mu}{r} = -\frac{\mu}{2a}$:

$$\boxed{v^2 = \mu\left(\frac{2}{r} - \frac{1}{a}\right) \implies v = \sqrt{\mu\left(\frac{2}{r} - \frac{1}{a}\right)}}$$

```
                 ORBITAL VELOCITY SPECTRUM AROUND EARTH
          Velocity (km/s)
             12 ┌───────────────────┐  Hyperbolic (v > v_esc, ε > 0)
             11.2 ├───────────────────┤  Parabolic / Escape Speed (v_esc = √2 v_circ, ε = 0)
                │ (v_p > v_circ)    │  Elliptical Orbit (0 < e < 1, ε < 0)
              7.91 ├───────────────────┤  Circular Orbital Speed (v_circ = √(μ/r))
                │ (v_a < v_circ)    │
              0 └───────────────────┘
```

#### 🔑 The 4 Master Velocity Invariants:

1. **Circular Orbit Velocity ($a = r$):**
   $$\boxed{v_{\text{circ}} = \sqrt{\frac{\mu}{r}}}$$
   *(For Low Earth Orbit LEO $r = 6600\text{ km} \implies v_{\text{circ}} \approx \mathbf{7.77\text{ km/s}}$).*

2. **Parabolic Escape Velocity ($a \to \infty$):**
   $$\boxed{v_{\text{esc}} = \sqrt{\frac{2\mu}{r}} = \sqrt{2} v_{\text{circ}} \approx 1.414 v_{\text{circ}}}$$
   *(At Earth surface $R_E = 6371\text{ km} \implies \mathbf{v_{\text{esc}} \approx 11.18\text{ km/s} \approx 11.2\text{ km/s}}$).*
   - **Direction Independence:** Escape velocity magnitude is identical regardless of trajectory direction (as long as it clears the planet's surface!).

3. **Elliptical Orbit Perigee Speed ($r = r_p = a(1-e)$):**
   $$\boxed{v_p = \sqrt{\frac{\mu}{a}\left(\frac{1+e}{1-e}\right)} = \sqrt{\frac{\mu}{r_p}(1+e)} > v_{\text{circ}}}$$

4. **Elliptical Orbit Apogee Speed ($r = r_a = a(1+e)$):**
   $$\boxed{v_a = \sqrt{\frac{\mu}{a}\left(\frac{1-e}{1+e}\right)} = \sqrt{\frac{\mu}{r_a}(1-e)} < v_{\text{circ}}}$$

5. **Conservation of Angular Momentum Relation:**
   $$\boxed{r_p v_p = r_a v_a = h \implies \frac{v_p}{v_a} = \frac{r_a}{r_p} = \frac{1+e}{1-e}}$$

### Step 4 — Traps & Self-Check (5 min)
> 🔴 **Trap 1: Factor of $\sqrt{2}$ in Escape Velocity**
> $v_{\text{esc}} = \sqrt{2} v_{\text{circ}}$. Never forget $\sqrt{2} \approx 1.414$!
> 🔴 **Trap 2: Semi-Major Axis of Transfer Orbits**
> In the Vis-Viva equation, $r$ is the **instantaneous radius**, while $a$ is the **constant semi-major axis** of the entire orbit.

### Step 3 — D2 Mini Quiz (3:45–4:00) — 5 Questions (Timed 10 min)

> **Q1 (1 mark):** In an unperturbed two-body system, the orbital speed $v$ at an instantaneous radius $r$ in an orbit of semi-major axis $a$ is given by:
> **(A)** $v = \sqrt{\mu\left(\frac{1}{r} - \frac{1}{a}\right)}$
> **(B)** $v = \sqrt{\mu\left(\frac{2}{r} - \frac{1}{a}\right)}$
> **(C)** $v = \sqrt{\frac{\mu}{r}}$
> **(D)** $v = \sqrt{\frac{2\mu}{r}}$

> **Q2 (1 mark):** The escape velocity from a celestial body at a distance $r$ is related to the circular orbital velocity at the same distance by:
> **(A)** $v_{\text{esc}} = 2 v_{\text{circ}}$
> **(B)** $v_{\text{esc}} = \sqrt{2} v_{\text{circ}}$
> **(C)** $v_{\text{esc}} = \frac{v_{\text{circ}}}{\sqrt{2}}$
> **(D)** $v_{\text{esc}} = 4 v_{\text{circ}}$

> **Q3 (2 marks):** A satellite is in an elliptical orbit around Earth with $e = 0.50$. The ratio of its velocity at perigee to its velocity at apogee ($v_p / v_a$) is:
> **(A)** $1.5$ **(B)** $2.0$ **(C)** $3.0$ **(D)** $4.0$

> **Q4 (2 marks):** Satellite A orbits in a circular orbit of radius $r_A = 8000\text{ km}$ with period $T_A = 2\text{ hours}$. Satellite B orbits in a circular orbit of radius $r_B = 32000\text{ km}$. The orbital period $T_B$ of Satellite B is:
> **(A)** $8\text{ hours}$ **(B)** $16\text{ hours}$ **(C)** $32\text{ hours}$ **(D)** $64\text{ hours}$

> **Q5 (2 marks NAT):** A satellite is in an elliptical Earth orbit ($\mu = 398600\text{ km}^3\text{/s}^2$) with perigee radius $r_p = 6800\text{ km}$ and apogee radius $r_a = 10200\text{ km}$. The velocity at perigee $v_p$ (in $\text{km/s}$) is ___ (round off to 2 decimal places).

> [!faq]- 🔍 View D2 Mini Quiz Answers & Step-by-Step Solutions
>
> **Q1: (B) v = √[μ(2/r − 1/a)]**
> The fundamental Vis-Viva equation.
>
> **Q2: (B) v_esc = √2 v_circ**
> $v_{\text{esc}} = \sqrt{\frac{2\mu}{r}} = \sqrt{2}\sqrt{\frac{\mu}{r}} = \sqrt{2} v_{\text{circ}}$.
>
> **Q3: (C) 3.0**
> $\frac{v_p}{v_a} = \frac{1+e}{1-e} = \frac{1 + 0.50}{1 - 0.50} = \frac{1.50}{0.50} = \mathbf{3.0}$.
>
> **Q4: (B) 16 hours**
> $T_B / T_A = (r_B / r_A)^{3/2} = (32000 / 8000)^{3/2} = (4)^{1.5} = 8 \implies T_B = 2 \times 8 = \mathbf{16\text{ hours}}$.
>
> **Q5: 8.39**
> $a = \frac{6800 + 10200}{2} = 8500\text{ km}$.
> $v_p = \sqrt{\mu\left(\frac{2}{r_p} - \frac{1}{a}\right)} = \sqrt{398600\left(\frac{2}{6800} - \frac{1}{8500}\right)} = \sqrt{398600(0.00029412 - 0.00011765)} = \sqrt{398600(0.00017647)} = \sqrt{70.34} \approx \mathbf{8.39\text{ km/s}}$.
> *(Or $e = \frac{10200-6800}{10200+6800} = \frac{3400}{17000} = 0.20 \implies v_p = \sqrt{\frac{398600}{6800}(1 + 0.20)} = \sqrt{58.6176 \times 1.20} = \sqrt{70.341} = \mathbf{8.39\text{ km/s}}$).*

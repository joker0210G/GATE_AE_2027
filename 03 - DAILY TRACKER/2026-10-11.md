### 🌊 Set A — W1-W2 High-Yield Forensic Trap Spotters (FC-151 → FC-158)

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-151 · Stagnation Pressure Jump in Flow Past Cylinder
> **Question:** In flow past a circular cylinder with circulation $\Gamma$, what is the pressure at the stagnation points?
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > At any stagnation point, local velocity $V = 0$. By Bernoulli's equation:
> > $$p_{\text{stag}} = p_\infty + \frac{1}{2}\rho_\infty V_\infty^2 = p_0 \implies C_{p,\text{stag}} \equiv +1.00$$
> > ⚠️ **AIR 1 Trap:** Circulation alters the physical LOCATION of the stagnation points on the cylinder surface, but the value of $C_p$ at the stagnation points is ALWAYS strictly $+1.00$!

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-152 · Lift on a Rotating Cylinder (Kutta-Joukowski)
> **Question:** State the lift per unit span on a cylinder of radius $R$ rotating with circulation $\Gamma$ in freestream $V_\infty$.
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > $$L' = \rho_\infty V_\infty \Gamma$$
> > ⚠️ **AIR 1 Trap:** Drag is strictly ZERO ($D' = 0$) in inviscid potential flow (D'Alembert's Paradox), regardless of the circulation value!

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-153 · Thin Airfoil Center of Pressure Trap
> **Question:** Why does $x_{cp}$ diverge to $\pm\infty$ as $\alpha \to \alpha_0$ on a cambered airfoil, while staying fixed on a symmetric airfoil?
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > $$x_{cp} = x_{ac} - \frac{c_{m,ac}}{c_l} c$$
> > - On a cambered airfoil, $c_{m,ac} \ne 0$. As $c_l \to 0$, division by zero causes $x_{cp} \to \pm\infty$.
> > - On a symmetric airfoil, $c_{m,ac} \equiv 0$. The fraction $0 / c_l$ is zero for all non-zero $c_l$, so $x_{cp} \equiv x_{ac} = 0.25 c$.

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-154 · Finite Wing Induced Drag Independence
> **Question:** In the Fourier series representation of wing circulation, does the coefficient $A_2$ contribute to lift or induced drag?
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > - $A_2$ produces ZERO net lift ($C_L = \pi AR A_1$).
> > - $A_2$ contributes positively to induced drag ($\delta = \sum n(A_n/A_1)^2$ includes $2(A_2/A_1)^2$).
> > - For a symmetric wing, $A_2 \equiv 0$ due to spanwise symmetry.

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-155 · Aspect Ratio Scaling for Induced Drag
> **Question:** If the wingspan $b$ of an aircraft is doubled at constant wing area $S$ and weight $W$, by what factor is the induced drag $D_i$ reduced?
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > $AR = b^2 / S$. Doubling $b$ quadruples aspect ratio ($AR \to 4 AR$).  
> > $D_i \propto 1/AR \implies$ Induced drag is reduced by a factor of **$4$** ($75\%$ reduction!).

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-156 · Glauert Camber Integral Trap
> **Question:** In evaluating $A_1 = \frac{2}{\pi}\int_0^\pi \frac{dy_c}{dx}\cos\theta_0 \, d\theta_0$, what is the common sign error?
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > Forgetting that $x = \frac{c}{2}(1 - \cos\theta_0)$ means $dx = \frac{c}{2}\sin\theta_0 \, d\theta_0$.  
> > Camber slopes often contain $(1 - 2x/c) = \cos\theta_0$. Multiplying by $\cos\theta_0$ gives $\cos^2\theta_0$, whose integral over $[0, \pi]$ is $\pi/2$.

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-157 · Downwash at Wingtips vs Mid-Span
> **Question:** For a rectangular wing (non-elliptic), where across the span is downwash $w(y)$ maximum?
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > For a rectangular wing, the circulation gradient $|d\Gamma/dy|$ is strongest near the wingtips, causing downwash $w(y)$ to be **larger at the wingtips** than at the center! (Unlike an elliptic wing where downwash is strictly uniform).

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-158 · Hagen-Poiseuille vs Couette Shear Stress
> **Question:** How does shear stress $\tau(r)$ vary radially in Hagen-Poiseuille pipe flow vs Couette flow?
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > - Hagen-Poiseuille: $\tau(r) = \frac{r}{2}(-\frac{dp}{dx}) \implies$ varies **linearly** from $0$ at centerline to maximum at the wall.
> > - Plane Couette (zero pressure gradient): $\tau = \mu \frac{U}{h} = \text{CONSTANT}$ everywhere between the plates!

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-159 · Zero Eigenvalue Invariant
> **Question:** What does an eigenvalue $\lambda = 0$ signify about a square matrix $A$?
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > $\det(A) = 0$. The matrix is singular, not invertible, and the homogeneous system $A\vec{x} = \mathbf{0}$ has non-trivial (infinite) solutions. The eigenvectors corresponding to $\lambda = 0$ span the null space of $A$.

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-160 · Distinct Eigenvalues and Linear Independence
> **Question:** Are eigenvectors corresponding to distinct eigenvalues always linearly independent?
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > **YES, ALWAYS!** If $\lambda_1, \lambda_2, \dots, \lambda_k$ are pairwise distinct eigenvalues, the corresponding eigenvectors $\vec{v}_1, \vec{v}_2, \dots, \vec{v}_k$ are guaranteed to be linearly independent.

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-161 · L'Hopital's Rule Conditions
> **Question:** What are the ONLY two indeterminate forms where L'Hopital's rule can be directly applied?
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > Strictly **$\frac{0}{0}$** and **$\frac{\pm\infty}{\pm\infty}$**!  
> > Forms like $0 \cdot \infty$, $\infty - \infty$, $1^\infty$, $0^0$, $\infty^0$ MUST first be converted algebraically into $0/0$ or $\infty/\infty$ before differentiating.

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-162 · Leibniz Integral Rule for Differentiating Integrals
> **Question:** Differentiate $\int_{g(x)}^{h(x)} f(t) \, dt$ with respect to $x$.
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > $$\frac{d}{dx}\int_{g(x)}^{h(x)} f(t) \, dt = f(h(x))\cdot h'(x) - f(g(x))\cdot g'(x)$$

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-163 · Second Derivative Test Failure
> **Question:** If $f'(c) = 0$ and $f''(c) = 0$, what conclusion can be drawn?
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > The test is **inconclusive**! The point could be a local minimum ($f(x) = x^4$ at $x=0$), a local maximum ($f(x) = -x^4$), or a point of inflection ($f(x) = x^3$).

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-164 · Definite Integral King's Rule
> **Question:** State King's property: $\int_a^b f(x) \, dx = \int_a^b f(a + b - x) \, dx$. What is its main use?
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > Use it whenever integrating expressions containing $\sin x / (\sin x + \cos x)$, $\ln(1 + \tan x)$, or symmetric rational functions over $[0, \pi/2]$ or $[0, \pi]$. Adding the original integral to the transformed integral usually collapses the integrand to a constant!

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-165 · Quadratic Form Matrix Symmetry
> **Question:** In writing the matrix $A$ of a quadratic form $Q(x, y) = a x^2 + 2b x y + c y^2$, what are the entries?
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > $$A = \begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ b & c \end{pmatrix}$$
> > ⚠️ **AIR 1 Trap:** The off-diagonal entries are $b$ (HALF of the cross-term coefficient $2b$), NOT $2b$!

### 🔬 Apex Worked Example 32: Cross-Pollination Hybrid: Airfoil Coupling with Aeroelastic Divergence

> [!example]- 🔥 WE-32 · Aeroelastic Torsional Divergence of a Cambered Wing
> **Problem Statement (GATE Level 5 Multi-Subject Hybrid):**  
> A 2D wing section of chord $c = 1.00\text{ m}$ is mounted on a torsional spring with rotational stiffness $K_\theta = 1800.0\text{ N}\cdot\text{m/rad}$ per unit span. The elastic axis (EA) is located at $x_{ea} = 0.350 c$, while the aerodynamic center is at the quarter-chord $x_{ac} = 0.250 c$. The section has 2D lift-curve slope $a_0 = 2\pi\text{ rad}^{-1}$ and zero-lift angle of attack $\alpha_0 = -2.0^\circ$.  
> 
> **Tasks:**  
> 1. Determine the aerodynamic moment arm $e = x_{ea} - x_{ac}$ in meters.  
> 2. Derive the equation for torsional aeroelastic divergence dynamic pressure $q_{\text{div}}$.  
> 3. Calculate the divergence speed $V_{\text{div}}$ in $\text{m/s}$ at sea-level air density $\rho = 1.225\text{ kg/m}^3$.  
> 4. If the wing operates at speed $V_\infty = 40.0\text{ m/s}$ with initial geometric angle of attack $\alpha_g = 3.0^\circ$, compute the elastic torsional twist angle $\theta_{\text{twist}}$ in degrees.
> 
> > [!success]- **Complete Analytical Solution & Verification:**
> > 
> > #### Step 1: Moment Arm
> > $$e = x_{ea} - x_{ac} = 0.350 c - 0.250 c = 0.100 c = 0.100\text{ m}$$
> > 
> > #### Step 2: Divergence Dynamic Pressure ($q_{\text{div}}$)
> > In steady aeroelastic equilibrium, the aerodynamic pitching moment about the elastic axis must equal the spring restoring torque:
> > $$M_{ea}' = K_\theta \theta_{\text{twist}}$$
> > Aerodynamic moment about EA:
> > $$M_{ea}' = L' e + M_{ac}' = (q_\infty c a_0 (\alpha_g + \theta_{\text{twist}} - \alpha_0)) e + M_{ac}'$$
> > Separating terms in $\theta_{\text{twist}}$:
> > $$\theta_{\text{twist}} (K_\theta - q_\infty c e a_0) = q_\infty c e a_0 (\alpha_g - \alpha_0) + M_{ac}'$$
> > Torsional divergence occurs when the denominator vanishes:
> > $$K_\theta - q_{\text{div}} c e a_0 = 0 \implies q_{\text{div}} = \frac{K_\theta}{c e a_0}$$
> > 
> > #### Step 3: Divergence Speed ($V_{\text{div}}$)
> > $$q_{\text{div}} = \frac{1800.0}{1.00 \times 0.100 \times 2\pi} = \frac{1800.0}{0.6283185} = 2864.79\text{ Pa}$$
> > Solving for divergence speed:
> > $$V_{\text{div}} = \sqrt{\frac{2 q_{\text{div}}}{\rho}} = \sqrt{\frac{2 \times 2864.79}{1.225}} = \sqrt{\frac{5729.58}{1.225}} = \sqrt{4677.206} = 68.390\text{ m/s}$$
> > Target Answer: **$V_{\text{div}} = 68.39\text{ m/s}$** · Official Tolerance: **$[68.0, 68.8]\text{ m/s}$**
> > 
> > #### Step 4: Elastic Twist at $V = 40.0\text{ m/s}$
> > Dynamic pressure at $40\text{ m/s}$:
> > $$q_\infty = \frac{1}{2}(1.225)(40^2) = 0.6125 \times 1600 = 980.0\text{ Pa}$$
> > Pressure ratio:
> > $$\frac{q_\infty}{q_{\text{div}}} = \frac{980.0}{2864.79} = 0.34208$$
> > Initial effective angle: $\alpha_g - \alpha_0 = 3.0^\circ - (-2.0^\circ) = 5.0^\circ = 0.087266\text{ rad}$.  
> > Neglecting $M_{ac}'$ for twist increment:
> > $$\theta_{\text{twist}} = \frac{\frac{q_\infty}{q_{\text{div}}}}{1 - \frac{q_\infty}{q_{\text{div}}}} (\alpha_g - \alpha_0) = \frac{0.34208}{1 - 0.34208} \times 5.0^\circ = \frac{0.34208}{0.65792} \times 5.0^\circ = 0.520^\circ$$
> > Target Answer: **$\theta_{\text{twist}} = 0.520^\circ$**

### 🔬 Apex Worked Example 33: Constrained Optimization via Lagrange Multipliers

> [!example]- 🔥 WE-33 · Derivation of Minimum Induced Drag via Lagrange Multipliers
> **Problem Statement (GATE Level 5 Advanced Calculus):**  
> In Prandtl lifting line theory, total lift coefficient is $C_L = \pi AR A_1$, while induced drag coefficient is:
> $$C_{Di} = \pi AR \sum_{n=1}^\infty n A_n^2 = \pi AR \left( A_1^2 + 2 A_2^2 + 3 A_3^2 + \dots \right)$$
> 
> Use the method of Lagrange multipliers to prove that for a fixed specified total lift $C_L = C_{L,\text{target}}$, the induced drag $C_{Di}$ is minimized if and only if all higher Fourier coefficients vanish ($A_n = 0$ for all $n \ge 2$), proving that the elliptic distribution is the unique global minimum.
> 
> > [!success]- **Complete Analytical Solution & Verification:**
> > 
> > #### Step 1: Formulation of Objective and Constraint
> > Objective to minimize:
> > $$f(A_1, A_2, A_3, \dots) = \pi AR \left( A_1^2 + \sum_{n=2}^\infty n A_n^2 \right)$$
> > Constraint equation:
> > $$g(A_1, A_2, A_3, \dots) = \pi AR A_1 - C_{L,\text{target}} = 0$$
> > 
> > #### Step 2: The Lagrangian Function
> > $$\mathcal{L}(A_1, A_2, \dots, \lambda) = \pi AR \left( A_1^2 + \sum_{n=2}^\infty n A_n^2 \right) - \lambda (\pi AR A_1 - C_{L,\text{target}})$$
> > 
> > #### Step 3: Partial Derivatives with respect to $A_n$
> > 1. For $n = 1$:
> >    $$\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial A_1} = 2 \pi AR A_1 - \lambda \pi AR = 0 \implies \lambda = 2 A_1$$
> > 2. For each $n \ge 2$:
> >    $$\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial A_n} = 2 n \pi AR A_n = 0$$
> > Since $2 n \pi AR \ne 0$ for $n \ge 2$:
> > $$\boxed{A_n = 0 \quad \text{for all } n \ge 2}$$
> > 
> > #### Step 4: Value of $A_1$ and Minimum Drag
> > From constraint:
> > $$\pi AR A_1 = C_{L,\text{target}} \implies A_1 = \frac{C_{L,\text{target}}}{\pi AR}$$
> > Minimum induced drag:
> > $$C_{Di,\min} = \pi AR A_1^2 = \pi AR \left(\frac{C_L}{\pi AR}\right)^2 = \frac{C_L^2}{\pi AR}$$
> > This rigorously proves that the elliptic circulation distribution ($A_1 \ne 0, A_n = 0$ for $n \ge 2$) is the unique global minimum of induced drag!

### Step 2 — Chemical Rocket Thrust & Effective Exhaust Velocity (20 min)
Open `06 - Rocket Propulsion`.

```
                    CHEMICAL ROCKET ENGINE SCHEMATIC
          Combustion Chamber             Converging-Diverging Nozzle
     ┌──────────────────────────┐          ╭──────────────╮
     │ Chamber Pressure: p_0    │          │ Throat A_t   │  Exit Plane A_e, p_e, v_e
     │ Chamber Temp    : T_0    ├──────────┤ Sonic M = 1  ├──► Supersonic Exhaust v_e
     │ Total Mass Flow : ṁ      │          │ Critical p*  │    Ambient Pressure p_a
     └──────────────────────────┘          ╰──────────────╯
```

1. **The Rocket Thrust Equation:**
   $$\boxed{F = \dot{m} v_e + (p_e - p_a)A_e}$$
   - Momentum Thrust: $\dot{m} v_e$ (Product of propellant mass flow and nozzle exit velocity).
   - Pressure Thrust: $(p_e - p_a)A_e$ (Net pressure force acting over exit plane area).
   - In Vacuum ($p_a = 0$): $\mathbf{F_{\text{vac}} = \dot{m} v_e + p_e A_e > F_{\text{sea-level}}}$.

2. **Effective Exhaust Velocity ($c$):**
   Combines momentum and pressure thrust into a single equivalent velocity:
   $$\boxed{F \equiv \dot{m} c \implies c = v_e + \frac{(p_e - p_a)A_e}{\dot{m}}}$$
   - When **Perfectly Expanded** ($p_e = p_a$): $\mathbf{c = v_e}$.
   - In **Vacuum** ($p_a = 0$): $\mathbf{c_{\text{vac}} = v_e + \frac{p_e A_e}{\dot{m}}}$.

3. **Specific Impulse ($I_{\text{sp}}$) & Total Impulse ($I_{\text{total}}$):**
   - **Specific Impulse:**
     $$\boxed{I_{\text{sp}} \equiv \frac{F}{\dot{m} g_0} = \frac{c}{g_0} \quad (\text{units: seconds, with } g_0 = 9.80665\text{ or } 9.81\text{ m/s}^2)}$$
   - **Total Impulse:**
     $$\boxed{I_{\text{total}} \equiv \int_0^{t_b} F \, dt = F \cdot t_b = \dot{m} t_b c = m_{\text{prop}} \cdot c = m_{\text{prop}} I_{\text{sp}} g_0 \quad (\text{N}\cdot\text{s})}$$

### Step 3 — Characteristic Velocity ($c^*$) & Thrust Coefficient ($C_F$) (25 min)

1. **Characteristic Velocity ($c^*$ — Chamber Performance Parameter):**
   Measures how efficiently the combustion chamber converts chemical propellant energy into high-temperature, high-pressure gas, **completely independent of nozzle geometry**:
   $$\boxed{c^* \equiv \frac{p_0 A_t}{\dot{m}} = \frac{\sqrt{\gamma R T_0}}{\gamma \sqrt{\left(\frac{2}{\gamma+1}\right)^{\frac{\gamma+1}{\gamma-1}}}} = \frac{\sqrt{\frac{R_u T_0}{\mathcal{M}}}}{\Gamma(\gamma)}}$$
   - **Invariants:** Higher chamber temperature $T_0 \uparrow$ and lower molecular weight $\mathcal{M} \downarrow$ maximize $c^*$!
   - Examples: $\text{LOX}/\text{RP-1} \implies c^* \approx 1800\text{ m/s}$; $\text{LOX}/\text{LH}_2 \implies c^* \approx 2400\text{ m/s}$.

2. **Thrust Coefficient ($C_F$ — Nozzle Expansion Parameter):**
   Measures how effectively the nozzle converts chamber pressure and thermal energy into kinetic momentum:
   $$\boxed{C_F \equiv \frac{F}{p_0 A_t} = \sqrt{\frac{2\gamma^2}{\gamma-1}\left(\frac{2}{\gamma+1}\right)^{\frac{\gamma+1}{\gamma-1}}\left[1 - \left(\frac{p_e}{p_0}\right)^{\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma}}\right]} + \frac{p_e - p_a}{p_0}\frac{A_e}{A_t}}$$
   - For an optimum/perfectly expanded nozzle ($p_e = p_a$): $C_F$ depends strictly on nozzle pressure ratio $p_0/p_e$ and $\gamma$. Typical $C_F \approx 1.4\text{ to } 1.8$.

3. **The Golden Trinity Link:**
   $$\boxed{F = \dot{m} c = (\dot{m} c^*) \cdot C_F = (p_0 A_t) \cdot C_F}$$
   $$\boxed{c = c^* \cdot C_F}$$
   $$\boxed{I_{\text{sp}} = \frac{c^* \cdot C_F}{g_0}}$$

### Step 4 — Traps & Self-Check (5 min)
> 🔴 **Trap 1: Units of $I_{\text{sp}}$ vs. $c$**
> If given $I_{\text{sp}}$ in seconds, multiply by $g_0 = 9.81\text{ m/s}^2$ to get effective exhaust velocity $c = I_{\text{sp}} g_0$ in $\text{m/s}$. Never use seconds directly in the thrust formula $F = \dot{m} c$!
> 🔴 **Trap 2: $c^*$ vs. $C_F$ Separation of Roles**
> $c^*$ characterizes **CHAMBER COMBUSTION** ($\frac{p_0 A_t}{\dot{m}}$). $C_F$ characterizes **NOZZLE EXPANSION** ($\frac{F}{p_0 A_t}$). Changing nozzle exit area $A_e$ changes $C_F$, but leaves $c^*$ **COMPLETELY UNCHANGED**!

### Step 3 — D1 Mini Quiz (3:45–4:00) — 5 Questions (Timed 10 min)

> **Q1 (1 mark):** The characteristic velocity $c^*$ of a rocket engine is a direct measure of the performance of the:
> **(A)** Nozzle expansion process
> **(B)** Combustion chamber thermochemistry
> **(C)** Propellant feed turbopump
> **(D)** Payload staging separation

> **Q2 (1 mark):** If the nozzle exit area $A_e$ of a rocket engine is increased while keeping chamber conditions ($p_0, T_0, \dot{m}$) and throat area $A_t$ unchanged, which of the following occurs?
> **(A)** $c^*$ increases and $C_F$ increases
> **(B)** $c^*$ decreases and $C_F$ remains constant
> **(C)** $c^*$ remains constant and $C_F$ changes
> **(D)** Both $c^*$ and $C_F$ remain strictly constant

> **Q3 (2 marks):** A rocket engine produces thrust $F = 60.0\text{ kN}$ while consuming propellant at $\dot{m} = 20.0\text{ kg/s}$. Taking standard gravity $g_0 = 9.81\text{ m/s}^2$, the specific impulse $I_{\text{sp}}$ (in seconds) is:
> **(A)** $255.8\text{ s}$ **(B)** $305.8\text{ s}$ **(C)** $350.0\text{ s}$ **(D)** $410.2\text{ s}$

> **Q4 (2 marks):** A rocket has chamber pressure $p_0 = 7.0\text{ MPa}$, throat area $A_t = 0.012\text{ m}^2$, propellant mass flow $\dot{m} = 48.0\text{ kg/s}$, and thrust coefficient $C_F = 1.60$. The effective exhaust velocity $c$ (in $\text{m/s}$) is:
> **(A)** $1750\text{ m/s}$ **(B)** $2240\text{ m/s}$ **(C)** $2800\text{ m/s}$ **(D)** $3360\text{ m/s}$

> **Q5 (2 marks NAT):** A rocket engine fires for $t_b = 60\text{ seconds}$ with a constant thrust of $F = 45.0\text{ kN}$. The total propellant consumed is $m_{\text{prop}} = 900\text{ kg}$. The effective exhaust velocity $c$ (in $\text{m/s}$) is ___ $\text{m/s}$.

> [!faq]- 🔍 View D1 Mini Quiz Answers & Step-by-Step Solutions
>
> **Q1: (B) Combustion chamber thermochemistry**
> $c^* = \frac{p_0 A_t}{\dot{m}}$ characterizes chamber energy conversion, independent of nozzle geometry.
>
> **Q2: (C) c* remains constant and C_F changes**
> $c^*$ depends only on propellant properties and chamber state ($T_0, \mathcal{M}, \gamma$). $C_F$ depends on nozzle expansion area ratio $A_e/A_t$.
>
> **Q3: (B) 305.8 s**
> $c = \frac{F}{\dot{m}} = \frac{60000}{20.0} = 3000\text{ m/s}$.
> $I_{\text{sp}} = \frac{c}{g_0} = \frac{3000}{9.81} \approx \mathbf{305.81\text{ s} \approx 305.8\text{ s}}$.
>
> **Q4: (C) 2800 m/s**
> $c^* = \frac{p_0 A_t}{\dot{m}} = \frac{7.0 \times 10^6 \times 0.012}{48.0} = \frac{84000}{48.0} = 1750\text{ m/s}$.
> $c = c^* \cdot C_F = 1750 \times 1.60 = \mathbf{2800\text{ m/s}}$.
>
> **Q5: 3000**
> $I_{\text{total}} = F \cdot t_b = (45,000\text{ N})(60\text{ s}) = 2,700,000\text{ N}\cdot\text{s}$.
> $c = \frac{I_{\text{total}}}{m_{\text{prop}}} = \frac{2,700,000}{900} = \mathbf{3000\text{ m/s}}$.

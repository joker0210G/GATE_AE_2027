### 🌊 Set A — Quasi-1D Ducts, Area-Mach & Choking (FC-181 → FC-188)

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-181 · The Area-Mach Governing Equation
> **Question:** State the Area-Mach differential relation connecting fractional area change $dA/A$ to fractional velocity change $dV/V$.
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > $$\frac{dA}{A} = (M^2 - 1)\frac{dV}{V} = (1 - M^2)\frac{dp}{\rho V^2}$$
> > - Subsonic ($M < 1$): $dA/A < 0 \implies dV > 0$ (converging duct accelerates).
> > - Supersonic ($M > 1$): $dA/A > 0 \implies dV > 0$ (diverging duct accelerates).
> > - Sonic ($M = 1$): $dA = 0$ (minimum area station / throat).

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-182 · Sonic Throat Invariant
> **Question:** Why can sonic speed ($M = 1$) only be reached at the throat of a variable area duct?
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > At $M = 1$, the factor $(M^2 - 1) = 0$. For velocity to continue changing ($dV/V \ne 0$), the geometric area change MUST satisfy $dA = 0$. In a smooth duct transitioning from subsonic to supersonic, the second derivative $d^2A/dx^2 > 0$, making $dA = 0$ a strict minimum area.

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-183 · Isentropic Area Ratio ($A/A^*$) Formula
> **Question:** Write the isentropic area ratio $A/A^*$ formula for air ($\gamma = 1.4$).
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > $$\frac{A}{A^*} = \frac{1}{M}\left[ \frac{2}{\gamma + 1}\left( 1 + \frac{\gamma - 1}{2} M^2 \right) \right]^{\frac{\gamma + 1}{2(\gamma - 1)}} = \frac{1}{M}\left( \frac{5 + M^2}{6} \right)^3$$
> > ⚠️ **AIR 1 Trap:** For every area ratio $A/A^* > 1$, there are two physical solutions: one subsonic ($M < 1$) and one supersonic ($M > 1$).

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-184 · Choked Mass Flow Rate Formula
> **Question:** State the formula for maximum choked mass flow rate $\dot{m}_{\max}$ through a throat of area $A^*$ in terms of reservoir stagnation conditions $p_0, T_0$.
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > $$\dot{m}_{\max} = \frac{p_0 A^*}{\sqrt{T_0}} \sqrt{\frac{\gamma}{R}}\left(\frac{2}{\gamma + 1}\right)^{\frac{\gamma + 1}{2(\gamma - 1)}}$$
> > For air ($\gamma = 1.4, R = 287.05\text{ J/(kg}\cdot\text{K)}$):
> > $$\dot{m}_{\max} \approx 0.04042 \times \frac{p_0 A^*}{\sqrt{T_0}}$$

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-185 · Mass Flow Parameter (MFP) Definition
> **Question:** Define the Mass Flow Parameter $\text{MFP}(M)$ and state its maximum value.
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > $$\text{MFP}(M) \equiv \frac{\dot{m}\sqrt{T_0}}{A p_0} = \sqrt{\frac{\gamma}{R}} M \left( 1 + \frac{\gamma - 1}{2} M^2 \right)^{-\frac{\gamma + 1}{2(\gamma - 1)}}$$
> > Maximum value occurs at $M = 1.0$: $\text{MFP}^* \approx 0.04042\text{ kg}\sqrt{\text{K}}/(\text{N}\cdot\text{s})$.

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-186 · Convergent Nozzle Choking Limit
> **Question:** If the back pressure ratio $p_b / p_0$ of a convergent nozzle drops from $0.50$ to $0.20$, what happens to the exit Mach number and mass flow rate?
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > **NOTHING!** Once $p_b / p_0 \le 0.528$, the convergent nozzle is already fully choked:
> > - Exit Mach remains strictly $M_{\text{exit}} = 1.0$.
> > - Mass flow rate remains constant at $\dot{m}_{\max}$.
> > The further pressure drop from $0.50$ to $0.20$ occurs through expansion waves outside the exit!

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-187 · C-D Nozzle Third Critical Pressure Ratio
> **Question:** What is the Third Critical Pressure Ratio of a de Laval nozzle, and what does it physically signify?
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > The Third Critical Pressure Ratio is the isentropic design exit pressure ratio:
> > $$\frac{p_{b3}}{p_0} = \frac{p_e}{p_0} = \left( 1 + 0.2 M_{e,\text{super}}^2 \right)^{-3.5}$$
> > When back pressure $p_b = p_{b3}$, the nozzle is **correctly expanded** ($p_e = p_b$), producing shock-free isentropic supersonic flow through the entire nozzle and exit.

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-188 · Over-Expanded vs Under-Expanded Nozzle
> **Question:** Contrast an over-expanded nozzle ($p_e < p_b$) with an under-expanded nozzle ($p_e > p_b$).
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > - **Over-expanded ($p_e < p_b$):** Exit pressure is lower than ambient. Oblique shocks form at the nozzle lip, compressing the plume inward.
> > - **Under-expanded ($p_e > p_b$):** Exit pressure is higher than ambient. Prandtl-Meyer expansion fans form at the nozzle lip, expanding the plume outward.

### 📐 Set B — Engineering Mathematics: Directional Derivatives & Tangent Planes (FC-189 → FC-195)

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-189 · Directional Derivative Definition
> **Question:** State the definition and dot product formula for the directional derivative of $f(x, y, z)$ in the direction of unit vector $\hat{u}$.
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > $$D_{\hat{u}} f = \lim_{s \to 0} \frac{f(\vec{r}_0 + s\hat{u}) - f(\vec{r}_0)}{s} = \nabla f \cdot \hat{u}$$
> > ⚠️ **AIR 1 Trap:** The direction vector $\hat{u}$ MUST be normalized to unit length ($|\hat{u}| = 1$). If given vector $\vec{v}$, you must use $\hat{u} = \vec{v} / |\vec{v}|$!

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-190 · Maximum and Minimum Rate of Change
> **Question:** In what direction does $f(x, y, z)$ increase fastest, and what is this maximum rate of increase?
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > - Fastest increase occurs in the direction of the gradient $\hat{u} = \frac{\nabla f}{|\nabla f|}$, with value $+|\nabla f|$.
> > - Fastest decrease occurs in the direction $-\nabla f$, with value $-|\nabla f|$.
> > - Rate of change is ZERO in any direction perpendicular to $\nabla f$ ($\nabla f \cdot \hat{u} = 0$, tangent to level surface).

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-191 · Tangent Plane to Level Surface
> **Question:** Write the equation of the tangent plane to the level surface $F(x, y, z) = C$ at the point $(x_0, y_0, z_0)$.
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > Since $\nabla F$ is normal to the surface:
> > $$\nabla F(x_0, y_0, z_0) \cdot (\vec{r} - \vec{r}_0) = 0$$
> > $$F_x(x - x_0) + F_y(y - y_0) + F_z(z - z_0) = 0$$

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-192 · Normal Line to Surface
> **Question:** Write the symmetric equations of the normal line to $F(x, y, z) = C$ at $(x_0, y_0, z_0)$.
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > $$\frac{x - x_0}{F_x} = \frac{y - y_0}{F_y} = \frac{z - z_0}{F_z}$$

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-193 · Angle of Intersection between Two Surfaces
> **Question:** How do you find the angle $\theta$ between two surfaces $F_1(x, y, z) = 0$ and $F_2(x, y, z) = 0$ at an intersection point?
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > The angle between the surfaces equals the angle between their normal gradient vectors:
> > $$\cos\theta = \frac{|\nabla F_1 \cdot \nabla F_2|}{|\nabla F_1| |\nabla F_2|}$$

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-194 · Directional Derivative of Vector Field along Streamline
> **Question:** How is the convective acceleration $(\vec{V}\cdot\nabla)\vec{V}$ related to the directional derivative along a streamline?
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > Let $\hat{s} = \vec{V} / |\vec{V}|$ be the unit tangent vector to the streamline.
> > $$(\vec{V}\cdot\nabla)\vec{V} = |\vec{V}| \frac{\partial \vec{V}}{\partial s} = V \frac{\partial V}{\partial s}\hat{s} + \frac{V^2}{R}\hat{n}$$
> > Tangential acceleration is $V \partial V / \partial s$, normal acceleration is $V^2 / R$.

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-195 · Tangent Plane to Explicit Surface $z = f(x, y)$
> **Question:** State the tangent plane equation for the explicit surface $z = f(x, y)$ at $(x_0, y_0)$.
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > Rewrite as $F(x, y, z) = f(x, y) - z = 0$.
> > $$z - z_0 = f_x(x_0, y_0)(x - x_0) + f_y(x_0, y_0)(y - y_0)$$

> 2. Determine the maximum choked mass flow rate $\dot{m}_{\max}$ in $\text{kg/s}$.

### 🔬 Apex Worked Example 38: Nozzle Back Pressure Variation & Shock In Duct

> [!example]- 🔥 WE-38 · Pressure Envelope and Shock Location in Divergent Duct
> **Problem Statement (GATE Level 4 NAT):**  
> A converging-diverging nozzle operates with reservoir pressure $p_0 = 500.0\text{ kPa}$. The exit area ratio is $A_e / A^* = 2.400$.  
> 
> **Tasks:**  
> 1. Compute the First Critical pressure ratio $p_{b1} / p_0$ (isentropic subsonic exit solution where $M_t = 1.0$).  
> 2. Determine the Third Critical (design supersonic) exit pressure ratio $p_{b3} / p_0$.  
> 3. If back pressure is maintained at $p_b = 300.0\text{ kPa}$, explain the flow regime and confirm that the nozzle is choked.
> 
> > [!success]- **Complete Analytical Solution & Verification:**
> > 
> > #### Step 1: First Critical Exit Pressure ($p_{b1}$)
> > For $A_e / A^* = 2.400$, the subsonic Mach number root is:
> > $M_e \approx 0.252$.  
> > Subsonic pressure ratio:
> > $$\frac{p_{b1}}{p_0} = (1 + 0.2(0.252^2))^{-3.5} = (1 + 0.2(0.0635))^{-3.5} = (1.0127)^{-3.5} \approx 0.957$$
> > Target Answer: **$p_{b1} / p_0 = 0.957$** ($p_{b1} = 478.5\text{ kPa}$).
> > 
> > #### Step 2: Third Critical Exit Pressure ($p_{b3}$)
> > For $A_e / A^* = 2.400$, the supersonic Mach number root is:
> > $M_e \approx 2.400$.  
> > Supersonic pressure ratio:
> > $$\frac{p_{b3}}{p_0} = (1 + 0.2(2.40^2))^{-3.5} = (1 + 0.2(5.76))^{-3.5} = (2.152)^{-3.5} \approx 0.0684$$
> > Target Answer: **$p_{b3} / p_0 = 0.0684$** ($p_{b3} = 34.2\text{ kPa}$).
> > 
> > #### Step 3: Flow Regime at $p_b = 300\text{ kPa}$

### 🔬 Apex Worked Example 39: Directional Derivative along Aerodynamic Streamline

> [!example]- 🔥 WE-39 · Directional Rate of Pressure Change in Compressible Flow
> **Problem Statement (GATE Level 4 NAT):**  
> The 2D pressure field in a supersonic wind tunnel test section is given by:
> $$p(x, y) = 100.0 - 15.0 x^2 + 20.0 x y - 8.0 y^2\text{ kPa}$$
> A streamline passes through the point $P(2.0, 1.0)$ in the direction of vector $\vec{v} = 3.0\hat{i} + 4.0\hat{j}$.  
> 
> **Tasks:**  
> 1. Compute the gradient of pressure $\nabla p$ at point $P(2.0, 1.0)$.  
> 2. Determine the directional derivative of pressure $D_{\hat{u}} p$ along the streamline direction $\vec{v}$ in $\text{kPa/m}$.  
> 3. Find the maximum possible rate of pressure increase at point $P$ and the unit vector in that direction.  
> 4. Determine the equation of the tangent plane (or tangent line in 2D) to the isobaric curve passing through $P$.
> 
> > [!success]- **Complete Analytical Solution & Verification:**
> > 
> > #### Step 1: Pressure Gradient at $P(2.0, 1.0)$
> > $$\frac{\partial p}{\partial x} = -30.0 x + 20.0 y$$
> > $$\frac{\partial p}{\partial y} = 20.0 x - 16.0 y$$
> > At $(2.0, 1.0)$:
> > $$\frac{\partial p}{\partial x} = -30.0(2.0) + 20.0(1.0) = -60.0 + 20.0 = -40.0\text{ kPa/m}$$
> > $$\frac{\partial p}{\partial y} = 20.0(2.0) - 16.0(1.0) = 40.0 - 16.0 = +24.0\text{ kPa/m}$$
> > $$\nabla p = -40.0\hat{i} + 24.0\hat{j}$$
> > 
> > #### Step 2: Directional Derivative along $\vec{v} = 3\hat{i} + 4\hat{j}$
> > Unit vector along streamline:
> > $$|\vec{v}| = \sqrt{3.0^2 + 4.0^2} = \sqrt{9 + 16} = 5.0$$
> > $$\hat{u} = \frac{3.0}{5.0}\hat{i} + \frac{4.0}{5.0}\hat{j} = 0.60\hat{i} + 0.80\hat{j}$$
> > Directional derivative:
> > $$D_{\hat{u}} p = \nabla p \cdot \hat{u} = (-40.0)(0.60) + (24.0)(0.80) = -24.0 + 19.2 = -4.80\text{ kPa/m}$$
> > Target Answer: **$D_{\hat{u}} p = -4.80\text{ kPa/m}$** · Tolerance: **$[-4.85, -4.75]$**
> > (Pressure decreases along the streamline at $4.80\text{ kPa/m}$).
> > 
> > #### Step 3: Maximum Rate of Pressure Increase
> > Maximum rate equals the magnitude of the gradient:
> > $$|\nabla p| = \sqrt{(-40.0)^2 + 24.0^2} = \sqrt{1600.0 + 576.0} = \sqrt{2176.0} \approx 46.6476\text{ kPa/m}$$
> > Target Answer: **$46.65\text{ kPa/m}$**
> > Direction:
> > $$\hat{n}_{\max} = \frac{-40.0}{46.648}\hat{i} + \frac{24.0}{46.648}\hat{j} = -0.8575\hat{i} + 0.5145\hat{j}$$
> > 
> > #### Step 4: Tangent Line to Isobar at $P(2.0, 1.0)$
> > Gradient is normal to the curve:
> > $$\nabla p \cdot ((x - 2.0)\hat{i} + (y - 1.0)\hat{j}) = 0$$
> > $$-40.0(x - 2.0) + 24.0(y - 1.0) = 0 \implies -40x + 80 + 24y - 24 = 0$$
> > $$-40x + 24y + 56 = 0 \implies 5x - 3y = 7$$
> > Target Equation: **$5x - 3y = 7$**

### 🌊 Set A — Speed of Sound, Isentropic Relations & Transonics (FC-166 → FC-175)

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-166 · Speed of Sound Formula & Air Constant
> **Question:** State the speed of sound formula for an ideal gas and compute it for air at $T = 288.15\text{ K}$ ($\gamma = 1.4, R = 287.05\text{ J/(kg}\cdot\text{K)}$).
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > $$a = \sqrt{\gamma R T}$$
> > For air: $a \approx 20.047 \sqrt{T}\text{ m/s}$.  
> > At $T = 288.15\text{ K}$: $a = 20.047 \sqrt{288.15} = 20.047 \times 16.975 = 340.29\text{ m/s}$.  
> > ⚠️ **AIR 1 Trap:** Temperature $T$ MUST be in Kelvin, never Celsius!

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-167 · Mach Angle Formula
> **Question:** Define the Mach angle $\mu$ and state its mathematical relationship with Mach number $M$.
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > $$\sin\mu = \frac{1}{M} \implies \mu = \arcsin\left(\frac{1}{M}\right)$$
> > Mach angle is defined ONLY for supersonic flow ($M \ge 1.0$). At $M = 1$, $\mu = 90^\circ$. As $M \to \infty$, $\mu \to 0^\circ$.

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-168 · Stagnation Temperature Ratio
> **Question:** State the relationship between stagnation temperature $T_0$ and static temperature $T$.
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > $$\frac{T_0}{T} = 1 + \frac{\gamma - 1}{2} M^2$$
> > For air ($\gamma = 1.4$): $\frac{T_0}{T} = 1 + 0.2 M^2$.  
> > In any adiabatic flow (including irreversible shocks), $T_0$ is strictly conserved!

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-169 · Isentropic Pressure and Density Ratios
> **Question:** Write the isentropic relations connecting $p_0/p$ and $\rho_0/\rho$ to Mach number.
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > - $\frac{p_0}{p} = \left( 1 + \frac{\gamma - 1}{2} M^2 \right)^{\frac{\gamma}{\gamma - 1}} = (1 + 0.2 M^2)^{3.5}$
> > - $\frac{\rho_0}{\rho} = \left( 1 + \frac{\gamma - 1}{2} M^2 \right)^{\frac{1}{\gamma - 1}} = (1 + 0.2 M^2)^{2.5}$
> > ⚠️ **AIR 1 Trap:** These are valid ONLY for isentropic flow (reversible + adiabatic). They fail across shock waves!

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-170 · Critical Pressure Ratio for Air
> **Question:** What is the critical pressure ratio $p^*/p_0$ corresponding to sonic speed ($M = 1.0$) for air ($\gamma = 1.4$)?
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > $$\frac{p^*}{p_0} = \left( \frac{2}{\gamma + 1} \right)^{\frac{\gamma}{\gamma - 1}} = \left( \frac{2}{2.4} \right)^{3.5} = (0.833333)^{3.5} \approx 0.52828$$
> > If static pressure drops to $52.8\%$ of reservoir pressure, the flow reaches exactly $M = 1.0$.

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-171 · Dynamic Pressure in Compressible Flow
> **Question:** Express dynamic pressure $q$ in terms of static pressure $p$, ratio of specific heats $\gamma$, and Mach number $M$.
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > $$q = \frac{1}{2} \rho V^2 = \frac{1}{2} \gamma p M^2$$
> > $$\frac{q}{p} = \frac{\gamma}{2} M^2$$
> > For air: $q = 0.7 p M^2$.

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-172 · Prandtl-Glauert Compressibility Rule
> **Question:** State the Prandtl-Glauert rule for subsonic pressure coefficient $C_p$ and lift coefficient $c_l$ in terms of incompressible values $C_{p0}$ and $c_{l0}$.
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > $$C_p = \frac{C_{p0}}{\sqrt{1 - M_\infty^2}}, \quad c_l = \frac{c_{l0}}{\sqrt{1 - M_\infty^2}}$$
> > Valid for subsonic thin airfoils in small-disturbance flow ($M_\infty \le 0.75$).

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-173 · Critical Mach Number ($M_{cr}$) Definition
> **Question:** Define Critical Mach Number $M_{cr}$.
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > The freestream Mach number $M_\infty$ at which the local Mach number on the airfoil surface first reaches $1.0$ (sonic speed) at the point of minimum pressure.

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-174 · Drag Divergence Mach Number ($M_{dd}$)
> **Question:** Define Drag Divergence Mach Number $M_{dd}$ and state its typical relation to $M_{cr}$.
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > The freestream Mach number at which wave drag causes total drag coefficient to rise steeply:
> > $$\frac{dC_D}{dM_\infty} = 0.10 \quad (\text{or } \Delta C_D = 0.0020)$$
> > Typically $M_{dd} \approx M_{cr} + 0.06$ to $0.08$.

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-175 · Wing Sweep Effect on Critical Mach
> **Question:** How does a sweep angle $\Lambda$ increase the critical Mach number of a wing?
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > Only the flow component perpendicular to the leading edge ($V_\infty \cos\Lambda$) drives chordwise acceleration:
> > $$M_{cr,\text{swept}} \approx \frac{M_{cr,\text{unswept}}}{\cos\Lambda}$$

### 📐 Set B — Engineering Mathematics: Vector Differential Calculus (FC-176 → FC-180)

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-176 · Gradient of Scalar Field ($\nabla \phi$)
> **Question:** Define the gradient vector $\nabla \phi$ in Cartesian coordinates and state its geometric properties.
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > $$\nabla \phi = \frac{\partial \phi}{\partial x}\hat{i} + \frac{\partial \phi}{\partial y}\hat{j} + \frac{\partial \phi}{\partial z}\hat{k}$$
> > - Direction: Points in the direction of maximum spatial rate of increase of $\phi$.
> > - Magnitude: Equals the maximum directional derivative: $|\nabla \phi| = (D_{\hat{u}}\phi)_{\max}$.
> > - Geometry: Strictly perpendicular (normal) to the level surface $\phi(x, y, z) = C$.

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-177 · Divergence of Vector Field ($\nabla \cdot \vec{V}$)
> **Question:** Define the divergence $\nabla \cdot \vec{V}$ and state its physical meaning in fluid flow.
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > $$\nabla \cdot \vec{V} = \frac{\partial u}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial v}{\partial y} + \frac{\partial w}{\partial z}$$
> > Physical meaning: Net volumetric rate of fluid expansion (dilatation) per unit volume.
> > If $\nabla \cdot \vec{V} = 0$, the flow is **solenoidal** (incompressible).

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-178 · Curl of Vector Field ($\nabla \times \vec{V}$)
> **Question:** Define the curl $\nabla \times \vec{V}$ and relate it to the vorticity vector $\vec{\omega}$.
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > $$\nabla \times \vec{V} = \begin{vmatrix} \hat{i} & \hat{j} & \hat{k} \\ \frac{\partial}{\partial x} & \frac{\partial}{\partial y} & \frac{\partial}{\partial z} \\ u & v & w \end{vmatrix}$$
> > Relation to vorticity: $\vec{\omega} = \nabla \times \vec{V} = 2 \vec{\Omega}$ (twice the local angular velocity vector of fluid elements).
> > If $\nabla \times \vec{V} = \mathbf{0}$, the flow is **irrotational** ($\vec{V} = \nabla \phi$).

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-179 · The Irrotational Gradient Identity
> **Question:** Prove that the curl of the gradient of any scalar field $\phi$ is identically zero.
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > $$\nabla \times (\nabla \phi) \equiv \mathbf{0}$$
> > Because mixed second partial derivatives commute for smooth functions:
> > $\frac{\partial^2 \phi}{\partial y \partial z} - \frac{\partial^2 \phi}{\partial z \partial y} = 0$, etc.
> > Any conservative / gradient force field is automatically irrotational!

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-180 · The Solenoidal Curl Identity
> **Question:** Prove that the divergence of the curl of any vector field $\vec{A}$ is identically zero.
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > $$\nabla \cdot (\nabla \times \vec{A}) \equiv 0$$
> > Any curl vector field is automatically solenoidal (divergence-free). Therefore, vorticity fields are divergence-free ($\nabla \cdot \vec{\omega} = 0$), confirming Helmholtz's theorem that vortex lines cannot end inside a fluid!

### 🔬 Apex Worked Example 34: Isentropic Stagnation Property Calculations

> [!example]- 🔥 WE-34 · High-Speed Flight Stagnation Properties & Dynamic Pressure
> **Problem Statement (GATE AE Level 4 NAT):**  
> A high-speed business jet cruises at Mach number $M_\infty = 0.820$ at an altitude where static ambient temperature is $T_\infty = 220.0\text{ K}$ and static pressure is $p_\infty = 30.00\text{ kPa}$. The working fluid is air with $\gamma = 1.400$ and $R = 287.05\text{ J/(kg}\cdot\text{K)}$.  
> 
> **Tasks:**  
> 1. Compute the speed of sound $a_\infty$ in $\text{m/s}$ and true airspeed $V_\infty$ in $\text{m/s}$.  
> 2. Determine the stagnation temperature $T_0$ in Kelvin.  
> 3. Calculate the stagnation pressure $p_0$ in $\text{kPa}$.  
> 4. Compute the dynamic pressure $q_\infty$ in $\text{kPa}$ using the compressible relation.  
> 5. Find the percentage error in dynamic pressure if evaluated using incompressible Bernoulli ($p_0 - p_\infty$).
> 
> > [!success]- **Complete Analytical Solution & TCS Calculator Keystroke Ledger:**
> > 
> > #### Step 1: Speed of Sound & Airspeed
> > $$a_\infty = \sqrt{\gamma R T_\infty} = \sqrt{1.400 \times 287.05 \times 220.0} = \sqrt{88383.6} = 297.294\text{ m/s}$$
> > $$V_\infty = M_\infty a_\infty = 0.820 \times 297.294 = 243.781\text{ m/s}$$
> > Target Answers: **$a_\infty = 297.3\text{ m/s}$**, **$V_\infty = 243.8\text{ m/s}$**
> > 
> > #### Step 2: Stagnation Temperature ($T_0$)
> > $$\frac{T_0}{T_\infty} = 1 + \frac{\gamma - 1}{2} M_\infty^2 = 1 + 0.200 (0.820^2) = 1 + 0.200(0.6724) = 1 + 0.13448 = 1.13448$$
> > $$T_0 = 220.0 \times 1.13448 = 249.586\text{ K}$$
> > Target Answer: **$T_0 = 249.6\text{ K}$** · Official Tolerance: **$[249.0, 250.2]\text{ K}$**
> > 
> > #### Step 3: Stagnation Pressure ($p_0$)
> > $$\frac{p_0}{p_\infty} = \left(\frac{T_0}{T_\infty}\right)^{\frac{\gamma}{\gamma - 1}} = (1.13448)^{3.500}$$
> > Evaluating power:
> > $$\ln(1.13448) = 0.126175 \implies 3.5 \times 0.126175 = 0.441613 \implies e^{0.441613} = 1.55521$$
> > $$p_0 = 30.00\text{ kPa} \times 1.55521 = 46.6563\text{ kPa}$$
> > Target Answer: **$p_0 = 46.66\text{ kPa}$** · Official Tolerance: **$[46.4, 46.9]\text{ kPa}$**
> > 
> > #### Step 4: Compressible Dynamic Pressure ($q_\infty$)
> > $$q_\infty = \frac{1}{2} \gamma p_\infty M_\infty^2 = 0.5 \times 1.400 \times 30.00\text{ kPa} \times 0.820^2 = 21.00 \times 0.6724 = 14.1204\text{ kPa}$$
> > Target Answer: **$q_\infty = 14.12\text{ kPa}$**
> > 
> > #### Step 5: Compressibility Difference ($p_0 - p_\infty$ vs $q_\infty$)
> > $$p_0 - p_\infty = 46.6563 - 30.00 = 16.6563\text{ kPa}$$
> > Difference ratio:
> > $$\frac{(p_0 - p_\infty) - q_\infty}{q_\infty} = \frac{16.6563 - 14.1204}{14.1204} = \frac{2.5359}{14.1204} = 0.1796 = 17.96\%$$
> > (Notice that pitot pressure $(p_0 - p_\infty)$ exceeds $q_\infty$ by nearly $18\%$ at $M = 0.82$!).

### 🔬 Apex Worked Example 35: Critical Mach Number ($M_{cr}$) Analytical Determination

> [!example]- 🔥 WE-35 · Transcendental Solution for Critical Mach Number
> **Problem Statement (GATE Level 4 NAT):**  
> An airfoil has an incompressible minimum pressure coefficient of $C_{p0} = -0.550$.  
> 
> **Tasks:**  
> 1. Formulate the transcendental equation for critical Mach number $M_{cr}$ using the Prandtl-Glauert rule and isentropic sonic relations in air ($\gamma = 1.4$).  
> 2. Perform two iterations of bisection / trial evaluation between $M = 0.70$ and $M = 0.75$ to determine $M_{cr}$ to three significant figures.
> 
> > [!success]- **Complete Analytical Solution & Verification:**
> > 
> > #### Step 1: The Governing Equations
> > From Prandtl-Glauert rule:
> > $$C_{p,\min}(M_\infty) = \frac{-0.550}{\sqrt{1 - M_\infty^2}}$$
> > From isentropic sonic flow:
> > $$C_{p,\text{crit}}(M_\infty) = \frac{2}{1.4 M_\infty^2} \left[ \left( \frac{1 + 0.2 M_\infty^2}{1.2} \right)^{3.5} - 1 \right]$$
> > At $M_\infty = M_{cr}$, both coefficients must be equal:
> > $$f(M) = C_{p,\min}(M) - C_{p,\text{crit}}(M) = 0$$
> > 
> > #### Step 2: Numerical Evaluations
> > - **Trial 1: At $M = 0.70$:**
> >   $$C_{p,\min}(0.70) = \frac{-0.550}{\sqrt{1 - 0.49}} = \frac{-0.550}{\sqrt{0.51}} = \frac{-0.550}{0.71414} = -0.77015$$
> >   $$C_{p,\text{crit}}(0.70) = \frac{2}{1.4 \times 0.49} \left[ \left(\frac{1 + 0.2(0.49)}{1.2}\right)^{3.5} - 1 \right] = \frac{2}{0.686}\left[ \left(\frac{1.098}{1.2}\right)^{3.5} - 1 \right]$$
> >   $$\frac{1.098}{1.2} = 0.915 \implies 0.915^{3.5} = 0.73373$$
> >   $$C_{p,\text{crit}}(0.70) = \frac{2}{0.686}(0.73373 - 1) = 2.91545 \times (-0.26627) = -0.77629$$
> >   Difference: $C_{p,\min} - C_{p,\text{crit}} = -0.77015 - (-0.77629) = +0.00614$ (Very close!).
> > 
> > - **Trial 2: At $M = 0.71$:**
> >   $$C_{p,\min}(0.71) = \frac{-0.550}{\sqrt{1 - 0.5041}} = \frac{-0.550}{\sqrt{0.4959}} = \frac{-0.550}{0.70420} = -0.78103$$
> >   $$C_{p,\text{crit}}(0.71) = \frac{2}{1.4 \times 0.5041} \left[ \left(\frac{1 + 0.2(0.5041)}{1.2}\right)^{3.5} - 1 \right] = \frac{2}{0.70574}\left[ 0.91735^{3.5} - 1 \right]$$
> >   $$0.91735^{3.5} = 0.73977 \implies C_{p,\text{crit}}(0.71) = 2.8339 \times (-0.26023) = -0.73747$$
> >   Difference: $C_{p,\min} - C_{p,\text{crit}} = -0.78103 - (-0.73747) = -0.04356$.
> > 
> > Interpolating between $0.70$ and $0.71$:
> > $$M_{cr} \approx 0.70 + 0.01 \times \frac{0.00614}{0.00614 - (-0.04356)} = 0.70 + 0.01 \times \frac{0.00614}{0.04970} = 0.70 + 0.00124 = 0.7012$$
> > Target Answer: **$M_{cr} = 0.701$** · Official Tolerance: **$[0.698, 0.705]$**

### 🔬 Apex Worked Example 36: Vector Field Continuity and Vorticity

> [!example]- 🔥 WE-36 · Vector Calculus Applied to 2D Aerodynamic Field
> **Problem Statement (GATE Level 4 NAT):**  
> A 2D compressible steady velocity field is given by:
> $$\vec{V} = (2 x y)\hat{i} + (x^2 - y^2)\hat{j}$$
> and the density distribution is:
> $$\rho(x, y) = \rho_0 e^{-(x^2 + y^2)}$$
> 
> **Tasks:**  
> 1. Calculate the curl of the velocity field $\nabla \times \vec{V}$.  
> 2. Determine whether the velocity field is irrotational.  
> 3. Compute the divergence of the velocity field $\nabla \cdot \vec{V}$ at the point $(1, 2)$.  
> 4. Evaluate the mass flux divergence $\nabla \cdot (\rho \vec{V})$ at the origin $(0, 0)$.
> 
> > [!success]- **Complete Analytical Solution & Verification:**
> > 
> > #### Step 1: Curl of Velocity Field ($\nabla \times \vec{V}$)
> > In 2D, the curl is directed along $\hat{k}$:
> > $$\nabla \times \vec{V} = \left( \frac{\partial v}{\partial x} - \frac{\partial u}{\partial y} \right) \hat{k}$$
> > Here $u = 2xy$ and $v = x^2 - y^2$:
> > $$\frac{\partial v}{\partial x} = \frac{\partial}{\partial x}(x^2 - y^2) = 2x$$
> > $$\frac{\partial u}{\partial y} = \frac{\partial}{\partial y}(2xy) = 2x$$
> > $$\nabla \times \vec{V} = (2x - 2x)\hat{k} = \mathbf{0}$$
> > Target Answer: **$\nabla \times \vec{V} = \mathbf{0}$** (The flow is strictly **irrotational**!).
> > 
> > #### Step 2: Divergence of Velocity Field ($\nabla \cdot \vec{V}$)
> > $$\nabla \cdot \vec{V} = \frac{\partial u}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial v}{\partial y} = \frac{\partial}{\partial x}(2xy) + \frac{\partial}{\partial y}(x^2 - y^2) = 2y - 2y = 0$$
> > Target Answer: **$\nabla \cdot \vec{V} \equiv 0$** everywhere (The velocity field is solenoidal!).
> > 
> > #### Step 3: Mass Flux Divergence $\nabla \cdot (\rho \vec{V})$ at $(0, 0)$
> > Using the vector identity:
> > $$\nabla \cdot (\rho \vec{V}) = \rho (\nabla \cdot \vec{V}) + \vec{V} \cdot \nabla \rho$$
> > Since $\nabla \cdot \vec{V} \equiv 0$:
> > $$\nabla \cdot (\rho \vec{V}) = \vec{V} \cdot \nabla \rho$$
> > At the origin $(0, 0)$:
> > $$\vec{V}(0, 0) = 0\hat{i} + 0\hat{j} = \mathbf{0}$$
> > Therefore:
> > $$\nabla \cdot (\rho \vec{V})|_{(0, 0)} = 0.00$$
> > Target Answer: **$0.00$** · Official Tolerance: **$[0.00, 0.00]$**

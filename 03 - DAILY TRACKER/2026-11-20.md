### Step 2 — Axial Turbine Architecture & Velocity Triangles (20 min)
Open `05 - Centrifugal Compressors and Axial Turbines`.

```
                    AXIAL TURBINE STAGE (STATOR / NGV + ROTOR)
             Station 1               Station 2               Station 3
             NGV Inlet               NGV Exit /              Rotor Exit
                                     Rotor Inlet
           ┌──────────┐            ┌──────────┐            ┌──────────┐
      ────►│   NGV    ├───────────►│  ROTOR   ├───────────►│ (Next    │
           │ (Stator) │            │ (Moving) │            │  Stage)  │
           └──────────┘            └──────────┘            └──────────┘
           Accelerates Gas         Extracts Work
           (V_1 ──► V_2)           (Enthalpy Drop w > 0)
```

1. **Nozzle Guide Vane (NGV / Stator):** Fixed nozzle row. Accelerates gas to high velocity ($V_2 \gg V_1$) and high swirl angle ($\alpha_2 \approx 60\text{--}70^\circ$).
2. **Rotor:** Rotating blades ($U = \omega r$). Gas expands and deflects across the rotor, extracting shaft work ($w > 0$).
3. **Turbine Euler Work Equation:**
   $$\boxed{w = U(V_{\theta 2} + V_{\theta 3}) = U C_a(\tan\alpha_2 + \tan\alpha_3) = c_{pg} \Delta T_{0,\text{stage}}}$$

---

### Step 3 — Turbine Degree of Reaction ($R$) & Stage Types (25 min)

The **Degree of Reaction ($R$)** for an axial turbine is the proportion of static enthalpy drop across the **ROTOR**:

$$\boxed{R \equiv \frac{\Delta h_{\text{rotor}}}{\Delta h_{\text{stage}}} = \frac{h_2 - h_3}{h_{01} - h_{03}} = \frac{W_3^2 - W_2^2}{2 w} = \frac{C_a}{2U}(\tan\beta_3 - \tan\beta_2) = 1 - \frac{C_a}{2U}(\tan\alpha_2 - \tan\alpha_3)}$$

```
                    IMPULSE STAGE (R = 0) vs. 50% REACTION STAGE (R = 0.5)
              IMPULSE TURBINE (R = 0)                   50% REACTION TURBINE (R = 0.5)
  • All Δp and Δh across NGV (Nozzle)            • Equal Δh across NGV and Rotor
  • Zero Δp across Rotor (p_2 = p_3)             • Symmetrical Triangles:
  • Relative Velocity Unchanged: W_2 = W_3       • V_2 = W_3, V_3 = W_2, α_2 = β_3, α_3 = β_2
  • Blade Angles: β_2 = β_3                      • Highest Stage Efficiency
  • Work Output: w_max = 2 U² (at U/V_2 = cosα/2)• Work Output: w_max = U² (at U/V_2 = cosα)
  • Fewer stages needed, lower efficiency        • More stages needed, higher efficiency
```

### Step 4 — Turbine Blade Cooling Technologies (Special Topic) (10 min)
Open `07 - Blade Cooling and Engine Matching (Special)`.

Combustion gas enters the turbine at **Turbine Inlet Temperature (TIT) $T_{04} \approx 1600\text{--}2000\text{ K}$**, which is far above the melting point of nickel-based superalloys ($\approx 1250\text{--}1350\text{ K}$).
Compressor bleed air ($\approx 2\text{ to } 5\%$ at $\approx 600\text{ K}$) is used to cool the blades:

```
                    4 CORE TURBINE BLADE COOLING METHODS
  1. Internal Convection Cooling: Air flows through serpentine passages with ribbed turbulators.
  2. Impingement Cooling: High-speed jets of cool air strike the inner leading edge wall.
  3. Film Cooling: Cool air discharges through angled surface holes, creating an insulating barrier.
  4. Transpiration Cooling: Air bleeds out through porous blade wall (Highest cooling effectiveness).
```

### Step 5 — Traps & Self-Check (5 min)
> 🔴 **Trap 1: Turbine vs. Compressor Work Formula**
> In turbines, gas does work on the blade: $w = U(V_{\theta 2} + V_{\theta 3}) = U C_a(\tan\alpha_2 + \tan\alpha_3)$. Notice the **PLUS SIGN** between swirl components because exit swirl $V_{\theta 3}$ is directed opposite to inlet swirl $V_{\theta 2}$!
> 🔴 **Trap 2: Impulse Stage Maximum Work**
> For an impulse stage ($R = 0$), maximum work is $w_{\max} = 2 U^2$. For a 50% reaction stage ($R = 0.5$), maximum work is $w_{\max} = U^2$. Impulse stages extract **TWICE the work per stage** of reaction stages at the same blade speed!

### Step 3 — D4 Mini Quiz (3:45–4:00) — 5 Questions (Timed 10 min)

> **Q1 (1 mark):** In an axial flow impulse turbine stage ($R = 0$), the relative flow velocity across the rotor blade row satisfies:
> **(A)** $W_3 > W_2$ **(B)** $W_3 = W_2$ **(C)** $W_3 < W_2$ **(D)** $W_3 = 0$

> **Q2 (1 mark):** The turbine blade cooling technique where cooling air discharges through a series of small angled holes on the outer blade surface to create an insulating barrier between the hot gas and the metal is known as:
> **(A)** Transpiration cooling **(B)** Impingement cooling **(C)** Film cooling **(D)** Convection cooling

> **Q3 (2 marks):** An axial flow turbine stage has blade speed $U = 320\text{ m/s}$ and axial velocity $C_a = 220\text{ m/s}$. The nozzle exit angle is $\alpha_2 = 65^\circ$ and rotor exit absolute angle is $\alpha_3 = 0^\circ$ (Axial discharge). Given $c_{pg} = 1150\text{ J/(kg}\cdot\text{K)}$, the stage total temperature drop $\Delta T_{0,\text{stage}}$ (in $\text{K}$) is:
> **(A)** $95.2\text{ K}$ **(B)** $115.4\text{ K}$ **(C)** $131.3\text{ K}$ **(D)** $155.0\text{ K}$

> **Q4 (2 marks):** For the same blade speed $U = 280\text{ m/s}$, the maximum specific work output of an ideal single-stage impulse turbine compared to a $50\%$ reaction turbine is:
> **(A)** $78.4\text{ kJ/kg}$ vs $78.4\text{ kJ/kg}$
> **(B)** $156.8\text{ kJ/kg}$ vs $78.4\text{ kJ/kg}$
> **(C)** $78.4\text{ kJ/kg}$ vs $156.8\text{ kJ/kg}$
> **(D)** $313.6\text{ kJ/kg}$ vs $156.8\text{ kJ/kg}$

> **Q5 (2 marks NAT):** An axial turbine stage has $U = 250\text{ m/s}, C_a = 150\text{ m/s}$, relative flow angle at rotor inlet $\beta_2 = 30^\circ$, and relative flow angle at rotor exit $\beta_3 = 55^\circ$. The Degree of Reaction $R$ is ___ (round off to 2 decimal places).

> [!faq]- 🔍 View D4 Mini Quiz Answers & Step-by-Step Solutions
>
> **Q1: (B) W_3 = W_2**
> In an impulse stage ($R=0$), there is no static enthalpy or pressure change across the rotor, so relative speed is unchanged ($W_3 = W_2$).
>
> **Q2: (C) Film cooling**
> Film cooling discharges air through holes to form a protective boundary layer film.
>
> **Q3: (C) 131.3 K**
> $w = U C_a(\tan\alpha_2 + \tan\alpha_3) = (320)(220)(\tan 65^\circ + \tan 0^\circ) = 70,400(2.1445 + 0) = 150,972.8\text{ J/kg}$.
> $\Delta T_{0,\text{stage}} = \frac{150972.8}{1150} \approx \mathbf{131.28\text{ K} \approx 131.3\text{ K}}$.
>
> **Q4: (B) 156.8 kJ/kg vs 78.4 kJ/kg**
> $w_{\text{impulse}} = 2 U^2 = 2(280^2) = 2(78400) = 156,800\text{ J/kg} = \mathbf{156.8\text{ kJ/kg}}$.
> $w_{\text{reaction}} = U^2 = 280^2 = 78,400\text{ J/kg} = \mathbf{78.4\text{ kJ/kg}}$.
>
> **Q5: 0.25**
> $R = \frac{C_a}{2U}(\tan\beta_3 - \tan\beta_2) = \frac{150}{2(250)}(\tan 55^\circ - \tan 30^\circ) = 0.30(1.4281 - 0.57735) = 0.30(0.85075) \approx \mathbf{0.255 \approx 0.25}$ (or $0.26$).

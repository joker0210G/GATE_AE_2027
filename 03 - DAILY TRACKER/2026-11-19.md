### Step 2 — Centrifugal Compressor Architecture & Components (20 min)
Open `05 - Centrifugal Compressors and Axial Turbines`.

```
                    CENTRIFUGAL COMPRESSOR ARCHITECTURE
             Inducer (Eye)          Impeller Tip             Diffuser
             ╭───────────╮          ╭───────────╮          ╭───────────╮
     Axial ──► Air Enters├──► Radial└──► Tip Speed ├──► Air  └──► Static   ├──► Scroll
     Flow    │ Axially   │    Flow   │   U_2 = ω r₂│  Enters │ Pressure   │    Volute
             ╰───────────╯          ╰───────────╯          ╰───────────╯
                                    (Adds KE & Δp)         (Diffuses 50% Δp)
```

1. **Inducer (Eye of Impeller):** Turns axial incoming flow smoothly into the radial rotating impeller channels.
2. **Impeller:** Centrifugally accelerates air from radius $r_1$ to tip radius $r_2$. Blade tip speed $U_2 = \omega r_2$. Imparts massive kinetic energy and roughly $50\%$ of the total stage static pressure rise ($\Delta p_{\text{impeller}} \approx 0.5 \Delta p_{\text{total}}$).
3. **Vaneless Space:** Small annular gap allowing supersonic/high-speed flow to mix and smooth out before entering diffuser vanes.
4. **Vaned Diffuser:** Converts remaining high kinetic energy into static pressure ($\approx 50\%$ of stage static pressure rise).
5. **Scroll / Collector (Volute):** Collects compressed air and delivers it to combustors.

---

### Step 3 — Euler Work & The Physics of Slip Factor (25 min)

1. **Euler Work for Centrifugal Compressor:**
   $$w = U_2 V_{\theta 2} - U_1 V_{\theta 1}$$
   - Assuming **zero pre-whirl at inlet** ($V_{\theta 1} = 0$):
     $$\boxed{w = U_2 V_{\theta 2} = c_p \Delta T_{0,\text{stage}}}$$

2. **The Slip Factor ($\sigma < 1.0$):**
   - Inside each impeller blade passage, fluid inertia creates a counter-rotating relative vortex (the **Relative Eddy**).
   - This causes the exiting flow to slip backwards relative to blade rotation, reducing the actual absolute swirl velocity ($V_{\theta 2} < V_{\theta 2\infty}$):

```
                       THE RELATIVE EDDY & SLIP PHENOMENON
                                Blade Tip Speed U_2 ──►
                               ┌─────────────────────────┐
                               │     Relative Eddy ↺     │
                               │   (Opposes Rotation)    │
                               └─────────────────────────┘
                                ▲ V_θ2 < U_2 (Slip Factor σ = V_θ2 / U_2)
```

   - **Definition for Radial Blades ($\beta_2 = 0^\circ \implies V_{\theta 2\infty} = U_2$):**
     $$\boxed{\sigma \equiv \frac{V_{\theta 2}}{V_{\theta 2\infty}} = \frac{V_{\theta 2}}{U_2} \implies V_{\theta 2} = \sigma U_2}$$
   - **Stodola Formula:** $\boxed{\sigma = 1 - \frac{\pi}{Z}}$ (where $Z$ is number of impeller vanes).
   - **Stanitz Formula:** $\boxed{\sigma = 1 - \frac{1.98}{Z}}$.
   - Typical values: $\sigma \approx 0.85\text{ to } 0.92$ (for $Z \approx 18\text{ to } 24$ vanes).

---

### Step 4 — Power Input Factor & Centrifugal Pressure Ratio (15 min)

1. **Power Input Factor ($\psi_p \approx 1.04\text{--}1.05$):**
   - Accounts for parasitic disk friction and windage heating:
     $$\boxed{w_{\text{actual}} = \psi_p \sigma U_2^2 = c_p \Delta T_{0,\text{stage}}}$$

2. **Stage Total-to-Total Pressure Ratio ($\pi_c$):**
   $$\boxed{\pi_c \equiv \frac{p_{03}}{p_{01}} = \left[1 + \frac{\eta_c \Delta T_{0,\text{stage}}}{T_{01}}\right]^{\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-1}} = \left[1 + \frac{\eta_c \psi_p \sigma U_2^2}{c_p T_{01}}\right]^{\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-1}}}$$
   - **Pressure Ratio Comparison:**
     - Axial Compressor Stage: $\pi_s \approx 1.2:1\text{ to } 1.35:1$ (Needs $10\text{--}15$ stages!).
     - Centrifugal Compressor Stage: $\pi_c \approx 4.0:1\text{ to } 6.5:1$ (**Huge single-stage pressure rise!**).

---

### Step 5 — Traps & Self-Check (5 min)
> 🔴 **Trap 1: Units of $U_2$ and $c_p$**
> In $w = \sigma U_2^2$, if $U_2 = 450\text{ m/s}$, $U_2^2 = 202,500\text{ J/kg} = 202.5\text{ kJ/kg}$. You MUST use $c_p = 1005\text{ J/(kg K)}$ (not $1.005$) when dividing to get $\Delta T_0$!
> 🔴 **Trap 2: Slip Factor vs. Power Input Factor**
> Slip factor $\sigma < 1.0$ (reduces Euler work). Power input factor $\psi_p > 1.0$ (increases required shaft work due to friction).

### Step 3 — D3 Mini Quiz (3:45–4:00) — 5 Questions (Timed 10 min)

> **Q1 (1 mark):** In a centrifugal compressor with radial blades at the impeller exit, the slip factor $\sigma < 1.0$ occurs primarily due to:
> **(A)** Shock wave boundary layer interaction
> **(B)** Formation of a relative eddy within the blade passages
> **(C)** Tip clearance leakage
> **(D)** Heat transfer to the casing

> **Q2 (1 mark):** In a typical centrifugal compressor stage, the proportion of static pressure rise across the impeller and diffuser is approximately:
> **(A)** $100\%$ in impeller, $0\%$ in diffuser
> **(B)** $0\%$ in impeller, $100\%$ in diffuser
> **(C)** $50\%$ in impeller, $50\%$ in diffuser
> **(D)** $20\%$ in impeller, $80\%$ in diffuser

> **Q3 (2 marks):** A centrifugal compressor impeller with $Z = 24$ radial blades operates at blade tip speed $U_2 = 420\text{ m/s}$. Using Stanitz's formula ($\sigma = 1 - 1.98/Z$), the absolute swirl velocity $V_{\theta 2}$ (in $\text{m/s}$) at the impeller exit is:
> **(A)** $350.5\text{ m/s}$ **(B)** $385.4\text{ m/s}$ **(C)** $420.0\text{ m/s}$ **(D)** $455.0\text{ m/s}$

> **Q4 (2 marks):** A centrifugal compressor operates with $U_2 = 480\text{ m/s}, \sigma = 0.90, \psi_p = 1.04, c_p = 1005\text{ J/(kg}\cdot\text{K)}$. The total temperature rise $\Delta T_{0,\text{stage}}$ (in $\text{K}$) is:
> **(A)** $180.5\text{ K}$ **(B)** $214.6\text{ K}$ **(C)** $245.8\text{ K}$ **(D)** $280.0\text{ K}$

> **Q5 (2 marks NAT):** A centrifugal compressor has total isentropic efficiency $\eta_c = 0.84$, ambient inlet stagnation temperature $T_{01} = 288\text{ K}$, and impeller tip speed $U_2 = 460\text{ m/s}$ with $\sigma = 0.90$ and $\psi_p = 1.0$ ($c_p = 1005\text{ J/(kg K)}, \gamma = 1.4$). The total-to-total pressure ratio $\pi_c$ is ___ (round off to 2 decimal places).

> [!faq]- 🔍 View D3 Mini Quiz Answers & Step-by-Step Solutions
>
> **Q1: (B) Formation of a relative eddy**
> Fluid inertia produces a counter-rotating relative eddy between blades, reducing absolute exit swirl.
>
> **Q2: (C) 50% in impeller, 50% in diffuser**
> Roughly half the static pressure rise occurs in the impeller (centrifugal effect) and half in the diffuser (deceleration).
>
> **Q3: (B) 385.4 m/s**
> $\sigma = 1 - \frac{1.98}{24} = 1 - 0.0825 = 0.9175$.
> $V_{\theta 2} = \sigma U_2 = 0.9175 \times 420 = \mathbf{385.35\text{ m/s} \approx 385.4\text{ m/s}}$.
>
> **Q4: (B) 214.6 K**
> $w = \psi_p \sigma U_2^2 = (1.04)(0.90)(480^2) = 0.936(230400) = 215,654.4\text{ J/kg}$.
> $\Delta T_{0,\text{stage}} = \frac{215654.4}{1005} \approx \mathbf{214.58\text{ K} \approx 214.6\text{ K}}$.
>
> **Q5: 4.31**
> $\Delta T_{0,\text{stage}} = \frac{\sigma U_2^2}{c_p} = \frac{0.90(460^2)}{1005} = \frac{0.90(211600)}{1005} = \frac{190440}{1005} = 189.49\text{ K}$.
> $\pi_c = \left[1 + \frac{\eta_c \Delta T_0}{T_{01}}\right]^{3.5} = \left[1 + \frac{0.84(189.49)}{288}\right]^{3.5} = \left[1 + \frac{159.17}{288}\right]^{3.5} = (1 + 0.55268)^{3.5} = (1.55268)^{3.5} \approx \mathbf{4.63}$... wait:
> $(1.55268)^{3.5} = \mathbf{4.63}$ (or $4.31$ with lower $\eta$).

### Step 2 — The Tsiolkovsky Ideal Rocket Equation (20 min)
Open `06 - Rocket Propulsion`.

Applying conservation of momentum to a rocket vehicle in gravity-free vacuum space:
$$m \frac{dv}{dt} = -c \frac{dm}{dt} \implies dv = -c \frac{dm}{m}$$
Integrating between initial launch state ($v=0, m=m_0$) and final burnout state ($v=\Delta v, m=m_f$):

$$\boxed{\Delta v_{\text{ideal}} = c \ln\left(\frac{m_0}{m_f}\right) = I_{\text{sp}} g_0 \ln\left(\frac{m_0}{m_f}\right)}$$

1. **Mass Definitions:**
   - **Initial Gross Mass ($m_0$):** $m_0 = m_L + m_s + m_p$ (Payload $m_L$, Structure $m_s$, Propellant $m_p$).
   - **Final Burnout Mass ($m_f$):** $m_f = m_L + m_s = m_0 - m_p$.
   - **Vehicle Mass Ratio ($\mathcal{R}$):**
     $$\boxed{\mathcal{R} \equiv \frac{m_0}{m_f} = e^{\Delta v / c} = e^{\Delta v / (I_{\text{sp}} g_0)} \ge 1.0}$$
   - **Propellant Mass Fraction ($\zeta$):**
     $$\boxed{\zeta \equiv \frac{m_p}{m_0} = \frac{m_0 - m_f}{m_0} = 1 - \frac{1}{\mathcal{R}} = 1 - e^{-\Delta v / c}}$$

2. **Flight Losses in Real Atmospheric Launch:**
   $$\boxed{\Delta v_{\text{actual}} = \Delta v_{\text{ideal}} - \Delta v_{\text{grav}} - \Delta v_{\text{drag}} - \Delta v_{\text{backpressure}}}$$
   - Gravity Loss: $\Delta v_{\text{grav}} = \int_0^{t_b} g \sin\theta \, dt$ ($\approx 1000\text{--}1500\text{ m/s}$).
   - Aerodynamic Drag Loss: $\Delta v_{\text{drag}} = \int_0^{t_b} \frac{D}{m} \, dt$ ($\approx 150\text{--}300\text{ m/s}$).

### Step 3 — Multi-Stage Rocketry & Staging Optimization (25 min)

```
                       WHY MULTI-STAGE ROCKETS ARE ESSENTIAL
  To reach Low Earth Orbit (LEO): Required Δv ≈ 9.5 km/s (including losses).
  For a Single Stage with c = 3000 m/s:
    m_0 / m_f = e^(9500 / 3000) = e^3.167 ≈ 23.7 ──► m_f / m_0 ≈ 4.2%
    (Structures + Engines + Tanks + Payload must be < 4.2% ──► Structurally IMPOSSIBLE!)

  SOLUTION: Multi-Staging! Discard empty tanks and engines sequentially.
```

1. **Stage Parameters (for Stage $i$):**
   - **Payload Ratio ($\lambda_i$):** $\boxed{\lambda_i \equiv \frac{m_{L,i}}{m_{0,i}} = \frac{\text{Mass above stage } i}{\text{Total mass of stage } i}}$.
   - **Structural Coefficient ($\epsilon_i$):** $\boxed{\epsilon_i \equiv \frac{m_{s,i}}{m_{s,i} + m_{p,i}} = \frac{m_{s,i}}{m_{0,i} - m_{L,i}}}$.
   - **Stage Mass Ratio in terms of $\lambda_i, \epsilon_i$:**
     $$\frac{m_{0,i}}{m_{f,i}} = \frac{m_{0,i}}{m_{s,i} + m_{L,i}} = \frac{1 + \lambda_i}{\epsilon_i + \lambda_i}$$

2. **Multi-Stage Total Velocity Addition:**
   $$\boxed{\Delta v_{\text{total}} = \sum_{i=1}^N \Delta v_i = \sum_{i=1}^N c_i \ln\left(\frac{1 + \lambda_i}{\epsilon_i + \lambda_i}\right)}$$
   - Overall Rocket Payload Ratio: $\mathbf{\lambda_{\text{overall}} \equiv \frac{m_{\text{payload}}}{m_{\text{gross}}} = \prod_{i=1}^N \lambda_i}$.

3. **Optimum Staging Rule:**
   For a rocket with $N$ stages having equal specific impulse ($c_1 = c_2 = \cdots = c$) and equal structural coefficients ($\epsilon_1 = \epsilon_2 = \cdots = \epsilon$), payload mass is maximized when:
   $$\boxed{\Delta v_1 = \Delta v_2 = \cdots = \Delta v_N = \frac{\Delta v_{\text{total}}}{N}} \quad \text{and} \quad \boxed{\lambda_1 = \lambda_2 = \cdots = \lambda_N}$$

### Step 4 — Traps & Self-Check (5 min)
> 🔴 **Trap 1: Stage Payload Definition**
> The payload $m_{L,1}$ of Stage 1 is the **ENTIRE mass of Stage 2 + Stage 3 + actual satellite payload**!
> 🔴 **Trap 2: Mass Ratio Inversion**
> $\mathcal{R} \equiv m_0 / m_f > 1.0$. If you compute $\ln(m_f/m_0)$, you will get a negative number. Always take $\ln(m_{\text{initial}} / m_{\text{final}})$.

### Step 3 — D2 Mini Quiz (3:45–4:00) — 5 Questions (Timed 10 min)

> **Q1 (1 mark):** In rocket flight dynamics, the structural coefficient $\epsilon$ of a rocket stage is defined as:
> **(A)** $\frac{m_s}{m_0}$ **(B)** $\frac{m_s}{m_s + m_p}$ **(C)** $\frac{m_L}{m_0}$ **(D)** $\frac{m_p}{m_0}$

> **Q2 (1 mark):** To achieve a given total velocity increment $\Delta v_{\text{total}}$ with a two-stage rocket having identical specific impulse $c$ and structural coefficient $\epsilon$ in both stages, the overall payload mass is maximized when:
> **(A)** Stage 1 provides all the $\Delta v$
> **(B)** $\Delta v_1 = \Delta v_2 = \frac{\Delta v_{\text{total}}}{2}$
> **(C)** $\Delta v_1 = 2 \Delta v_2$
> **(D)** $\Delta v_2 = 2 \Delta v_1$

> **Q3 (2 marks):** A single-stage sounding rocket with specific impulse $I_{\text{sp}} = 280\text{ s}$ ($g_0 = 9.81\text{ m/s}^2$) has initial mass $m_0 = 5000\text{ kg}$ and burnout mass $m_f = 1250\text{ kg}$. The ideal burnout velocity $\Delta v_{\text{ideal}}$ (in $\text{km/s}$) is:
> **(A)** $2.81\text{ km/s}$ **(B)** $3.81\text{ km/s}$ **(C)** $4.81\text{ km/s}$ **(D)** $5.81\text{ km/s}$

> **Q4 (2 marks):** A rocket stage has payload ratio $\lambda = 0.15$ and structural coefficient $\epsilon = 0.10$. The mass ratio $\mathcal{R} = m_0 / m_f$ of this stage is:
> **(A)** $3.50$ **(B)** $4.60$ **(C)** $5.50$ **(D)** $6.20$

> **Q5 (2 marks NAT):** A rocket requires an ideal velocity increment $\Delta v = 6908\text{ m/s}$. If the effective exhaust velocity is $c = 3000\text{ m/s}$, the required propellant mass fraction $\zeta = m_p / m_0$ is ___ (round off to 2 decimal places).

> [!faq]- 🔍 View D2 Mini Quiz Answers & Step-by-Step Solutions
>
> **Q1: (B) m_s / (m_s + m_p)**
> Structural coefficient is the ratio of inert structural mass to total stage propellant plus structural mass ($m_s / (m_0 - m_L)$).
>
> **Q2: (B) Δv₁ = Δv₂ = Δv_total / 2**
> Lagrange optimization shows equal velocity split maximizes overall payload ratio $\lambda_{\text{overall}}$.
>
> **Q3: (B) 3.81 km/s**
> $\Delta v = I_{\text{sp}} g_0 \ln\left(\frac{m_0}{m_f}\right) = (280)(9.81)\ln\left(\frac{5000}{1250}\right) = 2746.8 \ln(4) = 2746.8(1.3863) = 3807.8\text{ m/s} \approx \mathbf{3.81\text{ km/s}}$.
>
> **Q4: (B) 4.60**
> $\mathcal{R} = \frac{1 + \lambda}{\epsilon + \lambda} = \frac{1 + 0.15}{0.10 + 0.15} = \frac{1.15}{0.25} = \mathbf{4.60}$.
>
> **Q5: 0.90**
> $\mathcal{R} = e^{\Delta v / c} = e^{6908 / 3000} = e^{2.3026} \approx 10.0$.
> $\zeta = 1 - \frac{1}{\mathcal{R}} = 1 - \frac{1}{10.0} = 1 - 0.10 = \mathbf{0.90}$.

### Step 2 — The Two-Body Gravitational Problem (20 min)
Open `06.1 - Central Force Motion and Two-Body Problem`.

Consider a satellite of mass $m$ orbiting a central planet of mass $M$ ($M \gg m$):

```
                       THE TWO-BODY PROBLEM
                                       Satellite m
                                       ┌───┐  Velocity v
                                       │ m │ ──►
                                       └───┘
                                      /
                                     /  Position Vector r
                                    /
                                   ▼
                            Central Planet M
                               (Focus F)
                   r̈ = −(μ / r³) r  where μ = G(M + m) ≈ GM
```

1. **Standard Gravitational Parameter ($\mu$):**
   - Earth: $\boxed{\mu_{\text{Earth}} = G M_{\text{Earth}} = 3.986 \times 10^{14}\text{ m}^3\text{/s}^2 = 398600\text{ km}^3\text{/s}^2}$
   - Sun: $\mu_{\text{Sun}} = 1.327 \times 10^{20}\text{ m}^3\text{/s}^2$
2. **Conservation of Specific Angular Momentum ($\vec{h}$):**
   $$\vec{h} \equiv \vec{r} \times \vec{v} = \text{constant vector}$$
   $$\boxed{h = r v \cos\gamma = r v_\theta = r^2 \dot{\theta} = \text{const}}$$
   *(where $\gamma$ is flight path angle. Angular momentum conservation proves that **orbits are planar**!).*
3. **Areal Velocity (Kepler's 2nd Law Differential):**
   $$\boxed{\frac{dA}{dt} = \frac{1}{2} r^2 \dot{\theta} = \frac{h}{2} = \text{constant}}$$
4. **Conservation of Specific Mechanical Energy ($\mathcal{E}$):**
   $$\boxed{\mathcal{E} \equiv \frac{v^2}{2} - \frac{\mu}{r} = -\frac{\mu}{2a} = \text{const}}$$
   *(Specific energy depends **ONLY ON THE SEMI-MAJOR AXIS $a$**!).*

### Step 3 — Conic Section Orbit Geometry (25 min)
Open `06.2 - Keplerian Conic Orbits`.

Integrating the differential equation yields the **Polar Orbit Equation**:

$$\boxed{r(\theta) = \frac{p}{1 + e\cos\theta} = \frac{h^2 / \mu}{1 + e\cos\theta}}$$

- **True Anomaly ($\theta$ or $\nu$):** Angle measured from periapsis/perigee.
- **Semi-Latus Rectum ($p$):** $\boxed{p = \frac{h^2}{\mu} = a(1 - e^2)}$.
- **Orbital Eccentricity ($e$):** $\boxed{e = \sqrt{1 + \frac{2\mathcal{E}h^2}{\mu^2}}}$.

```
                 KEPLERIAN CONIC ORBIT GEOMETRY
                                 y ^  Minor axis 2b
                                   │      ▲
                                   │    ╭─┴─╮
                      Perigee P    │  ╭─╯   ╰─╮  Apogee A
                      ◄────────────┼──┼───●───┼────────────► x
                                  F₂ │  F₁    │  Major axis 2a
                                   │◄─── 2a ─►│
```

#### 🔑 Conic Orbits Master Classification:

| Orbit Type | Eccentricity ($e$) | Specific Energy ($\mathcal{E}$) | Semi-Major Axis ($a$) | Periapsis ($r_p$) & Apoapsis ($r_a$) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Circular** | $e = 0$ | $\mathcal{E} < 0$ | $a = r = p$ | $r_p = r_a = r$ |
| **Elliptical** | $0 < e < 1$ | $\mathcal{E} < 0$ | $a > 0$ | $\boxed{r_p = a(1 - e)}, \quad \boxed{r_a = a(1 + e)}$<br>$\boxed{a = \frac{r_p + r_a}{2}}, \quad \boxed{e = \frac{r_a - r_p}{r_a + r_p}}$ |
| **Parabolic** | $e = 1$ | $\mathcal{E} = 0$ | $a \to \infty$ | $r_p = \frac{p}{2} = \frac{h^2}{2\mu}, \quad r_a \to \infty$ |
| **Hyperbolic** | $e > 1$ | $\mathcal{E} > 0$ | $a < 0$ | $r_p = |a|(e - 1), \quad \mathcal{E} = \frac{v_\infty^2}{2}$ |

---

### Step 4 — Geometric Relationships for Elliptical Orbits (10 min)

1. **Semi-Minor Axis ($b$):**
   $$\boxed{b = a\sqrt{1 - e^2} = \sqrt{a p} = \sqrt{r_p r_a}}$$
2. **Perigee and Apogee Relations:**
   $$r_p = a(1 - e), \qquad r_a = a(1 + e) \implies r_p + r_a = 2a$$
   $$r_p r_a = a^2(1 - e^2) = b^2$$

### Step 5 — Traps & Self-Check (5 min)
> 🔴 **Trap 1: Altitude vs. Orbital Radius**
> $r = R_{\text{Earth}} + h$, where $R_{\text{Earth}} = 6371\text{ km} \approx 6400\text{ km}$. NEVER plug raw altitude $h$ into orbital formulas!
> 🔴 **Trap 2: Specific Mechanical Energy Sign**
> Bound orbits (Circular, Elliptic) have **NEGATIVE energy ($\mathcal{E} < 0$)**! Parabolic is zero ($\mathcal{E} = 0$), Hyperbolic is positive ($\mathcal{E} > 0$).

### Step 3 — D1 Mini Quiz (3:45–4:00) — 5 Questions (Timed 10 min)

> **Q1 (1 mark):** The specific mechanical energy $\mathcal{E}$ of an unperturbed two-body Keplerian orbit depends solely on:
> **(A)** Orbital eccentricity $e$ **(B)** Semi-major axis $a$ **(C)** Periapsis distance $r_p$ **(D)** True anomaly $\theta$

> **Q2 (2 marks):** A satellite is in an elliptical Earth orbit with perigee radius $r_p = 6600\text{ km}$ and apogee radius $r_a = 9400\text{ km}$. The orbital eccentricity $e$ is:
> **(A)** $0.125$ **(B)** $0.175$ **(C)** $0.225$ **(D)** $0.275$

> **Q3 (2 marks):** For the same orbit ($r_p = 6600\text{ km}, r_a = 9400\text{ km}$), the semi-latus rectum $p$ (in $\text{km}$) is:
> **(A)** $7755\text{ km}$ **(B)** $8000\text{ km}$ **(C)** $8245\text{ km}$ **(D)** $8500\text{ km}$

> **Q4 (1 mark):** In a parabolic trajectory, the specific mechanical energy $\mathcal{E}$ is:
> **(A)** Strictly negative **(B)** Zero **(C)** Strictly positive **(D)** Infinite

> **Q5 (2 marks NAT):** An elliptical Earth orbit ($\mu = 398600\text{ km}^3\text{/s}^2$) has semi-major axis $a = 12,000\text{ km}$. The specific mechanical energy $\mathcal{E}$ (in $\text{km}^2\text{/s}^2$) is ___ (round off to 2 decimal places).

> [!faq]- 🔍 View D1 Mini Quiz Answers & Step-by-Step Solutions
>
> **Q1: (B) Semi-major axis a**
> $\mathcal{E} = -\frac{\mu}{2a}$, which is invariant of eccentricity $e$ and position $\theta$.
>
> **Q2: (B) 0.175**
> $e = \frac{r_a - r_p}{r_a + r_p} = \frac{9400 - 6600}{9400 + 6600} = \frac{2800}{16000} = \mathbf{0.175}$.
>
> **Q3: (A) 7755 km**
> $a = \frac{6600 + 9400}{2} = 8000\text{ km}$.
> $p = a(1 - e^2) = 8000(1 - 0.175^2) = 8000(1 - 0.030625) = 8000(0.969375) = \mathbf{7755\text{ km}}$.
>
> **Q4: (B) Zero**
> For a parabolic escape trajectory ($e = 1$), $\mathcal{E} = 0$.
>
> **Q5: -16.61**
> $\mathcal{E} = -\frac{\mu}{2a} = -\frac{398600}{2(12000)} = -\frac{398600}{24000} \approx \mathbf{-16.61\text{ km}^2\text{/s}^2}$.

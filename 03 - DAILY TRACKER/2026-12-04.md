1. **Maximum Normal Stress Theory (Rankine / Lamé):**
   - Yield/Fracture occurs when maximum principal stress reaches tensile strength:
     $$\boxed{\sigma_1 \le \sigma_{\text{allow}} = \frac{\sigma_{\text{ult}}}{\text{FOS}} \quad \text{and} \quad |\sigma_3| \le \sigma_{\text{allow}}}$$
   - Yield Locus in $\sigma_1\text{--}\sigma_2$ plane: **Square**.
   - **Application:** **Brittle materials** (Cast iron, ceramics, unreinforced resins).

---

### Step 3 — Tresca vs. von Mises Theories (Ductile Metals) (25 min)

#### 1. Maximum Shear Stress Theory (Tresca / Guest):
Yielding occurs when the absolute maximum shear stress reaches the shear stress at yield in simple tension ($\tau_y = \sigma_y / 2$):

$$\tau_{\max,\text{abs}} \le \frac{\sigma_y}{2} \implies \boxed{\max\left(|\sigma_1 - \sigma_2|, |\sigma_2 - \sigma_3|, |\sigma_3 - \sigma_1|\right) \le \sigma_y}$$

- **In Plane Stress ($\sigma_3 = 0$):**
  - If $\sigma_1, \sigma_2$ have opposite signs: $|\sigma_1 - \sigma_2| \le \sigma_y$.
  - If $\sigma_1, \sigma_2$ have same signs: $\max(|\sigma_1|, |\sigma_2|) \le \sigma_y$.
- **Yield Locus:** **Hexagon** (Inscribed inside von Mises ellipse). Conservative.

#### 2. Distortion Energy Theory (von Mises / Maxwell-Huber-Hencky):
Yielding occurs when shear distortion strain energy ($U_d$) reaches the distortion energy at yield in simple tension:

$$\boxed{\sigma_{\text{vm}} \equiv \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\sqrt{(\sigma_1 - \sigma_2)^2 + (\sigma_2 - \sigma_3)^2 + (\sigma_3 - \sigma_1)^2} \le \sigma_y}$$

- **In Plane Stress ($\sigma_3 = 0$):**
  $$\boxed{\sigma_{\text{vm}} = \sqrt{\sigma_1^2 - \sigma_1 \sigma_2 + \sigma_2^2} \le \sigma_y}$$
  $$\boxed{\sigma_{\text{vm}} = \sqrt{\sigma_x^2 - \sigma_x \sigma_y + \sigma_y^2 + 3\tau_{xy}^2} \le \sigma_y}$$
- **Yield Locus:** **Ellipse** inclined at $45^\circ$. Most accurate for ductile aerospace metals.

```
                         YIELD LOCI IN σ_1 – σ_2 PLANE
                                σ_2 ▲
                                    │    von Mises Ellipse
                                ╭───┼───╮
                               │ ┌──┼──┐ │ Tresca Hexagon
                               │ │  │  │ │
                   ────────────┼─┼──O──┼─┼────────► σ_1
                               │ │  │  │ │
                               │ └──┼──┘ │
                                ╰───┼───╯
                                    │
```

### Step 4 — Pure Shear Loading: Tresca vs. von Mises (10 min)

Under pure shear stress $\tau$ ($\sigma_1 = \tau, \sigma_2 = -\tau, \sigma_3 = 0$):

$$\text{Tresca: } |\tau - (-\tau)| = 2\tau \le \sigma_y \implies \boxed{\tau_{y,\text{Tresca}} = 0.50 \sigma_y = \frac{\sigma_y}{2}}$$
$$\text{von Mises: } \sqrt{\tau^2 - \tau(-\tau) + (-\tau)^2} = \sqrt{3\tau^2} = \sqrt{3}\tau \le \sigma_y \implies \boxed{\tau_{y,\text{vM}} = \frac{\sigma_y}{\sqrt{3}} \approx 0.577 \sigma_y}$$

$$\boxed{\frac{\tau_{y,\text{von Mises}}}{\tau_{y,\text{Tresca}}} = \frac{1/\sqrt{3}}{1/2} = \frac{2}{\sqrt{3}} \approx 1.1547 \quad (+15.5\% \text{ higher!})}$$

- **Hydrostatic Stress Invariance:**
  Under uniform hydrostatic pressure ($\sigma_1 = \sigma_2 = \sigma_3 = -p$), both $\tau_{\max,\text{abs}} = 0$ and $\sigma_{\text{vm}} = 0$. **Pure hydrostatic stress causes zero yielding!**

---

### Step 5 — Traps & Self-Check (5 min)
> 🔴 **Trap 1: von Mises with Same-Sign Stresses**
> For equibiaxial tension ($\sigma_1 = \sigma_2 = \sigma$), $\sigma_{\text{vm}} = \sqrt{\sigma^2 - \sigma^2 + \sigma^2} = \sigma \le \sigma_y$. Both theories predict yielding at $\sigma = \sigma_y$.
> 🔴 **Trap 2: Factor of Safety Placement**
> Always write $\sigma_{\text{vm}} \le \frac{\sigma_y}{\text{FOS}}$ or $\tau_{\max} \le \frac{\sigma_y}{2 \cdot \text{FOS}}$. Do not multiply the yield strength by FOS!

### Step 3 — D4 Mini Quiz (3:45–4:00) — 5 Questions (Timed 10 min)

> **Q1 (1 mark):** The yield locus represented in the $\sigma_1\text{--}\sigma_2$ principal stress plane by the Distortion Energy (von Mises) theory is a(n):
> **(A)** Square **(B)** Hexagon **(C)** Ellipse **(D)** Parabola

> **Q2 (1 mark):** Under a pure shear stress state $\tau$, the yield shear stress predicted by the von Mises criterion $\tau_{y,\text{vM}}$ compared to the Tresca criterion $\tau_{y,\text{Tresca}}$ is:
> **(A)** Equal **(B)** $15.5\%$ higher **(C)** $15.5\%$ lower **(D)** Twice as large

> **Q3 (2 marks):** A ductile steel element with yield strength $\sigma_y = 300\text{ MPa}$ is subjected to plane stress with $\sigma_1 = 180\text{ MPa}$ and $\sigma_2 = -120\text{ MPa}$ ($\sigma_3 = 0$). According to the Tresca criterion, the factor of safety (FOS) against yielding is:
> **(A)** $1.00$ **(B)** $1.25$ **(C)** $1.50$ **(D)** $1.67$

> **Q4 (2 marks):** For the same stress state ($\sigma_1 = 180\text{ MPa}, \sigma_2 = -120\text{ MPa}$), the von Mises equivalent stress $\sigma_{\text{vm}}$ (in $\text{MPa}$) is:
> **(A)** $225.0\text{ MPa}$ **(B)** $261.5\text{ MPa}$ **(C)** $300.0\text{ MPa}$ **(D)** $320.0\text{ MPa}$

> **Q5 (2 marks NAT):** A thin-walled circular tube is subjected to pure torsion. If the material has yield strength $\sigma_y = 346.41\text{ MPa}$, the shear stress $\tau$ (in $\text{MPa}$) at which yielding begins according to the von Mises criterion is ___ $\text{MPa}$.

> [!faq]- 🔍 View D4 Mini Quiz Answers & Step-by-Step Solutions
>
> **Q1: (C) Ellipse**
> von Mises yield locus is an ellipse inclined at $45^\circ$ in the $\sigma_1\text{--}\sigma_2$ plane.
>
> **Q2: (B) 15.5% higher**
> $\frac{\tau_{y,\text{vM}}}{\tau_{y,\text{Tresca}}} = \frac{\sigma_y/\sqrt{3}}{\sigma_y/2} = \frac{2}{\sqrt{3}} \approx 1.1547 \implies \mathbf{+15.5\%}$.
>
> **Q3: (A) 1.00**
> In plane stress with opposite signs: $|\sigma_1 - \sigma_2| = |180 - (-120)| = 300\text{ MPa}$.
> $\text{FOS} = \frac{\sigma_y}{|\sigma_1 - \sigma_2|} = \frac{300}{300} = \mathbf{1.00}$.
>
> **Q4: (B) 261.5 MPa**
> $\sigma_{\text{vm}} = \sqrt{180^2 - (180)(-120) + (-120)^2} = \sqrt{32400 + 21600 + 14400} = \sqrt{68400} \approx \mathbf{261.53\text{ MPa} \approx 261.5\text{ MPa}}$.
>
> **Q5: 200**
> $\tau_y = \frac{\sigma_y}{\sqrt{3}} = \frac{346.41}{1.73205} = \mathbf{200.0\text{ MPa}}$.

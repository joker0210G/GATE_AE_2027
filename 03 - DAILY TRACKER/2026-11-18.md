## ⏰ HOUR 1 (0:00 – 1:00): CONCEPT SCAN & ACTIVE RECALL

### Step 1 — D1 Carry-Forward Review (5 min)
Quickly recall: Axial velocity relation $U = C_a(\tan\alpha + \tan\beta)$, Euler work $w = U C_a(\tan\beta_1 - \tan\beta_2) = c_p\Delta T_{0,\text{stage}}$, and flow coefficient $\phi = C_a/U$.

---

### Step 2 — Degree of Reaction ($R$) Definition & Derivation (20 min)
Open `04 - Axial Compressors`.

The **Degree of Reaction ($R$)** measures the proportion of static enthalpy (or pressure) rise that occurs in the **ROTOR** relative to the entire stage:

$$\boxed{R \equiv \frac{\Delta h_{\text{rotor}}}{\Delta h_{\text{stage}}} = \frac{h_2 - h_1}{h_{03} - h_{01}} = \frac{h_2 - h_1}{w}}$$

1. **Enthalpy Rise across Rotor:**
   From relative energy conservation in the rotor:
   $$h_1 + \frac{W_1^2}{2} = h_2 + \frac{W_2^2}{2} \implies h_2 - h_1 = \frac{W_1^2 - W_2^2}{2} = \frac{W_{\theta 1}^2 - W_{\theta 2}^2}{2} = \frac{C_a^2(\tan^2\beta_1 - \tan^2\beta_2)}{2}$$
2. **Master Degree of Reaction Formulas:**
   $$R = \frac{C_a^2(\tan^2\beta_1 - \tan^2\beta_2)}{2 U C_a(\tan\beta_1 - \tan\beta_2)} = \frac{C_a(\tan\beta_1 + \tan\beta_2)}{2U}$$
   $$\boxed{R = \frac{C_a}{2U}(\tan\beta_1 + \tan\beta_2) = \frac{\phi}{2}(\tan\beta_1 + \tan\beta_2)}$$
   $$\boxed{R = 1 - \frac{V_{\theta 1} + V_{\theta 2}}{2U} = 1 - \frac{C_a}{2U}(\tan\alpha_1 + \tan\alpha_2)}$$

---

### Step 3 — Special Cases of Degree of Reaction ($R$) (20 min)

```
                    THE SPECTRUM OF REACTION STAGES
       R = 0 (Impulse)               R = 0.5 (50% Reaction)         R = 1.0 (100% Reaction)
  • W_1 = W_2 (β_1 = β_2)        • Symmetrical Triangles        • V_1 = V_2 (α_1 = α_2)
  • No diffusion in rotor        • V_1 = W_2, V_2 = W_1         • Stator only turns flow
  • All Δp in stator             • α_1 = β_2, α_2 = β_1         • All Δp in rotor
                                 • Δh_rotor = Δh_stator = w/2
```

#### 🔑 The 50% Symmetrical Stage ($R = 0.5$):
Setting $R = 0.5 \implies \tan\beta_1 + \tan\beta_2 = \frac{U}{C_a}$. Since $\tan\alpha_1 + \tan\beta_1 = \frac{U}{C_a}$, we get:
$$\boxed{\alpha_1 = \beta_2, \qquad \beta_1 = \alpha_2, \qquad V_1 = W_2, \qquad W_1 = V_2}$$
- **Advantages of $R = 0.5$:** Symmetrical blading, equal aerodynamic loading, maximum stage efficiency $\eta_s$, and optimum boundary layer behavior.

---

### Step 4 — Work Done Factor & Stage Pressure Ratio (15 min)

1. **Work Done Factor ($\Omega$ or $\lambda < 1.0$):**
   - Due to 3D boundary layer growth along the casing and hub annulus, the axial velocity $C_a$ is non-uniform (higher in the core, lower at walls).
   - This reduces the effective flow deflection and work capability:
     $$\boxed{w_{\text{actual}} = \Omega \cdot U C_a(\tan\beta_1 - \tan\beta_2) = c_p \Delta T_{0,\text{stage}}}$$
   - Values: $\Omega \approx 0.98$ (Stage 1) $\to 0.93$ (Stage 4) $\to 0.85$ (Stage 8+).

2. **Stage Total-to-Total Pressure Ratio ($\pi_s$):**
   $$\boxed{\pi_s \equiv \frac{p_{03}}{p_{01}} = \left[1 + \frac{\eta_s \Delta T_{0,\text{stage}}}{T_{01}}\right]^{\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-1}} = \left[1 + \frac{\eta_s \Omega U C_a(\tan\beta_1 - \tan\beta_2)}{c_p T_{01}}\right]^{\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-1}}}$$
   - **Multi-Stage Overall Pressure Ratio:** $\mathbf{\pi_{\text{overall}} = \pi_{s1} \cdot \pi_{s2} \cdots \pi_{sN} = \prod_{i=1}^N \pi_{s,i}}$.

---

### Step 5 — Traps & Self-Check (5 min)
> 🔴 **Trap 1: Degree of Reaction Sign in Turbines vs. Compressors**
> For compressors: $R = 1 - \frac{V_{\theta 1} + V_{\theta 2}}{2U}$. For turbines: $R = 1 - \frac{V_{\theta 2} - V_{\theta 3}}{2U}$. Do not confuse the two!
> 🔴 **Trap 2: Work Done Factor Value**
> $\Omega \le 1.0$ ALWAYS (never $> 1$). It represents aerodynamic deficit due to annulus boundary layer blockage.

### Step 3 — D2 Mini Quiz (3:45–4:00) — 5 Questions (Timed 10 min)

> **Q2 (1 mark):** The Work Done Factor ($\Omega < 1.0$) in a multi-stage axial compressor accounts for:
> **(A)** Mechanical bearing friction
> **(B)** Reduction in work capacity due to 3D annulus boundary layer blockage
> **(C)** Blade cooling flow loss
> **(D)** Combustor pressure drop

> **Q3 (2 marks):** An axial compressor stage has flow coefficient $\phi = 0.60$, rotor inlet relative angle $\beta_1 = 50^\circ$, and rotor exit relative angle $\beta_2 = 20^\circ$. The Degree of Reaction $R$ is:
> **(A)** $0.355$ **(B)** $0.467$ **(C)** $0.555$ **(D)** $0.667$

> **Q4 (2 marks):** An axial compressor stage with stage efficiency $\eta_s = 0.90$ operates with $T_{01} = 290\text{ K}$ and produces a stage temperature rise $\Delta T_{0,\text{stage}} = 25\text{ K}$ ($\gamma = 1.4$). The stage total-to-total pressure ratio $\pi_s$ is:
> **(A)** $1.15$ **(B)** $1.30$ **(C)** $1.45$ **(D)** $1.60$

> **Q5 (2 marks NAT):** An axial compressor stage with $50\%$ degree of reaction has $U = 220\text{ m/s}$ and $C_a = 110\text{ m/s}$. Air enters the stator at $\alpha_2 = 50^\circ$. The relative flow angle $\beta_2$ at the rotor exit (in degrees) is ___$^\circ$ (round off to 1 decimal place).

> [!faq]- 🔍 View D2 Mini Quiz Answers & Step-by-Step Solutions
>
> **Q1: (C) α₁ = β₂ and β₁ = α₂**
> In a 50% reaction stage, the rotor and stator velocity triangles are mirror images (symmetrical).
>
> **Q2: (B) Reduction in work capacity due to 3D boundary layer blockage**
> Annulus boundary layers cause axial velocity peaking in the core, reducing blade deflection.
>
> **Q3: (B) 0.467**
> $R = \frac{\phi}{2}(\tan\beta_1 + \tan\beta_2) = \frac{0.60}{2}(\tan 50^\circ + \tan 20^\circ) = 0.30(1.19175 + 0.36397) = 0.30(1.5557) \approx \mathbf{0.4667 \approx 0.467}$.
>
> **Q4: (B) 1.30**
> $\pi_s = \left[1 + \frac{\eta_s \Delta T_0}{T_{01}}\right]^{\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-1}} = \left[1 + \frac{0.90(25)}{290}\right]^{3.5} = \left[1 + \frac{22.5}{290}\right]^{3.5} = (1 + 0.077586)^{3.5} = (1.077586)^{3.5} \approx \mathbf{1.2996 \approx 1.30}$.
>
> **Q5: 14.0**
> For $R = 0.5$, $\beta_1 = \alpha_2 = 50^\circ$.
> From fundamental geometry: $\tan\alpha_1 + \tan\beta_1 = \frac{U}{C_a} \implies \tan\alpha_1 + \tan 50^\circ = \frac{220}{110} = 2.0$.
> $\tan\alpha_1 = 2.0 - 1.19175 = 0.80825 \implies \alpha_1 = 38.95^\circ$... wait:
> For $R = 0.5$, $\beta_2 = \alpha_1$. Thus $\beta_2 = \alpha_1 \approx \mathbf{38.9^\circ}$ (or $\tan\beta_2 = \frac{U}{C_a} - \tan\alpha_2 = 2.0 - 1.19175 = 0.80825 \implies \mathbf{\beta_2 \approx 39.0^\circ}$).

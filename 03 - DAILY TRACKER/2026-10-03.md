## 07:45–08:45 · 🌅 Morning Flashcard Battery & GA

### 🌊 Set A — Exact Navier-Stokes Solutions (FC-31 → FC-45)

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-31 · Plane Couette Flow Profile
> **Question:** State the governing ODE, boundary conditions, and velocity profile for steady, incompressible laminar flow between infinite parallel plates where lower wall ($y=0$) is fixed and upper wall ($y=h$) moves at velocity $U$ with zero pressure gradient.
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > - Governing ODE: $\frac{d^2u}{dy^2} = 0$
> > - Boundary Conditions: $u(0) = 0, \quad u(h) = U$
> > - Velocity Profile: $u(y) = U \frac{y}{h}$ (linear profile)
> > ⚠️ **AIR-1 Trap:** The shear stress $\tau = \mu \frac{U}{h}$ is strictly **constant everywhere across the gap**.

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-32 · Vorticity in Simple Couette Flow
> **Question:** In simple plane Couette flow ($u = U y/h, v=0, w=0$), all streamlines are straight parallel lines. Is the flow irrotational? Calculate the vorticity vector $\vec{\omega}$.
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > - The flow is **rotational**: $\vec{\omega} = \nabla \times \vec{V} = \left(\frac{\partial v}{\partial x} - \frac{\partial u}{\partial y}\right)\hat{k} = -\frac{U}{h}\hat{k} \ne 0$.
> > ⚠️ **AIR-1 Trap:** "Straight streamlines imply irrotational flow" is a lethal myth! Vorticity depends on velocity gradients perpendicular to streamlines (shear), not streamline curvature.

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-33 · Generalized Couette Velocity Profile
> **Question:** Write the velocity profile for generalized Couette flow with upper wall moving at $+U$ at $y=h$, stationary lower wall at $y=0$, and an imposed constant streamwise pressure gradient $\frac{dp}{dx}$.
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > $$u(y) = U \frac{y}{h} + \frac{1}{2\mu}\left(-\frac{dp}{dx}\right) y (h - y)$$
> > In terms of dimensionless parameter $P = \frac{h^2}{2\mu U}\left(-\frac{dp}{dx}\right)$ and $\eta = \frac{y}{h}$:
> > $$\frac{u}{U} = \eta + P \eta (1 - \eta)$$
> > ⚠️ **AIR-1 Trap:** Notice the term is $\left(-\frac{dp}{dx}\right)$. A favorable pressure gradient ($dp/dx < 0$) produces a positive parabolic addition.

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-34 · Reverse Flow Onset in Generalized Couette
> **Question:** In generalized Couette flow, on which wall does reverse flow occur first under an adverse pressure gradient ($dp/dx > 0$), and what is the critical threshold value of $P$?
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > - Wall shear at lower fixed plate ($y=0$): $\left.\frac{du}{dy}\right|_{y=0} = \frac{U}{h}[1 + P]$.
> > - Backflow occurs when $\left.\frac{du}{dy}\right|_{y=0} \le 0 \implies \mathbf{P \le -1} \iff \mathbf{\frac{dp}{dx} \ge +\frac{2\mu U}{h^2}}$.
> > ⚠️ **AIR-1 Trap:** Reverse flow always starts at the **stationary wall** ($y=0$), never at the moving wall ($y=h$), because fluid near the stationary wall has zero base momentum!

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-35 · Plane Poiseuille Velocity Ratios
> **Question:** For laminar flow between fixed parallel plates (plane Poiseuille flow), what is the ratio of average velocity to maximum velocity ($u_{\text{avg}} / u_{\max}$)?
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > $$\frac{u_{\text{avg}}}{u_{\max}} = \mathbf{\frac{2}{3}} \approx 0.667$$
> > where $u_{\max} = \frac{h^2}{8\mu}\left(-\frac{dp}{dx}\right)$ and $u_{\text{avg}} = \frac{h^2}{12\mu}\left(-\frac{dp}{dx}\right)$.
> > ⚠️ **AIR-1 Trap:** Do not confuse with circular pipe flow where the ratio is $1/2$!

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-36 · Plane Poiseuille Wall Shear Stress
> **Question:** Express the wall shear stress $\tau_w$ in plane Poiseuille flow in terms of channel height $h$, pressure gradient $-\frac{dp}{dx}$, and average velocity $u_{\text{avg}}$.
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > $$\tau_w = \frac{h}{2}\left(-\frac{dp}{dx}\right) = \frac{4\mu u_{\max}}{h} = \frac{6\mu u_{\text{avg}}}{h}$$
> > ⚠️ **AIR-1 Trap:** At the channel centerline ($y = h/2$), shear stress is **identically zero** ($\tau = 0$).

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-37 · Plane Poiseuille Friction Factor
> **Question:** What is the Darcy friction factor $f$ for laminar flow between fixed parallel plates separated by gap $h$, using hydraulic diameter $D_h = 2h$?
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > $$f = \frac{96}{Re_{D_h}} \quad \text{where} \quad Re_{D_h} = \frac{\rho u_{\text{avg}} (2h)}{\mu}$$
> > ⚠️ **AIR-1 Trap:** Circular pipe is $64/Re$, while parallel plates channel is $96/Re$!

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-38 · Hagen-Poiseuille Velocity Profile
> **Question:** Write the velocity profile $u(r)$ for steady, fully developed laminar flow in a horizontal circular pipe of radius $R$ and diameter $D$.
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > $$u(r) = \frac{1}{4\mu}\left(-\frac{dp}{dx}\right)(R^2 - r^2) = u_{\max}\left(1 - \frac{r^2}{R^2}\right)$$
> > Centerline velocity: $u_{\max} = \frac{R^2}{4\mu}\left(-\frac{dp}{dx}\right) = \frac{D^2}{16\mu}\left(-\frac{dp}{dx}\right)$.
> > ⚠️ **AIR-1 Trap:** The profile is a paraboloid of revolution, with zero velocity at $r=R$ and maximum at $r=0$.

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-39 · Hagen-Poiseuille Velocity Ratio
> **Question:** What is the ratio of average velocity to maximum velocity ($u_{\text{avg}} / u_{\max}$) in Hagen-Poiseuille circular pipe flow?
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > $$\frac{u_{\text{avg}}}{u_{\max}} = \mathbf{\frac{1}{2}} = 0.500$$
> > ⚠️ **AIR-1 Trap:** Integration across circular area $\int u(r) 2\pi r dr$ introduces an extra factor of $r$, yielding $1/2$ instead of the 2D Cartesian value of $2/3$.

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-40 · Hagen-Poiseuille Law for Discharge
> **Question:** State the Hagen-Poiseuille equation for volumetric flow rate $Q$ through a circular pipe of diameter $D$ and length $L$ with pressure drop $\Delta p$.
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > $$Q = \frac{\pi R^4}{8\mu L}\Delta p = \frac{\pi D^4}{128\mu L}\Delta p$$
> > ⚠️ **AIR-1 Trap:** Notice $D^4$ dependence! If diameter increases by 10% ($1.1D$), flow rate increases by $1.1^4 = 1.4641$ (a 46.4% increase at constant $\Delta p$).

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-41 · Viscous Pressure Drop in Pipe
> **Question:** Express the pressure drop $\Delta p$ over length $L$ in Hagen-Poiseuille flow in terms of (a) average velocity $u_{\text{avg}}$, and (b) volumetric flow rate $Q$.
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > - (a) In terms of $u_{\text{avg}}$: $\Delta p = \frac{32\mu u_{\text{avg}} L}{D^2}$
> > - (b) In terms of $Q$: $\Delta p = \frac{128\mu Q L}{\pi D^4}$
> > ⚠️ **AIR-1 Trap:** Doubling pipe diameter at constant flow rate $Q$ reduces pressure drop by a factor of $2^4 = 16$. Doubling diameter at constant average velocity $u_{\text{avg}}$ reduces pressure drop by $2^2 = 4$.

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-42 · Pipe Wall Shear Stress
> **Question:** What is the wall shear stress $\tau_w$ in Hagen-Poiseuille circular pipe flow in terms of pressure gradient and average velocity?
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > $$\tau_w = \frac{R}{2}\left(-\frac{dp}{dx}\right) = \frac{D}{4}\left(-\frac{dp}{dx}\right) = \frac{4\mu u_{\max}}{R} = \frac{8\mu u_{\text{avg}}}{D}$$
> > ⚠️ **AIR-1 Trap:** Notice $\tau_w = \frac{D}{4}\frac{\Delta p}{L}$. A common error is writing $\tau_w = \frac{D}{2}\frac{\Delta p}{L}$ by forgetting the factor of 4 from force balance $\Delta p (\pi R^2) = \tau_w (2\pi R L) \implies \tau_w = \Delta p R / (2L) = \Delta p D / (4L)$.

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-43 · Darcy vs Fanning Friction Factor
> **Question:** Contrast the Darcy friction factor ($f$) and Fanning skin friction coefficient ($c_f$) for laminar circular pipe flow.
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > - Darcy friction factor: $f = \frac{4\tau_w}{\frac{1}{2}\rho u_{\text{avg}}^2} = \mathbf{\frac{64}{Re_D}}$
> > - Fanning friction factor: $c_f = \frac{\tau_w}{\frac{1}{2}\rho u_{\text{avg}}^2} = \frac{f}{4} = \mathbf{\frac{16}{Re_D}}$
> > ⚠️ **AIR-1 Trap:** Civil/Mechanical equations typically use Darcy $f$ ($64/Re$), while Aerodynamics and boundary layer literature frequently use Fanning $c_f$ ($16/Re$). Confusing them produces a factor of 4 error!

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-44 · Pumping Power Scaling Law
> **Question:** How does the pumping power $P_{\text{pump}}$ required to drive laminar pipe flow scale with pipe diameter $D$ at (a) fixed flow rate $Q$, and (b) fixed average velocity $u_{\text{avg}}$?
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > Pumping power: $P_{\text{pump}} = Q \Delta p$.
> > - (a) At fixed $Q$: $P_{\text{pump}} = Q \left(\frac{128\mu Q L}{\pi D^4}\right) \propto \mathbf{D^{-4}}$
> > - (b) At fixed $u_{\text{avg}}$: $P_{\text{pump}} = \left(\frac{\pi D^2}{4} u_{\text{avg}}\right) \left(\frac{32\mu u_{\text{avg}} L}{D^2}\right) \propto \mathbf{D^0} = \text{constant}$
> > ⚠️ **AIR-1 Trap:** Most industrial pipeline optimization problems keep $Q$ fixed, so increasing diameter drastically saves power ($D \times 2 \implies P \div 16$).

> [!question]- 🃏 Flashcard: FC-45 · Internal Shear Stress Distribution in Pipe
> **Question:** How does shear stress $\tau(r)$ vary across the pipe cross-section in Hagen-Poiseuille flow?
> > [!success]- **Answer & AIR 1 Traps:**
> > $$\tau(r) = \tau_w \frac{r}{R} = \frac{r}{2}\left(-\frac{dp}{dx}\right)$$
> > - At centerline ($r = 0$): $\tau = 0$ (velocity is maximum, gradient is zero).
> > - At wall ($r = R$): $\tau = \tau_w$ (maximum shear stress).
> > ⚠️ **AIR-1 Trap:** Even though velocity profile is parabolic, the shear stress distribution is strictly **linear with radius**!

> [!question]- 🧩 GA-01: Time & Work Rates
> **Problem:** Pump A can empty a fuel tank in 6 hours. Pump B can empty the same tank in 9 hours. If both pumps operate together, how many hours does it take to empty the tank?
> > [!success]- **Answer & Keystrokes:**
> > Combined rate: $\frac{1}{6} + \frac{1}{9} = \frac{3 + 2}{18} = \frac{5}{18}$ tank/hour.  
> > Time required: $T = \frac{18}{5} = \mathbf{3.6 \text{ hours}}$ (3 hours 36 minutes).

> [!question]- 🧩 GA-02: Spatial Reasoning / Cube Unfolding
> **Problem:** A standard cube with opposite faces summing to 7 is unfolded into a cross. If face 4 is at the center, and face 1 is adjacent to the left, which face is on the far right?
> > [!success]- **Answer:**
> > Opposite face to 1 is $7 - 1 = \mathbf{6}$. In an unfolded net, opposing faces are separated by one intervening square.

> [!question]- 🧩 GA-03: Percentage & Proportions
> **Problem:** The diameter of a circular nozzle is decreased by 20%. By what percentage does the exit dynamic pressure increase for an incompressible flow with constant volumetric flow rate?
> > [!success]- **Answer:**
> > $A \propto D^2$. New diameter $D_2 = 0.8 D_1 \implies A_2 = (0.8)^2 A_1 = 0.64 A_1$.  
> > Velocity $V_2 = Q/A_2 = V_1 / 0.64 = 1.5625 V_1$.  
> > Dynamic pressure $q = \frac{1}{2}\rho V^2 \propto V^2 \implies q_2 = (1.5625)^2 q_1 = 2.4414 q_1$.  
> > Percentage increase: $(2.4414 - 1) \times 100\% = \mathbf{144.14\%}$.

> [!question]- 🧩 GA-04: Syllogism & Logical Deduction
> **Statement 1:** All steady flows with zero velocity curl are potential flows.  
> **Statement 2:** Some potential flows have non-zero circulation.  
> **Conclusion:** Can a flow with zero curl around an obstacle have circulation?
> > [!success]- **Answer:**
> > **YES.** In a multiply connected domain (e.g. flow past a circular cylinder with a line vortex at origin), $\nabla \times \vec{V} = 0$ everywhere in the fluid, but circulation $\Gamma = \oint \vec{V}\cdot d\vec{r} \ne 0$ because the contour encloses the singularity.

> [!question]- 🧩 GA-05: Critical Reasoning / Data Sufficiency
> **Problem:** To find the mass flow rate of gas through a pipeline, which of the following is sufficient?  
> (I) Pipe diameter and fluid density.  
> (II) Volumetric flow rate and fluid temperature.  
> (III) Fluid density and volumetric flow rate.
> > [!success]- **Answer:**
> > **(III) alone is sufficient:** $\dot{m} = \rho Q$. (I) lacks velocity; (II) lacks pressure/density.

> [!question]- 🧩 GA-06: Vocabulary / Analogies
> **Analogy:** LAMINAR : STREAMLINE :: TURBULENT : ?  
> (A) STEADY  
> (B) EDDY  
> (C) VISCOUS  
> (D) COMPRESSIBLE
> > [!success]- **Answer:**
> > **(B) EDDY.** Laminar flow is characterized by smooth streamlines; turbulent flow is characterized by chaotic swirling eddies.

> [!question]- 🧩 GA-07: Numerical Series
> **Series:** $2, 6, 12, 20, 30, 42, ?$
> > [!success]- **Answer:**
> > Pattern: $1\times 2=2, 2\times 3=6, 3\times 4=12, 4\times 5=20, 5\times 6=30, 6\times 7=42, 7\times 8=\mathbf{56}$.

> [!question]- 🧩 GA-08: Speed, Distance & Time
> **Problem:** An inspection drone flies against a headwind of 10 m/s for 1200 m and returns with the tailwind. Its still-air cruising speed is 30 m/s. What is the total round-trip flight time?
> > [!success]- **Answer:**
> > Outbound speed: $30 - 10 = 20 \text{ m/s} \implies t_1 = 1200 / 20 = 60 \text{ s}$.  
> > Inbound speed: $30 + 10 = 40 \text{ m/s} \implies t_2 = 1200 / 40 = 30 \text{ s}$.  
> > Total time: $60 + 30 = \mathbf{90 \text{ seconds}}$ (1.5 minutes).

> [!question]- 🧩 GA-09: Probability / Dice
> **Problem:** What is the probability of rolling a sum of at least 10 with two standard six-sided dice?
> > [!success]- **Answer:**
> > Favorable outcomes: $(4,6), (5,5), (5,6), (6,4), (6,5), (6,6) \implies 6$ outcomes.  
> > Total outcomes: 36.  
> > Probability: $6/36 = \mathbf{1/6} \approx 0.1667$.

> [!question]- 🧩 GA-10: Error Spotting in Sentence
> **Sentence:** "Neither the flight director nor the instrumentation engineers was able to detect the pressure sensor failure before takeoff."
> > [!success]- **Answer:**
> > Error: Subject-verb agreement with "Neither... nor". The verb agrees with the closer subject ("instrumentation engineers" - plural). Correct verb is **"were able"**, not "was able".

### 🔨 WE-1 — Generalized Couette Flow with Incipient Wall Separation

#### Problem Statement
A viscous fluid ($\mu = 0.15 \text{ Pa}\cdot\text{s}$, $\rho = 850 \text{ kg/m}^3$) is sheared between two infinite horizontal parallel plates separated by gap $h = 4 \text{ mm}$. The lower plate ($y = 0$) is stationary, while the upper plate ($y = h$) moves with velocity $U = 3.0 \text{ m/s}$ in the $+x$ direction. An adverse pressure gradient $\frac{dp}{dx}$ is imposed along the channel.
1. Find the critical value of the pressure gradient $\left(\frac{dp}{dx}\right)_{\text{crit}}$ at which reverse flow initiates on the lower wall.
2. If the imposed pressure gradient is $\frac{dp}{dx} = +45,000 \text{ Pa/m}$ (which exceeds critical):
   - Calculate the velocity at $y = 1.0 \text{ mm}$.
   - Find the position $y_{\min}$ where the reverse velocity is maximum, and compute this minimum velocity $u_{\min}$.
   - Determine the net volumetric discharge per unit plate width $q$.

---

#### Step-by-Step Solution

##### Step 1: Critical Adverse Pressure Gradient
From generalized Couette velocity equation:
$$u(y) = U \frac{y}{h} + \frac{1}{2\mu}\left(-\frac{dp}{dx}\right) y (h - y)$$

Evaluating shear stress at lower fixed wall $y = 0$:
$$\left.\frac{du}{dy}\right|_{y=0} = \frac{U}{h} - \frac{h}{2\mu}\frac{dp}{dx}$$

For incipient backflow, $\left.\frac{du}{dy}\right|_{y=0} = 0$:
$$\left(\frac{dp}{dx}\right)_{\text{crit}} = \frac{2\mu U}{h^2} = \frac{2 \times 0.15 \times 3.0}{(0.004)^2} = \frac{0.90}{1.6 \times 10^{-5}} = \mathbf{+56,250 \text{ Pa/m}} = \mathbf{+56.25 \text{ kPa/m}}$$

##### Step 2: Evaluation at $\frac{dp}{dx} = +45,000 \text{ Pa/m}$
Wait! Check $P$:
$$P = \frac{h^2}{2\mu U}\left(-\frac{dp}{dx}\right) = \frac{1.6 \times 10^{-5}}{0.90} (-45000) = -0.80$$
Since $P = -0.80 > -1$, reverse flow does NOT occur for $+45 \text{ kPa/m}$!  
Let's analyze $\frac{dp}{dx} = +90,000 \text{ Pa/m}$ where $P = -1.60 < -1$ (true backflow):

Using $\frac{dp}{dx} = +90,000 \text{ Pa/m}$:
$$u(y) = 3.0 \left(\frac{y}{0.004}\right) - \frac{90000}{2 \times 0.15} y(0.004 - y) = 750 y - 300000 y(0.004 - y)$$
$$u(y) = 750 y - 1200 y + 300000 y^2 = -450 y + 300000 y^2$$

At $y = 1 \text{ mm} = 0.001 \text{ m}$:
$$u(0.001) = -450(0.001) + 300000(0.001)^2 = -0.450 + 0.300 = \mathbf{-0.150 \text{ m/s}}$$
*(Negative velocity confirms reverse flow near the lower wall!)*

##### Step 3: Location and Magnitude of Maximum Reverse Velocity
Set $\frac{du}{dy} = 0$:
$$\frac{du}{dy} = -450 + 600000 y = 0 \implies y_{\min} = \frac{450}{600000} = 7.5 \times 10^{-4} \text{ m} = \mathbf{0.75 \text{ mm}}$$

Minimum (maximum reverse) velocity:
$$u_{\min} = -450(0.00075) + 300000(0.00075)^2 = -0.3375 + 0.16875 = \mathbf{-0.16875 \text{ m/s}} \approx \mathbf{-0.169 \text{ m/s}}$$

##### Step 4: Net Volumetric Discharge per Unit Width
$$q = \int_0^h u(y) dy = \frac{1}{2} U h - \frac{h^3}{12\mu}\frac{dp}{dx}$$
First term: $\frac{1}{2} \times 3.0 \times 0.004 = 0.0060 \text{ m}^2\text{/s}$.  
Second term: $\frac{(0.004)^3}{12 \times 0.15} \times 90000 = \frac{6.4 \times 10^{-8}}{1.8} \times 90000 = 3.5556 \times 10^{-8} \times 90000 = 0.0032 \text{ m}^2\text{/s}$.  
$$q = 0.0060 - 0.0032 = \mathbf{0.0028 \text{ m}^2\text{/s}} = 2.80 \times 10^{-3} \text{ m}^2\text{/s}$$

> [!danger]- 🪤 Distractor Autopsy — WE-1
> - **Trap 1:** Assuming backflow occurs on the moving plate. Moving plate has $u(h) = +U = 3.0 \text{ m/s}$ locked by the no-slip condition.
> - **Trap 2:** Sign of pressure gradient. Favorable is $dp/dx < 0$, adverse is $dp/dx > 0$. Using negative sign incorrectly leads to $P = +1.6$, predicting an exaggerated forward jet.

### 🔨 WE-2 — Hagen-Poiseuille Viscous Pipe Flow & Pumping Power

#### Problem Statement
Hydraulic fluid ($\rho = 860 \text{ kg/m}^3$, dynamic viscosity $\mu = 0.040 \text{ Pa}\cdot\text{s}$) flows through a horizontal steel pipe of internal diameter $D = 20 \text{ mm}$ and length $L = 50 \text{ m}$. The measured pressure drop across the pipe is $\Delta p = 320 \text{ kPa}$.
Compute:
1. The centerline velocity $u_{\max}$ and average flow velocity $u_{\text{avg}}$.
2. The volumetric flow rate $Q$ in liters per minute (L/min).
3. The Reynolds number $Re_D$ and verify that the flow is laminar.
4. The wall shear stress $\tau_w$ and shear stress at radius $r = 5 \text{ mm}$.
5. The hydraulic pumping power $P_{\text{pump}}$ required.

---

#### Step-by-Step Solution & TCS Calculator Keystrokes

##### Step 1: Centerline and Average Velocity
Radius $R = D/2 = 0.010 \text{ m}$.  
Pressure gradient: $\left(-\frac{dp}{dx}\right) = \frac{\Delta p}{L} = \frac{320,000 \text{ Pa}}{50 \text{ m}} = 6400 \text{ Pa/m}$.

Centerline maximum velocity:
$$u_{\max} = \frac{R^2}{4\mu}\left(-\frac{dp}{dx}\right) = \frac{(0.010)^2 \times 6400}{4 \times 0.040} = \frac{10^{-4} \times 6400}{0.160} = \frac{0.640}{0.160} = \mathbf{4.000 \text{ m/s}}$$

Average velocity:
$$u_{\text{avg}} = \frac{1}{2} u_{\max} = \mathbf{2.000 \text{ m/s}}$$

##### Step 2: Volumetric Flow Rate
Pipe cross-sectional area:
$$A = \frac{\pi D^2}{4} = \frac{\pi \times (0.020)^2}{4} = 3.14159 \times 10^{-4} \text{ m}^2$$

Volumetric flow rate:
$$Q = A u_{\text{avg}} = (3.14159 \times 10^{-4}) \times 2.0 = 6.2832 \times 10^{-4} \text{ m}^3\text{/s}$$
Convert to L/min ($1 \text{ m}^3\text{/s} = 60,000 \text{ L/min}$):
$$Q = (6.2832 \times 10^{-4}) \times 60,000 = \mathbf{37.70 \text{ L/min}}$$

##### Step 3: Reynolds Number Verification
$$Re_D = \frac{\rho u_{\text{avg}} D}{\mu} = \frac{860 \times 2.0 \times 0.020}{0.040} = \frac{34.40}{0.040} = \mathbf{860.0}$$
Since $Re_D = 860 < 2300$, the flow is unconditionally laminar.

##### Step 4: Wall Shear Stress & Radial Variation
Wall shear stress:
$$\tau_w = \frac{D}{4}\left(-\frac{dp}{dx}\right) = \frac{0.020}{4} \times 6400 = 0.005 \times 6400 = \mathbf{32.00 \text{ Pa}}$$

At $r = 5 \text{ mm} = 0.005 \text{ m}$ (midway between center and wall, $r/R = 0.5$):
$$\tau(r) = \tau_w \frac{r}{R} = 32.00 \times \frac{0.005}{0.010} = \mathbf{16.00 \text{ Pa}}$$

##### Step 5: Pumping Power
$$P_{\text{pump}} = Q \Delta p = (6.28318 \times 10^{-4} \text{ m}^3\text{/s}) \times (320,000 \text{ N/m}^2) = \mathbf{201.06 \text{ W}}$$

##### Calculator Ledger (MS/MR):
1. `0.01` `x²` `* 6400 / 0.16 =` → `4.00` → `MS` ($u_{\max}$)
2. `MR / 2 =` → `2.00` (display: $u_{\text{avg}}$)
3. `* pi * 0.01 x² =` → `0.000628318` → `MS` ($Q$)
4. `MR * 320000 =` → `201.06` ✅

### 🔨 WE-3 — Stratified Two-Fluid Couette Flow (Immiscible Viscous Layers)

#### Problem Statement
Two immiscible, incompressible viscous fluids are layered between two horizontal parallel plates separated by total distance $2h = 20 \text{ mm}$.  
- Layer 1 (lower, $0 \le y \le h$): Water ($\mu_1 = 1.0 \times 10^{-3} \text{ Pa}\cdot\text{s}$, thickness $h = 10 \text{ mm}$).
- Layer 2 (upper, $h \le y \le 2h$): Engine oil ($\mu_2 = 1.0 \times 10^{-1} \text{ Pa}\cdot\text{s} = 100 \mu_1$, thickness $h = 10 \text{ mm}$).  
Lower plate ($y = 0$) is fixed. Upper plate ($y = 2h$) moves at velocity $U = 10 \text{ m/s}$ in $+x$. Pressure gradient is zero ($\frac{dp}{dx} = 0$).

Determine:
1. The interface velocity $u_{\text{int}}$ at $y = h$.
2. The constant shear stress $\tau$ transmitted through both fluids.
3. The ratio of velocity gradients $\left(\frac{du_1}{dy}\right) / \left(\frac{du_2}{dy}\right)$.

---

#### Step-by-Step Solution

##### Step 1: Physics of the Interface
Since $\frac{dp}{dx} = 0$, in both layers the Navier-Stokes equation reduces to $\frac{d^2u}{dy^2} = 0$, which yields linear velocity profiles in each layer:
$$u_1(y) = C_1 y \quad (0 \le y \le h, \text{ since } u_1(0) = 0)$$
$$u_2(y) = C_2 (y - h) + u_{\text{int}} \quad (h \le y \le 2h)$$

At the upper boundary $y = 2h$:
$$u_2(2h) = U \implies C_2 h + u_{\text{int}} = U \implies C_2 = \frac{U - u_{\text{int}}}{h}$$

##### Step 2: Interfacial Shear Stress Balance
For steady flow with no accelerating surface mass, the shear stress must be continuous across the interface:
$$\tau_1 = \tau_2 = \tau$$
$$\mu_1 \frac{du_1}{dy} = \mu_2 \frac{du_2}{dy} \implies \mu_1 \left(\frac{u_{\text{int}}}{h}\right) = \mu_2 \left(\frac{U - u_{\text{int}}}{h}\right)$$

##### Step 3: Interface Velocity Derivation
Cancelling $h$:
$$\mu_1 u_{\text{int}} = \mu_2 U - \mu_2 u_{\text{int}} \implies (\mu_1 + \mu_2) u_{\text{int}} = \mu_2 U$$
$$\bbox[12px,border:2px solid #2196F3,background-color:#0d1117]{ u_{\text{int}} = U \frac{\mu_2}{\mu_1 + \mu_2} = U \frac{1}{\frac{\mu_1}{\mu_2} + 1} }$$

Substitute numerical values ($\mu_2 = 100 \mu_1$, $U = 10 \text{ m/s}$):
$$u_{\text{int}} = 10 \times \frac{100}{1 + 100} = 10 \times \frac{100}{101} = \mathbf{9.901 \text{ m/s}}$$

##### Step 4: Velocity Gradients & Shear Stress
In Layer 1 (Water, low viscosity):
$$\frac{du_1}{dy} = \frac{u_{\text{int}}}{h} = \frac{9.901}{0.010} = \mathbf{990.1 \text{ s}^{-1}}$$

In Layer 2 (Oil, high viscosity):
$$\frac{du_2}{dy} = \frac{U - u_{\text{int}}}{h} = \frac{10 - 9.901}{0.010} = \frac{0.09901}{0.010} = \mathbf{9.901 \text{ s}^{-1}}$$

Ratio of velocity gradients:
$$\frac{du_1/dy}{du_2/dy} = \frac{\mu_2}{\mu_1} = \mathbf{100}$$

Shear stress everywhere:
$$\tau = \mu_1 \frac{du_1}{dy} = 10^{-3} \times 990.1 = \mathbf{0.9901 \text{ Pa}}$$
*(Check: $\mu_2 \frac{du_2}{dy} = 0.10 \times 9.901 = 0.9901 \text{ Pa}$ ✅)*

> [!danger]- 🪤 Distractor Autopsy — WE-3
> - **The Intuition Trap:** Aspirants intuitively assume the interface velocity is halfway ($U/2 = 5 \text{ m/s}$). That only holds if both fluids have identical viscosity ($\mu_1 = \mu_2$). The higher viscosity fluid acts almost like a rigid solid slab, dragging the interface at nearly plate velocity ($99\%$ of $U$)!

> [!question]- 📝 TA-06: Incipient Backflow in Couette Flow
> **Question (MSQ):** In generalized Couette flow between a fixed bottom wall ($y=0$) and top wall ($y=h$) moving at speed $U$ under pressure gradient $dp/dx$:
> (A) Reverse flow initiates when $dp/dx = +2\mu U / h^2$.  
> (B) Reverse flow always initiates at the top moving wall.  
> (C) The shear stress on the moving wall is always greater than that on the fixed wall when $dp/dx > 0$.  
> (D) The velocity profile is purely linear when $dp/dx = 0$.
> > [!success]- **Solution:**
> > - (A) is TRUE: $\left.\frac{du}{dy}\right|_{y=0} = \frac{U}{h} - \frac{h}{2\mu}\frac{dp}{dx} = 0 \implies \frac{dp}{dx} = \frac{2\mu U}{h^2}$.
> > - (B) is FALSE: Backflow begins on the stationary wall ($y=0$).
> > - (C) is TRUE: $\tau(h) = \mu \frac{U}{h} + \frac{h}{2}\frac{dp}{dx}$ while $\tau(0) = \mu \frac{U}{h} - \frac{h}{2}\frac{dp}{dx}$. When $dp/dx > 0$, $\tau(h) > \tau(0)$.
> > - (D) is TRUE: When $dp/dx = 0$, $u(y) = U y/h$.  
> > **Correct Options: (A), (C), (D).**

> [!question]- 📝 TA-07: Darcy Friction Factor from Pressure Loss
> **Question (NAT):** Water ($\rho = 1000 \text{ kg/m}^3, \mu = 10^{-3} \text{ Pa}\cdot\text{s}$) flows through a 25 mm pipe with average velocity $0.080 \text{ m/s}$. The Darcy friction factor $f$ is ________. [Round to 3 decimal places]
> > [!success]- **Solution & Official IIT Tolerance:**
> > $Re_D = \frac{1000 \times 0.080 \times 0.025}{10^{-3}} = 2000$.  
> > Laminar regime ($Re \le 2300$).  
> > $f = \frac{64}{Re_D} = \frac{64}{2000} = \mathbf{0.032}$.  
> > **Official Tolerance:** $[0.031, 0.033]$

> [!question]- 📝 TA-08: Radial Location Where Local Velocity Equals Average Velocity
> **Question (NAT):** In Hagen-Poiseuille laminar circular pipe flow of radius $R$, the radial location $r$ where the local velocity equals the cross-sectional average velocity ($u(r) = u_{\text{avg}}$) is $k R$. The value of $k$ is ________. [Round to 3 decimal places]
> > [!success]- **Solution & Official IIT Tolerance:**
> > Velocity profile: $u(r) = u_{\max}\left(1 - \frac{r^2}{R^2}\right)$.  
> > Set $u(r) = u_{\text{avg}} = \frac{1}{2} u_{\max}$:
> > $$\frac{1}{2} u_{\max} = u_{\max}\left(1 - \frac{r^2}{R^2}\right) \implies 1 - \frac{r^2}{R^2} = 0.5 \implies \frac{r^2}{R^2} = 0.5$$
> > $$r = R \sqrt{0.5} = \frac{R}{\sqrt{2}} \approx \mathbf{0.707 R} \implies k = \mathbf{0.707}$$
> > **Official Tolerance:** $[0.705, 0.709]$

> [!question]- 📝 TA-09: Discharge Ratio Between Laminar Pipes
> **Question (MCQ):** Two identical pumps deliver oil through two pipes of equal length. Pipe 1 has diameter $D$, and Pipe 2 has diameter $1.5 D$. If the pressure drop across both pipes is identical and flow is laminar, the ratio of flow rates $Q_2 / Q_1$ is:

> (B) 2.25  
> (C) 3.38  
> (D) 5.06
> > [!success]- **Solution:**
> > Hagen-Poiseuille: $Q \propto D^4$ for fixed $\Delta p, L, \mu$.

> [!question]- 📝 TA-10: Wall Shear Stress at Specified Pressure Drop
> **Question (NAT):** In a 40 mm diameter horizontal pipe of length 30 m carrying fuel oil, the measured pressure drop is 48 kPa. The wall shear stress $\tau_w$ in Pa is ________. [Round to integer]
> > [!success]- **Solution & Official IIT Tolerance:**

> [!question]- 📝 TA-11: Kinetic Energy Correction Factor in Hagen-Poiseuille Flow
> **Question (NAT):** The kinetic energy correction factor $\alpha = \frac{1}{A u_{\text{avg}}^3}\iint u^3 dA$ for fully developed laminar circular pipe flow is ________. [Round to 1 decimal place]
> > [!success]- **Solution & Official IIT Tolerance:**
> > Velocity profile: $u(r) = 2 u_{\text{avg}}(1 - \eta^2)$ where $\eta = r/R$.  
> > Area element: $dA = 2\pi r dr = 2\pi R^2 \eta d\eta$. Area $A = \pi R^2$.  
> > $$\iint u^3 dA = \int_0^1 [2 u_{\text{avg}}(1 - \eta^2)]^3 (2\pi R^2 \eta d\eta) = 16\pi R^2 u_{\text{avg}}^3 \int_0^1 (1 - \eta^2)^3 \eta d\eta$$
> > Substitute $z = \eta^2, dz = 2\eta d\eta$:  
> > $$\int_0^1 (1-z)^3 \frac{dz}{2} = \frac{1}{2} \left[ -\frac{(1-z)^4}{4} \right]_0^1 = \frac{1}{8}$$
> > $$\iint u^3 dA = 16\pi R^2 u_{\text{avg}}^3 \left(\frac{1}{8}\right) = 2\pi R^2 u_{\text{avg}}^3 = 2 A u_{\text{avg}}^3$$
> > $$\alpha = \frac{2 A u_{\text{avg}}^3}{A u_{\text{avg}}^3} = \mathbf{2.0}$$
> > **Official Tolerance:** $[1.95, 2.05]$  
> > *(Note: For turbulent pipe flow, $\alpha \approx 1.05$.)*

> [!question]- 📝 TA-12: Momentum Flux Correction Factor in Hagen-Poiseuille Flow
> **Question (NAT):** The momentum flux correction factor $\beta = \frac{1}{A u_{\text{avg}}^2}\iint u^2 dA$ for Hagen-Poiseuille flow is expressed as a fraction $a/b$ in lowest terms. The value of $\beta$ as a decimal is ________. [Round to 2 decimal places]
> > [!success]- **Solution & Official IIT Tolerance:**
> > $$\iint u^2 dA = \int_0^1 [2 u_{\text{avg}}(1 - \eta^2)]^2 (2\pi R^2 \eta d\eta) = 8\pi R^2 u_{\text{avg}}^2 \int_0^1 (1 - 2\eta^2 + \eta^4) \eta d\eta$$
> > Let $z = \eta^2$: $\int_0^1 (1 - 2z + z^2)\frac{dz}{2} = \frac{1}{2}[1 - 1 + 1/3] = \frac{1}{6}$.  
> > $$\iint u^2 dA = 8 A u_{\text{avg}}^2 \left(\frac{1}{6}\right) = \frac{4}{3} A u_{\text{avg}}^2 \implies \beta = \mathbf{\frac{4}{3}} \approx \mathbf{1.33}$$
> > **Official Tolerance:** $[1.32, 1.34]$

> [!question]- 🧮 TM-01: Parameter for Unique Solution
> **Question (MCQ):** The system of linear equations:
> $$x + y + z = 6$$
> $$x + 2y + 3z = 10$$
> $$x + 2y + \lambda z = \mu$$
> has a UNIQUE solution if:
> (A) $\lambda = 3, \mu = 10$  
> (B) $\lambda \ne 3, \mu \in \mathbb{R}$  
> (C) $\lambda = 3, \mu \ne 10$  
> (D) $\lambda \ne 3, \mu = 10$ only
> > [!success]- **Solution:**
> > Set up augmented matrix and apply row operations ($R_2 \to R_2 - R_1, R_3 \to R_3 - R_2$):
> > $$\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 & 1 & | & 6 \\ 0 & 1 & 2 & | & 4 \\ 0 & 0 & \lambda - 3 & | & \mu - 10 \end{bmatrix}$$
> > For a unique solution, $\text{rank}(A) = \text{rank}(A|B) = 3$ (number of unknowns).  
> > This requires $\lambda - 3 \ne 0 \implies \mathbf{\lambda \ne 3}$, while $\mu$ can take **any real value**.  
> > **Correct Option: (B).**

> [!question]- 🧮 TM-02: Parameter for Infinitely Many Solutions
> **Question (NAT):** In the system of equations from TM-01, the system has INFINITELY MANY solutions when $\lambda = 3$ and $\mu =$ ________.
> > [!success]- **Answer:**
> > For infinite solutions, $\text{rank}(A) = \text{rank}(A|B) < 3$.  
> > Third row must vanish: $\lambda - 3 = 0 \implies \lambda = 3$ and $\mu - 10 = 0 \implies \mathbf{\mu = 10}$.

> [!question]- 🧮 TM-03: Parameter for Inconsistent System (No Solution)

> [!question]- 🧮 TM-07: Consistency of Non-Square System
> **Question (MSQ):** Consider a system of $m$ linear equations in $n$ unknowns ($AX = B$):
> (A) If $m < n$, the system can never have a unique solution.  
> (B) If $m > n$, the system can never have infinitely many solutions.  
> (C) The system is consistent if and only if $B$ lies in the column space of $A$.  
> (D) If $\text{rank}(A) = m$, the system is consistent for every vector $B$.
> > [!success]- **Solution:**
> > - (A) is TRUE: When unknowns exceed equations, rank cannot equal $n$, so a unique solution is impossible.
> > - (B) is FALSE: If dependent equations are added, an underdetermined sub-system can still have infinitely many solutions.
> > - (C) is TRUE: $AX$ is a linear combination of columns of $A$.
> > - (D) is TRUE: If row rank equals $m$, the augmented matrix rank cannot exceed $m$, so $\text{rank}(A|B) = \text{rank}(A) = m$ always.  
> > **Correct Options: (A), (C), (D).**
